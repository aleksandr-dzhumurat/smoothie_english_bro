## Tips on suggesting solutions and providing arguments

* **Tip 1** Make sure you understand the business task — your solution should be aimed at solving a certain problem that the stakeholder wants your help with.
* **Tip 2** Acknowledge your stakeholder’s opinion if your idea or solution differs from the one they’re suggesting.
* **Tip 3** Express certainty, expertise, and objectivity. It’s OK to use power words like *highly effective* or *significantly improve*, but when they’re backed up with data or figures.
* **Tip 4** Provide examples and/or references to best practices if applicable.
* **Tip 5** To sound convincing, clearly explain the benefits of your suggestions. When discussing several options, point out their drawbacks as well, if any, so the stakeholder can make an informed decision.

## Tips on writing a well-structured follow-up email

* **Tip 1** Keep your emails brief and to the point. Make sure the sections or parts your email consists of are easy to notice and the information is easy to find.
* **Tip 2** Check your email for mistakes and typos before sending it. It may be a good idea to run it through a spell checker.
* **Tip 3** Read your email once again before sending it and make sure you’ve covered all the necessary items.
* **Tip 4** Don’t mix very formal and informal styles in one email.
* **Tip 5** Don’t stick to a formal tone even if everyone else in the company uses a more relaxed tone.

**Tip 1**

Each person should have a chance to contribute. When brainstorming, all sorts of ideas are invited as long as they deal with the main area of focus.

**Tip 2**

Everyone should have a chance to speak freely, but make sure you wait for the right moment to interrupt your peer if you want to link some valid point to their idea.

**Tip 3**

A good practice is to avoid criticizing other people’s ideas. Before expressing your disagreement you can say something like

*“That’s a valid point, but…”*

.

**Tip 4**

It’s great if you can build on other people’s ideas. Often an idea suggested by one person can trigger a bigger or better idea by another person.

**Tip 5**

The ideas should lead to making a decision or identifying next steps.

## Tips on demonstrating critical thinking skills when answering a behavioural question

When interviewers ask questions targeting critical thinking skills they are interested in the following:

- Your ability to provide arguments. Constructing logical arguments and using them to negotiate with others is very important for an analyst’s job.
- Your ability to choose the right style of communication. Critical thinking means that you understand the needs of other and can choose the right type of communication for a particular person or situation.
- Your approach to making decisions. Strong critical thinking skills help you choose the most valid arguments, identify potential problems and benefits and make decisions based on that.

There are a few tips that can help you handle such questions and demonstrate your critical thinking skills.

**Tip 1. Make sure you know what to demonstrate**

Critical thinking skills may include things like identifying others’ needs, clearly communicating your ideas, choosing the correct type of communication, convincing other people, highlighting the positive aspects of an option or solution. Think of the cases that include some of these aspects.

**Tip 2. Research the company culture**

Try to find out more about the company culture and values to choose the most relevant case from your experience. Besides demonstrating your skills, it will also show the interviewer that you will fit well into the organization.

**Tip 3. Choose a specific example**

Situations that require critical thinking skills may be participating in a brainstorming session, hypothesizing, making a difficult decision, resolving a disagreement, reaching an agreement or compromise, convincing a stakeholder.

**Tip 4. Focus on your actions**

Describe how you gathered, analyzed, prioritized, and communicated information and how that helped you make decisions or achieve results. Make sure your actions are logical, specific, and relevant to the question you are answering.

**Tip 5. Use the STAR method**

It makes your answer more structured and allows you to give concrete examples. That makes it easier for an interviewer to follow your story and appreciate your skills.
