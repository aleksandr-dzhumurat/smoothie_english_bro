## Doing Small Talk

### Greeting People and Replying to Greetings
- Hi (name), how are you? — Not bad, thanks. How about you?
- Hi (name), how are you doing? — Doing good, thank you.
- How's it going? — Fine/Well, thank you.

### Beginning Small Talk
- What's the weather like in (city)?
- How's the weather (where you are/over there)?
- How's your (onboarding/project/day) going?
- What time is it there?
- How's your (first working week/day) been so far?
- Are you working from (home/the office)?
- Where are you based?
- Have you ever been to (city/country)?
- Are you an hour ahead of us?
- By the way, it looks really sunny where you are.

### Giving Short Comments
- Great! / Oh, I see / Yeah / Right.
- Happy to hear that. / I am happy to hear that.
- What a pain/bummer. / Same here…

### Giving Longer Comments
- I'd love to travel there one day, it's on my list.
- We're (three hours) ahead of you.
- It's been so hot these days.

### Follow-Up Questions
- How long have you lived there?
- Whereabouts in (Zurich) do you live?

### Ending Small Talk
- Let's get started, shall we?
- Let's kick off.
- Let's kick off with an overview of the project.

## Building Rapport

### Finding Common Ground
- Are you keen on (verb+*ing*)?
- How did you get into (verb+*ing*)?
- We're having a/an (name of event). Are you gonna come?
- [Are you] going anywhere nice?
- What are you up to this weekend?

### Talking About Interests
- I'm (not) really into (yoga).
- I'm really fond of (cooking).
- It's a great stress-buster.
- I'm [a bit of] a/an (opera/baseball) fan.
- I enjoy going out.

### Talking About Plans
- I haven't got anything booked.
- I'm taking a few days off.
- I'm gonna have a staycation myself later this year.
- Have you been able to get away for a break this year?

### Example Dialogues

**Dialogue 1**
> **A:** You're telling me! I enjoy going out with the team. Well, catching up is always good, you know.
> **B:** Definitely. I don't wanna miss out on that paintball game. It was so much fun last time we played!
> **A:** Well, I don't have an opera to look forward to! I'll be working I guess.

**Dialogue 2**
> **Jan:** Fine, thanks. So Rob, what did you do before joining us?
> **Rob:** Well, I worked as a data analyst for a medical services company. But now, as part of your team, **I'm going to be in charge of the data management system on the project**.
> **Jan:** Sounds great, we have a really great team. I'm sure your past work experience will help you catch up easily in your new workplace.
> **Rob:** And what do you do at the company, Jan?
> **Jan:** Oh, I'm a UX designer, and I've been part of this project for almost two years.
> **Rob:** Sounds great! What do you think of it?
> **Jan:** Well, it's not bad. I work closely with the product team. The team's really nice but it can get a bit stressful when you have to meet deadlines, you know.
> **Rob:** Yeah, meeting deadlines can be tough.

**Dialogue 3**
> **Nick:** Morning everyone! Sorry to keep you waiting. I was tied up in a meeting with our new customer and it kept me a bit longer than planned. Erm, let's get started, shall we?

## Giving a Self-Presentation

### Talking About Your Role and Responsibilities
- I'm a (position) with (more than/over) … years of professional experience.
- I'm currently a (position) at (company) where I …

### Talking About Your Background and Work Experience
- I started out as a (position) working at (company).
- My background is in (area).
- I've always had an interest in …
- Previously, I worked at (company).

### Talking About Your Skills and Knowledge
- I have a(n) (extensive/good/thorough/deep) knowledge of …
- I'm (also) proficient in (something/verb+*ing*).

### Saying How You Feel About the Job
- I think my skills and experience make me a good fit for the job.

## Using the STAR Framework to Talk About Past Experiences

### Situation
- On my previous project …
- In my last job, I worked with …
- When I was working on the ABC project, …

### Task
- The customer wanted us to …
- I had to … / The task was to …

### Action
- I spent a few days (collecting data).
- I developed …
- So, what I did was I (investigated the problem).

### Result
- As a result, … / So, …
- I learned a lot from the experience.

## Grammar Notes

### Need vs Should

The verb **need** describes a responsibility — something necessary to do. After need, use **to + infinitive**.

The verb **should** is used for advice, followed by bare infinitive (without to).

### Suggestions

**I would suggest** + verb+*ing*
- ✗ *I would suggest to build a vertical dashboard.*
- ✓ *I would suggest **building** a vertical dashboard.*

The verbs **propose** and **recommend** follow the same pattern.

**What I/we could do is** + verb
- ✓ *What we could do is **add** these tables to the bottom of the tab.*

### Conditional Tenses

*   **1st** — If I commit, I will do anything. [If + Present S., Future S.]
*   **2nd** — If I committed, I would do anything. [If + Past S., would..]

**Imagined conditions:**
1. **If the weather improves, we'll go for a walk.** = `If - Present Simple, Future.` (possible/likely)
2. **If the weather improved, we could go for a walk.** = `If - Past Simple, could/should/would/might + V1` (unlikely)
3. **If the weather had improved, we could have gone for a walk.** = `If Past Perfect, modal + Present Perfect` (impossible)

### "Couldn't help but + bare infinitive"

Means **"не мог не сделать что-то"** / "не удержался от".

- She couldn't help but laugh. — Она не могла не засмеяться.
- He couldn't help but notice. — Он не мог не заметить.

## Working on a Team — Quick Phrases

*   I'll talk to you about this later then.
*   My priority was the user research, so I mainly worked on that.
*   Yesterday I was working on data quality checks.
*   I'm afraid I'm blocked on this research task. I can't move on until I hear from Mark.
*   Let me know if you need a hand with the report. Let me know how that goes, OK?
*   I've already discussed it with the stakeholders, so I'm on track.
*   So yesterday I was working on the revenue forecasting piece but ran into numerous issues with setting up an AWS environment. I've already reached out to the engineer so that we can decide on our next steps.
*   I'm going to schedule a call with Elena later this week so that we can agree on the strategy. If anyone wants to join us, hit me up on Slack.
*   I've identified the suboptimal piece of the SQL query, and now I'm going to get in touch with the system's DBA.

## Conflict Resolution

### Focusing on the Positive Aspect
*   Despite our differences, we managed to reach a compromise.
*   Despite the fact that we had different **opinions on the matter**, we managed to reach a compromise.
*   Although we didn't agree on some points, we were able to reach a compromise.

### Expressing Your Opinion
*   I felt that insufficient data could possibly affect the result.
*   I believe that it isn't the best idea to use that methodology.

### Rephrasing (softening language)
*   They were a wrong choice ➞ They weren't the best choice.
*   The customer's requirements weren't reasonable enough.
*   The deadline wasn't quite realistic to meet.
*   The dataset wasn't of good quality which made our solution not so reliable.

### Using Softening Phrases
*   It seemed that this happened because the roles weren't quite clear.
*   It looked like the team didn't understand my point of view.
*   I knew it could possibly lead to a negative result.

### Key Considerations
*   Reaching out to someone personally and trying to find ways to agree on an optimal strategy is always the best thing to do when there is a disagreement.
*   What makes great team players is the ability to put their egos aside and work towards the common goal.
*   When facing challenging situations, you might act on impulse and offend a teammate.
