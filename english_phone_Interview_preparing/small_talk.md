# Need vs Should

In sentence 1, the speaker is describing Helen’s responsibility, something that is necessary for her to do (to talk to the stakeholders). The verb **need** is often used in this context. After need, you use **to + infinitive**.

The verb **should** is very often used for advice, and it’s followed by bare infinitive (infinitive without to). So, if you look at the phrase that follows (sit together), you know that your guess is probably correct and the speaker is using the verb should.

# Suggestions

Note the verb patterns used after the following phrases:

## *I would suggest* + verb+*ing*

- ✗ *I would suggest to build a vertical dashboard.*
- ✓ *I would suggest **building** a vertical dashboard.*

The verbs **propose** and **recommend** follow the same pattern.

## *What I/we could do is* + verb

- ✓ *What we could do is **add** these tables to the bottom of the tab.*

# Conditional tenses

More detailed:

*   **1st** - If I commit, I will do anything. [If + Present S., I Future S.]
*   **2nd** - If I committed, I would do anything. [If + Past S., I would..]

*   If the weather is warm, we will go out.
*   If the weather was warm, we would go out.

**Explanation**

**Grammar - Imagined conditions**

There are different types of conditions. Some are (1) *possible* or *likely* (возможно или вероятно), others are (2) *unlikely* (маловероятно), and others are (3) *impossible* (невозможно):

1.  **If the weather improves, we'll go for a walk.** = `If - Present Simple, Future.`
    *   It is possible or likely that the weather will improve.
2.  **If the weather improved, we could go for a walk.** = `If - Past Simple, could / should / would / might + V1`
    *   It is not likely that the weather will improve.
3.  **If the weather had improved, we could have gone for a walk.**
    *   The weather did not improve - fine weather is therefore an impossible condition.
    *   `If Past Perfect, modal + Present Perfect`

## Conflict resolution

### Focusing on the positive aspect

*   Despite our differences, we managed to reach a compromise.
*   Despite the fact that we had different **opinions on the matter**, we managed to reach a compromise.
*   Although we didn’t agree on some points, we were able to reach a compromise.
*   Despite having a lot of obstacles to overcome, we managed to implement a desired solution.
*   Although I was new to the project, I managed to choose an appropriate process to perform the analysis.

### Expressing your opinion

*   I felt that insufficient data could possibly affect the result.
*   I believe that it isn’t the best idea to use that methodology.

### Rephrasing

*   They were a wrong choice ➞ They weren’t the best choice.
*   The customer’s requirements weren’t reasonable enough.
*   The team wasn’t ready to make a switch to the new approach at that moment.
*   I knew that it wasn’t the best choice/option to proceed without the experiment stage.
*   The deadline wasn’t quite realistic to meet.
*   The dataset wasn’t of good quality which made our solution not so reliable.

### Using softening phrases

*   It seemed that this happened because the roles weren’t quite clear.
*   It looked like the team didn’t understand my point of view.
*   I knew it could possibly lead to a negative result.
*   I knew that if we continued like that, we could possibly run into serious problems later.

*   It looked like there was a flaw in the strategy, and I expressed concerns about it.
*   Despite my manager’s solid reasoning, I felt that it was a temporary measure which could affect the result.
*   We tried to find ways to fix the issue.
*   My colleagues agreed on the new strategy.
*   I was really glad that we reached a compromise.

### Key Considerations

*   It’s normal for a team to face challenges. However, if a team seems to have a lot of conflict, there might be an issue with the management.
*   Minor disagreements don’t affect a team negatively if they in the end have a positive outcome.
*   You need to understand that people express their concerns not because they want to be negative but because they want to warn you about a risk for the business.
*   Reaching out to someone personally and trying to find ways to agree on an optimal strategy is always the best thing to do when there is a disagreement.
*   I think a lack of understanding was the main cause of our conflict. So, after we had heard each other, we were able to reach a compromise.
*   Remember, it’s important to find ways not to let any conflict escalate — that’s why the best team players are also great mediators.
*   Creating a friendly environment is possible only if people in the team are ready to face challenges together and are eager to find ways to reach a compromise. One can’t exist without the other.
*   When everyone agrees on the course of action, it’s pretty easy to maintain a good relationship. But everything changes when your teammates don’t see eye to eye with you.
*   What makes great team players is the ability to put their egos aside and work towards the common goal. This type of approach always has a positive outcome.
*   When facing challenging situations, you might act on impulse and offend a teammate. Lacking it can seriously affect your interaction with the team.
