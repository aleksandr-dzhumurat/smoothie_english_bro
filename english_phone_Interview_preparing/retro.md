**Tip 1**

Invest time in preparation for retros. Think over the positives and what contributed to them; analyze the negatives and what they resulted from.

**Tip 2**

If you’re asked to leave some comments in writing (for example, on a Miro board), formulate a short phrase that signalizes a problem or highlights a success.

**Tip 3**

When speaking, provide facts and details to show reasons and results.

**Tip 4**

Invest effort in analyzing the team’s performance without personalizing it.

**Tip 5**

Describe what you think objectively and not emotionally. Always be as clear as possible.

**Tip 6**

Take part in discussions of action items: join, disagree, deal with disagreements politely.

# **Useful language**

**Saying what went well**

- I thought it was very helpful to …
- It was really nice to see how …

**Saying what didn’t go well**

- I wish I’d/we’d [3rd form of verb]
*I wish I’d found out about this request before the sprint started.*
- I had a hard time [verb+*ing*] because *…
I had a hard time figuring out how to re-prioritize my tasks because of the new requirement.*
- because/as/since [subject+verb]
*It didn’t go well as I wasn’t aware of this requirement.*
- because of [noun]
*It didn’t go well because of the new requirement.*
- So,/As a result, [subject+verb]
*The machines isn’t powerful enough, and, as a result, it’s slowing the team down.*

**Suggesting improvements**

- I think it would be very helpful if we could …
- It might be better to …
- We’ve got to make sure that …

The speaker defines a “blur word” as a word that can mean different things to different people. Such words are not specific. Some of the examples of “blur” words from the video are “defensive”, “proactive”, “reliable”.

after presenting your data point you need to outline the impact of the data point on you or the project.

“This is what I’m thinking we should do, but what are your thoughts on it?” is a good way to wrap up your feedback message.

### Productive discussion

**Tip 1**

Ask questions. It will involve the team and encourage them to take part in a problem-solving process. It also helps if someone goes off topic and speaks a lot. In this case you can say something like “What you’re sharing is interesting, but we don’t have time to discuss it fully now. Could you share your most important concern related to our goal?”

**Tip 2**

Avoid hiding negative feedback in positive comments. If you surround criticism with compliments, people might not notice the criticism and get the overall impression that everything is good.

**Tip 3**

Contribute to the discussion. If you generally hesitate to participate in a discussion, try to say something at the beginning of a meeting. You can agree or disagree with someone, offer your point of view or add to someone’s comment. Using your voice early will help you get comfortable expressing yourself.

**Tip 4**

It will feel like a smoother transition if you interject between agenda points or topics. If you’re joining in the middle of the discussion, make sure that what you’re adding is relevant to the topic.

**Tip 5**

Remind the team about the time limits and bring the conversation back on track if necessary.

# **Useful language**

**Taking and keeping your turn in discussions**

- Sorry to interrupt.
- Sorry to butt in. (informal)
- Can I just say something here?
- Sorry, just one more thing.

**Stopping an unproductive discussion**

- I suppose that’s a little off topic.
- Can we park that (until the end of the meeting) and first look at …?

**Dealing with misunderstanding**

- Sorry, that’s not what I mean.
- Sorry if I didn’t make that clear.

### Discussion case

Naomi: The switch to the new VM hasn’t been smooth. Unfortunately it’s not as powerful as the previous one and it’s slowing us down.
Linda: I see your point but we had to make the decision fast. And the same standard machines were assigned to all the teams.
Keira: Oh, by the way, have you heard that the business team has a new team member?
Naomi: Yeah, but I suppose that’s a little off topic. Let’s focus on our VM first.
Jan: Sorry to butt in. I have to add that we’ve already lost five working days just adapting to it. And we still don’t feel very comfortable working with it.
Linda: So what can we do to improve the situation?
Naomi: Well, it would be great if the machines had at least 16 GB of RAM so that we can run Dataiku and Power BI. And we’ve got to make sure that all the drivers for working with databases are already installed. What do you think?
Jan: Yeah, that sounds reasonable. It could save us some time.
Linda: Right, that makes sense. I’ll see what we can do about that.
Keira: Do you mean you’ll ask the support team to install the necessary drivers?
Linda: Sorry, that’s not what I mean. I wanted to say that I’ll find out if we can change the current VMs to something that suits our requirements.
