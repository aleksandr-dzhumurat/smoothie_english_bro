## Tips for a Successful Performance Review

Performance reviews give you an excellent chance to discuss how you’re doing and how you can do better at your job. Take this opportunity to self-reflect, recollect your achievements, discuss future expectations, and set goals. When preparing for the meeting, it may be a good idea to:

*   **Tip 1:** Review the agenda, think about the topics you’d like to cover, and prepare a list of questions to the manager.
*   **Tip 2:** Come up with good examples referring to specific tasks and projects that demonstrate your best work. When describing those, mention the impact and the value those achievements brought to the company and how they’re connected with business objectives.
*   **Tip 3:** Think of some professional areas that could be improved. Understanding your weak points is as important as describing your strengths. This will help you determine the areas where you’d like to learn and grow professionally.
*   **Tip 4:** Try to come up with some “positive language” when talking about the areas in which you need to improve. You might want to avoid saying: “I’m bad at…”, ”This is where I failed”. Instead, say: “Here’s an area I want to work on”, “This is what I’ve learned”.
*   **Tip 5:** Ask about future expectations your manager has of you and if they can help you succeed.
*   **Tip 6:** Think of examples when you showed initiative or helped other team members.

### More Tips
*   To learn more about the performance review process, you can find some information about what managers do to prepare for it.
*   It’s a good idea to find out about the format of the performance review, topics of discussion, and possible questions — it’ll help you feel more confident.
*   Creating a document highlighting your achievements will help you to remember why you’re a great worker and remind you about the things you can mention during your meeting with the manager.
*   It’s a good idea to go through the recommendations from the previous performance review and think about how you acted on them.
*   If you want to look confident and proactive, you might want to prepare 2-3 questions for your manager.
*   *(Avoid)* Asking your manager what you can expect from a performance review meeting — it will show that you lack confidence.
*   *(Avoid)* Thinking about your weaknesses before the meeting as it’ll have a negative effect on your confidence.

## Useful Language

### Setting Objectives
*   One thing I’m going to do is …
*   I intend to … *(== going to)*
*   I’d like to focus on …

### Commenting on Progress
*   I’m really happy with …
*   I’ve put a lot of effort in …
*   I’m making progress in / with …
*   *[Over the last month]* I’ve been working with [someone] / on [project]
*   I feel I did a great job *[leading the team]*
*   I struggled to …

### Talking About Areas for Improvement
*   Something I need to work on is …
*   I think … is an area for improvement.

### Lexicon
*   **become better at something** = get more professional at something
    *   *Example:* I’ve **become better at** communicating things clearly.
*   **a sense of achievement** = a proud feeling of success
    *   *Example:* The stakeholder’s feedback gave me a great **sense of achievement**.
*   **room for improvement** = the possibility to improve/grow
    *   *Example:* I believe there’s still **room for improvement** in this area.
*   **be involved in something** = having a part in something / be included in something
    *   *Example:* I’ve never **been involved in** mentoring and I’d like to give it a try.
*   **benefit from something** = receive an advantage/profit
    *   *Example:* I enjoyed the webinar and really **benefited from** it.
*   **expand my [communication/organizational] skills/knowledge** = develop some skills/knowledge
    *   *Example:* I feel that the training course helped me to **expand my knowledge** in the field.
*   **try my hand at something** = start doing some new activity
    *   *Example:* I’d like to **try my hand at** mentoring a group of students.

## Example Texts

Natasha, a senior analyst, and Pasha, a junior analyst, have had a performance review session with their manager. 

**Natasha**
> I’m really happy with how I managed to improve my leadership skills. Before the current project I’d been actively involved in mentoring and I’d monitored the work of one junior analyst. But this project was the first time I had a chance to lead a team. So I’ve put a lot of effort into communicating goals, setting specific deadlines, and facilitating meetings. I also managed to maintain a friendly and supportive atmosphere in the team. I feel I did a great job leading five people and I got really positive feedback during the team retro. I intend to develop this skill set further so that I can handle a bigger team.
> 
> I believe that my ability to work in a team and take the initiative has been quite valuable during this period. I’ve been involved in a lot of processes and communicated proactively with the team and stakeholders. For example, I raised a question about owning team communications and we managed to find a person who wanted to work on that. As a result, our communications improved significantly, we all stayed in sync and promptly dealt with any misunderstandings. The stakeholders really appreciated that and their feedback gave me a great sense of achievement.
> 
> Something I’d like to work on in the next 6 months is my AWS expertise. One thing I’m going to do is take a training course. I’d also like to have an opportunity to apply the new knowledge on our project. I think not only me, but also the whole team will benefit from this.

**Pasha**
> On my current project I’m involved in requirements gathering so I have to communicate with the stakeholders a lot. At first, when I was conducting meetings with them, I struggled to cover all the important things and stay within the time limit. When the stakeholders went off-topic, I couldn’t always get them back on track. So, I decided to learn more about meeting facilitation and found a series of articles with really useful tips. I applied some of them, for example I started sharing the agenda before the meeting and sending out follow-up emails after it. Now we manage to answer more questions during our sessions and finish them on time. So I feel I’m making progress with meeting facilitation but I believe there’s still room for improvement in this area.
> 
> For the next 6 months I’d like to focus on mentoring a bit more. I’ve been working with one mentee for the last month and I feel it has helped me expand my communication and organizational skills. I’ve become better at communicating things clearly, setting goals and tracking progress. My mentee also finds our collaboration effective and helpful. I’d like to try my hand at mentoring a group.
> 
> I have a lot of doubts when I have to make important decisions, so I ask others for advice. I tend to rely on past experience instead of looking for new solutions. So I think making decisions and solving complex problems are my areas for improvement. I think I might need some help from you. Can we talk about that?

## Practical Cases

### Rephrasing Sentences
*   *I had a chance to try my hand at machine learning* ➞ **I had an opportunity to work with machine learning.**
*   *I intend to expand my skills in machine learning.* ➞ **I’m going to learn more about machine learning.**
*   *I’ve become better at data storytelling.* ➞ **My data storytelling skills have improved.**
*   *I’ve been involved in creating a training program for junior analysts.* ➞ **I’ve taken part in creating a training program for junior analysts.**
*   *Demonstrating my dashboard for the first time gave me a real sense of achievement.* ➞ **I felt really proud when I first demonstrated my dashboard.**
*   *As for data storytelling, I think there’s room for improvement there.* ➞ **I think my data storytelling skills can become better.**
*   *I think I can benefit from a training course on negotiations.* ➞ **A training course on negotiations may be helpful for me.**

### Speaking Practice
> I have been actively involved in requirements gathering on this project and I feel I’m becoming better at negotiating solutions. However, when it comes to finding the most relevant and convincing arguments, there’s still room for improvement.

> Well, data visualization is the area where I need to expand my knowledge. I feel that all stakeholders benefit from good data storytelling, especially if we align our story with their business goals. Perhaps I should take a training course to develop this skill set.

> I started mentoring a junior colleague several months ago and I’m really happy with how it goes. Seeing my mentee’s progress gives me a great sense of achievement. The whole experience allows me to see things from a different perspective. You know, I’d like to try my hand at mentoring a group.
