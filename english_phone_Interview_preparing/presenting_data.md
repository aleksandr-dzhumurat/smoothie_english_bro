## Sharing Insights

### Introducing Findings
- Having analyzed *[this data]*, I've come up with …
- Let me walk you through …

### Referring to Visuals
- As you can see from this *[table/graph]*, …
- This table represents …

### Making Conclusions (less certain)
- We might expect that …
- These *[scenarios/data]* suggest that …
- It appears that …

### Making Conclusions (more certain)
- It's safe to *[say/conclude]* that …
- This is a strong indicator that …

### Giving Recommendations
- It would probably make sense to …
- It's probably worth [verb+*ing*] …
- It might be reasonable to …

### Research Vocabulary
- make an assumption
- try out different options
- take something into account
- use something as a benchmark
- relationship between something
- look promising
- seem attractive
- be applicable to something
- do research

## Research Phrases

*   We can **make an assumption that** people in this region are willing to pay a premium.
*   These restaurants **look more promising** if we take the order amount into account.
*   **Daytime deliveries seem more attractive** for the drivers.
*   The Palo Alto pricing policy **would not be applicable to** Colorado.
*   You can **benchmark that against** publicly available statistics.
*   I've **done some research on** working conditions preferences and this is what I've found.
*   To establish a correlation, we might need to change the variables and **try out different options**.
*   We can **set a benchmark for** weekly Zoom-time and analyze the performance level.
*   Our goal would be to find the optimal **relationship between** the price and the proportion of subscribers.
*   **Conducting some research** into the company's culture.

### Research Lexicon

*   **make an assumption** = accept that something is true although there's no proof
*   **try out different options** = test different options to see if they're effective
*   **take something into account** = analyze a situation when making a decision
*   **use something as a benchmark** = use something as a point of reference
*   **relationship between something** = correlation between things
*   **look promising** = show signs of future success
*   **seem attractive** = give the impression of having more positive characteristics
*   **be applicable to something** = be relevant to
*   **do research** = do a detailed study of a subject

## Presenting a Dashboard

### Tips

**Tip 1. Be clear on the purpose.**
Start by telling the audience what problems the dashboard can solve or what questions it can give answers to.

**Tip 2. Don't assume your audience knows your dashboard.**
Take your time to explain what the measures mean and what the different pieces of information are.

**Tip 3. Guide your audience through the presentation.**
Comment on what you're demonstrating. Don't jump from one section to another at great speed.

**Tip 4. Draw attention to each area you're discussing.**
Direct your audience to where you want them to look. Refer to the location of elements on the dashboard.

**Tip 5. Take advantage of use cases.**
Give examples to demonstrate how the dashboard should be used, and communicate the insights found.

**Tip 6. Present only what your audience needs to know.**
Tell different stories to different audiences.

**Tip 7. Invite questions.**
Ask the audience for feedback to confirm or challenge your assumptions.

### Announcing the Purpose
- The dashboard shows / represents / displays / illustrates …
- The dashboard gives an overview of …

### Guiding the Audience
- Let's go through …
- Now, let's move on to …
- Now let's take a (closer) look at …
- If we *[select …]*, we'll *[see that …]*
- We can drill down into *[this category]* to *[explore which items are more popular]*.

### Drawing Attention to Visuals
- As you can see, …
- The graph displays …
- If we hover over *[a point on the graph]*, a tooltip will appear (showing …)
- We can use the filter pane below to …
- Clicking on *[a region]* will filter the dashboard to display *[the data relevant to this region]*.

### Drawing Attention to Location
- across the top / across the bottom (across == from left to right)
- at the top / at the bottom
- on the left / on the right

### Closing the Demo
- So, this is basically how you can explore this dashboard (to …)
- Now I'll be happy to hear your feedback and answer your questions.

### Dashboard Phrases — Practice

- So, this is basically how you can explore this dashboard to get a quick insight into the data for Quality Assurance.
- Let's go through the process of downloading our dashboard into a different file format.
- The Navigational Site Map section appears across the bottom of the dashboard.
- The dashboard gives an overview of the projects and related customers.
- You can drill down into this data by clicking on the title of the chart.
- At the top, we can choose the sets of products for the dashboard to display and below that, we can play with different time periods.
- As you can see, only the consumer segment has hit the target so far.

## Describing Graphs and Charts

### Introducing the Visual
- Let's have a look at …
- It shows / illustrates / represents …
- As you can see from *[this chart]*, …

### Talking About Trends
- *[Sales]* dropped / went down *[by 5% / from 9,000 to 5,000]*.
- *[Profits]* fell sharply/slightly.
- We see a (dramatic/sudden/slight) decline/drop in (revenue).
- *[Sales]* increased / went up (by one third / by half).
- *[Profits]* rose sharply/slightly.
- We see a/an (large) increase in *[numbers]*.
- We can see (there's) a (clear) upward/downward trend (in the number of accidents).
- Compared to 2019, …

### Talking About Percentages and Proportions
- The (vast) majority of *[users are satisfied with their experience]*.
- Just under / A little over *[10 percent of the bugs are low priority]*.
- About / roughly *[one third of the bugs are high priority]*.

### Giving a Summary/Evaluation
- Overall, we can see that …
- This seems to suggest that …

### Practice Sentences — Describing Charts

- It shows the breakdown of issues by priority.
- The majority of issues are high priority, making up about 55%.
- Critical priority issues represent just under a third of all the issues.
- We see a slight decline in ticket volume.
- This is roughly 70% longer compared to either Q1 or Q2.
- There's a clear upward trend in the number of tickets.
- This pie chart illustrates **distribution of sales** by regions.
- As you can see from this graph, the performance has **dropped by a little over 10%** since last quarter.
- The number of visitors **has increased by 5%** this month.
- Our profit in this category **fell sharply from $3,000 to $1,900**.
- We can see that there's a clear downward **trend in the number of cars** sold.
- We see a dramatic **decline in prices**.
- The **vast majority of our clients** are located in South Africa.
- Compared to last year, **sales rose slightly**.
- **Roughly 20% of viewers** prefer the comedy genre.
- Overall, we can see that there's a **large increase in demand**.
- The sales **went down by 10%** last month.
- The number of users **went up sharply** after we changed the subscription price.
- This **seems to suggest** that we aren't dealing with the problem effectively.

### Longer Chart Description Examples

> We can see that people's interest in backpacks **went down** from **just under 6,000** in 2019 to a little over 5,000 in 2020. But after this decrease we can **see a large increase**, especially in 2022 where the numbers rose sharply.

> This pie chart **displays** the places where people from our target region traveled on vacation. As we can see **from this chart**, the majority of destinations are located in Europe with a **little over 30%** choosing Türkiye. It's followed by Greece with **about 20%** of travelers. The rest of the countries make up **just under 30%**. So, **overall**, we can see that it might be worth focusing on Türkiye and Greece as tours there would bring the most revenue.

## Structuring a Presentation

### Starting the Presentation
- Hello/Hi everyone. Thank you for making the time to come. *(more formal)*
- Thanks for joining. *(less formal)*
- Today I'll be talking about …
- I'll be happy to answer your questions as we go along.
- I'll leave some time to answer your questions at the end.

### Moving Between Parts
- I'd like to start by *[talking about …]*
- Now turning to …
- Now I'd like to dive into …
- Now let's go through …
- Let's move on to …
- Let me walk you through …

### Ending the Presentation
- I guess that about wraps it up.
- If you have any questions, feel free to ask them.
- As a follow-up, I'll *[send the link to the recording / share the slide deck]*.

### Handling Questions
- I'm not completely sure but I'll find that out for you.
- I'll get back to you on that.
- [*Nick*], do you have an answer to that?
- Thank you for bringing that up.
- That's a little outside the scope of *[our presentation]*. Do you mind if we discuss it after *[the demo]*?

## Tips on Suggesting Solutions and Providing Arguments

1. Make sure you understand the business task.
2. Acknowledge your stakeholder's opinion if your idea differs from theirs.
3. Express certainty, expertise, and objectivity. Use power words like *highly effective* backed up with data.
4. Provide examples and references to best practices.
5. Clearly explain the benefits. Point out drawbacks as well so the stakeholder can make an informed decision.

### Pros and Cons Phrases
- The upside of this solution is that the dashboard will load faster.
- One point in support of that is the speed of implementation.
- An argument in favor of this method is that we won't have to refresh the dashboard manually.
- The drawback of this visualization is that it takes up a lot of space.
- One argument against that is longer processing time.
- The primary downside of that approach is that it will increase the system load.
- I can see both sides of the issue.

### Practice Sentences
- One really good thing about this option is that you can see all the information on one page.
- I would suggest adding another tab to the dashboard.
- The downside is that you'll need to switch between the tabs.
- OK, what we could do is run an experiment.
- One of the limitations of this kind of dashboard is that it works slower.
- And what this gives you is more engaged users.
- And not only that, it'll help to reduce the churn rate.
- Decluttering the main page is a tried and tested practice.
- I'm sure that you will see the benefits of this.
- The downside of this option is that users will spend too long on this page.

## Tips on Writing Follow-Up Emails

1. Keep emails brief and to the point. Make sections easy to notice.
2. Check for mistakes and typos before sending.
3. Read once more and make sure you've covered all necessary items.
4. Don't mix very formal and informal styles.
5. Don't use overly formal tone if everyone else uses a relaxed tone.

## Data Storytelling Tips

1. **Know your audience.** Leadership wants outcomes and insights. Technical audience wants raw data and action points.
2. **Construct the narrative:** Context → Characters → Conflict → Resolution.
3. **Make it memorable.** Use visuals, deliver meaning, engage with questions, use recaps and summaries.

### Presentation Flow Example

> *Let me walk you through my main findings. Having analyzed the demographic differences between the European countries, I've come up with the following findings.*
>
> *First, we looked at the population size. This chart represents the relationship between the population and the happiness score. It appears that there is little to no correlation between the two.*
>
> *As you can see from this chart, the correlation coefficient between health and happiness is 0.50. So it's safe to say that there is a positive correlation between them.*
>
> *The correlation coefficient here is 0.70, which is a strong indicator that richer countries have healthier population. Knowing the relationship between health and happiness, we might expect that there's a similar correlation between happiness and economic status.*
>
> *As a next step, it's probably worth checking that assumption since a lot of the research is going to be based on that.*
