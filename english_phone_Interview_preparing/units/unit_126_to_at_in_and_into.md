# Unit 126 — to, at, in and into

# Theory

## A to — movement towards a place or event

With verbs of movement (*go, come, travel, drive, fly, return, be taken*) we use **to**:

| go to Brazil | come to my flat | drive to the coast |
|---|---|---|
| go back to Poland | go to the dentist | be taken to hospital |
| return to Cairo | go to a wedding | fly to Oslo |

- *When are you going back to Poland?* (not ~~going back in Poland~~)
- *Two cyclists were taken to hospital.*

The same **to** appears in *Welcome to …, a trip to …, a visit to …, on my/the way to …*:
- *Welcome to Edinburgh!* (not ~~Welcome in~~)
- *Our trip to Lisbon was cancelled.*

Contrast movement (**to**) with position (**in / at**):
- *She's moving to Canada.* but *She lives in Canada.*
- *Can you come to the meeting?* but *I'll see you at the meeting.*

**been to**: *I've been to Greece twice, but never to Athens.* / *He's never been to a rock concert.*

## B get to / arrive in / arrive at

- **get to** a place: *We got to the campsite after dark.* / *What time did you get to the office?*
- **arrive in** a town or country: *They arrived in Lima / in Peru on Sunday.*
- **arrive at** buildings, smaller places, events: *We arrived at the hotel / at the airport / at the party late.*
- Never ~~arrive to~~.

## C home — no preposition

*go home, come home, get home, arrive home, on the way home* — no *to*:
- *What time did you get home?* (not ~~get to home~~)
- *I bumped into Alex on my way home.*

## D into — entering; out of, on, off

**into** = enter (a room, building, car, container):
- *He knocked, walked into the office and sat down.*
- *A wasp flew into the car.*
- *The money goes straight into my savings account.*

With *go / get / put* we often use **in** instead of *into*:
- *She got in(to) the car and drove off.* / *Put the receipt back in the envelope.*

The opposite of *into* is **out of**: *He got out of the taxi and went into the bank.*

For buses, trains and planes: **get on / get off**:
- *I got on the wrong bus.* / *Get off at the third stop.*

**See also:** been to → Units 7–8; in/at/on (position) → Units 123–125; at home → Unit 125A; into and in → Unit 138A.

### Question 38

#### Question
One of these sentences contains a mistake. Which one, and what is the correction?

#### Answer
**"It was midnight when we got to home" is wrong; it should be "got home".** *Home* takes no preposition after verbs of movement: *go home, get home, arrive home, on the way home*. The other sentences are correct: *got to the station*, *on my way to work*, *came home*.

#### Correct variant
¯\_(ツ)_/¯

#### Variants
1. "We got to the station just before the train left" — should be *got the station*.
2. "It was midnight when we got to home" — should be *got home*. (Correct)
3. "I saw the accident on my way to work" — should be *on my way work*.
4. "She came home very late" — should be *came to home*.
