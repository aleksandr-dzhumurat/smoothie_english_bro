# Unit 140 — Phrasal verbs 4 on/off (1)

# Theory

## A on and off for lights, machines etc.

We say *the light is on / leave the light on / put the light on*, and *turn* or *switch* something *on / off*:

| Phrasal verb | Example |
|---|---|
| turn / switch (the TV, the heating, a lamp) on / off | Could you switch the fan on? It's boiling in here. / I turned the radio off and went to sleep. |
| leave something on | Don't leave the oven on when you go out. |
| put on music / a song; put the kettle on | Put some music on while we cook. / I'll put the kettle on and make us some tea. |

Pronoun rule applies: *switch it on*, *turn them off* (not ~~turn off them~~).

## B on and off for events

| Phrasal verb | Meaning | Example |
|---|---|---|
| go on | happen | There are police everywhere — what's going on? |
| call something off | cancel it | The match was called off because the pitch was flooded. |
| put something off / put off doing something | postpone, delay | They've put the wedding off until next spring. / Stop putting off going to the dentist. |

Note *put off + -ing*: ❌ *put off to decide* ✅ *put off deciding*.

## C on and off for clothes etc.

| Phrasal verb | Example |
|---|---|
| put on clothes / glasses / make-up / a seat belt | It's freezing — put a scarf on. / Please put your seat belt on. |
| put on weight (= get heavier) | I've put on three kilos since Christmas. |
| try on clothes (to check the fit) | Can I try these jeans on before I buy them? |
| take off clothes / glasses | He took his boots off at the door. |

## D off = away from a person or place

| Phrasal verb | Meaning | Example |
|---|---|---|
| be off (to a place / on holiday) | be leaving, be going | I'm off to the dentist — back in an hour. / They're off on holiday tomorrow. |
| walk off / run off / drive off / ride off / go off | leave, go away | The thief grabbed the bag and ran off. / She got in the van and drove off. |
| set off | start a journey | We set off at dawn to beat the traffic. |
| take off | leave the ground (a plane) | The plane took off on time despite the fog. |
| see somebody off | go to the station / airport to say goodbye | All her friends came to the airport to see her off. |

## E Contrast: take off (clothes) vs take off (plane)

*Take off* has two meanings in this unit: with an object it means remove clothes (*take your hat off*); without an object it means a plane leaves the ground (*the plane took off*). Context makes the meaning clear.

### Question 24

#### Question
"The outdoor concert has been ______ because of the thunderstorm — everybody will get a refund." Which phrasal verb is correct?

#### Answer
The correct answer is "has been **called off**" (= cancelled). *Call off* means to cancel an event completely, which matches the refund. *Put off* means to postpone (the concert would happen later, so no refund would be needed), *go on* means to happen, and *set off* means to start a journey.

#### Correct variant
¯\_(ツ)_/¯

#### Variants
1. put off
2. gone on
3. called off (Correct)
4. set off

### Question 25

#### Question
Which sentence is wrong, and why?

#### Answer
The wrong sentence is "I keep putting off to book my eye test." *Put off* is followed by an **-ing** form: "putting off **booking**." The other sentences are correct: *what's going on* = what's happening, *see her off* = say goodbye at the station, and *set off* = start a journey.

#### Correct variant
¯\_(ツ)_/¯

#### Variants
1. I keep putting off to book my eye test. (Correct)
2. There's a crowd outside the bank — what's going on?
3. We went to the station to see her off.
4. If we set off at six, we'll miss the traffic.

### Question 26

#### Question
In a clothes shop: "I like this coat, but I'm not sure about the size. Can I ______?" Which is correct?

#### Answer
The correct answer is "Can I **try it on**?" *Try on* clothes = put them on to see if they fit; with the pronoun *it*, the object goes between *try* and *on*. "Try on it" breaks the pronoun rule. *Put it on* just means wear it (not test the fit), and *take it off* means remove it.

#### Correct variant
¯\_(ツ)_/¯

#### Variants
1. try on it
2. put it on
3. take it off
4. try it on (Correct)

### Question 27

#### Question
Compare: (a) "It was sunny, so Hugo ______ his jumper." (b) "After a two-hour delay, our plane finally ______." Which pair completes both sentences correctly?

#### Answer
Both gaps take **took off**: (a) *take off* + object = remove clothes ("took his jumper off" / "took off his jumper"); (b) *take off* without an object = a plane leaves the ground. This is the same phrasal verb with two different meanings. *Set off* is for people starting a journey, not planes, and *put off* means postpone.

#### Correct variant
¯\_(ツ)_/¯

#### Variants
1. (a) put off, (b) set off
2. (a) took off, (b) took off (Correct)
3. (a) took off, (b) set off
4. (a) set off, (b) took off

### Question 28

#### Question
"Right, I'm ______ to the market. Before I go, shall I ______ the kettle so the water's hot when you get up?" Choose the correct completions.

#### Answer
The correct completion is "I'm **off** to the market … shall I **put** the kettle **on**?" *Be off (to a place)* = be leaving for that place. *Put the kettle on* is the fixed expression for switching on the kettle to boil water. "I'm on to the market" and "turn the kettle off" (which would stop the water heating) do not fit the meaning.

#### Correct variant
¯\_(ツ)_/¯

#### Variants
1. off … put … on (Correct)
2. on … put … off
3. off … turn … off
4. away … set … on
