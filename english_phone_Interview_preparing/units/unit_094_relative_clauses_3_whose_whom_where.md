# Unit 94 — Relative clauses 3: whose/whom/where

# Theory

## A whose

**Whose** shows possession inside a relative clause. It replaces *his/her/their/its*:

- On the train I sat next to a man whose suitcase kept falling on my feet. (= his suitcase kept falling)
- An orphan is a child whose parents have died. (= their parents have died)
- I met a woman whose brother works at your company. (= her brother works at your company)

*Whose* is used mostly for people. Compare *who* and *whose*:

| | example | meaning |
|---|---|---|
| who | I met a woman who works at your company. | she works there |
| whose | I met a woman whose brother works at your company. | her brother works there |

Do not confuse **whose** with **who's**. They sound the same, but *who's* = *who is* or *who has*:

- I have a colleague who's learning Japanese. (who's = who is)
- I have a colleague who's just moved to Osaka. (who's = who has)
- I have a colleague whose wife is Japanese. (possession)

## B whom

**Whom** can replace *who* when it is the **object** of the verb in the relative clause (see Unit 93B):

- Dr Ahmed is a teacher whom I respect enormously. (I respect him)

A preposition can go directly before *whom*: *to whom, with whom, for whom*:

- Everyone needs colleagues with whom they can share ideas. (they can share ideas with them)

*Whom* is **formal** and rare in speech. In everyday English we prefer to leave the pronoun out or use *who/that*, with the preposition at the end:

- a teacher I respect enormously / a teacher who/that I respect enormously
- colleagues you can share ideas with / colleagues who/that you can share ideas with

## C where

**Where** introduces a relative clause about a **place**:

- We visited the village where my grandfather was born. (he was born there)
- The café where we first met has closed down.
- I'd love to work somewhere where the winters are mild.

## D the day / the time / the reason …

After time words such as *the day, the year, the last time*, we usually use no relative word (or *that*):

- Saturday is the day (that) we move house.
- The last time (that) I saw Ella, she was about to start university.
- Do you remember the year (that) we went to Portugal?

After *the reason* we can use nothing, *that*, or *why*:

- The reason (that/why) I'm writing is to confirm the booking.
- The reason (that/why) he left the company was the long commute.

#### Answer
The correct sentence is "I met a woman **whose** son is a famous chef." *Whose* replaces the possessive *her* (her son is a famous chef). *Who's* means *who is / who has* and would produce "who is son", which makes no sense. *Who* would need the woman herself to be the subject of *is*, not her son. *Which* is not used for people, and *which her* is not a structure.

#### Answer

#### Question
You want to say this in a more formal way for a written report: "We spoke to a consultant. She wasn't very helpful." Which sentence is the correct formal version?

#### Answer
The correct formal version is "The consultant **to whom** we spoke wasn't very helpful." *Whom* is the formal object form, and a preposition can be placed directly before it (*to whom*). Option 1 is grammatical but it is the informal version — the preposition at the end with the pronoun omitted. Option 2 is wrong because we do not use *whom* when the preposition stays at the end of the clause (that should be *who/that* or nothing). 2. The consultant whom we spoke to wasn't very helpful.

### Question 34

#### Question
Choose the sentence that is grammatically correct.

#### Answer
The correct sentence is "The reason **she resigned** was that she wanted to travel." After *the reason* we can use nothing (as here), *that*, or *why*; all three are acceptable. Option 1 is wrong because *because* is not used after *the reason* to introduce a relative clause — "the reason because" is a common learner error. Option 2 uses *where* after *the day*, but *where* is only for places; after *the day* we use nothing or *that*. Option 4 uses *whose* where *when/that* or nothing is needed after *the last time*.

#### Correct variant

2. Do you remember the day where we first met?
