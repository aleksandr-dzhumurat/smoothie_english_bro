# Unit 93 — Relative clauses 2: clauses with and without who/that/which

# Theory

## A When who/that/which is the subject — you must keep it

If the relative pronoun is the **subject** of the verb in the relative clause, it cannot be left out:

- The boy who broke the window has apologised. (the boy broke the window → *who* = subject)
- Where is the bag that was under the chair? (the bag was under the chair → *that* = subject)

❌ *The boy broke the window has apologised.* — a relative clause needs its subject.

## B When who/that/which is the object — you can leave it out

If the relative pronoun is the **object** of the verb in the clause (and the clause has its own subject), you can omit it:

| with pronoun | without pronoun |
|---|---|
| The dentist who I visited was very gentle. | The dentist I visited was very gentle. |
| Have you seen the photos that Emma took? | Have you seen the photos Emma took? |
| The jacket which he bought was too small. | The jacket he bought was too small. |
| Is there anything that we can bring? | Is there anything we can bring? |

Test: *I visited the dentist* → "the dentist" is the object → the pronoun can go.

Do not repeat the object with *it/them/him*:

- the photos Emma took ❌ *the photos Emma took them*
- the jacket he bought ❌ *the jacket he bought it*

## C Prepositions at the end of the relative clause

When the relative clause contains a preposition (*to, in, for, with, about* …), the preposition normally stays at the end, after the verb:

- Two sentences: Laura is talking to a man. Do you know him?
- One sentence: Do you know the man Laura is talking **to**? (or … the man who/that Laura is talking to)
- Two sentences: I stayed in a hostel. It was very noisy.
- One sentence: The hostel I stayed **in** was very noisy. (or The hostel that/which I stayed in …)

More examples:

- Is this the file you were searching **for**?
- The couple we sat next **to** at the wedding were from Dublin.

Again, do not add an extra pronoun: the file you were searching for ❌ *the file you were searching for it*.

## D everything (that) … / all (that) … vs what

- Everything (that) he promised came true. ❌ *Everything what he promised*
- She spent all the money (that) she earned. ❌ *all the money what she earned*

**What** = "the thing(s) that" and is used **without** a preceding noun:

- What he promised came true. (= The things that he promised)

#### Answer
