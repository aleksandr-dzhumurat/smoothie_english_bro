# Unit 139 — Phrasal verbs 3 out

# Theory

## A out = not burning, not shining

| Phrasal verb | Example |
|---|---|
| go out (a light / fire) | The candles went out when the wind blew through the tent. |
| put out a fire / a cigarette / a light | The campers put the fire out with sand before they left. |
| turn out a light | Remember to turn the lights out when you leave. |
| blow out a candle | The birthday girl blew out all seven candles at once. |

*Go out* has no object (the light itself goes out); *put out / turn out / blow out* take an object (somebody does it).

## B work out — several meanings

| Meaning | Example |
|---|---|
| do physical exercise | Jamal works out every morning before work. |
| develop, turn out well / badly | I hope your new job works out. / Their business partnership didn't work out. |
| calculations: *work out at* | The rent works out at £40 a night per person. |
| work (something) out = calculate | Can you work out how much we each owe? |
| work out / figure out = understand, find the answer | We still can't work out why the alarm keeps going off. / … figure out why … |

## C Other verbs + out

| Phrasal verb | Meaning | Example |
|---|---|---|
| carry out (an order / experiment / survey / plan) | do, perform | The lab carried out tests on the water samples. |
| find out (that / what / when …); find out about | get information | I found out that the museum is free on Sundays. / Let's find out about train times. |
| give out / hand out | distribute to each person | Volunteers handed out water to the runners. |
| point something out (to somebody) | draw attention to it | A colleague pointed out a spelling mistake in the report. |
| run out (of something) | use it all up | We've run out of coffee — can you get some? |
| sort something out | solve, put in order | IT will sort out the login problem this afternoon. |
| turn out to be … / turn out nice / turn out that … | become clear in the end | The "antique" turned out to be a modern copy. / The day turned out sunny. / It turned out that we'd been at the same school. |
| try out (a machine / an idea) | test it | We're trying out a new booking system this month. |

## D Object position reminder

Noun objects can go either side: *sort out the problem* / *sort the problem out*. Pronouns go in the middle: *sort it out*, *point it out*, *try them out*, *work it out* (not ~~work out it~~).

**See also:** Phrasal verbs 1 (Introduction) → Unit 137; more verbs + out → Unit 138.

## Extra practice

### Question 33

#### Question
"The bill for the four of us was £86, so it ______ at £21.50 each." Which phrasal verb fits?

#### Answer
The correct answer is "it **works out** at £21.50 each." *Work out (at)* is used for the result of a calculation. *Turn out* means to become clear in the end, *sort out* means to solve or organise, and *carry out* means to perform a task or order — none of them is used for arithmetic results.

#### Correct variant
¯\_(ツ)_/¯

#### Variants
1. turns out
2. works out (Correct)
3. sorts out
4. carries out

### Question 34

#### Question
Which sentence contains a mistake?

#### Answer
The wrong sentence is "We had a problem with the booking, but the agent sorted out it quickly." With a pronoun object, the pronoun must go between the verb and the particle: "sorted **it out**." The other sentences are correct: *ran out of*, *carried out* and *turned out to be* are all used properly.

#### Correct variant
¯\_(ツ)_/¯

#### Variants
1. We ran out of milk halfway through breakfast.
2. The council carried out a survey of local traffic.
3. We had a problem with the booking, but the agent sorted out it quickly. (Correct)
4. The stranger on the train turned out to be my cousin's neighbour.

### Question 35

#### Question
Compare the two gaps: (a) "I ______ at the gym twice a week." (b) "The detectives couldn't ______ how the thief had got in." Which pair of phrasal verbs is correct?

#### Answer
Both gaps take **work out**: (a) *work out* = do physical exercise; (b) *work out* = understand / find the answer (here *figure out* would also be possible). This shows the different meanings of the same phrasal verb. *Try out* means to test something, *find out* means to obtain information (possible in (b) but not in (a)), and *carry out* means to perform.

#### Correct variant
¯\_(ツ)_/¯

#### Variants
1. (a) try out, (b) find out
2. (a) work out, (b) carry out
3. (a) work out, (b) work out (Correct)
4. (a) carry out, (b) work out

### Question 37

#### Question
A storm knocks the power off in a village. Which sentence describes the lights correctly?

#### Answer
The correct sentence is "All the street lights **went out** at once." *Go out* is used when a light or fire stops shining or burning by itself, with no object. *Put out* and *turn out* need somebody to do the action and an object ("someone turned the lights out"). "The lights put out" is ungrammatical, and "blew out" is used for candles and flames, not electric lights.

#### Correct variant
¯\_(ツ)_/¯

#### Variants
1. All the street lights put out at once.
2. All the street lights went out at once. (Correct)
3. All the street lights turned out at once.
4. All the street lights blew out at once.
