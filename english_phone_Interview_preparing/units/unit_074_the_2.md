# Unit 74 — the 2 (school / the school etc.)

# Theory

## A school vs the school

| school (no article) | the school (a specific building) |
|---|---|
| Oscar is eight. He goes to school every weekday. He's at school now. School finishes at half past three. | Oscar's father wants to talk to the head teacher, so he has gone to the school. He's at the school now. |
| We think of *school* as a general idea: children learning in a classroom. The speaker is a pupil/student. | We think of a particular building. The speaker is a visitor, not a pupil. |

So: a pupil *goes to school / is at school*; a parent, electrician or inspector *goes to the school / is at the school*.

## B prison, hospital, university, college, church

The same contrast applies to these places. **No article** when we mean the institution and its normal purpose (student, patient, prisoner, worshipper); **the** when we mean a specific building visited for another reason.

| general idea (no the) | specific building (the) |
|---|---|
| After school, Lucy hopes to go to university / go to college. (as a student) | I drove to the university to return a library book. (as a visitor) |
| Grandad fell and was taken to hospital. He's still in hospital. (as a patient) | Meera went to the hospital to see Grandad. She's at the hospital now. (as a visitor) |
| Their neighbour is in prison for fraud. (he is a prisoner) | A film crew went to the prison to shoot a documentary. |
| Aunt Rita goes to church every Sunday. (for a service) | Some builders went to the church to fix the bell tower. |

With most **other** places you need **the**: *the station, the cinema, the bank, the airport* (see Units 72C and 73D).

## C bed, work, home

These are used **without the** in fixed expressions:

- **bed**: *go to bed, be in bed, get out of bed*. → *I usually go to bed around eleven.* / *She's still in bed.* ❌ *in the bed*
  But **the bed** = the piece of furniture: *The cat was asleep on the bed.*
- **work**: *go to work, be at work, start work, finish work, leave work*. → *Dan didn't go to work on Friday.* / *What time do you finish work?* ❌ *finish the work* (that would mean a particular task)
- **home**: *go home, come home, get home, arrive home, be (at) home, stay at home, work at home*. → *It's getting late — let's go home.* / *I worked at home yesterday.* Note: **no preposition** with *go/come/get/arrive home* (❌ *go to home*).

**See also:** the → Units 72–73, 75–78; Prepositions (at school / in hospital etc.) → Units 123–125; home → Unit 126C; American English → Appendix 7.

### Question 42

#### Question
Which sentence is correct about Farida, a 15-year-old pupil, on a normal Tuesday morning?

#### Answer
The correct sentence is *Farida is at school; lessons started at nine.* Farida is a pupil, so we think of *school* as a general idea (children learning), not as a specific building, and no article is used: *be at school, go to school*. *At the school* would suggest she is visiting the building for some other reason (as a parent or a repair worker would). *In a school* is not the fixed expression, and *at school* + *the lessons* is less natural, but the key error in the other options is the article before *school*.

#### Correct variant
¯\_(ツ)_/¯

#### Variants
1. Farida is at the school; lessons started at nine.
2. Farida is at school; lessons started at nine. (Correct)
3. Farida is in a school; lessons started at nine.
4. Farida is at the school; the lessons started at nine.

### Question 43

#### Question
Compare:

A. Ben's grandmother was taken to hospital after a fall.
B. Ben went to the hospital yesterday with a bunch of flowers.

Which explanation of the two articles is correct?

#### Answer
Both sentences are correct, and the difference is the speaker's role. In A the grandmother is a **patient**, so *hospital* is the general idea of "a place where sick people are treated" and takes **no article**. In B Ben is a **visitor**: he goes to a specific building for a reason other than treatment, so we say **the hospital**. Option 1 wrongly "corrects" A; option 2 wrongly says British English never uses *the hospital*; option 4 confuses the rule with first vs second mention.

#### Correct variant
¯\_(ツ)_/¯

#### Variants
1. A is wrong; it should be *taken to the hospital* because a hospital is a building.
2. B is wrong; in British English *hospital* never takes *the*.
3. Both are correct: patients are *in/to hospital* (general idea), visitors go *to the hospital* (specific building). (Correct)
4. Both are correct only because A is the first mention and B the second.

### Question 44

#### Question
Spot the mistake: "My father was in the prison for two years for tax evasion. When he came out he went to church every week and never missed work."

#### Answer
The mistake is *in the prison*. The father was a **prisoner**, so we think of prison as an institution and use **no article**: *in prison for two years*. *The prison* would be correct only for a specific building visited by someone who is not a prisoner (*The lawyer drove to the prison*). *Go to church* (for services) and *miss work* are both correctly used without an article.

#### Correct variant
¯\_(ツ)_/¯

#### Variants
1. *went to church* should be *went to the church*.
2. *in the prison* should be *in prison*, because he was a prisoner. (Correct)
3. *missed work* should be *missed the work*.
4. *for two years* should be *for the two years*.

### Question 46

#### Question
Complete the sentence with the correct expression: "Jonas finished ___ at six, drove ___ and was ___ by seven."

#### Answer
The correct sentence is *Jonas finished work at six, drove home and was at home by seven.* **finish work** takes no article (the job in general, not a particular task); **drive/go/get home** takes **no preposition and no article**; **be at home** takes *at* but no article. *The work* would refer to a specific piece of work, *to home* is a common error (home is treated like an adverb after verbs of movement), and *at the home* is wrong.

#### Correct variant
