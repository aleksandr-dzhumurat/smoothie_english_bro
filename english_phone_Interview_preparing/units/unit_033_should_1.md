# Unit 33 — should 1

# Theory

## A. should = it's a good / the right thing to do

**You should do something** means it is a good idea or the right thing to do. Use it for advice and opinions:

- Your cough sounds terrible. You **should** see a doctor.
- The council **should** build more cycle lanes.
- a: **Should** we book the tickets now? b: Yes, I think we **should**.
- That cyclist **should be wearing** lights – it's dark. (should + be + -ing for something happening now)

**You shouldn't do something** = it isn't a good thing to do:

- You **shouldn't** leave your bag on the seat when you go to the counter.

*Should* often appears with **I think / I don't think / Do you think …?**:

- I **think** they **should** apologise.
- I **don't think** you **should** sign anything yet.
- **Do you think** I **should** accept the offer? – Yes, I think you **should**.

*Should* is weaker than *must* / *have to*:

| should | must / have to |
|---|---|
| You **should** renew your passport soon. (good idea) | You **must** renew your passport. (no choice) |

## B. should for what is expected / normal

Use *should* when something is **not as it ought to be**:

- The bus **should be** here by now. Where is it? (= it isn't here, and that's not normal)
- This bill is wrong. It **should say** £40, not £60.

And to say what you **expect** to happen:

- Kemal has practised for months, so he **should** do well in the concert. (= I expect him to)
- The road is empty at this hour; the drive **shouldn't** take more than twenty minutes.

## C. should have (done) – the past

**You should have done something** = you didn't do it, but it would have been the right thing:

- The concert was fantastic. You **should have come**. (= you didn't come)
- The parcel **should have arrived** by now. (= it hasn't, and I expected it)

**You shouldn't have done something** = you did it, but it was a mistake:

- My eyes hurt. I **shouldn't have stayed** up so late. (= I did stay up late)
- He **shouldn't have been driving** so fast in the rain. (= he was driving fast)

Compare present and past:

| now / future | past |
|---|---|
| You look exhausted. You **should rest**. | You worked all weekend. You **should have rested**. |

## D. ought to

**Ought to** can replace *should* in every use above. Note the **to**:

- Do you think I **ought to** call them? (= should I call them?)
- Nadia **ought not to** eat so much sugar. (= shouldn't eat)
- You **ought to have told** me earlier. (= should have told)

### Question 32

#### Question
A friend says: "My laptop keeps crashing, but I've never installed the updates." You want to give gentle advice, not an order. Which sentence is most appropriate?

#### Answer
"You **should** install the updates." *Should* expresses advice – it is a good idea to do it – which is exactly the tone wanted. "You must install the updates" is much stronger and suggests there is no alternative. "You should to install" wrongly adds *to* after the modal *should* (that is the pattern for *ought to*). "You ought install" is missing the *to* that *ought* requires.

#### Correct variant
¯\_(ツ)_/¯

#### Variants
1. You must install the updates.
2. You should to install the updates.
3. You should install the updates. (Correct)
4. You ought install the updates.

### Question 33

#### Question
It's 9.15. The bakery's sign says it opens at 8.30, but the door is still locked. Which sentence describes the situation correctly?

#### Answer
"The bakery **should be** open by now." This is the use of *should* for something that is not as we expect or as it ought to be – it is normally open at this time, but it isn't. "Should have been open" would suggest a past period that is over ("it should have been open yesterday"), but the speaker is talking about now. "Must be open" is a deduction that it *is* open, which contradicts the locked door. "Should open by now" is ungrammatical here – *should* needs the state verb *be*.

#### Correct variant
¯\_(ツ)_/¯

#### Variants
1. The bakery should have been open by now.
2. The bakery must be open by now.
3. The bakery should open by now.
4. The bakery should be open by now. (Correct)

### Question 34

#### Question
Lena spent her whole salary on the first day of the month and now has nothing left for rent. Which sentence expresses that this was a mistake she already made?

#### Answer
"She **shouldn't have spent** her whole salary on the first day." *Shouldn't have + past participle* means *she did it, and it was a bad thing to do*. "She shouldn't spend" is advice about the present or future, not a comment on what already happened. "She should have spent" would say that spending it was the right thing and she failed to do it – the opposite of the meaning. "She shouldn't had spent" is not a correct form (*have*, not *had*, after a modal).

### Question 36

#### Question
One of these sentences is incorrect. Which one, and what is the problem?

#### Answer
"I think you **ought** apologise to her" is incorrect: *ought* is always followed by **to** – *you ought to apologise*. The other sentences are fine. "Do you think I should apply?" shows the common pattern *Do you think … should*; "You ought to have called first" is the correct past form of *ought to*; "We shouldn't have taken the motorway" correctly uses *shouldn't have + past participle* for a past mistake.

#### Correct variant
¯\_(ツ)_/¯

#### Variants
1. Do you think I should apply for the scholarship?
2. You ought to have called before you came round.
3. I think you ought apologise to her. (Correct)
4. We shouldn't have taken the motorway – the traffic was awful.
