# Unit 86 — no/none/any nothing/nobody etc.

# Theory

## A no and none

**no + noun** = *not a* or *not any*:
- We couldn’t phone – there was no signal. (= there wasn’t a signal)
- Yusuf will have no difficulty passing. (= won’t have any difficulty)
- There were no seats free. (= there weren’t any seats free)

*no + noun* can start a sentence:
- No explanation was offered for the delay.

**none** is used **without** a noun, or with **of**:
- "How many tickets are left?" "None." (= no tickets)
- The cake’s gone – there’s none left.
- None of this luggage is ours.

Three ways to say the same thing:
- I have **no** cash. / "How much cash have you got?" "**None**." **or** "I haven’t got **any**."

After **none of + plural**, the verb can be singular or plural:
- None of the shops was open. / None of the shops were open.

## B nothing / nobody / no-one / nowhere

These can begin a sentence or stand alone as short answers:
- "Who called?" "Nobody. / No-one."
- "What did you buy?" "Nothing."
- "Where did you go?" "Nowhere – I stayed in."

They are also used after a verb, especially *be* and *have*:
- The fridge is empty – there’s nothing in it.
- They had nowhere to park.

nothing / nobody etc. = **not + anything / anybody** etc.:

| negative word | = not + any- |
|---|---|
| She said nothing. | She didn’t say anything. |
| I told nobody. | I didn’t tell anybody. |
| We have nowhere to sit. | We don’t have anywhere to sit. |

With *nothing / nobody* etc. the verb is **positive** – no double negatives:
- I heard nothing. (❌ I didn’t hear nothing)

## C they / them / their after nobody / no-one

- Nobody is perfect, are they?
- No-one brought their ID, so no-one got in.

## D no- vs any-

**any-** can mean "it doesn’t matter which/what/who" (Unit 85D). Compare:
- There was no taxi, so we walked. / You can take any taxi – they all go to the centre.
- "What do you want to drink?" "Nothing, thanks." / I’m so thirsty I’d drink anything.
- It’s a horrible job. Nobody wants it. / It’s an easy job. Anybody could do it.

**See also:** Unit 85 (some and any), Unit 88 (none of …), Unit 106B (any bigger / no better).

### Question 45

#### Question
Complete the sentence: "The village was tiny. There was ____ bank, and ____ of the shops sold newspapers."

#### Answer
**no … none.** Before a noun (*bank*) we use *no*; before *of* we use *none* (*none of the shops*). *None bank* is impossible because *none* never directly precedes a noun, and *no of the shops* is also ungrammatical. *Any* would need a negative verb (*there wasn’t any bank*).

#### Correct variant
¯\_(ツ)_/¯

#### Variants
1. none … no
2. no … none (Correct)
3. any … none
4. no … any

### Question 46

#### Question
Which sentence is wrong, and why?

A) I didn’t recognise anybody at the reunion.
B) I recognised nobody at the reunion.
C) I didn’t recognise nobody at the reunion.
D) There was nobody I recognised at the reunion.

#### Answer
**C is wrong.** English does not combine a negative verb with *nobody/nothing/nowhere*: it should be either *I didn’t recognise anybody* (negative verb + any-) or *I recognised nobody* (positive verb + no-). A, B and D each use exactly one negative element and are correct.

#### Correct variant
¯\_(ツ)_/¯

#### Variants
1. A — *anybody* should be *somebody* because the sentence is about a real person.
2. B — *nobody* cannot follow a verb; it must start the sentence.
3. C — it is a double negative; it should be *didn’t recognise anybody* or *recognised nobody*. (Correct)
4. D — *there was* cannot be followed by *nobody*.

### Question 48

#### Question
Two sentences, two different meanings. Choose the pair where BOTH sentences are correct and the second means "it doesn’t matter who".

#### Answer

### Question 49

#### Question
Which sentence correctly refers back to *no-one*?

#### Answer
**"No-one remembered to bring their passport."** After *nobody/no-one* we normally use *they/them/their* to refer back, avoiding *his or her*. *Its* is for things; *ours* changes the meaning; *his* is not wrong grammatically but is not the neutral form the book teaches, and the option given uses *his* with a mismatched plural noun *passports*.

#### Correct variant
¯\_(ツ)_/¯

#### Variants
1. No-one remembered to bring its passport.
2. No-one remembered to bring their passport. (Correct)
3. No-one remembered to bring ours passport.
4. No-one remembered to bring his passports.
