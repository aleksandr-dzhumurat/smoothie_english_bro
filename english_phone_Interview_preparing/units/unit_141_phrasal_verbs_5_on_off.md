# Unit 141 — Phrasal verbs 5: on/off (2)

# Theory

## A verb + on = continue

When **on** follows a verb, it very often adds the idea of *continuing* rather than stopping.

| Phrasal verb | Meaning | Pattern |
|---|---|---|
| **go on** | continue (of an event, a situation) | The lecture **went on** for nearly three hours. |
| **go on / carry on doing** something | continue doing it | She can't **carry on working** twelve hours a day. |
| **go on with / carry on with** something | continue it | Sorry I interrupted — please **go on with** your story. |
| **keep on doing** (or **keep doing**) something | do it again and again, or without stopping | My phone **keeps on ringing** during meetings. (or *keeps ringing*) |
| **drive on / walk on / read on / play on** | continue driving, walking, reading, playing | The road was blocked, so we couldn't **drive on**. |

Note the patterns: *carry on **doing*** (❌ carry on to do) and *carry on **with** + noun*.

## B get on

**Get on** has three separate meanings you need to keep apart:

1. **get on** = make progress, manage. *How is your daughter **getting on** at her new school?*
2. **get on (with somebody)** = have a good relationship. *My flatmate and I **get on** really well.* / *Those two colleagues just don't **get on**.*
3. **get on with something** = continue a task, usually after a break or interruption. *Stop chatting and **get on with** your homework.*

## C verb + off

Here **off** does not have one single meaning; learn each verb as a unit.

| Phrasal verb | Meaning | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **doze off / drop off / nod off** | fall asleep (often unintentionally) | Grandad **nodded off** in front of the television. |
| **finish** something **off** | complete the last part of it | I've nearly written the report; I'll **finish it off** after lunch. |
| **go off** | (of an alarm) sound | My alarm **went off** at six, but I ignored it. |
| **put** somebody **off** (doing) something | make them not want it / not want to do it | The smell of the kitchen **put me off** eating there. / The high fees **put him off** applying. |
| **rip** somebody **off** / **be ripped off** | cheat somebody / be charged far too much | Forty pounds for a taxi from the station? You **were ripped off**. |
| **show off** | try to impress people with what you can do or know | Nobody likes it when Marco **shows off** about his salary. |
| **tell** somebody **off** | speak angrily to somebody because they did wrong | The teacher **told the boys off** for running in the corridor. |

Remember: *put off* in this unit means "discourage" (*The price put me off*), not "postpone" (*We put off the meeting*) — that meaning is a different phrasal verb with the same form.

**Common mistakes:** ❌ *He keeps on to interrupt* ✅ *He keeps (on) interrupting*; ❌ *I don't get on him* ✅ *I don't get on **with** him*; ❌ *She told off me* ✅ *She told **me** off*.

**See also:** go on / carry on / keep on → Unit 53B; Phrasal verbs 1 (Introduction) → Unit 137; more verbs + on/off → Unit 140; American English → Appendix 7.

### Question 30

#### Question
A colleague says: "I hate cold calls. Every day the same salesman phones me — he _____ trying to sell me insurance." Which option correctly completes the sentence?

#### Answer
The correct completion is **keeps on** (or *keeps*): *he keeps on trying to sell me insurance*. **Keep on + -ing** means to do something repeatedly, which fits "every day the same salesman". After *keep on* the verb must be in the -ing form, so *keeps on to try* is wrong. *Gets on trying* is not a possible combination, and *goes off* means an alarm sounds, so it makes no sense here.

#### Correct variant

#### Variants
1. keeps on to try
2. gets on trying
3. keeps on trying (Correct)
4. goes off trying

### Question 33

#### Question

#### Answer

### Question 34

#### Question
Choose the pair of verbs that completes both sentences correctly:
(a) The smoke alarm _____ in the middle of the night, but it was a false alarm.

#### Answer
The answer is **went off / dozed off**. **Go off** is what an alarm does when it makes its sound, so *The smoke alarm went off*. **Doze off** (also *drop off*, *nod off*) means to fall asleep, so *I dozed off halfway through*. *Finished off* means completed the last part of something, which fits neither gap; *told off* requires an object and means "criticised angrily"; *showed off* means "tried to impress" and cannot describe an alarm.

#### Correct variant

2. went off / dozed off (Correct)
