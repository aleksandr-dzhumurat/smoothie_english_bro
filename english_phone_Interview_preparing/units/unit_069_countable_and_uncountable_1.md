# Unit 69 — Countable and uncountable 1

# Theory

## A Two types of noun

| Countable | Uncountable |
|---|---|
| I ate **an apple**. / I love **apples**. | I ate **bread**. / I love **bread**. |
| has a singular and a plural form (*apple / apples*) | has one form only – no plural (❌ *breads*) |
| can be used with numbers: *one apple, three apples* | cannot be counted directly: ❌ *one bread, two breads* |

Typical countable nouns: *a chair, a coin, a song, a beach, a battery, a mistake, a job.*
Typical uncountable nouns: *furniture, money, music, sand, electricity, luck, work, water, rice, salt.*

| Countable | Uncountable |
|---|---|
| Ferran was humming **a tune**. | Ferran was listening to **music**. |
| There’s **a coin** on the floor. | There’s **money** on the floor. |
| Is there **a bank** near here? | Is there **sand** on the beach? |
| It was **an accident**. | It was **bad luck**. |
| We need more **chairs**. | We need more **furniture**. |
| The remote needs new **batteries**. | The house has no **electricity**. |

## B a/an and using nouns alone

| Countable | Uncountable |
|---|---|
| **a/an** is possible with the singular: *a spoon, an egg, a student* | no **a/an**: ❌ *a rice, a money, an advice*. Use a measure word instead: *a bag of rice, a piece of advice, a bottle of water* |
| a singular countable noun **cannot stand alone** – it needs *a/the/my/this* etc.: ✅ *Would you like **a** biscuit?* ❌ *Would you like biscuit?* ✅ *There was **an** accident.* ❌ *There was accident.* | an uncountable noun **can stand alone**: *I drink **tea** every morning.* / *There’s **dust** on the shelf.* / *Can you smell **smoke**?* |
| a **plural** countable noun can stand alone for things in general: *I like **biscuits**.* / ***Mistakes** happen.* | |

## C some / any and many / much / few / little

| with plural countable nouns | with uncountable nouns |
|---|---|
| We bought **some** postcards. | We bought **some** bread. |
| Are there **any** tickets left? | Is there **any** milk left? |
| I didn’t take **many** photos. | I didn’t do **much** work. |
| I have **a few** errands to run. | I have **a little** time to spare. |

Remember: *many / few* go with countable plurals, *much / little* go with uncountables (❌ *much photos*, ❌ *many work*).

### Question 41

#### Question
Which sentence is correct?

#### Answer
"My cousin works for **an** advertising agency." *Agency* is a singular countable noun, and singular countable nouns cannot be used alone – they need *a/an*, *the*, *my*, etc. *Works for advertising agency* leaves the noun bare. *An advertising agencies* mixes a singular article with a plural noun. *Some advertising agency* is not how we refer to one specific workplace in this context (*some* with a singular noun means "a certain, unspecified", which sounds odd here).

#### Correct variant
¯\_(ツ)_/¯

#### Variants
1. My cousin works for advertising agency.
2. My cousin works for an advertising agency. (Correct)
3. My cousin works for an advertising agencies.
4. My cousin works for some advertising agency.

### Question 43

#### Question
Which of these sentences contains a mistake, and why?

A. There isn’t much traffic on Sunday mornings.
B. How many suitcases are you taking?
C. We didn’t get much replies to the survey.
D. I have a little homework to finish.

#### Answer
Sentence **C** is wrong. *Replies* is a plural countable noun, so it needs *many*, not *much*: "We didn’t get **many** replies to the survey." *Much* is only used with uncountable nouns like *traffic* (sentence A) and *homework* (which correctly takes *a little* in sentence D). Sentence B correctly pairs *many* with the countable plural *suitcases*.

#### Correct variant

### Question 44

#### Question
You are at a hardware shop. Which is the correct way to ask for the thing you need to hang a picture?

#### Answer
"I need **a nail** and **some string**." *Nail* is countable, so one nail is *a nail*; *string* (the material) is uncountable, so it takes *some* and no article. *A string* would refer to one specific string (e.g. on a guitar), not the material. *Nail* alone is a bare singular countable noun, which is not allowed, and *some nail* is wrong because *some* with a countable noun requires the plural (*some nails*).

#### Correct variant
¯\_(ツ)_/¯

#### Variants
1. I need nail and a string.
2. I need a nail and some string. (Correct)
3. I need some nail and a string.
4. I need a nail and some strings.

### Question 45

#### Question
Choose the correct pair to complete the sentence: "There ______ no ______ in the torch, so it won’t switch on."

#### Answer
"There **are** no **batteries** in the torch." *Battery* is a countable noun; a torch normally takes more than one, so the plural *batteries* with the plural verb *are* is right. *There is no battery* is grammatical but describes a torch that takes a single battery – a less natural default and also not offered with the right verb here. *Batterys* is a spelling error (consonant + *y* → *-ies*), and *battery* with *are* has subject–verb disagreement.

#### Correct variant
¯\_(ツ)_/¯

#### Variants
1. is … batteries
2. are … battery
3. are … batteries (Correct)
4. are … batterys
