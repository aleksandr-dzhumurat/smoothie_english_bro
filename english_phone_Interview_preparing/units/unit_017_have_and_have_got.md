# Unit 17 — have and have got

# Theory

## A have and have got for possession, relationships, illnesses, appointments

For "owning / having" meanings — possessions, family members, illnesses, appointments, characteristics — *have* and *have got* mean the same. Either is correct:

| have | have got |
|---|---|
| We **have** a small balcony. | We**'ve got** a small balcony. |
| Noor **has** three sisters. | Noor**'s got** three sisters. |
| I **have** a sore throat. | I**'ve got** a sore throat. |
| The café **has** free wifi. | The café**'s got** free wifi. |
| I **have** a dentist's appointment at four. | I**'ve got** a dentist's appointment at four. |

With these meanings we do **not** use continuous forms:

- We love the campsite. We **have / have got** a pitch right by the lake. ❌ (not *We're having a pitch*)

In the **past** we use *had*, normally without *got*:

- Grandpa **had** a moustache when he was young. ❌ (not *had got*)

## B Questions and negatives

There are three patterns in the present; the first two are the common ones.

| Question | Negative |
|---|---|
| **Do** you **have** a spare charger? | I **don't have** a spare charger. |
| **Have** you **got** a spare charger? | I **haven't got** a spare charger. |
| **Have** you a spare charger? (less usual) | I **haven't** a spare charger. (less usual) |

Third person: *Does she have …? / Has she got …?* — *She doesn't have … / She hasn't got …*

In the **past**, questions and negatives use *did / didn't*:

- **Did** you **have** a bike when you lived in Amsterdam?
- I **didn't have** any cash, so I couldn't pay.
- Grandpa **had** a moustache, **didn't** he?

## C have for things we do or experience

*Have* (but **not** *have got*) is also used for actions and experiences:

| have + … |
|---|
| breakfast / lunch / dinner / a coffee / something to drink |
| a bath / a shower / a swim / a nap / a break / a rest / a holiday / a party |
| an accident / an experience / a dream / a nightmare |
| a look (at something) / a chat / a discussion / an argument (with somebody) |
| trouble / difficulty / fun / a good time |
| a baby (= give birth) |

Compare:

- I usually **have** (= eat) porridge for breakfast. ❌ (not *I've got porridge for breakfast* in this sense)
- I**'ve got / I have** some porridge in the cupboard. (possession) ✅

Because these are actions, **continuous forms are possible**:

- Don't ring now — Amir**'s having** a shower.
- We**'re having** a lovely time in Crete.

Questions and negatives with these expressions use **do / does / did**:

- I **don't** usually **have** a big lunch. ❌ (not *I usually haven't*)
- Where **does** Lena **have** lunch?
- **Did** you **have** any trouble parking? ❌ (not *Had you*)
