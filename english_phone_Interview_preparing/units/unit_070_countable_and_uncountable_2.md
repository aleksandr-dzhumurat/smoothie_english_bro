# Unit 70 — Countable and uncountable 2

# Theory

## A Nouns that can be both

Many nouns are countable in one meaning and uncountable in another:

| Countable (a specific thing) | Uncountable (the general idea / material) |
|---|---|
| I heard **a noise** in the attic. (= one particular sound) | The kids are making too much **noise**. (= noise in general) |
| She grabbed **a paper** at the station. (= a newspaper) | The printer has run out of **paper**. (= the material) |
| There’s **a hair** on your jacket. (= one single hair) | Marco has curly **hair**. (❌ *hairs*) (= all the hair on his head) |
| The flat has three **rooms**. (= rooms in a building) | Is there **room** for one more in the car? (= space) |
| I had some strange **experiences** in Peru. (= things that happened) | The job requires five years’ **experience**. (❌ *experiences*) |
| We had **a lovely time** at the wedding. | Sorry, I don’t have **time** to talk now. |
| There was **a light** on in the office. (= a lamp) | Plants need **light** to grow. |
| Pass me **a glass**, please. (= a drinking vessel) | The window is made of **glass**. (= material) |

Drinks are normally uncountable (*I don’t drink **coffee***), but in a café you can count servings: *Three **teas** and **a** hot chocolate, please.*

## B Nouns that are usually uncountable

Learn these – they are countable in many other languages but **not** in English:

*accommodation, advice, baggage, behaviour, bread, chaos, damage, furniture, information, luck, luggage, news, permission, progress, scenery, traffic, travel, weather, work*

Rules:
- No **a/an**: *Let me give you **some advice**.* (❌ *an advice*) / *We had **terrible weather**.* (❌ *a terrible weather*)
- No plural: *They sell second-hand **furniture**.* (❌ *furnitures*) / *Thanks for **the information**.* (❌ *informations*)
- **News** looks plural but is uncountable and takes a singular verb: *The **news is** good.* (❌ *The news are*)
- **Travel** means travelling in general; for one occasion use **a trip / a journey**: *How was your **journey**?* (❌ *your travel*)
- Use a measure word to count: *a loaf of bread, a piece of furniture, a piece/bit of news, an item of luggage, a stroke of luck.*

## C Countable vs uncountable near-synonyms

| Countable | Uncountable |
|---|---|
| I’ve found **a job**. | I’ve found **work**. (❌ *a work*) |
| What **a** stunning **view**! | What stunning **scenery**! |
| It’s **a** gorgeous **day**. | It’s gorgeous **weather**. |
| We had too many **bags**. | We had too much **luggage / baggage**. |
| These **tables** are new. | This **furniture** is new. |
| That’s **a** useful **tip**. | That’s useful **advice**. |
| There were hundreds of **lorries**. | There was heavy **traffic**. |

#### Question
Choose the correct forms: "We stayed at a lovely hotel. The ______ was excellent, and we had ______ from the balcony."

#### Answer
"The **accommodation** was excellent, and we had **a wonderful view** from the balcony." *Accommodation* is uncountable in British English, so it has no plural and no article. *View* is countable (one particular view from one place), so it takes *a*; its uncountable near-synonym is *scenery* (*wonderful scenery*, no article). *Accommodations* and *a wonderful scenery* are both errors of countability.

#### Correct variant

2. accommodation … a wonderful view (Correct)

### Question 39

#### Question
Which sentence is INCORRECT, and why?

A. The news about the merger was a complete surprise.
B. Could you give me some information about evening classes?
C. Sara has made good progress with her Portuguese.
D. Our travel to Vienna took nine hours because of a strike.

#### Answer
Sentence **D** is wrong. *Travel* is an uncountable noun meaning travelling in general; a single occasion is *a journey* or *a trip*: "Our **journey** to Vienna took nine hours." Sentence A is correct because *news* is uncountable and takes a singular verb (*was*). Sentence B correctly uses *some information* (no plural). Sentence C correctly uses *progress* without an article.

#### Correct variant
¯\_(ツ)_/¯

2. Sentence B – *some information* should be *some informations*.
3. Sentence C – *good progress* should be *a good progress*.

An interviewer says: "You’ve worked in three different countries – tell me about that." Which reply uses *experience* correctly?

#### Answer
"I’ve had a lot of **interesting experiences** abroad, and I now have plenty of **experience** of working in international teams." *Experience* is countable when it means an event that happened to you (*an experience, experiences*) and uncountable when it means the knowledge or skill gained over time (*experience*, no plural). *A lot of interesting experience abroad* is possible but the option pairs it with *experiences of working*, which is wrong for skill; the other distractors reverse or pluralise the wrong noun.

### Question 41

#### Question
Your friend is going hiking in Scotland in November. Which sentence is grammatically correct?

#### Answer
"I hope you have **good weather** – there will be **a lot of traffic** on the way." *Weather* and *traffic* are uncountable: no *a/an* (❌ *a good weather*, ❌ *a heavy traffic*) and no plural (❌ *weathers*, ❌ *traffics*). *Many* goes only with countable plurals, so *many traffics* is doubly wrong. *A lot of* works with both countable and uncountable nouns, so *a lot of traffic* is fine.

#### Correct variant
¯\_(ツ)_/¯

#### Variants
1. I hope you have a good weather – there will be a lot of traffic on the way.
2. I hope you have good weather – there will be many traffics on the way.
3. I hope you have good weathers – there will be a heavy traffic on the way.
4. I hope you have good weather – there will be a lot of traffic on the way. (Correct)
