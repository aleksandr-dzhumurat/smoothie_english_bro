# Unit 22 — will and shall 2

# Theory

## A. will for predictions, not for arrangements

*Will* is **not** used for things somebody has already arranged or decided:

- Marta**'s working** late on Thursday. (not *Marta will work*)
- **Are you going to** watch the match tonight? (not *will you watch*)

We use *will* to say what we **know or believe** about the future – a prediction, an expectation, a belief – rather than a report of someone's plans:

- Paolo has a job interview tomorrow. I'm sure he**'ll** get it – he's very well prepared.
- The city has changed so much. When you come back, you **won't** recognise the centre.
- Careful, that pan is hot. If you touch it, you**'ll** burn your hand.
- When **will** the results be published?

Compare a plan with a prediction:

- I think Nadia **is going to** the concert. (= I believe she has already decided to go)
- I think Nadia **will go** to the concert. (= I predict she will decide to go)

## B. Common phrases with will

| Phrase | Example |
|---|---|
| probably | We**'ll probably** be a bit late. |
| I'm sure | **I'm sure** the interview **will** go well. |
| I think | Do you **think** Ravi **will** like the gift? |
| I don't think | **I don't think** the shop **will** be open on Sunday. |
| I wonder | **I wonder** who **will** win. |

After **I hope**, we generally use the **present**, not *will*:

- I hope the flight **isn't** delayed. (not *won't be*)
- I hope she **gets** the job.

## C. will about the present

Occasionally *will* describes what we assume is true **now**:

- Don't ring Kemal at the moment. He**'ll** be in a meeting. (= he is probably in a meeting now)

## D. shall

*Shall* is normally used only with **I** and **we**, and it means the same as *will*:

| Full form | Contracted (usual in speech) |
|---|---|
| I shall / I will | I'll |
| we shall / we will | we'll |

- I **shall** be away next week. (or I **will** be away)
- We **shall** probably move house in the spring. (or We **will** …)

The negative is **shall not** or **shan't**:

- I **shan't** be able to come tomorrow. (or I **won't** be able to)

Do **not** use *shall* with *he/she/it/you/they*:

- ❌ He shall be furious. ✅ He **will** be furious.

### Question 40

#### Question
Two sentences: (a) "I think Ines is going to the wedding." (b) "I think Ines will go to the wedding." What is the difference in meaning?

#### Answer
In (a), *is going to* refers to an existing plan: the speaker believes Ines has **already decided** to go. In (b), *will go* is a prediction: the speaker thinks Ines **will decide** to go, but no decision has been made yet. Both are grammatical; the difference is between reporting an arrangement and predicting a future decision. Neither sentence implies certainty over the other in terms of how sure the speaker feels.

#### Correct variant

### Question 42

#### Question
Which statement about *shall* is correct?

#### Answer
*Shall* is normally used only with *I* and *we*, where it means the same as *will* (*I shall be late* = *I will be late*); in everyday speech *I'll / we'll* are far more common. Its negative is *shall not* or *shan't*. It is **not** used with *he/she/it/you/they* – "She shall be angry" is wrong; say "She **will** be angry". Variant 4 is a misconception: *shall* is not more emphatic than *will* in this use.

#### Correct variant
¯\_(ツ)_/¯

#### Variants
1. *Shall* can be used with any subject, exactly like *will*.
2. *Shall* has no negative form; you must use *won't* instead.
3. *Shall* is used mainly with *I* and *we*, means the same as *will*, and its negative is *shan't*; it is not used with *he/she/they*. (Correct)
