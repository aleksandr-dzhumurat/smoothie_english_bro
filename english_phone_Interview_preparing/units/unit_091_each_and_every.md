# Unit 91 — each and every

# Theory

## A Each vs every: separate items vs a group

*Each* and *every* are close in meaning, and in many sentences either works:

- Each morning I check my email. / Every morning I check my email.

The difference is in how the speaker sees the items.

| | each | every |
|---|---|---|
| viewpoint | one by one, separately | as a whole group (≈ *all*) |
| typical number | small | large |
| example | Check each answer before you hand in the test. (= go through them one at a time) | Every light in the office was switched off. (= all the lights) |

- Small number: There were three candidates. Each candidate gave a short talk. / In chess, each player starts with sixteen pieces.
- Large number: Maria has visited every capital city in Europe. / He knows every song on that album.

Two special rules:

- Only *each* can refer to **two** things: In doubles tennis, each pair has two players. (not every pair)
- Only *every* expresses **frequency** (how often): I go swimming every Tuesday. (not each Tuesday) / The lift stops at every floor. / Trains leave every twenty minutes. (not each twenty minutes)

## B Structures with each and every

| each | every |
|---|---|
| each + noun: Each room has a balcony. | every + noun: Every room has a balcony. |
| each alone: The rooms are all different. Each is unique. | every never stands alone ❌ *Every is unique.* ✅ Every one is unique. |
| each one: Each one is unique. | every one: I have tried all the dishes, and every one was excellent. |
| each of the/these/them: Each of the rooms was painted a different colour. Each of them was empty. ❌ *each of rooms* | every one of the/those/them: I have read every one of those reports. ❌ *every of those reports* |

## C each in mid-position and after a price

*Each* can go in the middle of the sentence, after the subject:

- The guests were each handed a glass of champagne. (= Each guest was handed …)
- We were each asked to sign the form.

After a price or quantity, *each* means "for one":

- These postcards cost a pound each.
- The tickets were thirty euros each.

## D everyone vs every one

- **everyone** (one word) = everybody; only for people: Everyone laughed at the joke.
- **every one** (two words) = each individual thing or person: Tom owns dozens of vinyl records and he has listened to every one. (= every record)

Compare: ❌ *I smashed six plates and everyone broke.* ✅ … every one broke.

**See also:** each other ➜ Unit 82C; all and every ➜ Unit 90.

#### Question
A coach is explaining the rules of a game to two teams: "In this game, ___ team has five players." Which word fits, and why?

#### Answer
The correct sentence is "In this game, **each** team has five players." *Each*, but not *every*, can be used when we are talking about two things; there are only two teams here, so *every team* is not possible. *Each of team* is wrong because *each of* must be followed by *the/these/them* etc. (each of the teams). *Every one team* is not a structure in English.

### Question 31

#### Question
Which of the following sentences contains a mistake in the use of *each* / *every*?

#### Answer
The wrong sentence is "There's a ferry to the island **each** two hours." When we say how often something happens, we must use *every* (every two hours, every day, every ten minutes), not *each*. The other three sentences are fine: *each* is natural for a small number seen one by one (three drawers), *every* for a large group (all the letters), and "Each of them" is the correct structure with *of*.

#### Correct variant
¯\_(ツ)_/¯

#### Variants
1. The desk has three drawers. Each drawer is locked.
2. There's a ferry to the island each two hours. (Correct)
3. She answered every letter she received that year.
4. I have four cousins. Each of them lives abroad.

### Question 32

#### Question
A friend asks: "Did you enjoy all the short stories in the collection?" You want to say that you liked all of them, one by one. Which reply is grammatically correct?

#### Answer
"Yes, **every one**." (or "Yes, each one.") is correct. *Every* cannot stand alone without a noun, so "Yes, every." is impossible; you must say *every one*. "Yes, everyone." is wrong because *everyone* (one word) refers only to people, and stories are things. "Yes, every of them." is wrong because *every of* does not exist; the correct form is *every one of them*.

#### Correct variant
¯\_(ツ)_/¯

#### Variants
1. Yes, every.
2. Yes, everyone.
3. Yes, every one. (Correct)
4. Yes, every of them.

### Question 33

#### Question
Rewrite this idea using *each*: "One of these pens costs two pounds." Which sentence is correct?

#### Answer
The correct sentence is "These pens are **two pounds each**." After a price, *each* means "for one item", and it comes at the end of the sentence. "Each these pens are two pounds" is wrong because *each* + *these* needs *of* (each of these pens). "These pens are each two pounds every" is nonsense — *every* cannot end a sentence like that. "Every pen are two pounds" has a verb agreement error: *every pen* is singular and takes *is*.

#### Correct variant
¯\_(ツ)_/¯

#### Variants
1. Each these pens are two pounds.
2. These pens are two pounds each. (Correct)
3. These pens are each two pounds every.
4. Every pen are two pounds.

### Question 34

#### Question
Choose the sentence in which *everyone* / *every one* is used correctly.

#### Answer
The correct sentence is "The waiter dropped a tray of cups and **every one** broke." *Every one* (two words) is used for things or people considered individually — here, the cups. *Everyone* (one word) means *everybody* and refers only to people, so "everyone broke" and "everyone of the windows" are wrong when talking about cups and windows. In the fourth option, the person is talking about people, so it should be "Everyone was pleased", not "Every one" — although *every one* is technically possible for people, the natural word for "everybody" is *everyone*, and *every one* here would need a reference group (every one of them).

#### Correct variant
¯\_(ツ)_/¯

#### Variants
1. The waiter dropped a tray of cups and everyone broke.
2. The waiter dropped a tray of cups and every one broke. (Correct)
3. Everyone of the windows was dirty.
4. Every one was pleased with the result of the meeting.
