# Unit 75 — the 3 (children / the children)

# Theory

## A Things in general → no the

When we talk about people or things **in general** — a whole class, not a particular group — we use plural or uncountable nouns **without the**:

- I'm scared of spiders. ❌ *the spiders* (= spiders in general)
- Nurses work longer hours than most office workers.
- Do you know anyone who repairs clocks?
- Technology has changed enormously in twenty years.
- Do you like jazz / Thai food / old cars?
- My favourite sport is tennis / cycling / swimming.
- Her best subject at university was chemistry / economics / Spanish.

**most** + noun, never *the most*: *Most cafés have wifi now.* ❌ *The most cafés*

## B Specific things → the

**the** points to particular, identifiable people or things.

| in general (no the) | specific (the) |
|---|---|
| Children need plenty of sleep. | The children are already asleep. (= our children) |
| I can't work without music. | The film was dull, but the music was wonderful. (= the music in that film) |
| All bicycles have brakes. | All the bicycles in this shop are second-hand. |
| Salt isn't good for your blood pressure. | Could you pass the salt? (= the salt on this table) |
| Italian people eat a lot of pasta. | The Italian people I met in Rome were very welcoming. (= only those I met) |

## C General vs specific is not always sharp

Adding a describing word or clause does **not** automatically make a noun specific. If the phrase still names a *kind* of thing, there is still no *the*.

| still general (no the) | specific (the) |
|---|---|
| I enjoy working with animals. | — |
| I enjoy working with animals that are well trained. (not all animals, but still a type) | I enjoy working with the animals at the shelter. (= a particular group) |
| Do you like tea? | — |
| Do you like green tea with lemon? (a type of tea, still general) | The tea they served at the meeting was cold. (= a particular tea) |

Ask yourself: am I talking about **a kind** of thing (no *the*) or **a particular one/group** that the listener can identify (*the*)?

### Question 39

#### Question
Spot the sentence with a mistake and explain why it is wrong.

A. The teachers at my daughter's school are very dedicated.
B. The teachers are usually paid less than the lawyers.
C. Teachers are usually paid less than lawyers.
D. Do you know the teachers who run the chess club?

#### Answer
Sentence B is wrong. It is a **general** comparison between two professions, so both nouns should appear without an article: *Teachers are usually paid less than lawyers* (sentence C). *The teachers* / *the lawyers* would refer to specific, identifiable groups, which is not the meaning. Sentences A and D correctly use **the** because the teachers are identified by *at my daughter's school* and *who run the chess club*.

#### Correct variant
¯\_(ツ)_/¯

#### Variants
1. Sentence A: *at my daughter's school* makes it general, so *the* must go.
2. Sentence B: it is a general statement about professions, so no *the*. (Correct)
3. Sentence C: plural nouns always need *the* when they are compared.
4. Sentence D: *who run the chess club* is a general idea, so no *the*.

### Question 41

#### Question
Which explanation of the difference between these two sentences is correct?

A. I don't like coffee with sugar.
B. I didn't like the coffee at the conference.

#### Answer
Both sentences are correct. In A, *coffee with sugar* is still **a type of coffee** — the phrase *with sugar* narrows the meaning but does not identify one particular coffee — so no article is used. In B, *the coffee at the conference* is **a specific coffee** the speaker actually drank on one occasion, so **the** is used. Adding descriptive words does not automatically require *the*; what matters is whether the listener can identify a particular thing.

#### Correct variant
¯\_(ツ)_/¯

#### Variants
1. A is wrong: *with sugar* makes the coffee specific, so it should be *the coffee with sugar*.
2. B is wrong: *coffee* is uncountable, so it can never take *the*.
3. Both are correct: A names a kind of coffee (general, no *the*); B refers to a particular coffee drunk on one occasion (specific, *the*). (Correct)
4. Both sentences mean the same; *the* is optional with uncountable nouns.
