# Unit 49 — Questions 1

# Theory

## A — Word order: the subject comes after the first verb

In a statement the subject comes before the verb; in a question the **subject moves after the first (auxiliary) verb**:

| Statement (subject + verb) | Question (verb + subject) |
|---|---|
| Noor **has** finished. | **Has Noor** finished? |
| The shop **will** open. | When **will the shop** open? |
| The bridge **was** repaired. | When **was the bridge** repaired? |

Only the first verb moves: *Is Daniel working late tonight?* ❌ *Is working Daniel late tonight?*

## B — do / does / did in simple tenses

Present simple and past simple have no auxiliary in statements, so questions add **do / does / did** and the main verb goes back to its base form:

- You live here. → **Do you live** here?
- The course starts in May. → When **does the course start**? (not *does the course starts*)
- She sold the car. → **Did she sell** the car? (not *did she sold*)
- The bus stopped. → Why **did the bus stop**?

### who / what / which as subject — no do/does/did

When the question word **is itself the subject**, the word order stays as in a statement and there is no *do/does/did*:

| Question word = object | Question word = subject |
|---|---|
| Who **did** Ali call? (Ali called somebody) | Who **called** Ali? (somebody called Ali) |
| What **did** you break? | What **broke**? |
| Which flavour **do** you want? | Which flavour **tastes** best? |

- Who wants a coffee? ❌ *Who does want*
- What happened at the meeting? ❌ *What did happen*
- How many students passed? ❌ *did pass*

## C — Prepositions at the end

With *who / what / which / where*, a preposition normally goes at the **end** of the question:

- Where do you come **from**?
- Who is this present **for**?
- What is the film **about**?
- Which company did she work **for**?

Formal style can put the preposition first with *whom*: *For whom is this present?*

## D — Negative questions

Negative questions (*isn't … ? didn't … ? haven't … ?*) are used:

- to show **surprise**: *Didn't you know the shop had closed?*
- when we **expect agreement**: *'Isn't this the café we went to last year?' 'Yes, it is.'*
- with **Why**: *Why don't you take a break?* / *Why wasn't the door locked?* ❌ *Why you don't take a break?*

Note what *yes* and *no* mean after a negative question: *'Don't you like it?' 'Yes.'* = Yes, I like it. *'No.'* = No, I don't like it.

### Question 38

#### Question
You want to know the time your friend's flight arrives. Which question is correct?

#### Answer
"What time **does your flight arrive**?" A present simple question needs *does* (third person singular) and the main verb in its base form. "Does your flight arrives" doubles the third-person *-s*. "Your flight arrives at what time" is statement order and not a standard question. "Arrives your flight" puts the main verb before the subject, which English doesn't do in the present simple — an auxiliary is required.

#### Correct variant
¯\_(ツ)_/¯

#### Variants
1. What time does your flight arrives?
2. What time arrives your flight?
3. What time does your flight arrive? (Correct)
4. What time your flight does arrive?

### Question 39

#### Question
Somebody left a message for you on your desk, and you want to know who. Which question is correct?

#### Answer
"Who **left** this message?" Here *who* is the **subject** of the sentence (somebody left the message), so the word order stays as in a statement and *did* is not used. "Who did leave this message?" adds an unnecessary auxiliary — this order is only right when *who* is the object (e.g. *Who did you leave the message for?*). "Who left this message for" changes the meaning and lacks an object. "Who was left this message" is a passive that would mean somebody received the message.

#### Correct variant
¯\_(ツ)_/¯

#### Variants
1. Who did leave this message?
2. Who left this message? (Correct)
3. Who did left this message?
4. Who was left this message?

### Question 40

#### Question
Your colleague looks upset after a phone call. You ask about the other person on the line. Which question is **correct**?

A. Who did you talk to?
B. To who did you talk?
C. Who talked to you?
D. Who did talk to you?

#### Answer
Both A and C are grammatically correct, but they mean different things. A — "Who did you talk to?" — asks about the object (the other person), with the preposition at the end, which is the natural place for it. C — "Who talked to you?" — asks about the subject (the caller) with no *did*. B is wrong because *to who* is not used; formal English would be *To whom did you talk?*. D is wrong because when *who* is the subject, *did* is not added.

#### Correct variant
¯\_(ツ)_/¯

#### Variants
1. Only B — prepositions must go at the start of a question.
2. Only D — past simple questions always need *did*.
3. A and C are both correct; they ask different things. (Correct)
4. Only A — *who* can never be the subject of a question.

#### Answer

"You aren't going to the concert?" is a statement with rising intonation — possible in speech but not the question form the unit teaches. "Don't you going" wrongly combines *do* with an *-ing* form. "Are you not go" uses the base form instead of *going*.

### Question 42

#### Question
Spot the mistake. A learner wrote: "Why Laura didn't come to the training yesterday? What did happen?"

#### Answer
Both questions are wrong. The first needs question order after *Why*: "Why **didn't Laura come** to the training yesterday?" — the auxiliary *didn't* goes before the subject. In the second, *what* is the **subject** (something happened), so *did* must not be used: "What **happened**?" Options that correct only one of the two questions are incomplete.

#### Correct variant
¯\_(ツ)_/¯

#### Variants
1. Only the first question is wrong; *What did happen?* is correct emphatic English.
2. Only the second question is wrong; *Why Laura didn't come* is acceptable after *why*.
3. Both are wrong: *Why didn't Laura come …?* and *What happened?* (Correct)
4. Neither is wrong.
