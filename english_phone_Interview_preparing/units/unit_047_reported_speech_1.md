# Unit 47 — Reported speech 1 (he said that …)

# Theory

## A — Direct and reported speech

There are two ways to tell someone what another person said:

- **Direct speech** repeats the exact words in quotation marks: *Nadia said, 'I'm starving.'*
- **Reported speech** puts the words into your own sentence: *Nadia said (that) she was starving.*

## B — The verb usually moves back into the past

In reported speech the reporting verb is normally past (*said, told me*), and the verbs in the reported clause usually move **one step back** as well. *That* can be left out.

| Direct speech | Reported speech |
|---|---|
| am / is | was |
| are | were |
| do / does | did |
| have / has | had |
| will | would |
| can | could |
| want / know / go / live etc. | wanted / knew / went / lived etc. |

Pronouns and time words change too when needed (*I → he/she, my → her, tomorrow → the next day*).

Examples:

- 'I'm looking for a new flat.' → Yusuf said he **was looking** for a new flat.
- 'We don't eat meat.' → They told me they **didn't eat** meat.
- 'I've never been to Japan.' → Chloe said she **had never been** to Japan.
- 'I'll email you the report.' → He said he **would email** me the report.
- 'I can't hear you.' → She said she **couldn't hear** me.
- 'My brother lives in Oslo.' → Lena said her brother **lived** in Oslo.

Longer reports keep the same rule throughout:

- 'I'm moving house next week, so I'll be busy.' → Marco said he was moving house the following week, so he would be busy.

## C — Past simple in direct speech

If the original words were already in the past simple, you have a choice: keep the **past simple** or change it to the **past perfect**.

- 'I missed the bus, so I walked.' →
  Ines said (that) she **missed** the bus, so she **walked**. *or*
  Ines said (that) she **had missed** the bus, so she **had walked**.

Both are correct; the past perfect emphasises that the event happened before the moment of speaking.

## Common mistakes

- ❌ He said me that he was tired. ✅ He **told me** / He **said** that he was tired.
- ❌ She said she will come. (when the situation is finished) ✅ She said she **would** come.
- ❌ They said they are having lunch. (reporting a past conversation) ✅ They said they **were having** lunch.

### Question 19

#### Question
Earlier today your colleague Petra said: "I can't come to the meeting because I'm waiting for a delivery." Later you tell your manager what she said. Which report is correct?

#### Answer
"Petra said she **couldn't come** to the meeting because she **was waiting** for a delivery." In reported speech the verbs move back: *can't → couldn't*, *am waiting → was waiting*, and *I → she*. "Can't come … is waiting" keeps the present forms of direct speech. "Couldn't come … waited" changes the continuous to a simple past, which loses the "in progress" meaning. "Couldn't came" is not a valid form — after *could* the base verb is needed.

#### Correct variant
¯\_(ツ)_/¯

#### Variants
1. Petra said she can't come to the meeting because she is waiting for a delivery.
2. Petra said she couldn't come to the meeting because she was waiting for a delivery. (Correct)
3. Petra said she couldn't come to the meeting because she waited for a delivery.
4. Petra said she couldn't came to the meeting because she was waiting for a delivery.

### Question 20

#### Question
Last week Owen said: "I'll pick you up at seven." He didn't turn up. Which sentence correctly reports what he said?

#### Answer
"Owen said he **would pick** me **up** at seven." *Will* changes to *would* in reported speech, and *you* becomes *me* because the speaker is now reporting words addressed to them. "Will pick me up" keeps the direct-speech future even though the arrangement is finished. "Would picked" wrongly uses a past form after *would*. "Will pick you up" changes neither the verb nor the pronoun.

#### Correct variant
¯\_(ツ)_/¯

#### Variants
1. Owen said he would pick me up at seven. (Correct)
2. Owen said he will pick me up at seven.
3. Owen said he would picked me up at seven.
4. Owen said he will pick you up at seven.

### Question 21

#### Question
Amira said: "I've already booked the tickets." Which reported version is correct?

#### Answer
"Amira said she **had already booked** the tickets." The present perfect (*have booked*) moves back to the past perfect (*had booked*) in reported speech. "Has already booked" keeps the direct-speech tense unchanged. "Already booked" (past simple) is a possible informal shortcut in some contexts but is not the systematic change the rule describes and loses the perfect aspect. "Had already book" lacks the past participle.

#### Correct variant
¯\_(ツ)_/¯

#### Variants
1. Amira said she has already booked the tickets.
2. Amira said she had already book the tickets.
3. Amira said she had already booked the tickets. (Correct)

### Question 22

#### Question
Yesterday Bruno said: "I lost my keys, so I slept at a friend's place." Which of the following reports is **acceptable**?

#### Answer
Both "Bruno said he **lost** his keys, so he **slept** at a friend's place" and "Bruno said he **had lost** his keys, so he **had slept** at a friend's place" are correct. When the direct speech is in the past simple, it can either stay in the past simple or move back to the past perfect — both are standard. Changing to present forms (*loses / sleeps*) or to *would* is wrong because the original was a finished past event.

#### Correct variant
¯\_(ツ)_/¯

#### Variants
1. Only *he had lost his keys, so he had slept* — past simple must always become past perfect.
2. Only *he lost his keys, so he slept* — the past perfect is never used in reported speech.
3. Either *he lost … slept* or *he had lost … had slept* — both are correct. (Correct)
4. *He would lose his keys, so he would sleep* — past simple becomes *would* + verb.

### Question 23

#### Question
Spot the mistake in this report of a conversation that happened last month: "Hugo said me that his parents are coming to visit and that he will need the spare room."

#### Answer
The sentence has two problems. First, *say* is not followed directly by a person: it should be "Hugo **told me**" (or simply "Hugo said that…"). Second, because the conversation was last month and the visit has already happened, the verbs should move back: "his parents **were coming** to visit and he **would need** the spare room." The corrected version: *Hugo told me that his parents were coming to visit and that he would need the spare room.*

#### Correct variant
¯\_(ツ)_/¯

#### Variants
1. Only *said me* is wrong; *are coming* and *will need* are fine because they describe the future.
2. Only the tenses are wrong; *said me* is a normal informal way of saying *told me*.
3. Nothing is wrong: reported speech never changes the original verbs.
4. *Said me* should be *told me*, and the verbs should move back to *were coming* and *would need*. (Correct)
