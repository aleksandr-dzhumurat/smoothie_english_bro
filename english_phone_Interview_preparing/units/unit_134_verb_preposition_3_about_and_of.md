# Unit 134 — Verb + preposition 3: about and of

# Theory

With several common verbs, *about* and *of* (and sometimes *from*) give clearly different meanings. Learn each pair as a contrast.

## A hear about / hear of / hear from

| Form | Meaning | Example |
|---|---|---|
| hear ABOUT … | be told the news of something | Did you hear about the flood in the old town? |
| hear OF … | know that somebody/something exists | "Who's Clara Voss?" "No idea — I've never heard of her." |
| hear FROM … | receive contact (a call, a letter) from somebody | I haven't heard from my cousin since he moved to Perth. |

❌ *I've never heard from that band.* (if you mean you don't know it) ✅ *I've never heard of that band.*

## B think about / think of

| Form | Meaning | Example |
|---|---|---|
| think ABOUT something | consider it, concentrate on it | Give me a week to think about your offer. |
| think OF something | produce an idea, remember | Who thought of the name for the café? / I can't think of her surname. |
| think OF (opinion) | *What do you think of …? / I don't think much of …* | What did you think of the new stadium? |

Where the difference is small, both work: *I often think of / think about my old school friends.* Both are also fine for future plans: *We're thinking of / about moving to the coast.*

Contrast: "I'll **think about** it" (I'll consider it) vs "I couldn't **think of** an answer" (no idea came).

## C dream about / dream of

- **dream ABOUT** = while asleep: *I dreamt about my old teacher last night.*
- **dream OF / ABOUT being or doing** = imagine a wished-for future: *As a boy he dreamt of becoming a pilot.*
- **I wouldn't dream OF doing** = I would never do it: *Cheat in the exam? I wouldn't dream of it.*

## D complain about / complain of

- **complain (TO somebody) ABOUT** = say you're not satisfied: *We complained to the landlord about the damp.*
- **complain OF** a pain / an illness = say you have it: *The patient complained of dizziness.*

## E remind about / remind of

- **remind somebody ABOUT** = tell them not to forget: *Can you remind me about the dentist on Thursday?*
- **remind somebody OF** = make them remember by resemblance: *That song reminds me of our first holiday.* / *Who does she remind you of?*

**See also:** remind somebody to … → Unit 55B.

### Question 35

#### Question
"Do you know a writer called Tomas Reinholt?" "No, I've never ______ him." Which is correct?

#### Answer
The correct answer is "I've never **heard of** him." *Hear of* somebody means to know that they exist. *Hear from* somebody means to receive a letter or call from them, which makes no sense here — you can't be in contact with someone you don't know. *Hear about* would mean to be told news about him, which also doesn't fit the question "Do you know …?".

#### Correct variant
¯\_(ツ)_/¯

#### Variants
1. heard from
2. heard of (Correct)
3. heard about
4. heard

#### Question
Two friends at a job interview debrief: "They offered me the post, but I asked for a few days to ______ it before deciding." Which fits?

#### Answer
The correct answer is "to **think about** it." *Think about* means to consider or concentrate your mind on something — exactly what you do before making a decision. *Think of* is used for producing an idea or giving an opinion, so "think of it" here would be the wrong choice. "Think on" and "think over about" are not correct patterns.

### Question 37

#### Question
Which sentence contains a mistake?

#### Answer
The wrong sentence is "It was Kim's suggestion. She thought about it first." When *think* means "produce an idea / come up with", we use *think OF*: "She **thought of** it first." The others are correct: *think of* for an opinion ("What do you think of …?"), *dream of* for "would never do", and *hear about* for being told news.

#### Correct variant
¯\_(ツ)_/¯

#### Variants
1. What do you think of the new logo?
2. I wouldn't dream of borrowing your car without asking.
3. It was Kim's suggestion. She thought about it first. (Correct)
4. Did you hear about the strike at the airport?

### Question 38

#### Question
Compare: (a) "Can you ______ me ______ the meeting tomorrow? I might forget." (b) "This village ______ me ______ the place where I grew up." Choose the correct prepositions for (a) and (b).

#### Answer
The correct pair is (a) **remind … about**, (b) **remind … of**. *Remind somebody about something* = tell them not to forget it. *Remind somebody of something* = cause them to remember it because of a resemblance or association. Swapping the two prepositions changes or breaks the meaning.

#### Correct variant
¯\_(ツ)_/¯

#### Variants
1. (a) about, (b) about
2. (a) of, (b) about
3. (a) about, (b) of (Correct)
4. (a) of, (b) of

### Question 39

#### Question
A doctor's note reads: "The patient came in ______ severe chest pains. She also ______ the long waiting time at reception." Which verbs + prepositions are right?

#### Answer
The correct completion is "came in **complaining of** severe chest pains. She also **complained about** the long waiting time." *Complain OF* a pain or illness = say that you have it. *Complain (to somebody) ABOUT* something = express dissatisfaction. Using *about* for the pain or *of* for the waiting time would be unidiomatic.

#### Correct variant
¯\_(ツ)_/¯

#### Variants
1. complaining about … complained of
2. complaining of … complained about (Correct)
3. complaining for … complained on
4. complaining of … complained of
