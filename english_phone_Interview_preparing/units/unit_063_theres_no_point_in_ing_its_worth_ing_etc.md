# Unit 63 — there’s no point in -ing, it’s worth -ing etc.

# Theory

## A there’s no point in / it’s no use / it’s no good

These three expressions say that an action is pointless or will not help. All are followed by **-ing**:

| expression | + -ing |
|---|---|
| there’s no point **in** | doing something |
| it’s no use | doing something |
| it’s no good | doing something |

- **There’s no point in buying** a printer if you never print anything.
- **There was no point in arguing**, so I let the matter drop.
- **It’s no use shouting** – she can’t hear you from here.
- **It’s no good pretending** you didn’t know; everyone saw the email.

Note the change of preposition with the noun *point*: **no point in** but **the point of**:
- There’s no point **in** learning Latin. / What’s the point **of** learning Latin?

## B it’s (not) worth doing

Use **worth** + **-ing** to say whether something deserves the effort:
- The museum is small but **it’s worth visiting**.
- The queue was two hours long, so **it wasn’t worth waiting**.

You can also say **worth it** / **not worth it**:
- Book the guided tour. **It’s worth it.**
- We didn’t bother with the upgrade. **It wasn’t worth it.**

And a thing can be *worth doing*, a place *worth seeing*, a question *worth asking*, etc.:
- That podcast is **worth listening to**.
- They opened the safe, but there was **nothing worth taking**.

## C have trouble / difficulty / a problem doing

After **have trouble**, **have difficulty** and **have a problem**, use **-ing**, not an infinitive:
- I **had trouble finding** your street. (❌ *trouble to find*)
- Did you **have difficulty understanding** the accent?
- We **had no problem parking** near the stadium.

## D spend / waste time doing; be busy doing

- She **spent the whole weekend marking** essays.
- I **waste far too much time scrolling** through my phone.
- Don’t call before six – I’ll **be busy cooking**.

## E go -ing for activities

Sports and leisure activities are often expressed as **go + -ing**: *go running, go climbing, go snowboarding, go kayaking, go cycling, go dancing, go sightseeing.*
- How often do you **go climbing**?
- We **went kayaking** on the lake last summer.
- Dad isn’t in – he**’s gone fishing**.
- I’ve **never been snowboarding**.

### Question 33

#### Question
Your flatmate keeps refreshing the airline website to see if the ticket price will drop, but the flight is tomorrow. Which is the correct way to tell them it’s pointless?

#### Answer
"There’s no point **in checking** the price again." *There’s no point* is followed by *in* + -ing. *To check* is an infinitive and cannot follow *point in*; *of checking* wrongly borrows the preposition from *the point of*; and *for checking* is not the pattern used with this expression at all. Only *no point in -ing* is correct.

#### Correct variant
¯\_(ツ)_/¯

#### Variants
1. There’s no point to check the price again.
2. There’s no point of checking the price again.
3. There’s no point in checking the price again. (Correct)
4. There’s no point for checking the price again.

#### Question
Complete the sentence: "I got the visa in the end, but I had a lot of difficulty ______ all the documents together."

#### Answer

The infinitive *to get* is the most frequent mistake because English learners expect *difficulty* to behave like the adjective *difficult* (*it was difficult to get*). *For getting* and *in get* are not possible structures here.

### Question 35

#### Question
Which of the following sentences is INCORRECT?

A. The old town is definitely worth a visit.
B. It isn’t worth to repair a phone that old.
C. The film is three hours long, but it’s worth seeing.
D. We didn’t stay for the fireworks – it wasn’t worth it.

#### Answer
Sentence **B** is wrong. After *worth* we use -ing, not an infinitive: "It isn’t worth **repairing** a phone that old." Sentence A is correct because *worth* can also be followed by a noun (*a visit*). Sentence C uses *worth seeing* correctly, and sentence D shows the fixed phrase *not worth it*. So the only error is the infinitive *to repair*.

#### Correct variant
¯\_(ツ)_/¯

#### Variants
1. Sentence A – *worth* must always be followed by -ing, never a noun.
2. Sentence B – *worth to repair* should be *worth repairing*. (Correct)
3. Sentence C – *worth seeing* should be *worth to see*.
4. Sentence D – *worth it* is not possible; it should be *worth doing it*.

#### Answer
