# Unit 4 — Present continuous and present simple 2 (I am doing and I do)

# Theory

## A Verbs not normally used in the continuous

Continuous forms (*I'm waiting, it's snowing*) describe actions that have started but are not finished. Some verbs describe **states** rather than actions, and these are not normally used with -ing, even when the state is true right now. We don't say ❌ *I am knowing* or ❌ *they are liking*; we say ✅ *I know*, ✅ *they like*.

| Group | Verbs |
|---|---|
| Wanting / liking | like, want, need, prefer |
| Thinking / knowing | know, realise, understand, recognise, believe, suppose, remember, mean |
| Being / having qualities | belong, fit, contain, consist, seem |

- I'm exhausted. I *need* a break. (not I'm needing)
- *Do you understand* the instructions?
- This jacket *doesn't fit* me any more.
- Omar *doesn't seem* very keen on the plan.

## B think

When **think** = *believe / have an opinion*, use the simple:

- I *think* the shop closes at six, but I'm not certain. (not I'm thinking)
- What *do you think* of the new design? (= what is your opinion?)

When **think** = *consider / use your mind*, the continuous is possible:

- Be quiet a moment — I*'m thinking*.
- Laila *is thinking of* moving to Canada. (= she is considering it)

## C see, hear, smell, taste, look, feel

With **see / hear / smell / taste** (perception), use the present simple:

- *Do you see* that bird on the roof? (not are you seeing)
- Something *smells* burnt in the kitchen.
- This coffee *tastes* bitter.

With **look** and **feel** (how somebody looks or feels *now*), both forms are possible:

- You *look* exhausted today. / You*'re looking* exhausted today.
- How *do you feel*? / How *are you feeling*?

But for a general habit only the simple works:

- I usually *feel* sleepy after lunch. (not I'm usually feeling)

## D am/is/are being

**He's being …** describes how somebody is **behaving** at the moment — behaviour they can control:

- Why *is* Tom *being* so rude tonight? He's normally very polite. (= behaving rudely now)
- 'Mind the wet floor.' 'Don't worry, I*'m being* careful.'

Compare with a permanent quality:

- Tom *is* rude. He never says thank you. (= his character in general)

*Being* is **not** used for states that are not behaviour:

- Anna *is* ill. (not is being ill)
- *Are* you cold? (not are you being cold)
- They *are* very happy together. (not are being happy)

### Question 32

#### Question
A customer in a bookshop says to the assistant: "____ this book? It's my nephew's birthday tomorrow." Which is the correct way to complete the sentence?

#### Answer
"Do you recommend" is correct. *Recommend* here expresses an opinion, and in any case the question asks for a general judgement, not an action in progress. More importantly, this question type tests state verbs: the tempting distractor "Are you liking" uses *like* in the continuous, which is not normally possible — *like* is a state verb. "Are you recommending" is unnatural for asking an opinion, and "Do you liking" is not a valid form.

#### Correct variant
¯\_(ツ)_/¯

#### Variants
1. Are you liking
2. Do you recommend (Correct)
3. Are you recommending

### Question 33

#### Question
Which sentence is wrong, and why?

#### Answer
"This box is containing old photographs" is wrong; it should be "This box contains old photographs." *Contain*, like *belong*, *consist* and *fit*, describes a state, not an action, so it is not used in continuous forms even when the state is true now. The other sentences are correct: "I'm using the printer" is an action in progress; "Do you remember her name?" correctly uses a state verb in the simple; "These shoes don't fit me" correctly uses *fit* in the simple.

#### Correct variant
¯\_(ツ)_/¯

#### Variants
1. Don't switch that off — I'm using the printer.
2. This box is containing old photographs. (Correct)

### Question 34

#### Question
Complete both sentences with the right form of *think*: (a) "I ____ (think) about quitting my job." (b) "What ____ (you / think) of the new manager?"

#### Answer
(a) "I'm thinking" — here *think* means *consider*, an activity going on in the speaker's mind, so the continuous is possible and natural. (b) "do you think" — here *think* means *have an opinion*, a state, so the present simple is required; "What are you thinking of the new manager?" is wrong. Using the simple in (a) ("I think about quitting") would describe a habit rather than a current process, and the continuous in (b) is not used for opinions.

#### Answer

"This is tasting wonderful" wrongly uses the continuous; "This is taste wonderful" lacks the -s; "This taste wonderful" lacks the third-person -s needed for the subject *this*.

### Question 36

#### Question
Choose the sentence in which *is being* is used correctly.

#### Answer
"Why is Grandpa being so stubborn about the doctor's appointment today?" is correct. *Am/is/are being* describes how somebody is **behaving** at the moment — something the person controls — and contrasts with their usual behaviour. The other options misuse it: "She is being ill" and "He is being tired" describe states, not behaviour, and must be "She is ill" / "He is tired"; "They are being married" wrongly uses *being* for a status — the correct form is "They are married."

#### Correct variant
¯\_(ツ)_/¯

#### Variants
1. She is being ill, so she stayed at home.
2. Why is Grandpa being so stubborn about the doctor's appointment today? (Correct)
3. He is being tired after the long flight.
4. They are being married with two children.
