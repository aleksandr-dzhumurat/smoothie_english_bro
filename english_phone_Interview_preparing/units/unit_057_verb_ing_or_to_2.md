# Unit 57 — Verb + -ing or to … 2 (try, need, help)

# Theory

## A try to … and try -ing

| try **to do** | try **doing** (or try **something**) |
|---|---|
| = attempt, make an effort — often without success. | = do it as an experiment, to see what happens. |
| I tried **to open** the jar, but the lid was stuck. | The jar wouldn't open, so I tried **running** it under hot water. |
| Please try **to arrive** before nine. | If the app freezes, try **closing** it and opening it again. |
| We tried **to book** a table, but the restaurant was full. | We tried **three restaurants**, but they were all full. |

Compare:

- I tried **to lift** the box, but it was far too heavy. (I made an effort; I couldn't.)
- The room felt cramped, so I tried **putting** the bed under the window. It didn't help, so I moved it back. (I did it as an experiment.)

## B need to … and need -ing

| need **to do** | something needs **doing** |
|---|---|
| = it is necessary for the subject to do it. | = it needs to be done (passive meaning). |
| I need **to renew** my passport. | My passport needs **renewing**. (= needs to be renewed) |
| You need **to water** the plants. | The plants need **watering**. |
| We need **to discuss** this carefully. | This needs **discussing** carefully. |

The subject of *need -ing* is the **thing that receives the action**, not the person doing it: ❌ ~~I need charging my phone~~ ✅ I need **to charge** my phone / My phone needs **charging**. With a person as subject doing the action, always use *need to do*.

## C help and can't help

*help* takes the infinitive **with or without to**:

- Everyone helped **(to) tidy** up afterwards.
- Could you help me **(to) find** my glasses?

**can't help doing** = can't stop yourself doing it:

- The joke was so bad I couldn't help **laughing**.
- I know it's none of my business, but I can't help **wondering** what happened.
- Sorry for fidgeting — I can't help **it**. (= I can't help fidgeting)

❌ ~~I couldn't help to laugh~~ ✅ I couldn't help **laughing**.

**See also:** Verb + -ing → Unit 53; Verb + to … → Units 54–55; Other verbs + -ing or to … → Units 56, 58.

## Extra practice

### Question 31

#### Question
Your laptop is running very slowly. A colleague suggests an experiment that might fix it. Which sentence is correct?

#### Answer
**"Try restarting it — that often helps."** *Try + -ing* means "do this as an experiment to see if it solves the problem". "Try to restart it" would mean "make an effort to restart it", implying restarting is difficult in itself, which is not the point. "Try restart it" is missing a verb form. "Try that you restart it" is not a possible structure after *try*.

#### Correct variant
¯\_(ツ)_/¯

#### Variants
1. Try to restart it — that often helps.
2. Try restarting it — that often helps. (Correct)
3. Try restart it — that often helps.
4. Try that you restart it — that often helps.

### Question 32

#### Question
Compare: (a) "I tried to reach the top shelf, but I'm too short." (b) "I tried standing on a chair, but I still couldn't reach it." Which statement about these sentences is correct?

#### Answer
**In (a) reaching the shelf was the goal I attempted and failed; in (b) standing on a chair was an experiment I actually carried out to achieve the goal.** *Try to do* = attempt (and here fail); *try doing* = actually do something as a test. Option 1 is wrong because the forms carry different meanings. Option 3 has the meanings reversed. Option 4 is wrong because both are grammatical.

#### Correct variant
¯\_(ツ)_/¯

#### Variants
1. Both sentences use *try* in the same way; -ing and *to …* are interchangeable here.
2. In (a) reaching the shelf was an attempt that failed; in (b) standing on a chair was something I actually did as an experiment. (Correct)
3. In (a) I actually reached the shelf; in (b) I only considered standing on a chair.
4. Only (a) is grammatical; *try* cannot be followed by -ing.

#### Question
Complete the sentence: "The grass is very long. It ______."

#### Answer
**"It needs cutting."** When the subject is the thing that receives the action, *need + -ing* has a passive meaning: *the grass needs cutting* = the grass needs to be cut. #### Question
Complete the sentence: "The comedian was so good that even the grumpiest people in the audience couldn't help ______."

#### Answer
**"couldn't help laughing."** *Can't help doing* means "can't stop yourself doing". It is always followed by -ing. ### Question 35

#### Question
Which of these sentences contains a mistake?

#### Answer
Sentence 2 is wrong: **"I need charging my phone before we leave"** should be **"I need to charge my phone"** (or **"My phone needs charging"**). *Need + -ing* is only used when the subject is the thing that receives the action; when a person is going to do something, *need to do* is required. The others are correct: "helped me carry" is fine because *help* can take the bare infinitive; "The windows need cleaning" is the correct passive-meaning form; "tried to call you" describes an unsuccessful attempt.

#### Correct variant
¯\_(ツ)_/¯

#### Variants
1. A stranger helped me carry the pram up the steps.
2. I need charging my phone before we leave. (Correct)
3. The windows need cleaning — you can hardly see through them.
4. I tried to call you several times, but there was no signal.
