# Unit 144 — Phrasal verbs 8 up (2)

# Theory

## A talking and ideas: bring up, come up, come up with, make up

- **bring up** a subject, a topic = introduce it in a conversation: *Whatever you do, don't bring up politics at dinner.* / *She brought the question of pay up at the meeting.*
- **come up** = be mentioned, arise in a conversation: *Your name came up when I was talking to the manager.*
- **come up with** an idea, a plan, a suggestion, a solution = think of it and produce it: *The team came up with a clever way to cut costs.*
- **make** something **up** = invent something untrue: *He said he'd been ill, but he made the whole story up.*

Compare **come up** (a topic appears) and **come up with** (a person produces an idea) — the *with* changes the meaning completely.

## B feelings, money, weather: cheer up, save up, clear up

- **cheer up** = become happier; **cheer** somebody **up** = make them happier: *Cheer up — it's only a game!* / *A walk by the sea always cheers me up.*
- **save up (for** something / **to do** something**)** = put money aside to buy or do something: *They're saving up for a house.* / *I'm saving up to go to Japan.*
- **clear up** (weather) = become bright after rain or cloud: *It poured all morning, but it cleared up by lunchtime.* (Don't confuse with *clear up* = tidy, Unit 143.)

## C destroying: blow up, tear up, beat up

| Phrasal verb | Meaning | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **blow up** | explode | *A gas cylinder blew up in the garage.* |
| **blow** something **up** | destroy it with explosives | *The old chimney was blown up in a controlled demolition.* |
| **tear** something **up** | tear it into pieces | *She tore up the contract and walked out.* |
| **beat** somebody **up** | hit somebody repeatedly and hurt them badly | *Two men were beaten up outside the club.* |

## D other verbs + up

| Phrasal verb | Meaning | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **break up / split up (with** somebody**)** | end a relationship, separate | *Nadia has split up with her boyfriend.* / *The band broke up in 2019.* |
| **do up** a coat, a zip, buttons, laces | fasten, tie | *Do your jacket up — it's windy.* |
| **do up** a house, a room | repair and improve, renovate | *They bought a run-down cottage and did it up.* |
| **look** something **up** (in a dictionary etc.) | find information in a reference book or online | *I didn't know the word, so I looked it up.* |
| **put up with** something/somebody | tolerate | *I can't put up with his lateness any longer.* |
| **hold** somebody/something **up** | delay | *Sorry I'm late — I was held up at the border.* / *The roadworks are holding up the whole project.* |
| **mix up** people/things, **get** them **mixed up** | confuse one with the other | *I always mix up the twins.* / *I got the two dates mixed up.* |

**Common mistakes:** ❌ *put up his behaviour* ✅ *put up **with** his behaviour*; ❌ *came up a great idea* ✅ *came up **with** a great idea*; ❌ *look up it* ✅ *look **it** up*.

**See also:** Phrasal verbs 1 (Introduction) → Unit 137; more verbs + up → Units 142–143; American English → Appendix 7.

### Question 28

#### Question
The marketing team had a problem and Priya suggested an unusual solution that nobody had thought of. Which sentence describes what Priya did?

#### Answer
The correct sentence is *Priya came up with a solution.* **Come up with** an idea, plan or solution means to think of it and produce it. *Came up a solution* is wrong because without **with** the verb **come up** means that a topic is mentioned or arises, and it takes no object. *Brought up a solution* would mean she raised the topic of a solution in conversation, not that she invented one. *Made up a solution* would suggest she invented something false.

#### Correct variant
¯\_(ツ)_/¯

#### Variants
1. Priya came up a solution.
2. Priya brought up a solution.
3. Priya came up with a solution. (Correct)
4. Priya made up a solution.

### Question 29

#### Question
Which sentence uses **do up** in the sense of "repair and improve"?

#### Answer
Sentence 4 is the one: *They spent a year doing up the farmhouse before they moved in* — they renovated it. In sentences 1 and 2, *do up* means to fasten (a coat, shoelaces). Sentence 3 does not contain **do up** at all; it uses **hold up** (= delay). The verb **do up** has both meanings, and the object tells you which: clothing and fastenings on the one hand, buildings and rooms on the other.

### Question 30

#### Question
Choose the pair that completes both sentences correctly:
(a) It was cloudy at breakfast, but the sky _____ by ten o'clock.

#### Answer
The answer is **cleared up / cheer him up**. **Clear up**, used about the weather, means to become bright after rain or cloud. **Cheer** somebody **up** means to make them happier, and with a pronoun object the pronoun goes between the verb and *up* (*cheer him up*, ❌ *cheer up him*). *Cheered up* does not describe the sky, *blew up* means exploded, and *save him up* is not possible — **save up** is about money.

#### Correct variant

2. cleared up / cheer him up (Correct)

### Question 31

#### Question
One of these sentences contains a mistake. Which one, and why?

#### Answer
Sentence 2 is wrong: *I don't know how she puts up her noisy neighbours* must be *I don't know how she **puts up with** her noisy neighbours.* **Put up with** (= tolerate) is a three-word phrasal verb, and **with** cannot be dropped; *put up* on its own means something quite different (e.g. put a picture up). Sentence 1 correctly uses *look it up*; sentence 3 correctly uses *split up*; sentence 4 correctly uses *hold you up*.

#### Correct variant
¯\_(ツ)_/¯

#### Variants
1. If you're not sure how to spell it, look it up.
2. I don't know how she puts up her noisy neighbours. (Correct)
3. Ana and Luis have split up after ten years together.
4. Go ahead without me — I don't want to hold you up.

### Question 32

#### Question
"Please don't _____ the accident when Tina is here — she still finds it very upsetting." Which option is correct?

#### Answer
The correct option is **bring up**: *don't bring up the accident*. **Bring up** a subject means to introduce it into a conversation. *Come up* cannot take an object — a subject *comes up* by itself (*The accident came up in conversation*). *Make up* means to invent something false, which does not fit; *mix up* means to confuse two people or things with each other.

#### Correct variant
¯\_(ツ)_/¯

#### Variants
1. come up
2. make up
3. mix up
4. bring up (Correct)
