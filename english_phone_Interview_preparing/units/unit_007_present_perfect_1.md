# Unit 7 — Present perfect 1 (I have done)

# Theory

## A Form

Picture Hannah standing at her front door, searching her bag. *She's lost her keys.* (= She has lost …) She lost them at some point, and she doesn't have them **now**.

The present perfect simple is **have/has + past participle**:

| Subject | have/has | past participle |
|---|---|---|
| I / we / you / they | have (I've, we've …) | finished, lost, done, been |
| he / she / it | has (he's, she's, it's) | finished, lost, done, been |

The past participle often ends in **-ed** (*decided, finished*), but many are irregular (*lost, done, written, gone, been*).

Negative: *haven't / hasn't + participle*. Question: *Have you seen …? Has it stopped …?*

## B New information with a connection to now

Saying "something **has happened**" usually presents **new information**:

- Oh no! *I've spilt* coffee on the keyboard.
- The motorway is closed. *There's been* a serious accident.
- Scientists *have discovered* a new species of frog.

The present perfect always links the past action to the **present result**:

- Hannah *has lost* her keys. (= she doesn't have them now)
- He gave me directions, but *I've forgotten* them. (= I can't remember them now)
- Raj is still at his desk. *He hasn't left* yet. (= he is here now)
- I can't find my glasses. *Have you seen* them? (= do you know where they are now?)

**gone (to)** vs. **been (to)**:

- Elif is away. *She has gone to* Turkey. (= she is there now, or on her way)
- Elif is back at work. *She has been to* Turkey. (= she went and has now returned)

## C just, already, yet

| Word | Meaning | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **just** | a short time ago | 'Coffee?' 'No thanks, *I've just had* one.' / *Have you just woken up?* |
| **already** | sooner than expected | 'Remember to book the tickets.' '*I've already booked* them.' / 'Is Ali still here?' 'No, *he's already gone*.' |
| **yet** | until now; we expect it to happen | *Has the parcel arrived yet?* / *I've packed, but I haven't printed the boarding pass yet.* |

*Yet* is used only in **questions and negatives**, normally at the end of the sentence.

## D Past simple is also possible

In these situations the past simple can be used instead, especially in informal or American English:

- Tara isn't here. *She's gone out.* **or** *She went out.*
- 'Hungry?' 'No, *I've just eaten*.' **or** 'No, *I just ate*.'
