# Unit 54 — Verb + to … (decide to … / forget to … etc.)

# Theory

## A Verbs followed by to … (infinitive)

These verbs are followed by **to + infinitive**:

| | | | |
|---|---|---|---|
| offer | plan | manage | deserve |
| agree | arrange | fail | afford |
| refuse | hope | promise | learn |
| decide | forget | threaten | tend |

- The last bus had gone, so we decided **to walk** home.
- Farah was struggling with the boxes, so I offered **to carry** one.
- I shouted, but failed **to get** anyone's attention.
- Marco tends **to arrive** a few minutes late.
- When did you learn **to swim**? (or learn **how to swim**)

The negative is **not to …**:

- We decided **not to buy** the flat after all.
- She promised **not to tell** anyone.

Remember that some verbs take -ing instead (see Unit 53): ❌ ~~enjoy to read~~ ✅ enjoy **reading**; ❌ ~~suggested to meet~~ ✅ suggested **meeting**; ❌ ~~thinking to buy~~ ✅ thinking **of buying**.

## B dare

After *dare* the *to* is optional: *I didn't dare **to ask** / didn't dare **ask**.* But after **dare not / daren't** there is no *to*: *I daren't **tell** her.* (not ~~daren't to tell~~)

## C seem, appear, pretend, claim

These verbs also take *to …*, and often a **continuous** or **perfect** infinitive:

| form | example | meaning |
|---|---|---|
| to do | They seem **to know** each other. | it seems that they know |
| to be doing | He pretended **to be sleeping**. | he pretended that he was sleeping |
| to have done | I seem **to have left** my wallet at home. | it seems that I have left it |
| not to have done | She claimed **not to have received** the letter. | she claimed she hadn't received it |

## D Question word + to …

After **ask, know, decide, remember, forget, learn, explain, understand, wonder** you can use a question word + *to …*:

- We asked **how to get** to the harbour.
- Have you decided **where to stay**?
- I don't know **whether to accept** the offer or not.
- Do you understand **what to do** next?

Also **show / tell / ask / advise / teach somebody what / how / where to do something**:

- Could you show me **how to reset** the router?
- Ask Dana — she'll tell you **what to bring**.

**See also:** Verb + -ing → Unit 53; Verb + object + to … (want etc.) → Unit 55; Verb + to … and -ing → Units 55C, 56–58.

### Question 37

#### Question
You want to say that Leo made a promise not to be late again. Which sentence is correct?

#### Answer
**"Leo promised not to be late again."** The negative infinitive is *not to + verb*; *not* goes before *to*. "Promised to not be" is sometimes heard but is not the standard form the book teaches. "Promised don't be" wrongly uses an imperative form. "Promised not being" applies the -ing pattern, but *promise* is a *to …* verb.

#### Correct variant
¯\_(ツ)_/¯

#### Variants
1. Leo promised to not be late again.
2. Leo promised don't be late again.
3. Leo promised not to be late again. (Correct)
4. Leo promised not being late again.

### Question 38

#### Question
You realise your keys are not in your pocket and you think you probably left them in the café. Which sentence with *seem* expresses this?

#### Answer
**"I seem to have left my keys in the café."** *Seem* + perfect infinitive (*to have done*) refers to an action that is already complete: it seems that I have left them. "I seem to leave" (present infinitive) would describe a habit — as if you always leave your keys there. "It seems that I leave" has the same problem. "I seem that I have left" is ungrammatical: *seem* takes *to …*, or you say *It seems that I have left …*.

#### Correct variant
¯\_(ツ)_/¯

#### Variants
1. I seem to leave my keys in the café.
2. I seem to have left my keys in the café. (Correct)
3. It seems that I leave my keys in the café.
4. I seem that I have left my keys in the café.

### Question 39

#### Question
You bought a new coffee machine and you can't work out how to use it. Which request is grammatically correct?

#### Answer
**"Can you show me how to use this machine?"** After *show/tell/teach + somebody*, we use *question word + to + infinitive*. "How using" wrongly uses -ing after a question word. "How I use" is a plain clause, which is not wrong in every context but is not the *question word + to …* structure being tested, and after *show me* the infinitive is the natural choice; the book's target form is *how to use*. "How to using" mixes *to* with an -ing form, which is impossible.

#### Correct variant
¯\_(ツ)_/¯

#### Variants
1. Can you show me how using this machine?
2. Can you show me how to using this machine?
3. Can you show me how to use this machine? (Correct)
4. Can you show me to use how this machine?

### Question 40

#### Question
Which of these sentences contains a mistake?

#### Answer

The others are correct: "afford to go" follows the *to …* pattern; "pretended not to notice" is a correct negative infinitive; "wondered whether to apply" uses *question word + to …* correctly.

#### Correct variant
