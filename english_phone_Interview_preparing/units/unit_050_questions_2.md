# Unit 50 — Questions 2 (do you know where … ? / he asked me where …)

# Theory

## A — Embedded questions: Do you know where … ? / I don't know why … / Could you tell me what … ?

A direct question has question word order (verb before subject). But when the question becomes **part of a longer sentence** — after *Do you know …?*, *Can you tell me …?*, *I wonder …*, *I have no idea …*, *Please explain …* — it goes back to **statement order** (subject before verb):

| Direct question | Embedded question |
|---|---|
| Where **is the** nearest bank? | Do you know where **the nearest bank is**? |
| How old **are they**? | I don't know how old **they are**. |
| When **will the** results come out? | Have you any idea when **the results will come out**? |
| Who **was that** man? | I wonder who **that man was**. |

❌ *Do you know where is the nearest bank?* ✅ *Do you know where the nearest bank is?*

### do / does / did disappear

In embedded questions there is no *do/does/did*; the main verb takes the tense itself:

- What time **does** the shop close? → Do you know what time the shop **closes**?
- Why **did** he resign? → Nobody knows why he **resigned**.
- What **do** you need? → Tell me what you **need**.

### if / whether for yes/no questions

When there is no question word, introduce the embedded question with **if** or **whether**:

- Is the museum open on Mondays? → Could you tell me **if/whether** the museum is open on Mondays?
- Did anyone phone? → I don't know **if/whether** anyone phoned.

Note: the whole sentence is a question only if the introducing part is (*Do you know …?* → question mark; *I don't know …* → full stop).

## B — Reported questions: he asked me where …

The same statement order is used when questions are **reported**, and — as in reported speech generally — the verb usually moves back into the past (see Unit 47):

- 'Where do you work?' → She asked me where I **worked**.
- 'How long have you lived here?' → He wanted to know how long I **had lived** there.
- 'Can you start on Monday?' → They asked **if/whether** I **could** start on Monday.
- 'Did you enjoy the course?' → She asked me **whether** I **had enjoyed** the course. (*or* … whether I enjoyed …)
- 'Why are you late?' → The teacher asked us why we **were** late.

Common reporting verbs: *ask, want to know, wonder*. ❌ *He asked me where did I work.* ✅ *He asked me where I worked.*

### Question 25

#### Question
You are lost in a new city and stop a passer-by. Which is the correct polite question?

#### Answer
"Excuse me, could you tell me where **the station is**?" When a question is embedded inside *Could you tell me …?*, the word order becomes statement order: subject (*the station*) before verb (*is*). "Where is the station" keeps direct-question inversion, which is wrong inside a longer sentence. "Where the station" has no verb. "Where does the station be" wrongly adds *does* and uses the base form *be*.

#### Correct variant
¯\_(ツ)_/¯

#### Variants
1. Could you tell me where is the station?
2. Could you tell me where the station is? (Correct)
3. Could you tell me where does the station be?
4. Could you tell me where the station?

#### Question
Direct question: "What time does the pharmacy close?" Complete the embedded version: "Do you know what time ___?"

#### Answer
"Do you know what time **the pharmacy closes**?" In an embedded question *does* disappears and the main verb carries the third-person *-s*: *the pharmacy closes*. "Does the pharmacy close" keeps the direct-question auxiliary and inversion. "The pharmacy does close" keeps *does* in an unnatural emphatic position. "The pharmacy close" drops the *-s* needed for a singular subject.

#### Correct variant

### Question 27

#### Question
You want to know whether your parcel has arrived, but you're not sure who to ask. Which sentence is correct?

#### Answer
"I don't know **if** my parcel **has arrived**." A yes/no question embedded in a sentence needs *if* or *whether* because there is no question word, and the order is statement order (*my parcel has arrived*). "I don't know has my parcel arrived" keeps direct inversion. "I don't know that my parcel has arrived" uses *that*, which turns it into a statement of fact rather than an open question. "I don't know if has my parcel arrived" adds *if* but keeps the wrong order.

#### Correct variant
¯\_(ツ)_/¯

#### Variants
1. I don't know has my parcel arrived.
2. I don't know if has my parcel arrived.
3. I don't know if my parcel has arrived. (Correct)
4. I don't know that my parcel has arrived.

### Question 28

#### Question
At a job interview, the interviewer asked: "Why did you leave your last job?" Later you tell a friend. Which report is correct?

#### Answer
"She asked me why I **had left** my last job" (or "… why I **left** my last job" — both are acceptable). Reported questions use statement order (*I had left*, not *had I left*), and the past simple can either stay or move back to the past perfect. "Why did I leave" keeps the direct-question inversion and *did*. "Why had I left" has the right tense but the wrong order. "Why I have left" uses a present perfect that doesn't match the original past simple.

#### Correct variant
¯\_(ツ)_/¯

#### Variants
1. She asked me why did I leave my last job.
2. She asked me why had I left my last job.
3. She asked me why I have left my last job.
4. She asked me why I had left my last job. (Correct)

### Question 29

#### Question
Spot the mistake. A learner wrote: "The customs officer asked me did I have anything to declare and where was I going."

#### Answer
Both reported questions are wrong. A reported yes/no question needs *if/whether* and statement order: "asked me **if/whether I had** anything to declare". A reported *wh*-question also needs statement order: "and **where I was going**". Corrected: *The customs officer asked me whether I had anything to declare and where I was going.* Reported questions never keep the inversion or the *did* of direct questions.

#### Correct variant
¯\_(ツ)_/¯

#### Variants
1. Only the first part is wrong; *where was I going* is correct reported speech.
2. Only the second part is wrong; *asked me did I have* is a standard reported question.
3. Both parts are wrong: *asked me if/whether I had anything to declare and where I was going*. (Correct)
4. Nothing is wrong: reported questions keep the original question order.
