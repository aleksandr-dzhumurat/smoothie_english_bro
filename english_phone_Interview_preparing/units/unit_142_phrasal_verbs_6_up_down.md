# Unit 142 — Phrasal verbs 6 up/down

# Theory

## A up and down as opposites

With many verbs, **up** and **down** keep their literal directions and form pairs of opposites:

| up | down |
|---|---|
| **put** something **up** (on a wall, a shelf etc.) — *We put the new mirror up in the hall.* | **take** something **down** — *After Christmas we took all the decorations down.* |
| **pick** something **up** — *A coin rolled under the table and I picked it up.* | **put** something **down** — *Put that knife down before you hurt yourself.* |
| **stand up** — *Everyone stood up when the judge came in.* | **sit down / bend down / lie down** — *She bent down to stroke the cat.* |
| **turn** something **up** (volume, heat) — *Turn the radio up, I can't hear the news.* | **turn** something **down** — *The heating is on full; turn it down a bit.* |

Word order reminder: with a noun object both *put up the picture* and *put the picture up* are possible, but with a pronoun the object goes in the middle: *put **it** up* (❌ put up it).

## B knock down, cut down, burn down

**Down** often means that something is brought to the ground or destroyed:

- **knock down** a building, **blow** something **down**, **cut** something **down**: *The old cinema was knocked down last year.* / *The gale blew several fences down.* / *They cut down the dead oak.*
- **be knocked down** (by a vehicle) = be hit and fall: *A cyclist was knocked down at the crossroads.*
- **burn down** = be destroyed by fire: *The barn burnt down in a few hours.*

## C down = becoming less

- **slow down** = go more slowly: *Slow down, the road is icy.*
- **calm down** = become calmer; **calm** somebody **down** = make them calmer: *Take a deep breath and calm down.* / *It took ages to calm the children down.*
- **cut down (on** something**)** = do, eat or drink less of it: *The doctor told him to cut down on salt.*

## D other verbs + down

| Phrasal verb | Meaning | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **break down** | stop working (machines, cars); fail (relationships, talks) | *The lift has broken down again.* / *The negotiations broke down after a week.* |
| **close down / shut down** | stop doing business | *The factory shut down and 200 people lost their jobs.* |
| **let** somebody **down** | disappoint them by not doing what they expected | *I promised to be there — I won't let you down.* |
| **turn** somebody/something **down** | refuse an offer, application, request | *He was offered a promotion but turned it down.* / *Her visa application was turned down.* |
| **write** something **down** | note it on paper so you don't forget | *Write the address down before you forget it.* |

Notice that **turn down** has two meanings: reduce (volume, heat — section A) and refuse (section D). Context tells you which.

**Common mistakes:** ❌ *The car was broken down* ✅ *The car **broke down*** (no passive — the car did it); ❌ *Please write down it* ✅ *Please write **it** down*; ❌ *cut down coffee* ✅ *cut down **on** coffee*.

### Question 27

#### Question
You are moving out of a rented flat. Your landlord says: "Before you leave, please _____ all the posters you stuck on the bedroom wall." Which option fits?

#### Answer
The correct option is **take down**: *please take down all the posters*. **Take** something **down** is the opposite of **put** something **up** and means to remove something from a wall or from a high position. *Put down* means to stop holding something and place it on a surface; *pick up* means to lift something from the floor or a low place; *turn down* means to reduce the volume/heat or to refuse — none of these describe removing posters from a wall.

#### Correct variant
¯\_(ツ)_/¯

#### Variants
1. put down
2. take down (Correct)
3. pick up
4. turn down

### Question 28

#### Question
Which sentence uses **turn down** in the sense of "refuse"?

#### Answer
Sentence 4 is the one: *Amira turned down a place at a top university to travel for a year* — she **refused** the offer. In sentence 1, *turn it down* means reduce the volume of the music; in sentence 2, *turn the oven down* means lower the temperature. Sentence 3 uses *turn up*, not *turn down*, and refers to increasing the heating. The verb **turn down** has both meanings, so you have to judge from context which is intended.

#### Correct variant
¯\_(ツ)_/¯

#### Variants
1. The neighbours will complain — can you turn it down a bit?
2. Turn the oven down or the cake will burn.
3. It's freezing in here. Let's turn the heating up.
4. Amira turned down a place at a top university to travel for a year. (Correct)

### Question 29

#### Question
One of these sentences is wrong. Which one, and what is the mistake?

#### Answer
Sentence 2 is wrong: *My laptop was broken down, so I couldn't send the file* should be *My laptop **broke down**, so I couldn't send the file.* **Break down** (= stop working) is intransitive: the machine itself breaks down, so it is not used in the passive. Sentence 1 correctly uses *be knocked down* (a passive that is fine, because somebody or something knocks a person down); sentence 3 correctly uses *burnt down*; sentence 4 correctly uses *cut down on* with "on" before the thing you reduce.

#### Correct variant
¯\_(ツ)_/¯

#### Variants
1. An elderly man was knocked down by a van outside the school.
2. My laptop was broken down, so I couldn't send the file. (Correct)
3. The warehouse burnt down before the fire brigade arrived.
4. I'm trying to cut down on sugary drinks.

### Question 31

#### Question
"Everyone was counting on Tom to organise the trip, but he didn't do anything and it was cancelled." Which sentence best sums up what Tom did?

#### Answer
The correct sentence is *He let everyone down.* **Let** somebody **down** means to disappoint people by failing to do what they hoped or expected you to do. *He turned everyone down* would mean he refused everyone's requests or offers, which is not the situation; *He closed everyone down* is not a natural use — **close down** is for businesses stopping trading; *He cut everyone down* is not a possible meaning here — **cut down** applies to trees or to reducing consumption.

#### Correct variant
¯\_(ツ)_/¯

#### Variants
1. He turned everyone down.
2. He closed everyone down.
3. He let everyone down. (Correct)
4. He cut everyone down.
