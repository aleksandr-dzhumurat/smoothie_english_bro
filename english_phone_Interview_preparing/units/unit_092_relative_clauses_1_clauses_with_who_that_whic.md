# Unit 92 — Relative clauses 1: clauses with who/that/which

# Theory

## A What a relative clause does; who for people

A relative clause is a part of a sentence that tells us **which** person or thing (or **what kind** of person or thing) we mean:

- the girl **who sits behind me in class** (tells us which girl)
- drivers **who ignore speed limits** (tells us what kind of drivers)

For people we use **who**:

- The man who runs the corner shop is from Portugal.
- I admire people who keep their promises.
- A dentist is someone who looks after your teeth.
- What's the name of the woman who interviewed you?

**That** is also possible for people, but **which** is not:

- The man that runs the corner shop is from Portugal. (not *the man which runs*)

In some sentences you must use *who* rather than *that* — see Unit 95.

## B that / which for things

For things we use **that** or **which** (not *who*):

- I prefer films that have a happy ending. / … films which have a happy ending.
- Leo works for a firm that builds bridges. / … a firm which builds bridges.
- The printer that broke yesterday has been fixed.

*That* is more common than *which* in this type of clause, but sometimes *which* is required — see Unit 95.

| refers to | possible words | not possible |
|---|---|---|
| people | who / that | which |
| things | that / which | who |

## C Don't repeat the subject with he/she/it/they

Inside the relative clause, *who/that/which* **replaces** the pronoun. Do not add *he/she/it/they* as well:

- Two sentences: I spoke to a Brazilian student. He is studying chemistry.
- One sentence: I spoke to a Brazilian student who is studying chemistry. ❌ *a student who he is studying*
- Two sentences: I can't find my glasses. They were on the desk.
- One sentence: Where are the glasses that were on the desk? ❌ *the glasses they were on the desk*

## D what vs that

**What** = "the thing(s) that". It already contains the idea of a noun, so it is not used after a noun or after *everything/something/all*:

- What she told me was a surprise. (= The thing that she told me)
- but: Everything that she told me was a surprise. ❌ *Everything what she told me*
- The bike that was stolen belonged to my sister. ❌ *The bike what was stolen*

Rule of thumb: if there is already a noun (or *everything/anything/all*) before the gap, use *that/which/who*, not *what*.

### Question 35

#### Question
A learner writes: "Everything what she cooks tastes wonderful." A teacher marks it wrong. Which correction is right, and what is the rule?

#### Answer
The correct version is "Everything **that** she cooks tastes wonderful." After *everything* (and after nouns, *something*, *all*, etc.) we use *that*, not *what*. *What* already means "the thing(s) that", so "everything what" would be like saying "everything the thing that". Option 1 keeps the error. Option 3 uses *who*, which refers to people, not to the things she cooks. Option 4 changes the meaning — *What she cooks* is grammatical, but it is a different sentence and does not keep *everything*.

#### Correct variant
¯\_(ツ)_/¯

#### Variants
1. Everything what she cooks tastes wonderful. — it is already correct.
2. Everything that she cooks tastes wonderful. — after "everything" we use "that", not "what". (Correct)
3. Everything who she cooks tastes wonderful. — "who" is the general relative pronoun.
4. Everything, what she cooks tastes wonderful. — a comma is needed before "what".
