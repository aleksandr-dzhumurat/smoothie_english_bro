# Unit 129 — Noun + preposition (reason for, cause of etc.)

# Theory

Many nouns are followed by a particular preposition. The preposition is fixed by the noun, not by the meaning of the following word, so these combinations have to be learnt.

## A noun + FOR

| noun | example |
|---|---|
| a demand for | *Demand for electric bikes has doubled.* |
| a need for | *There's a real need for more nurses.* |
| an excuse for | *There's no excuse for rudeness.* |
| a reason for | *Nobody gave a reason for the cancellation.* (not ~~reason of~~) |

## B noun + OF

| noun | example |
|---|---|
| a cause of | *Speeding is a major cause of accidents.* |
| a picture / photo / map / plan / drawing of | *She sent me a photo of her new puppy.* / *I bought a map of the island.* |
| an advantage / a disadvantage of | *One disadvantage of the flat is the noise.* |

But: *there is an advantage **in** doing / **to** doing something*: *There are advantages in working from home.* or *… advantages to working from home.*

## C noun + IN

**an increase / a decrease / a rise / a fall / a drop in** (prices, numbers, quality etc.):
- *There's been a sharp rise in rents this year.*
- *The company reported a fall in profits.*

## D noun + TO

| noun | example |
|---|---|
| damage to | *The storm caused damage to hundreds of roofs.* |
| an invitation to (a party / wedding etc.) | *We've had an invitation to their housewarming.* |
| a solution to a problem | *Is there a simple solution to this?* (not ~~solution of~~) |
| a key to a door | *Where's the key to the garage?* |
| an answer to a question / a reply to a letter or email | *I'm still waiting for a reply to my email.* |
| a reaction to something | *What was his reaction to the news?* |
| an attitude to / towards | *Her attitude to money is very relaxed.* or *attitude towards money* |

## E noun + WITH / BETWEEN

- **a relationship / a connection / contact WITH** one person or thing: *I've lost contact with most of my school friends.* / *He has a difficult relationship with his father.*
- **a relationship / a connection / contact / a difference BETWEEN** two people or things: *Is there a connection between diet and mood?* / *What's the difference between a lake and a pond?*

Common errors: ❌ *reason of*, ❌ *solution of*, ❌ *increase of prices* (when you mean the thing that increased), ❌ *difference with*. ✅ *reason for, solution to, increase in prices, difference between*.

**See also:** Adjective + preposition → Units 130–131; Verb + preposition → Units 132–136.

## Extra practice

### Question 35

#### Question
Complete: "The engineers still don't know the cause ___ the fault, and the manager refused to give any reason ___ the delay."

#### Answer
**cause of … reason for.** *Cause* is followed by *of* (*the cause of the explosion*) while *reason* is followed by *for* (*the reason for the delay*). Learners often swap them (*reason of*, *cause for*) because both nouns express "why"; the preposition is fixed by the noun, not the meaning.

#### Correct variant
¯\_(ツ)_/¯

#### Variants
1. cause for … reason of
2. cause of … reason of
3. cause of … reason for (Correct)
4. cause for … reason for

### Question 36

#### Question
Which sentence is correct?

#### Answer
**"There has been a big increase in the number of cyclists, but no rise in accidents."** *Increase, decrease, rise, fall* + *in* + the thing that changes. *Increase of* is possible only before an amount (*an increase of 10%*), not before the thing that has grown. *Rise on* is not used.

#### Correct variant
¯\_(ツ)_/¯

#### Variants
1. There has been a big increase of the number of cyclists, but no rise of accidents.
2. There has been a big increase in the number of cyclists, but no rise in accidents. (Correct)
3. There has been a big increase on the number of cyclists, but no rise on accidents.
4. There has been a big increase in the number of cyclists, but no rise of accidents.

### Question 37

#### Question
One sentence contains a mistake. Which, and how should it be corrected?

#### Answer
**"We need to find a solution of the parking problem" is wrong; it should be "a solution to the parking problem".** *Solution*, like *answer, reply, key, reaction, invitation* and *damage*, takes *to*. The other sentences are correct: *damage to*, *photo of*, *invitation to*.

#### Correct variant
¯\_(ツ)_/¯

#### Variants
1. "The flood caused damage to the bridge" — should be *damage of*.
2. "We need to find a solution of the parking problem" — should be *a solution to*. (Correct)
3. "Here's a photo of my grandparents" — should be *photo from*.
4. "Did you get an invitation to the launch?" — should be *invitation for*.

### Question 39

#### Question
Complete: "The main advantage ___ living near the office is the short commute, but there are also advantages ___ living further out — it's quieter, for one thing."

#### Answer
**advantage of … advantages in (or to).** *Advantage / disadvantage **of*** something is the basic pattern (*the advantage of living near the office*). When we say that an activity has advantages, we can also say *there are advantages **in** doing / **to** doing something*. *Advantage for* and *advantage about* are not used in this sense.

#### Correct variant
¯\_(ツ)_/¯

#### Variants
1. advantage for … advantages about
2. advantage of … advantages in (Correct)
3. advantage in … advantages for
4. advantage about … advantages of
