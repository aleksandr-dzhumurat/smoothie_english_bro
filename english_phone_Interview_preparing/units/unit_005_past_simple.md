# Unit 5 — Past simple (I did)

# Theory

## A What the past simple does

The past simple describes actions and states that are **finished** and belong to the past. Think of a short biography: *Marie Curie was a Polish-born physicist. She moved to Paris in 1891, studied at the Sorbonne, discovered two elements and won two Nobel Prizes. She died in 1934.* Every verb here — *was, moved, studied, discovered, won, died* — is past simple.

## B Regular and irregular forms

Regular verbs add **-ed**:

- I *live* in Bristol now, but for ten years I *lived* in Cardiff.
- They *offered* me the job and I *accepted* it straight away.
- The bus *stopped* suddenly and everyone *shouted*.
- Nina *passed* the interview because she *prepared* carefully.

Spelling: *stop → stopped, study → studied, travel → travelled*.

Many common verbs are **irregular** — the past form does not end in -ed and has to be learnt:

| Base | Past simple | Example |
|---|---|---|
| write | wrote | She *wrote* three letters yesterday. |
| see | saw | We *saw* a fox in the garden last night. |
| go | went | I *went* to Prague twice last year. |
| buy | bought | He *bought* a second-hand bike. |
| shut | shut | It was windy, so I *shut* the gate. |

## C Questions and negatives: did / didn't + infinitive

| Statement | Question | Negative |
|---|---|---|
| I *enjoyed* it | **did** you **enjoy**? | I **didn't enjoy** |
| she *saw* him | **did** she **see**? | she **didn't see** |
| they *went* | **did** they **go**? | they **didn't go** |

After *did / didn't* the verb goes back to its base form — no -ed, no irregular past.

- I *liked* the concert. *Did* you *like* it? (not Did you liked)
- How many tickets *did* they *sell*?
- I *didn't call* you because I *didn't have* my phone.
- 'Did you finish?' 'No, I *didn't*.'

*Do* can be the main verb too: *What did you do last weekend?* (not What did you last weekend?) / *I didn't do anything.* (not I didn't anything)

## D The past of be: was / were

| | Positive / negative | Question |
|---|---|---|
| I / he / she / it | was / wasn't | was I? was she? |
| we / you / they | were / weren't | were we? were they? |

*Be* does not use *did*:

- I *was* furious because the train *was* late.
- *Was* the hotel comfortable when you *were* in Rome?
- They *weren't* at home, so I left a note.
- I *wasn't* thirsty, so I *didn't order* a drink.
- *Did* you *stay* in, or *were* you out all evening?

❌ *Did you be tired?* ✅ *Were you tired?*

### Question 35

#### Question
You ask a friend about her weekend. Which question is grammatically correct?

#### Answer
"What did you do on Saturday?" is correct. Past simple questions use *did* + subject + base verb, and here *do* is also the main verb, so it must appear: *did you do*. "What did you on Saturday?" leaves out the main verb — a very common mistake. "What did you did on Saturday?" wrongly repeats the past form after *did*. "What you did on Saturday?" lacks the auxiliary and has statement word order.

#### Correct variant
¯\_(ツ)_/¯

#### Variants
1. What did you on Saturday?
2. What did you do on Saturday? (Correct)
3. What did you did on Saturday?
4. What you did on Saturday?

Complete the sentence with the correct pair of forms: "I ____ in a hurry, so I ____ time to have breakfast."

#### Answer
"was … didn't have" is correct. The first gap needs the past of *be* for the subject *I* (*was*); the second is a past simple negative of the ordinary verb *have*, which takes *didn't* + base form. "was … hadn't" is tempting, but *hadn't* as a plain negative of *have* (meaning "possess") is not standard in modern English — *didn't have* is required. "were … didn't have" uses the wrong form of *be* for *I*. "was … didn't had" wrongly keeps the past form after *didn't*.
