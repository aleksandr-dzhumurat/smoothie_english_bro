# Unit 78 — Names with and without the 2

# Theory

## A Streets, parks, and "place/person + noun" names → no the

Names of **streets, roads, squares and parks** normally take no article: *Baker Street, Sunset Boulevard, Trafalgar Square, Regent's Park, Riverside Drive*. ❌ *the Baker Street*

Many **public buildings and institutions** have two-word names in which the first word is a **place name or a person's name**: *Heathrow Airport, Waterloo Station, Oxford University, Durham Cathedral, Windsor Castle, Kensington Palace, Boston Harbour*. These are used **without the**.

Compare: *Kensington Palace* (Kensington = a place name) but *the Royal Palace* (*Royal* is an ordinary adjective, not a name).

## B Most other buildings → the

| type | examples |
|---|---|
| hotels | the Ritz Hotel, the Grand Hotel |
| theatres / cinemas | the Globe Theatre, the Rex (cinema) |
| museums / galleries | the Louvre, the Tate Gallery, the Science Museum |
| other buildings | the Shard, the Colosseum, the Sydney Opera House |

The noun is often dropped: *the Ritz*, *the Globe*, *the Tate*. Some names consist only of **the + noun**: *the Vatican, the Louvre, the Alhambra*.

## C Names with of → the

Names built with **of** normally take **the**: *the Bank of Scotland, the Museum of Natural History, the Palace of Westminster, the Statue of Liberty, the Gulf of Mexico*.

Note the pair: *the University of Edinburgh* but *Edinburgh University* (no *the*).

## D Names ending in -'s or -s → no the

Shops, restaurants, banks, hotels etc. named after a **person** end in **-'s / -s** and take no article: *Harrods, Sainsbury's, Lloyds (bank), Selfridges, Luigi's (restaurant), Claridge's (hotel)*. ❌ *the Harrods*

Churches named after **saints** follow the same pattern: *St Mary's Church, St Stephen's Cathedral*.

## E Newspapers and organisations vs companies

**Newspapers** and many **organisations** take **the**: *the Guardian, the Times, the New York Times, the United Nations, the World Health Organisation, the Red Crescent, the RSPCA*.

**Companies, airlines, brands, publishers** are usually **without the**: *Toyota, Samsung, Lufthansa, Emirates, Penguin Books, Oxford University Press*. ❌ *the Toyota*

**See also:** Names with and without the 1 → Unit 77.

### Question 37

#### Question
Spot the mistake: "We had lunch at the Marco's, then walked to St Anne's Church and finished the day at the Odeon."

#### Answer
The mistake is *the Marco's*. Restaurants, shops, banks and hotels named after a person end in **-'s / -s** and take **no article**: *Marco's*, like *Harrods* or *Sainsbury's*. *St Anne's Church* is correct (churches named after saints follow the same possessive pattern, no *the*), and *the Odeon* is correct because cinemas and theatres normally take **the**, with the noun *cinema* often left out.

#### Correct variant
¯\_(ツ)_/¯

#### Variants
1. *the Odeon* should be *Odeon*, since it is a name.
2. *St Anne's Church* should be *the St Anne's Church*.
3. *the Marco's* should be *Marco's*: names ending in -'s take no article. (Correct)
4. There is no mistake in the sentence.

### Question 39

#### Question
Why is *Buckingham Palace* used without *the*, but *the Royal Palace* with it? Choose the correct explanation.

#### Answer
*Buckingham* is a **name** (of a place), and two-word names of the form "place/person name + noun" (*Buckingham Palace, Gatwick Airport, Harvard University*) are used without an article. *Royal*, on the other hand, is an ordinary **adjective**, not a name, so *the Royal Palace* behaves like any noun phrase with a describing adjective and takes **the**. Option 1 is a made-up rule; option 3 wrongly claims all palaces take *the*; option 4 confuses this with the *of*-rule.

#### Correct variant
¯\_(ツ)_/¯

#### Variants
1. Names of two words never take *the*; names of one word always do.
2. *Buckingham* is a place name, so the two-word name takes no article; *Royal* is an ordinary adjective, so *the* is used as with any adjective + noun. (Correct)
3. All palaces take *the*; *Buckingham Palace* is simply an irregular exception to memorise.
4. *The Royal Palace* takes *the* because it is short for *the Palace of the Royals*.
