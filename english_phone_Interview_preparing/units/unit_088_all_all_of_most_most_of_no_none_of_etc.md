# Unit 88 — all / all of / most / most of / no / none of etc.

# Theory

## A Quantifier + noun (general statements)

*all, some, any, most, much, many, (a) little, (a) few, no* can go directly before a noun to make general statements:

- All birds lay eggs.
- Some trains have wi-fi, others don’t.
- Most teenagers have a phone.
- Many drivers ignore the speed limit.
- I have no idea what happened.

We do **not** insert *of* before a bare noun: ❌ *some of trains*, ❌ *most of teenagers*.

## B Quantifier + of + the / this / my … (specific groups)

When the noun has a determiner (*the, this, that, these, those, my, your, Tom’s* …), we use **of**:

| quantifier | + of + | determiner + noun |
|---|---|---|
| some of / most of / none of | of | the passengers |
| any of / much of / many of | of | these boxes |
| (a) few of / (a) little of | of | my colleagues |

- Most of the passengers were asleep. (❌ most of passengers)
- None of this rice is cooked.
- Have you tried any of those cafés?
- I spent much of the weekend marking essays.

**all** and **half** may drop *of* before a determiner:
- All my cousins live abroad. **or** All of my cousins …
- Half the class was absent. **or** Half of the class …

Compare general vs specific:
- All dogs need exercise. (dogs in general)
- All (of) these dogs need a walk. (a specific group)
- Most films are too long. (films in general)
- We enjoyed most of the films at the festival. (specific films)

## C Quantifier + of + it / us / you / them

Before a pronoun, **of** is compulsory – including after *all* and *half*:

- "Did you like the album?" "Some of it. Not all of it."
- "How many of your neighbours do you know?" "None of them. / A few of them."
- Do any of you fancy a game of cards? (to a group)
- All of us were soaked. (❌ all us)
- I’ve only eaten half of it. (❌ half it)

## D Quantifier standing alone

*some, most, all, half, none* etc. can be used without a following noun when the meaning is clear:
- Some laptops are light and some are heavy.
- Several shops were open, but most (of them) had closed by six.
- Half this pizza is yours and half (of it) is mine. (❌ the half)

**See also:** Units 75B, 90, 110D (all); Unit 85 (some and any); Unit 86 (no and none); Unit 87 (much/many/little/few); Unit 96B (all of whom / most of which).

### Question 39

#### Question
Which sentence is wrong, and how should it be corrected?

A) Most people enjoy a day off.
B) Most of the people at the wedding were relatives.
C) Most of people prefer the window seat.
D) I spend most of my evenings reading.

#### Answer
**C is wrong.** Before a bare noun with no determiner we do not use *of*: it should be **Most people prefer the window seat.** *Of* is required only when a determiner follows (*the, my, these* …), as in B and D, which are correct. A is a correct general statement.

### Question 41

#### Question
Which two sentences are BOTH correct?

#### Answer
**"All my books are second-hand." and "All of my books are second-hand."** After *all* (and *half*) the *of* is optional before a determiner such as *my*. *All of books* is wrong because there is no determiner; *all books are second-hand* is grammatical but is a general statement that no longer refers to the speaker’s books, so it does not pair as an equivalent. The other options include *all us* or *most of books*, which are ungrammatical.

#### Correct variant
¯\_(ツ)_/¯

#### Variants
1. All my books are second-hand. / All of my books are second-hand. (Correct)
2. All my books are second-hand. / All of books are second-hand.
3. All us bought books. / All of us bought books.
4. Most of books are second-hand. / Most books are second-hand.
