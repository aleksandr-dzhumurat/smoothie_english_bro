# Unit 3 — Present continuous and present simple 1 (I am doing and I do)

# Theory

## A The core contrast

| Present continuous (I am doing) | Present simple (I do) |
|---|---|
| Something happening **at or around** the moment of speaking; not finished. | Things **in general**, or things that happen **repeatedly**. |
| Careful — the kettle *is boiling*. | Water *boils* at 100 degrees. |
| Listen! What language *are they speaking*? | Excuse me, *do you speak* French? |
| We can go out now. It *isn't raining*. | It *doesn't rain* much here in August. |
| 'I'm busy.' 'What *are you doing*?' | What *do you* usually *do* on Fridays? |
| I*'m getting* thirsty. Let's find a café. | I always *get* thirsty after a run. |
| Tomas wants to move to Rome, so he*'s learning* Italian. | Most children *learn* to ride a bike before they are ten. |
| The number of electric cars *is rising* rapidly. | Every year the number of electric cars *rises* by several million. |

**Temporary vs. permanent:**

| Continuous = temporary | Simple = permanent |
|---|---|
| I*'m staying* at my brother's until my flat is ready. | My brother *lives* in Leeds. He has always lived there. |
| You*'re working* late tonight. | Nadia *works* hard most days — she isn't lazy. |

Rule of thumb: if you can add *now / at the moment / this week*, use the continuous; if you can add *usually / always / every day / in general*, use the simple.

❌ *How often are you going to the gym?* ✅ *How often do you go to the gym?*
❌ *I must leave. It gets late.* ✅ *I must leave. It's getting late.*

## B I always do vs. I'm always doing

**I always do something** = I do it every single time, as a rule:

- I always *lock* the back door before bed. (not I'm always locking)

**I'm always doing something** = I do it too often, or more often than is normal — often with a note of irritation or humour:

- I can't find my glasses again. I*'m always losing* them. (= I lose them far too often)
- My flatmate *is always leaving* dirty dishes in the sink. (= it happens annoyingly often)
- You*'re always checking* your emails. Can't you switch off for an hour?

Compare:
- Ravi *always arrives* at nine. (his regular habit)
- Ravi *is always arriving* late. (a complaint: too often)
