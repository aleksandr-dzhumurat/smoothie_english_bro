# Unit 36 — would

# Theory

## A. would for imagined situations

We use **would ('d) / wouldn't** to talk about a situation we **imagine** – something that is not real:

- It **would be** lovely to have a garden, but our flat is on the fifth floor.
- I**'d love** to learn the cello one day.
- a: Shall I complain to the manager? b: No, I **wouldn't bother**. (= in your situation I wouldn't)

**would have (done)** imagines something that **did not happen in the past**:

- Thanks for lending me the car. I don't know how I**'d have got** to the hospital otherwise.
- You missed a brilliant match. You **would have enjoyed** it.
- I didn't mention the mistake to Karim. He **wouldn't have been** happy.

Compare **would (do)** – now – and **would have (done)** – past:

| now | past |
|---|---|
| I **would help** you move, but my back hurts. | I **would have helped** you move, but my back hurt. |
| I'm not inviting Dana. She **wouldn't come** anyway. | I didn't invite Dana. She **wouldn't have come** anyway. |

*Would* is very common in sentences with **if** (Units 38–40): *I'd take the job if it were in London. I'd have taken the job if it had been in London.*

## B. will vs would; would as the past of will

**will ('ll)** = real, expected; **would ('d)** = imagined, blocked by some reason:

- I**'ll have** another coffee – I've got time. / I**'d have** another coffee, but I have to catch my train.
- I**'ll text** Nadia; I've got her number. / I**'d text** Nadia, but I don't have her number.

**would / wouldn't** can also be simply the **past of will / won't**, e.g. in reported speech:

| present | past |
|---|---|
| "I**'ll** send it tonight." | He said he**'d** send it that night. |
| "I **won't** forget." | She promised she **wouldn't** forget. |
| "The printer **won't** work!" | She was furious because the printer **wouldn't** work. |

**Somebody wouldn't do something** = they **refused** to do it:

- I begged him to see a doctor, but he **wouldn't go**.
- The old lock **wouldn't turn**. (= it "refused" to turn)

## C. would for regular past actions

*Would* can describe things that **happened regularly in the past**, like *used to* (Unit 18):

- Every Sunday my grandfather **would bake** bread and the whole house **would smell** of it.
- Whenever the teacher left the room, the class **would start** chatting.

Unlike *used to*, this *would* is not used for past **states** (❌ *We would live by the sea* → *We used to live by the sea*).

### Question 37

#### Question
Compare: (a) "I'll help you paint the fence." (b) "I'd help you paint the fence." What is the difference in meaning?

#### Answer
(a) with **will** is a real offer – the speaker intends to help. (b) with **would** signals an imagined situation: the speaker *would* help in principle, but something prevents it, so a *but*-clause is expected ("… but I've hurt my shoulder"). The difference is not one of politeness level, not a matter of past versus present, and (b) is not a reported statement – it is a first-person imagined offer.

#### Correct variant
¯\_(ツ)_/¯

#### Variants
1. (a) is a real offer; (b) implies the speaker is not actually going to help because something prevents it. (Correct)
2. (b) is simply a more polite way of saying (a); the meaning is the same.
3. (a) refers to the future, (b) reports a past promise.
4. (b) means the speaker helped in the past; (a) means they will help in the future.

### Question 38

#### Question
One of these sentences uses *would* for a past habit incorrectly. Which one, and why?

#### Answer
"When I was a child, we **would live** in a small village" is wrong. *Would* for regular past actions is used with **actions** (events that repeated), not with **states** such as *live*, *be*, *have*. For a past state, use *used to*: *we used to live in a small village*. The other sentences all describe repeated past actions (*would walk*, *would tell*, *would spend*) and are correct.

#### Correct variant
¯\_(ツ)_/¯

#### Variants
1. Every morning my aunt would walk to the market before it got busy.
2. When I was a child, we would live in a small village. (Correct)
3. Whenever we visited, Grandpa would tell the same three jokes.
4. In the holidays we would spend hours building dens in the woods.
