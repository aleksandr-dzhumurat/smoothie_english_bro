# Unit 56 — Verb + -ing or to … 1 (remember, regret etc.)

# Theory

## A Two groups of verbs

| verbs + **-ing** (not *to …*) | verbs + **to …** |
|---|---|
| admit, avoid, consider, deny, enjoy, fancy, finish, imagine, keep (on), mind, postpone, risk, stop, suggest | afford, agree, arrange, decide, deserve, fail, forget, hope, learn, manage, offer, plan, promise, refuse, tend |

- She **avoided answering** the question. / She **refused to answer** the question.
- I **don't mind waiting**. / I **can't afford to wait**.

(Examples of the first group: Unit 53; of the second: Unit 54.)

## B Verbs that take both — with a change of meaning

### remember

| remember **doing** | remember **to do** |
|---|---|
| = I did it, and now I have a memory of it. The memory comes **after** the action. | = I didn't forget; I remembered that I had to do it, and did it. The remembering comes **before** the action. |
| I clearly remember **posting** the letter — I was standing outside the bank. | Please remember **to post** the letter on your way to work. (= don't forget) |
| He remembers **seeing** the lights, but nothing after that. | I remembered **to switch off** the oven, but forgot the lights. |

### regret

| regret **doing** | regret **to say / to tell you / to inform you** |
|---|---|
| = I did it and I'm sorry now. | = I'm sorry that I have to say this (formal). |
| I regret **quitting** the course. | We regret **to inform** you that the position has been filled. |
| Do you regret **not travelling** more when you were young? | |

### go on

| go on **doing** | go on **to do** |
|---|---|
| = continue the same activity. | = finish one thing and then do/say something new. |
| Ignore the noise and go on **working**. | She started as a receptionist and went on **to become** director. |
| Prices can't go on **rising** forever. | After the weather, the presenter went on **to read** the sports news. |

## C Verbs that take both — with no change of meaning

**begin, start, continue, intend, bother**

- It began **snowing**. / It began **to snow**.
- Do you intend **staying** long? / Do you intend **to stay** long?
- Don't bother **replying**. / Don't bother **to reply**.

One restriction: we avoid **-ing after -ing**. *It's starting **to rain**.* (not usually ~~It's starting raining~~)

**See also:** Verb + -ing → Unit 53; Verb + to … → Units 54–55; Other verbs + -ing or to … → Units 57–58.

### Question 35

#### Question
You are leaving the house and your partner calls out a reminder about the back door, which you have not yet locked. Which sentence is correct?

#### Answer
**"Remember to lock the back door!"** *Remember to do* means "don't forget to do this" — the action is still in the future. "Remember locking" would mean "recall the memory of having locked it", which makes no sense as a reminder about something not yet done. "Remember lock" is missing *to*, and "remember that you lock" is not the structure used for reminders.

#### Correct variant
¯\_(ツ)_/¯

#### Variants
1. Remember locking the back door!
2. Remember to lock the back door! (Correct)
3. Remember lock the back door!
4. Remember that you lock the back door!

### Question 36

#### Question
A witness is describing an accident: she can clearly recall the moment the lorry crossed the line, but nothing after that. Which sentence expresses this?

#### Answer
**"She remembers seeing the lorry cross the line, but nothing after that."** She saw it, and now she has a memory of it — this is *remember + -ing* (memory after the action). "Remembers to see" would mean she didn't forget to see it, which is absurd. "Remembered to see" has the same problem plus the wrong tense. "Remembers to have seen" is not a standard English structure with *remember*.

#### Correct variant
¯\_(ツ)_/¯

#### Variants
1. She remembers to see the lorry cross the line, but nothing after that.
2. She remembered to see the lorry cross the line, but nothing after that.
3. She remembers seeing the lorry cross the line, but nothing after that. (Correct)
4. She remembers to have seen the lorry cross the line, but nothing after that.

### Question 37

#### Question
Which sentence is the correct formal way for a company to announce bad news in a letter?

#### Answer
**"We regret to inform you that your application was unsuccessful."** *Regret to say / to tell / to inform* is the fixed formal formula meaning "we are sorry that we have to tell you". "Regret informing you" would mean "we informed you and now we are sorry we did", which is not the intended meaning. "Regret inform you" lacks *to*, and "regret that inform you" is ungrammatical.

#### Correct variant
¯\_(ツ)_/¯

#### Variants
1. We regret informing you that your application was unsuccessful.
2. We regret inform you that your application was unsuccessful.
3. We regret to inform you that your application was unsuccessful. (Correct)
4. We regret that inform you that your application was unsuccessful.

### Question 38

#### Question
Compare the two sentences and choose the correct description:
(a) After the coffee break, the lecturer went on **talking** about the Roman economy.
(b) After the coffee break, the lecturer went on **to talk** about the Roman economy.

#### Answer
**In (a) the lecturer continued the same topic she had been discussing before the break; in (b) she moved on to a new topic.** *Go on doing* = continue the same activity; *go on to do* = finish one thing and then do something different. The two forms are not interchangeable here, so option 1 is wrong; option 3 has the meanings the wrong way round; option 4 is wrong because both sentences are grammatical.

#### Correct variant
¯\_(ツ)_/¯

#### Variants
1. Both sentences mean the same thing; the two forms are interchangeable after *go on*.
2. In (a) the lecturer continued the same topic; in (b) she moved on to a new topic. (Correct)
3. In (a) the lecturer moved on to a new topic; in (b) she continued the same topic.
4. Only (b) is grammatical; *go on* cannot be followed by -ing.

### Question 39

#### Question
Which of these sentences contains a mistake?

#### Answer
Sentence 3 is wrong: **"Look — it's beginning raining"** should be **"it's beginning to rain."** Although *begin* normally takes either -ing or *to …*, we avoid two -ing forms in a row, so after *beginning* we use the infinitive. The other sentences are correct: "started to feel" and "started feeling" would both be fine; "intend to sell / intend selling" are both acceptable after *intend*; "regret not saying" is a correct negative -ing form after *regret* meaning "I'm sorry I didn't".

#### Correct variant
¯\_(ツ)_/¯

#### Variants
1. About halfway through the film I started to feel sleepy.
2. Do you intend selling the house this year?
3. Look — it's beginning raining. (Correct)
4. I regret not saying goodbye to her properly.
