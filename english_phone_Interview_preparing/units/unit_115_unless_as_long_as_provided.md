# Unit 115 — unless / as long as / provided

# Theory

## A unless = except if

Imagine a car park that is for residents only. *You can't park here unless you live in the building.* This means:

- You can't park here **except if** you live in the building.
- You can park here **only if** you live in the building.

More examples:

- We'll eat outside **unless** it gets too windy. (= except if it gets too windy)
- **Unless** you book in advance, you won't get a table. (= except if you book)
- "Should I mention the delay to the client?" "**Not unless** they ask." (= only if they ask)
- Farid never takes a day off. He wouldn't stay at home **unless** he was seriously ill.
- Let's walk to the station – **unless** you'd rather take a taxi.

Very often **unless** can be replaced by **if … not**:

- **Unless** we hurry, we'll miss the start. = **If we don't** hurry, we'll miss the start.

Note that *unless* already contains a negative idea, so do not add another: ❌ *unless we don't hurry*.

## B as long as / so long as / provided (that) / providing (that)

These all mean **if** / **on condition that**. They introduce a condition that must be true for something to happen or be acceptable:

| expression | example |
|---|---|
| as long as | You can use my laptop **as long as** you don't install anything. |
| so long as | You can use my laptop **so long as** you don't install anything. |
| provided (that) | Cycling to work is pleasant **provided (that)** the weather's dry. |
| providing (that) | **Providing (that)** the flat has a balcony, I don't mind which floor it's on. |

Meaning: *You can use my laptop, but you must not install anything – that's the condition.* / *The flat must have a balcony; nothing else matters.*

Compare with **unless**:

- You can borrow the bike **as long as** you bring it back tonight. (condition for a positive result)
- You can't borrow the bike **unless** you bring it back tonight. (= except if …)

Both sentences describe the same condition, but *as long as / provided* attach it to a positive statement, while *unless* means "except if" and normally combines with a negative or unwanted result.

## C Future: present tense, not will

After **unless / as long as / so long as / provided / providing**, we use a **present tense** to talk about the future (as after *if* – see Unit 25):

- I won't go swimming **unless** the water **is** warmer. ❌ *unless the water will be warmer*
- **Providing** the trains **run** on time, we'll be there by ten. ❌ *providing the trains will run*
- You'll pass **as long as** you **revise** the main chapters.

### Question 31

#### Question
Rewrite with *unless*: "You must water the plants regularly, or they will die." Which sentence is correct?

#### Answer
"The plants will die **unless you water them** regularly." *Unless* means *except if*, so the sentence says the plants will die except if you water them – the same as the original. Sentence 1 adds a second negative (*unless you don't water*), which reverses the meaning: it would say the plants die if you *do* water them. Sentence 3 uses *will* after *unless*, which is not allowed for future conditions. Sentence 4 attaches *unless* to the wrong half of the idea and makes no sense.

#### Correct variant
¯\_(ツ)_/¯

#### Variants
1. The plants will die unless you don't water them regularly.
2. The plants will die unless you water them regularly. (Correct)
3. The plants will die unless you will water them regularly.
4. Unless the plants will die, you water them regularly.

### Question 32

#### Question
Which sentence has the same meaning as: "The pool is open to children only if an adult is with them"?

#### Answer
"Children can't use the pool **unless** an adult is with them." *Only if X* is equivalent to *not … unless X* (= *not … except if X*). Sentence 1 says the opposite – children are banned precisely when an adult is present. Sentence 3 makes the presence of an adult a ban rather than a permission. Sentence 4 uses *as long as* with a negative result, producing the wrong meaning: it would mean children may not swim whenever an adult is present.

#### Correct variant
¯\_(ツ)_/¯

#### Variants
1. Children can use the pool unless an adult is with them.
2. Children can't use the pool unless an adult is with them. (Correct)
3. Children can't use the pool provided an adult is with them.
4. Children can't use the pool as long as an adult is with them.
