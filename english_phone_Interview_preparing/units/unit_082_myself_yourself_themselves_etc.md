# Unit 82 — myself/yourself/themselves etc.

# Theory

## A Reflexive pronouns: subject = object

When the subject and the object of a verb are the same person or thing, we use a reflexive pronoun:

- Priya introduced herself to the new team. (Priya = subject and object)

| | singular (-self) | plural (-selves) |
|---|---|---|
| 1st person | myself | ourselves |
| 2nd person | yourself (one person) | yourselves (more than one) |
| 3rd person | himself / herself / itself | themselves |

- Don’t worry about my ticket – I’ll buy it for myself. (❌ for me)
- Did the kids enjoy themselves at the zoo?
- Do you ever talk to yourself when you’re working? (to one person)
- There’s plenty of food – help yourselves. (to a group)

Compare object pronoun vs reflexive:
- Marco showed me round the office. (two different people)
- I showed myself round the office. (same person)

## B Verbs that usually take no reflexive

We do **not** use myself etc. after **feel, relax, concentrate, meet**:
- I feel exhausted. (❌ feel myself exhausted)
- Turn the music off – I can’t concentrate. (❌ concentrate myself)
- Where shall we meet on Friday? (❌ meet ourselves)

We normally leave out the reflexive after **wash, shave, dress** too:
- She got up, showered, dressed and left. (❌ dressed herself)
- You can also say **get dressed**: I got dressed in a hurry.

## C -selves vs each other / one another

- **themselves** = the group acts on the group as a whole: The twins looked at themselves in the shop window. (each looked at their own reflection)
- **each other** = A acts on B and B acts on A: Nadia and Tom looked at each other and laughed. (Nadia → Tom, Tom → Nadia)

**One another** means the same as each other:
- How long have you two known each other / one another?
- The neighbours don’t speak to each other / one another.

## D Emphatic use: I did it myself

Reflexive pronouns can also add emphasis, meaning "that person and nobody else":

- "Who fixed the tap?" "I fixed it myself." (= I, not a plumber)
- Don’t ask me to write your essay. Write it yourself.
- We built the shed ourselves – it saved a fortune.
- The hotel itself was ordinary, but the view was stunning.
- Nobody thinks Ruth will win. Ruth doesn’t think so herself. / Ruth herself doesn’t think so.

### Question 42

#### Question
Anna sent Ben a birthday card, and Ben sent Anna one. Which sentence describes this?

#### Answer
**Anna and Ben sent each other birthday cards.** *Each other* (or *one another*) means A acts on B and B acts on A. *Sent themselves birthday cards* would mean each of them sent a card to himself/herself. *Sent them* refers to other people, and *sent themselves each other* is ungrammatical.

#### Correct variant
¯\_(ツ)_/¯

#### Variants

2. Anna and Ben sent them birthday cards.
3. Anna and Ben sent each other birthday cards. (Correct)
