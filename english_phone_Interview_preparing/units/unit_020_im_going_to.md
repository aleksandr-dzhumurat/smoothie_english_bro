# Unit 20 — I'm going to (do)

# Theory

## A I am going to do = I have decided to do it

*I'm going to do something* means I have already made up my mind; I intend to do it.

- "**Are you going to** apply for the job?" "No, I've decided against it."
- a: Petra has been given a big bonus. What **is she going to do** with it?
  b: She**'s going to** pay off her car loan.
- I**'m just going to** grab a coffee. Back in a minute.
- This milk smells off. I**'m not going to** drink it.

Form: **am/is/are + going to + infinitive**. Negative: *isn't going to*; question: *Is she going to … ?*

## B I am doing vs I am going to do

| I am doing (present continuous) | I am going to do |
|---|---|
| Already **fixed or arranged** — with other people, tickets, bookings | Already **decided**; may or may not be arranged |
| I**'m flying** to Oslo on Friday. I've bought the ticket. | Your desk is a mess. — I know, I**'m going to** tidy it. (decided, arranged with nobody) |
| What time **are you meeting** the agent? | I hate this hotel. Tomorrow I**'m going to** find somewhere else. |

Compare:

- I don't know what I**'m doing** at the weekend. (= I don't know my plans/schedule)
- I don't know what I**'m going to do** about the noisy neighbours. (= I haven't decided how to react)

In many everyday cases the difference is small and both forms are fine.

## C Something is going to happen — predicting from present evidence

We also use *going to* when the **situation now** makes it clear that something will happen:

- A toddler is running towards a puddle: He**'s going to** fall in!
- Look at that sky — it**'s going to** pour.
- I feel dizzy. I think I**'m going to** faint.
- Sales are down for the third month running, and things **are going to** get worse.

```
now (evidence) ────── going to ──────► future event
```

The clue is always something we can see, feel or know **now**.

## D I was going to do = I intended to, but didn't

*Was/were going to* describes a past intention that was **not** carried out:

- We **were going to** paint the kitchen at the weekend, but the paint didn't arrive.
- I **was just going to** call you when your message came through.

We can also say that something *was going to happen* but didn't:

- It looked as if the concert **was going to be** cancelled, but it went ahead.
- I thought I **was going to** miss the train, but it was late too.

**See also:** I am doing (future) → Unit 19A; I will and I'm going to → Unit 23.

### Question 27

#### Question
Your friend says, "I've bought a really expensive camera." You want to ask about her intentions. Which question is correct?

#### Answer
"What **are you going to** photograph with it?" Asking about someone's decided intention uses *be going to + infinitive*. "What do you go to photograph" is not a form of English. "What are you going to photographing" wrongly puts an *-ing* form after *going to*, which must be followed by the infinitive. "What you are going to photograph" has statement word order instead of question order.

#### Correct variant
¯\_(ツ)_/¯

#### Variants
1. What do you go to photograph with it?
2. What are you going to photograph with it? (Correct)
3. What are you going to photographing with it?
4. What you are going to photograph with it?

### Question 28

#### Question
A driver has just filled up, but the fuel gauge is still on empty and there is a wet patch under the car. What is the most natural comment?

#### Answer
"The tank **is going to** run dry soon — it's leaking." The present evidence (the wet patch, the gauge) makes it clear what will happen, and *going to* is the form for predictions based on what we can see now. "Is running dry soon" uses the present continuous, which is for arrangements, not predictions from evidence. "Runs dry soon" uses the present simple, which is for timetables. "Was going to run dry" describes a past intention or expectation that didn't happen.

#### Correct variant
¯\_(ツ)_/¯

#### Variants
1. The tank is running dry soon — it's leaking.
2. The tank runs dry soon — it's leaking.
3. The tank was going to run dry — it's leaking.
4. The tank is going to run dry soon — it's leaking. (Correct)

### Question 29

#### Question
Compare: (a) "I don't know what I**'m doing** tomorrow." (b) "I don't know what I**'m going to do** about the broken boiler." Which explanation is correct?

#### Answer
(a) means I don't know my schedule — nothing is arranged for tomorrow. (b) means I haven't decided how to deal with the boiler problem. *I'm doing* refers to arrangements that are (or aren't) fixed; *I'm going to do* refers to decisions and intentions. Both sentences are grammatical and they are not interchangeable in meaning here, although in many other cases the difference between the two forms is small.

#### Correct variant
¯\_(ツ)_/¯

#### Variants
1. (a) is about a fixed arrangement / schedule; (b) is about a decision not yet made. (Correct)
2. (a) is wrong because the present continuous cannot refer to the future.
3. Both mean exactly the same; (b) is simply longer.
4. (a) is about a decision; (b) is about a timetable.

### Question 30

#### Question
Complete the sentence: "We ______ go camping last weekend, but it rained the whole time, so we stayed at home."

#### Answer
"We **were going to** go camping last weekend, but …" *Was/were going to* expresses a past intention that was not carried out — they planned to camp but didn't. "Are going to" is a present intention and clashes with *last weekend* and the fact that the plan failed. "Went" would mean they actually camped, which contradicts *so we stayed at home*. "Had gone" says they had already gone camping before some other event, which does not fit.

#### Correct variant
¯\_(ツ)_/¯

#### Variants
1. are going to
2. went to
3. were going to (Correct)

### Question 31

#### Question
Which sentence is wrong, and why?

#### Answer
Sentence 2 is wrong: "Look at those clouds. It **will going to** rain" combines *will* with *going to*, which is impossible. The prediction is based on present evidence (the clouds), so the correct form is "It**'s going to** rain" (*is/are + going to + infinitive*). Sentence 1 is a correct negative statement of intention. Sentence 3 correctly uses *was going to* for an unfulfilled past plan. Sentence 4 is grammatical: *I'm going to London* is the present continuous used for a fixed arrangement, which fits "I've booked my seat".

#### Correct variant
¯\_(ツ)_/¯

#### Variants
1. I'm not going to answer any more emails today.
2. Look at those clouds. It will going to rain. (Correct)
3. I was going to text you, but my phone died.
4. I'm going to London on the 9.15 — I've booked my seat.
