# Unit 119 — during / for / while

# Theory

## A during

**During** + noun = at some point between the beginning and the end of something:

- Somebody's phone rang **during the concert**. (= at some time between the start and the end of the concert)
- We visited three islands **during our trip**.
- The pipes froze **during the night**.

With time words such as *the morning, the night, the winter, the holidays*, both **in** and **during** are usually possible:

- The roads are empty **in the early morning**. / … **during the early morning**.
- It snows a lot here **in winter**. / … **during the winter**.

## B for and during

**For** + a period of time tells us **how long** something lasts:

- We waited at the gate **for forty minutes**.
- Karim is going to Lisbon **for a fortnight**.
- I haven't spoken to Ellie **for months**.

**During** does **not** say how long; it says **when** (within which event). So *during* is never used with a length of time:

- ✅ It rained **for** six hours. ❌ *during six hours*
- ✅ They lived in Cairo **for** ten years. ❌ *during ten years*

Compare:

| question | answer |
|---|---|
| **When** did the lights go out? | **During** the storm. |
| **How long** were the lights out? | **For** about two hours. |

## C during and while

Both mean "in the period of", but the grammar is different:

| during + **noun** | while + **subject + verb** |
|---|---|
| Somebody stole my bag **during the match**. | Somebody stole my bag **while I was watching the match**. |
| He fainted **during the ceremony**. | He fainted **while he was standing in the sun**. |
| We chatted **during the break**. | We chatted **while we were having coffee**. |

More examples with **while**:

- I saw an old friend **while I was queuing** for tickets.
- **While you were asleep**, the plumber came.
- Leo cooked **while Maya set the table**. (two actions at the same time)

### while + present for the future

After **while** we use a **present tense** to talk about the future, not *will* (see Unit 25):

- I'll water your plants **while you're away**. ❌ *while you will be away*
- What will the children do **while we're at the meeting**? ❌ *while we'll be*

## Summary

| word | followed by | answers |
|---|---|---|
| during | noun | when? (within which event) |
| for | length of time | how long? |
| while | subject + verb | when? (within which activity) |

**See also:** for and since ➜ Unit 12A; while + -ing ➜ Unit 68B

#### Question
Complete the sentence: "The power was off ____ almost five hours yesterday." Which word is correct?

#### Answer
"The power was off **for** almost five hours yesterday." *Almost five hours* is a length of time, and only *for* answers "how long?". *During* is never used with a period of time to say how long something lasts – it says when something happens within an event (*during the storm*). *While* needs a subject + verb, and *in* five hours would mean "within a five-hour limit", not the duration of being off.

#### Correct variant

### Question 46

#### Question
Two answers to two questions about a concert. Which pair is correct? Q1: "When did you fall asleep?" Q2: "How long did you sleep?"

#### Answer

#### Question

#### Answer

Option 3 puts a clause after *during* in another way (*during walking* without a subject is not the normal pattern in this meaning), and option 4 uses *while* + noun, where *during the dog walk* is needed.

#### Correct variant

### Question 48

#### Question
Which sentence about a future arrangement is correct?

#### Answer
"Can you look after the cat **while we're** in Spain?" After *while* we use a present tense for future time, as after *if*, *when* and *until*. Sentence 1 wrongly uses *will be* after *while*.
