# Unit 12 — for and since / when … ? and how long … ?

# Theory

## A for + period, since + starting point

Both *for* and *since* say how long something has been going on, but they attach to different kinds of phrase.

| **for** + a length of time | **since** + the moment it started |
|---|---|
| for ten minutes | since 7 o'clock |
| for three weeks | since Tuesday |
| for a couple of years | since 2015 |
| for ages / for a long time | since Easter / since lunchtime |
| for most of my life | since I moved here / since we met |

- Farid has been running the bakery **for** eight years. ❌ (not *since eight years*)
- Farid has been running the bakery **since** 2018. (= from 2018 up to now)
- I haven't heard from Bea **for** a fortnight. / I haven't heard from Bea **since** the wedding.

Note that *since* can be followed by a whole clause in the past simple: *since I got up*, *since they arrived*.

## B Leaving out for; in instead of for; all …

In positive sentences *for* is often dropped:

- They've been together **(for)** five years.

In negative sentences you normally keep it:

- They haven't spoken to each other **for** five years. (*for* is needed)

In negatives you can also use *in*:

- I haven't been to the theatre **in** years. (= for years)

Do not use *for* with **all** (*all day, all week, all my life*):

- She's worked here **all her life**. ❌ (not *for all her life*)

## C when … ? (past simple) vs how long … ? (present perfect)

*When* asks for a point in time and takes the **past simple**. *How long* asks about a duration up to now and takes the **present perfect**.

| Question | Answer |
|---|---|
| **When did** the concert **start**? | It **started** at eight / an hour ago. |
| **How long has** the concert **been going on**? | It**'s been going on** for an hour / since eight. |
| **When did** you **buy** that bike? | Years ago / when I was a student. |
| **How long have** you **had** that bike? | For years / since I was a student. |

Answers with *ago* go with *when*; answers with *for/since* go with *how long*.

## D It's (been) … since …

A common way to say how long ago something last happened:

- **It's** three months **since** I last went swimming. or **It's been** three months since …
  (= I haven't been swimming for three months.)
- **It's** ages **since** we had a proper holiday. or **It's been** ages since …
- **How long is it since** you saw your grandparents? or **How long has it been since** …
  (= When did you last see them?)

The clause after *since* is in the past simple.
