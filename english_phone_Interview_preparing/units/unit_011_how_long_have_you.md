# Unit 11 — how long have you (been) … ?

# Theory

## A Present vs present perfect for situations that continue now

When a situation started in the past and is still true now, English uses the **present perfect** to say *how long* it has lasted. The present tense describes the situation itself; the present perfect measures its duration.

| Situation now (present) | Duration up to now (present perfect) |
|---|---|
| Marta lives in Lisbon. | Marta **has lived** in Lisbon **for six years**. |
| Omar is a vegetarian. | Omar **has been** a vegetarian **since 2019**. |
| They own a boat. | **How long have they had** their boat? |
| The kids are playing outside. | The kids **have been playing** outside **all afternoon**. |

Typical learner errors mix the two:

- ❌ How long are you here? ✅ How long **have you been** here?
- ❌ Marta lives in Lisbon for six years. ✅ Marta **has lived** in Lisbon for six years.
- ❌ We know each other since university. ✅ We**'ve known** each other since university.

Rule of thumb: if the sentence contains *since*, *for + period* or *how long*, and the situation still holds, you need *have/has + past participle*, not a present tense.

## B Simple or continuous?

- **Present perfect simple**: I've known / I've had / I've lived …
- **Present perfect continuous**: I've been studying / I've been waiting …

When asking or saying *how long*, the **continuous** is the more usual choice for actions:

- I**'ve been studying** Portuguese since March.
- The printer**'s been making** that noise all week.
- "How long **have you been cycling** to work?" "Since the petrol prices went up."

But some verbs (*know, like, believe, belong, mean, own* …) are not normally used in continuous forms, so they take the simple form even with *how long*:

- How long **have you known** your neighbours? ❌ (not *have you been knowing*)
- I**'ve had** this backpack for years. ❌ (not *I've been having*)

## C live and work: both forms possible

With *live* and *work*, either form is fine:

- Priya **has been working** at the clinic for a decade. / Priya **has worked** at the clinic for a decade.
- How long **have you been living** in that flat? / How long **have you lived** in that flat?

However, with **always**, use the simple form:

- I**'ve always worked** in hospitality. ❌ (not *I've always been working*)

## D Negative: I haven't … since/for …

To say that something last happened a certain time ago, use the **present perfect simple** in the negative:

- I **haven't spoken** to Leo **since** Tuesday. (= Tuesday was the last time)
- Nina **hasn't been** to the gym **for** months. (= the last visit was months ago)
