# Unit 103 — enough and too

# Theory

## A Position of enough

*Enough* goes **after** an adjective or adverb, but **before** a noun:

| after adjective / adverb | before noun |
|---|---|
| The water isn't warm enough. | We don't have enough plates. |
| You didn't practise often enough. | Is there enough light in here? |

❌ She isn't enough experienced. ✅ She isn't experienced enough.
❌ We haven't got time enough. ✅ We haven't got enough time.

*Enough* can also stand alone when the noun is understood: *"More rice?" "No thanks, I've had enough."*

## B too versus (not) enough

*Too* = more than is necessary or wanted; *not … enough* = less than is necessary:

- Slow down — you're speaking too quickly. (more than I can follow)
- Speak up — you're not speaking loudly enough. (less than I need)

With nouns, use *too much* (uncountable) or *too many* (plural) against *enough*:

- There's too much salt in the soup and not enough pepper.
- There were too many applicants and not enough interviewers.

## C enough / too + for … and to …

Add *for somebody/something* to say who or what it is (not) suitable for, and *to + infinitive* to say what is (not) possible:

- Is the room big enough for twelve people?
- These shoes are too tight for me.
- He isn't tall enough to reach the top shelf.
- It's too late to phone them now.

Both can appear together: *The path is just wide enough for two bikes to pass.*

## D too heavy to carry (no object pronoun)

When the subject of the sentence is the thing affected, the infinitive has **no** object pronoun:

- The tea was too hot to drink. (not *to drink it*)
- The box is too heavy to lift. (not *to lift it*)
- That ladder isn't safe enough to climb. (not *to climb it*)

Compare the equivalent longer forms: *The tea was so hot that we couldn't drink it.* — here *it* is needed because *tea* is not the subject of *drink*.

### Question 27

#### Question
A gym instructor is talking to a new member: "You won't be able to lift that weight yet. You're not ______." Which option completes the sentence correctly?

#### Answer
The correct answer is *strong enough*. *Enough* follows an adjective, so it must come after *strong*. *Enough strong* puts the word in the noun position, which is wrong for an adjective. *Too strong* gives the opposite meaning (more strength than necessary), and *strong too* is not a possible English word order.

#### Correct variant
¯\_(ツ)_/¯

#### Variants
1. enough strong
2. too strong
3. strong enough (Correct)
4. strong too

### Question 28

#### Question
Which sentence correctly describes a kitchen with fifteen chairs for a family of four, and only one small window?

#### Answer
The correct sentence is *There are too many chairs and not enough light.* *Chairs* is a plural countable noun, so "more than is needed" is *too many*; *too much chairs* is a common mistake because *much* only works with uncountable nouns. *Light* is uncountable and the meaning "less than is needed" is expressed by *not enough*, with *enough* before the noun (*not light enough* would treat *light* as an adjective and change the meaning to "not bright enough").

#### Correct variant
¯\_(ツ)_/¯

#### Variants
1. There are too much chairs and not enough light.
2. There are too many chairs and not enough light. (Correct)
3. There are too many chairs and not light enough.
4. There are enough too chairs and too little light.

### Question 29

#### Question
Rewrite as one sentence: "We couldn't read the sign. It was too far away." Which version is correct?

#### Answer
The correct sentence is *The sign was too far away to read.* When the subject of the sentence (*the sign*) is the thing that the infinitive verb acts on, we do not repeat it with a pronoun — so *to read it* is wrong. The pattern is *too + adjective + to + verb*. *Enough far away to read* is wrong both in word order and in meaning (it would suggest being far away was helpful). *For reading* is not the structure taught here; after *too + adjective* we use the *to*-infinitive.

#### Correct variant
¯\_(ツ)_/¯

#### Variants
1. The sign was too far away to read it.
2. The sign was enough far away to read.
3. The sign was too far away to read. (Correct)
4. The sign was too far away for reading it.

### Question 30

#### Question
Complete the dialogue: "Can your daughter vote in the election this year?" "No, she isn't ______ vote yet — she's only sixteen."

#### Answer
The correct answer is *old enough to*. After *enough* we use *to + infinitive* to say what is possible: *old enough to vote*. *Enough old to* has *enough* in the wrong position for an adjective. *Too young for* would be grammatically fine with a noun (*too young for the election*), but before a verb we need *to*, not *for*. *Old enough for* cannot be followed directly by the bare verb *vote*.

#### Correct variant

2. enough old to

### Question 31

#### Question
Which sentence contains a mistake, and why?
(a) The soup was so hot that we couldn't eat it.
(b) The soup was too hot to eat.
(c) The soup was too hot to eat it.
(d) The soup was too hot for the children to eat.

#### Answer
Sentence (c) is wrong. In *too hot to eat*, the subject *the soup* is understood as the object of *eat*, so adding *it* creates a second, redundant object. Sentence (a) is correct because the *that*-clause has its own subject (*we*) and so needs *it* as the object. Sentence (b) is the correct short form. Sentence (d) correctly combines *for + somebody* and *to + verb*, and again no object pronoun is added after *eat*.

#### Correct variant
¯\_(ツ)_/¯

#### Variants
1. Sentence (a) — after *so hot that* the pronoun *it* should be left out.
2. Sentence (c) — after *too hot to eat* the object pronoun *it* must not be added, because *the soup* is already understood as the object. (Correct)
3. Sentence (d) — *for the children* and *to eat* cannot appear in the same sentence.
4. Sentence (b) — *too hot to eat* is incomplete without an object.
