# Unit 107 — Comparative 3 (as … as / than)

# Theory

## A not as … as

Imagine three cities: Porto has 200,000 people, Bilbao 350,000 and Lyon 500,000. Bilbao is big; it is **bigger than** Porto; but it **isn't as big as** Lyon (= Lyon is bigger).

*Not as … as* says that the first thing has **less** of the quality than the second:

- The film wasn't as good as the book. (= the book was better)
- I don't earn as much as my sister. (= she earns more)
- He didn't play as well as he usually does.
- "Was it a two-hour drive?" "No, not as long as that."

You can also say *not so … as*: *It's not so cold as it was last week.*

*Less than* has a similar meaning: *I read less than I used to.* (= I don't read as much as I used to) / *The exam was less difficult than the mock.* (= it wasn't as difficult as …)

## B as … as in positive sentences and questions

In positive sentences and questions use *as … as* (not *so … as*):

- Take as much time as you need.
- Please reply as soon as you can.
- The bike is just as fast as the bus in rush hour.
- Is the second-hand one as reliable as the new one?

Multiples: *twice as … as, three times as … as*:

- Rent in the capital is twice as high as here.
- Their kitchen is three times as big as ours. (or *three times the size of ours*)

## C the same as

Say *the same as* (not *the same like*):

- Your phone is the same as mine.
- My brother is the same height as me.
- The menu is the same as it was last year.

❌ the same like ✅ the same as

## D than me / than I am

After *than* and *as*, use an object pronoun (*me, him, her, us, them*) or subject + verb:

| object pronoun | subject + verb |
|---|---|
| She's older than me. | She's older than I am. |
| He isn't as tall as her. | He isn't as tall as she is. |
| They earn more than us. | They earn more than we do. |
| I can't swim as fast as him. | I can't swim as fast as he can. |

*Than I* / *as he* on their own are not usual in modern English.

### Question 36

#### Question
Nora's flat has 60 square metres; Ben's has 85. Which sentence about Nora's flat is correct?

#### Answer
The correct sentence is *Nora's flat isn't as big as Ben's.* To say something has less of a quality, we use *not as + adjective + as*. *So big than* mixes the two structures — *than* belongs with a comparative (*bigger than*), not with *as/so*. *As big than* makes the same mistake. *As bigger as* wrongly puts a comparative inside *as … as*; the adjective must be in its base form.

#### Correct variant
¯\_(ツ)_/¯

#### Variants
1. Nora's flat isn't so big than Ben's.
2. Nora's flat isn't as big as Ben's. (Correct)
3. Nora's flat isn't as big than Ben's.
4. Nora's flat isn't as bigger as Ben's.

### Question 37

#### Question
Which sentence is wrong, and why?
(a) I'll call you back as soon as I can.
(b) You can borrow as many books as you like.
(c) Please get here so quickly as possible.
(d) Is your new job as interesting as the old one?

#### Answer
Sentence (c) is wrong. *So … as* can only be used in negative sentences (*not so quickly as*). In positive sentences and questions we must use *as … as*: *Please get here as quickly as possible.* Sentences (a) and (b) are correct positive uses of *as … as*, and sentence (d) is a correct question.

#### Correct variant
¯\_(ツ)_/¯

#### Variants
1. Sentence (a) — *as soon as* should be *so soon as*.
2. Sentence (c) — *so … as* is only used in negatives; it should be *as quickly as possible*. (Correct)
3. Sentence (d) — questions need *than*, not *as … as*.
4. Sentence (b) — *as many … as* is wrong with plural nouns; it should be *as much books as*.

### Question 40

#### Question
Two versions of a sentence: (a) "Maya runs faster than me." (b) "Maya runs faster than I do." Which statement about them is correct?

#### Answer
Both are correct and mean the same thing. After *than* (and after *as*) we can use either an object pronoun (*than me*) or a subject pronoun followed by a verb (*than I do*). What is not usual is a subject pronoun on its own: *faster than I* sounds unnatural in everyday modern English. The same choice applies to *as … as*: *as fast as him* or *as fast as he can*.

#### Correct variant
¯\_(ツ)_/¯

#### Variants
1. Only (a) is correct; *than* must always be followed by an object pronoun, never a clause.
2. Only (b) is correct; *than me* is a grammatical error in British English.
3. Both are correct and mean the same; the form to avoid is *than I* on its own. (Correct)
4. Neither is correct; the only acceptable form is *Maya runs faster than I*.
