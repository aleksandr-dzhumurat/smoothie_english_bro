# Unit 18 — used to (do)

# Theory

## A The basic idea

Situation: These days Kofi rarely goes to concerts — he prefers listening at home. But a few years ago he **used to go** to a gig almost every week.

*Kofi used to go to concerts* = he went to concerts regularly in the past, but he **doesn't any more**.

```
past  ──── he used to go to concerts ────► now: he doesn't go
```

## B Past habits and past states

**I used to do something** = I did it often / regularly in the past, but not now:

- I **used to cycle** to school, but now I take the bus.
- Rosa **used to spend** every summer at her grandparents' farm.
- "Do you still play chess?" "Not any more, but I **used to**." (= I used to play)

We also use *used to* for **states** that were true but are no longer true:

- That building **used to be** a post office. It's a gym now.
- I **used to think** maths was boring; now I teach it.
- I've got into jazz lately. I **never used to like** it.
- Dev **used to have** a beard.

## C There is no present form

*Used to* is **only past**. You cannot say *I use to do* for the present. For present habits and states, use the **present simple**.

| Past | Present |
|---|---|
| she **used to swim** | she **swims** |
| we **used to rent** a flat | we **rent** / we **own** a flat |
| there **used to be** a market here | there **is** a market here |

- We **used to live** on the coast, but now we **live** inland.
- There **used to be** three bakeries on this street. Now there **is** only one.

## D Questions and negatives

The normal question form is **did … use to … ?** (note the spelling *use*, not *used*, after *did*):

- **Did** you **use to** watch cartoons on Saturday mornings?

The negative is **didn't use to …** (*used not to …* is also possible but less common):

- I **didn't use to** enjoy cooking. (or *I used not to enjoy cooking.*)

❌ *I didn't used to* / ❌ *Did you used to* — after *did*, write *use*.

## E used to do vs was doing

- I **used to read** a lot. (= I read regularly in the past; I don't now — a past habit)
- I **was reading** when the doorbell rang. (= I was in the middle of reading at that moment)

*Used to* is about repeated or long-lasting past situations; the past continuous is about an action in progress at a specific moment.

## F used to do vs be used to doing

Do not confuse these two structures:

- I **used to work** nights. (= I worked nights in the past, but I don't now.)
- I**'m used to working** nights. (= I work nights and it isn't a problem for me — I'm accustomed to it.)

*Be used to* + **-ing**; *used to* + **infinitive**.

**See also:** Past continuous (I was doing) → Unit 6; would (= used to) → Unit 36; be/get used to (doing something) → Unit 61.

### Question 32

#### Question
Situation: Nina had a motorbike for years, but she sold it last spring and now drives a small car. Which sentence is correct?

#### Answer
"Nina **used to have** a motorbike, but she sold it last spring." *Used to + infinitive* is exactly for a past state or habit that no longer holds — she had a bike, she no longer does. "Was having" is a past continuous, which is wrong for possession and does not convey "not any more". "Uses to have" does not exist — *used to* has no present form. "Is used to having" means she is accustomed to having a motorbike, which is a different structure and meaning.

#### Correct variant
¯\_(ツ)_/¯

2. Nina uses to have a motorbike, but she sold it last spring.
3. Nina used to have a motorbike, but she sold it last spring. (Correct)
4. Nina is used to having a motorbike, but she sold it last spring.

### Question 36

#### Question
Compare: (a) "Marta **used to drive** on the left." (b) "Marta **is used to driving** on the left." Which explanation is correct?

#### Answer
(a) means Marta drove on the left in the past but no longer does — perhaps she moved from the UK to Spain. (b) means she currently drives on the left and it is normal for her — she is accustomed to it, perhaps after moving to the UK. *Used to + infinitive* is a past habit that has stopped; *be used to + -ing* is a present state of being accustomed to something. They are different structures with different meanings, not two forms of the same idea.

#### Correct variant
¯\_(ツ)_/¯

#### Variants
1. Both sentences mean Marta no longer drives on the left; (b) is more formal.
2. (a) is a past habit that has stopped; (b) means she is accustomed to it now. (Correct)
3. (a) means she is accustomed to it now; (b) means she did it in the past.
4. Sentence (b) is ungrammatical because *used to* must be followed by an infinitive.
