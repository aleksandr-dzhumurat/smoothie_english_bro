# Unit 61 — be/get used to … (I’m used to …)

# Theory

## A The idea

Imagine Marta moves from Spain to Norway. At first the long, dark winter afternoons feel strange to her – **she isn’t used to them**. Over the first year they gradually stop bothering her – **she gets used to the darkness**. Today it feels completely normal – **she is used to it**.

So:
- **be used to** = the present state: something is familiar, not new or strange.
- **get used to** = the process of becoming familiar with something.
- **not be used to** = something still feels new or strange.

## B Form

| | be / get used to | + |
|---|---|---|
| I’m / she’s / they were … | used to | **something** (a noun or pronoun) |
| I’ll get / we got … | used to | **doing** something (an -ing form) |

Examples:
- Ravi has worked nights for years. **He’s used to it.** / **He’s used to sleeping during the day.**
- The new glasses felt odd for a day or two because **I wasn’t used to them**.
- Our flat is next to a railway line. We hope **we’ll get used to the trains**, but right now they wake us up.
- Tomas started a job that begins at 6 a.m. He’s struggling because **he isn’t used to getting up so early**.
- You can also put a person before the -ing form: Elena’s son travels a lot and she doesn’t mind – **she’s used to him being away**.

## C -ing, not the infinitive

After **be/get used to**, the word **to** is a **preposition**, so it is followed by a noun, a pronoun or **-ing** – never by the bare infinitive.

- ❌ I’m used to **work** long hours. ✅ I’m used to **working** long hours.
- ❌ She got used to **drive** on the right. ✅ She got used to **driving** on the right.

Compare **to + infinitive**, where **to** is part of the verb pattern, not a preposition: *I don’t want **to work** long hours.* Here *to work* is correct because *want* takes an infinitive.

## D I am used to doing vs I used to do

These look similar but mean completely different things:

| | Meaning | Time | Form |
|---|---|---|---|
| **I am used to (doing)** | it is familiar / not strange for me | present (or past with *was*) | be + used to + noun / -ing |
| **I used to do** | I did it regularly in the past but not now | past only | used to + bare infinitive |

- **I’m used to the cold** here – I’ve lived in Helsinki for years. (familiar now)
- **I used to cycle** to work, but these days I take the tram. (past habit, finished)
- ❌ I’m used to cycle to work. ❌ I use to cycle to work every day now. (*used to do* has no present form)

### Question 28

#### Question
Nadia has just moved from a quiet village to the centre of Bangkok. The traffic noise keeps her awake, but she thinks that in a few months it won’t bother her any more. Which sentence expresses this correctly?

#### Answer
"I expect I’ll **get used to the noise**." *Get used to* describes the process of becoming familiar with something, which is exactly the future change Nadia expects. *Be used to* would describe the present state (it is already normal for her), which is not yet true. *Get used to* must be followed by a noun or an -ing form, so *get used to hear* is wrong, and *used to hear* means a past habit, not a future adjustment.

#### Correct variant

2. I expect I’ll get used to the noise. (Correct)
3. I expect I’ll get used to hear the noise.

### Question 30

#### Question
Which of these sentences contains a mistake, and why?

A. I used to live in Leeds, but I moved to Bristol two years ago.
B. When I first came here, I wasn’t used to the food.
C. My grandmother is used to live alone; she has done so since 1998.
D. It took me ages to get used to working from home.

#### Answer
Sentence **C** is wrong. It describes a present state (living alone is familiar to her), so the structure is *be used to + -ing*: "My grandmother **is used to living** alone." Writing *is used to live* mixes the present *be used to* with the bare infinitive that belongs only to the past-habit structure *used to do*. Sentences A (past habit), B (state in the past with *wasn’t used to* + noun) and D (*get used to* + -ing) are all correct.

#### Correct variant
¯\_(ツ)_/¯

#### Variants
1. Sentence A – *used to live* should be *was used to living*.
2. Sentence B – *wasn’t used to* cannot be followed by a noun.
3. Sentence C – *is used to live* should be *is used to living*. (Correct)
4. Sentence D – *get used to working* should be *get used to work*.

#### Answer
