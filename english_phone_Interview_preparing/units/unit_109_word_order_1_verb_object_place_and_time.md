# Unit 109 — Word order 1: verb + object; place and time

# Theory

## A Verb + object stay together

The verb and its object normally stand next to each other. Do not put an adverb or other phrase between them:

| subject | verb | object | other |
|---|---|---|---|
| Nadia | enjoys | her work | a lot. |
| The waiter | spoke | Italian | beautifully. |
| I | didn't check | my email | this morning. |
| Do you | drink | coffee | every day? |

❌ Nadia enjoys a lot her work. ✅ Nadia enjoys her work a lot.
❌ He speaks fluently Portuguese. ✅ He speaks Portuguese fluently.
❌ I forgot also my keys. ✅ I also forgot my keys.
❌ You'll find on the right a bakery. ✅ You'll find a bakery on the right.

## B Place goes with the verb (after the object)

The verb and the place expression (*where?*) belong together: *drive home, stay in a hotel, cycle to college*.

If there is an object, the order is **verb + object + place**:

| verb | object | place |
|---|---|---|
| We drove | the visitors | to the airport. |
| Please don't leave | your bags | in the corridor. |
| Did you meet | your husband | at university? |

❌ We drove to the airport the visitors. ✅ We drove the visitors to the airport.

## C Time goes after place

Time expressions (*when? how often? how long?*) normally come **after** place:

| | place | time |
|---|---|---|
| Ola cycles | to college | every day. |
| I'm flying | to Lisbon | on Friday. |
| They've worked | in that office | since 2019. |
| We have to be | at the station | by seven. |
| He gave me a lift | home | after the meeting. |
| You shouldn't stay | at the office | so late. |

❌ Ola cycles every day to college. ✅ Ola cycles to college every day.

Time can also go at the **beginning** of the sentence for emphasis or variety:

- On Friday I'm flying to Lisbon.
- After the meeting he gave me a lift home.

Some frequency adverbs (*always, never, usually, often*) go in the middle, next to the verb — see Unit 110.

### Question 33

#### Question
Which sentence has the correct word order?

#### Answer
The correct sentence is *My grandmother speaks Greek very well.* The verb (*speaks*) and its object (*Greek*) must stay together; the adverb phrase *very well* comes after them. *Speaks very well Greek* separates verb and object, which is the most common word-order mistake. *Very well speaks Greek* puts the adverb before the verb, which is not natural for a manner adverb like *well*. *Speaks Greek very good* is wrong for a different reason — it uses the adjective *good* instead of the adverb *well*.

#### Correct variant
¯\_(ツ)_/¯

#### Variants
1. My grandmother speaks very well Greek.
2. My grandmother speaks Greek very well. (Correct)
3. My grandmother very well speaks Greek.
4. My grandmother speaks Greek very good.

### Question 35

#### Question
Spot the mistake: "I lost my wallet on the train, and I lost also my headphones."

#### Answer
The mistake is *lost also my headphones*. *Also* must not come between the verb and its object; it goes before the verb: *I also lost my headphones*. The first part of the sentence, *I lost my wallet on the train*, is correct — verb, object, then place. Putting *also* at the very end (*I lost my headphones also*) is possible but is not the pattern the unit teaches; the point being tested is that nothing separates *lost* from *my headphones*.

#### Correct variant
¯\_(ツ)_/¯

#### Variants
1. *lost my wallet on the train* should be *lost on the train my wallet*.
2. *lost also my headphones* should be *also lost my headphones*. (Correct)
3. *on the train* should come at the beginning: *On the train I lost my wallet*, otherwise the sentence is wrong.
4. There is no mistake; *also* can go anywhere in the sentence.

### Question 37

#### Question
Which two sentences are both correct?
(a) Every Sunday we visit my parents in Bristol.
(b) We visit every Sunday my parents in Bristol.
(c) We visit my parents in Bristol every Sunday.
(d) We visit my parents every Sunday in Bristol.

#### Answer
Sentences (a) and (c) are correct. In (c), the order is verb + object (*visit my parents*), place (*in Bristol*), time (*every Sunday*). In (a), the time expression has moved to the front of the sentence, which is a normal alternative position. Sentence (b) is wrong because the time phrase separates the verb from its object. Sentence (d) is wrong because time is placed before place in the middle of the sentence, which is not the usual order.

#### Correct variant
¯\_(ツ)_/¯

#### Variants
1. (a) and (c) (Correct)
2. (b) and (c)
3. (a) and (d)
4. (c) and (d)
