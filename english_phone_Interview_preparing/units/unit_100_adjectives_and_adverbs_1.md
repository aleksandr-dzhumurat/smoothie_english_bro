# Unit 100 — Adjectives and adverbs 1 (quick/quickly)

# Theory

## A Forming adverbs: adjective + -ly

Most adverbs of manner are made by adding **-ly** to an adjective:

| adjective | slow | careful | quiet | bad | heavy | gentle | terrible |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| adverb | slowly | carefully | quietly | badly | heavily | gently | terribly |

- The lecture ended, and the students left slowly.
- Several passengers were badly hurt when the bus overturned.

(For spelling changes such as *heavy → heavily*, *gentle → gently*, see Appendix 6.)

Not every word ending in **-ly** is an adverb. These are **adjectives**: *friendly, lively, elderly, lonely, lovely, silly, ugly*.

- What a lovely garden! / She's a very friendly host. (adjectives describing nouns)

There is no adverb *friendlily*; instead say *in a friendly way*.

## B Adjective or adverb?

| adjective | adverb |
|---|---|
| describes a **noun** (a person or thing) | describes a **verb** (how something is done or happens) |
| usually goes **before the noun** | usually goes **after the verb** (or verb + object) |
| Ola is a fast runner. ❌ *a fastly runner* | Ola runs fast. |
| The team gave a careful answer. | The team answered carefully. ❌ *answered careful* |
| There was heavy traffic. | The traffic was moving slowly. ❌ *moving slow* |

Compare the two ways of saying the same idea:

- Nadia speaks fluent Spanish. (adjective + noun)
- Nadia speaks Spanish fluently. (verb + object + adverb)

## C Adjectives after be / look / feel / sound etc.

After *be* and after verbs like *look, feel, seem, sound, taste, smell*, we use an **adjective**, because we are describing the subject, not an action:

| adjective (state) | adverb (manner) |
|---|---|
| Please be quiet. | Please close the door quietly. |
| Her exam results were terrible. | She did terribly in the exam. |
| Why do you look so worried? | Why do you never take anything seriously? |
| I feel calm. | The nurse spoke calmly to the patient. |

## D Adverbs before adjectives, other adverbs and past participles

Adverbs can modify **adjectives** and **other adverbs**:

- reasonably priced (adverb + adjective)
- extremely sorry (adverb + adjective)
- remarkably quickly (adverb + adverb)

- It's a reasonably priced hotel and the staff are incredibly helpful.
- I'm extremely sorry — I completely forgot our appointment.
- He picked up the language remarkably quickly.
- The test was surprisingly short.

Adverbs also go before **past participles** (*injured, built, written, organised*):

- Several people were seriously injured. ❌ *serious injured*
- The event was badly organised. / The manual is clearly written.

## Common mistakes

- ❌ *She drives very good.* ✅ She drives very well.
- ❌ *He behaved very rude.* ✅ He behaved very rudely.
- ❌ *I'm terrible sorry.* ✅ I'm terribly sorry.

**See also:** Adjectives after be/look/feel etc. ➜ Unit 99C; Adjectives and adverbs 2 ➜ Unit 101.

### Question 40

#### Question
Choose the correct word: "The instructions were written so ___ that nobody could follow them." (bad / badly)

#### Answer
The correct word is **badly**: "The instructions were written so badly that nobody could follow them." Here we describe *how* the instructions were written — that is, the manner of the action — so an adverb is needed. *Bad* is an adjective and would describe a noun ("bad instructions"). *Worse* and *worst* are comparative and superlative adjective forms; they are not adverbs of manner and do not fit after *so … that*.

#### Correct variant
¯\_(ツ)_/¯

#### Variants
1. bad
2. badly (Correct)
3. worse
4. worst

Which of these sentences is wrong, and why?

#### Answer
The wrong sentence is "There was a **suddenly** change in the weather." *Change* is a noun, so it needs an adjective: "a sudden change". *Suddenly* is an adverb and can only describe a verb ("The weather changed suddenly").
