# Unit 110 — Word order 2: adverbs with the verb

# Theory

## A Mid-position adverbs

Certain adverbs normally go in the **middle** of the sentence, together with the verb. The main ones are: *always, usually, often, never, rarely, hardly ever, also, already, still, even, only, probably, definitely, almost*, and the quantifiers *all* and *both*.

- Rosa **usually** cycles to the office.
- We were cold and we were **also** hungry.
- The flight will **probably** be delayed.

## B One-word verb: adverb BEFORE the verb

| subject | adverb | verb | rest |
|---|---|---|---|
| Rosa | usually | cycles | to the office. |
| I | nearly | missed | the bus. |
| Our neighbours | rarely | speak | to anyone. |

- I washed the dishes and **also** swept the floor. (not *swept also*)
- "Do you need the address?" "No, I **already** know it."

These adverbs go before *have to*: *I **always** have to remind him.* (not *I have always to*)

**Exception — am/is/are/was/were:** the adverb goes **after** the verb *be*:

- She **is always** cheerful in the mornings. (not *always is*)
- The trains **aren't usually** this crowded.
- We **were both** exhausted.

## C Two-word (or longer) verb: adverb AFTER the first part

When the verb has an auxiliary (*can, will, have, doesn't, is …ing*), the adverb goes after the **first** auxiliary:

| verb 1 | adverb | verb 2 |
|---|---|---|
| I can | never | find my glasses. |
| He doesn't | often | eat out. |
| Are you | definitely | coming tomorrow? |
| The road will | probably | be closed. |
| They have | already | left. |

- You've **always** been generous to us.
- Karim can't swim. He can't **even** float.
- Do you **still** live in the same flat?

**Note on probably + negative:** *probably* goes **before** the negative auxiliary: *I **probably won't** be there.* or *I'll **probably not** be there.* (not *I won't probably*)

## D all and both

*All* and *both* take the same mid-position:

- We **all** enjoyed the concert. (not *enjoyed all*)
- My cousins **are both** doctors.
- The guests have **all** arrived.
- Leila and Tom have **both** passed their test.

## E Adverb before a short "echo" verb

When *is/will/did* etc. stands in for a whole phrase (Unit 51), the adverb comes **before** it:

- He promises he'll be on time, but he **never is**. (= he is never on time)
- I haven't tried skiing and I **never will**.
- She says she doesn't mind, but she **always does**.

### Question 42

#### Question
Spot the mistake: "We have usually to submit the report by Friday, but this week we can probably send it on Monday."

#### Answer
The mistake is *have usually to submit*. Mid-position adverbs go **before** *have to*: *We usually have to submit the report by Friday.* The second half is correct — *can* is the first part of a two-word verb, so *probably* correctly follows it: *we can probably send*. The rule "adverb after the first auxiliary" does not apply to *have to*, which behaves like an ordinary main verb here.

#### Correct variant
¯\_(ツ)_/¯

#### Variants
1. *can probably send* should be *probably can send*.
2. *have usually to submit* should be *usually have to submit*. (Correct)
3. Both halves are wrong: *usually have to* and *probably can*.
4. *have usually to submit* should be *have to usually submit*.
