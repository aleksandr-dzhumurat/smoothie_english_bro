# Unit 145 — Phrasal verbs 9 away/back

# Theory

## A away and back as opposites

**Away** means leaving a place, a person or home; **back** means returning to a place, a person or home.

| away = leaving | back = returning |
|---|---|
| **go away** (on holiday, from home) — *They're going away for the weekend.* | **be back / come back / get back** — *We'll be back on Sunday night.* / *What time did you get back?* |
| **drive away / walk away / run away / fly away / look away** — *The taxi dropped us off and drove away.* / *The pigeons flew away when the dog barked.* | **go back / come back / walk back / drive back** — *After the concert we walked back to the hotel.* |
| **blow away** (in the wind) — *My hat blew away on the pier.* | **give** something **back / take** something **back / put** something **back** — *Give me my pen back.* / *These trousers don't fit; I'll take them back to the shop.* / *Put the milk back in the fridge.* |
| **take** something **away** — *The waiter took the plates away.* | |

The same idea applies to any verb of movement: *walk / run / turn / drive* + **away** or **back**.

## B other verbs + away

| Phrasal verb | Meaning | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **get away** | escape, manage to leave | *The burglars got away in a stolen van.* / *I couldn't get away from the office until eight.* |
| **get away with** something | do something wrong and not be caught or punished | *He copied his essay from the internet and got away with it.* |
| **keep away (from …)** | not go near | *Keep away from the dog — it bites.* |
| **give** something **away** | give it to somebody because you no longer want it | *I gave most of my old books away to the library.* |
| **put** something **away** | put it back where it is normally kept | *Put your toys away before dinner.* |
| **throw** something **away** | put it in the rubbish | *Don't throw those jars away — I can reuse them.* |

Contrast **give away** (give something away because you don't want it) with **give back** (return something to its owner).

## C other verbs + back

| Phrasal verb | Meaning | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **wave / smile / shout / hit** somebody **back** | respond with the same action | *He waved at me and I waved back.* / *She hit him, so he hit her back.* |
| **call / phone / ring** somebody **back** | return a phone call | *I'm in a meeting — can I call you back later?* |
| **get back to** somebody | reply to them (by phone, email etc.) | *I've sent two emails, but nobody has got back to me.* |
| **look back (on** something**)** | think about the past | *Looking back on my school days, I realise how lucky I was.* |
| **pay** something **back / pay** somebody **back** | return money you borrowed | *Thanks for the loan — I'll pay you back on Friday.* / *He never paid the money back.* |

**Common mistakes:** ❌ *I'll come back you tomorrow* ✅ *I'll **get back to** you tomorrow*; ❌ *she got away it* ✅ *she got away **with** it*; ❌ *put away it* ✅ *put **it** away*.

**See also:** Phrasal verbs 1 (Introduction) → Unit 137.

## Extra practice

### Question 28

#### Question
Your neighbour has finished with the ladder she borrowed from you and hands it over. Which sentence correctly describes what she does?

#### Answer
The correct sentence is *She gives the ladder back.* **Give** something **back** means to return it to the person it belongs to. *Gives the ladder away* would mean she gives it to somebody as a gift because she doesn't want it — but it isn't hers to give. *Puts the ladder away* means she stores it in its usual place; *takes the ladder back* is used when you return something to a shop, or for taking your own property back from somebody.

#### Correct variant
¯\_(ツ)_/¯

#### Variants
1. She gives the ladder away.
2. She gives the ladder back. (Correct)
3. She puts the ladder away.
4. She takes the ladder back.

### Question 29

#### Question
Which sentence is wrong, and why?

#### Answer
Sentence 3 is wrong: *I left a message, but she never came back me* should be *she never **got back to** me* (or *called me back*). **Get back to** somebody means to reply to them by phone, email etc., and needs **to** before the person; *come back* is a verb of movement and cannot take a person as its object. Sentence 1 correctly uses *call you back*; sentence 2 correctly uses *got away with it*; sentence 4 correctly uses *look back on*.

#### Correct variant
¯\_(ツ)_/¯

2. He lied on his CV and got away with it for years.
3. I left a message, but she never came back me. (Correct)

### Question 30

#### Question
Choose the pair that completes both sentences correctly:
(a) The children were told to _____ from the frozen pond.
(b) The thief jumped over the wall and _____.

#### Answer
The answer is **keep away / got away**. **Keep away (from something)** means not to go near it, so *keep away from the frozen pond*. **Get away** means to escape, so *the thief got away*. *Got away with* requires an object naming the wrongdoing (*got away with the theft*), so *got away with* alone does not fit (b). *Give away* means to give something you no longer want to somebody else, and *go back* means return, which contradicts "jumped over the wall" to escape.

#### Correct variant
¯\_(ツ)_/¯

#### Variants
1. give away / got away with
2. keep away / went back
3. keep away / got away (Correct)
4. get away / gave away

### Question 31

#### Question
You lent your brother £200 last month. Today he hands you £200 and says something. Which is the correct sentence for him to say?

#### Answer
The correct sentence is *Here you are — I'm paying you back.* **Pay** somebody **back** means to return money you borrowed from them (also *pay the money back*). *Paying you away* is not a possible combination. *Giving you away* means handing you over to somebody else (for example at a wedding) — nothing to do with money. *Throwing you back* is not idiomatic in this situation; **throw away** is for rubbish and **throw back** is for objects you throw to someone in return.

#### Correct variant
¯\_(ツ)_/¯

#### Variants
1. Here you are — I'm paying you away.
2. Here you are — I'm giving you away.
3. Here you are — I'm throwing you back.
4. Here you are — I'm paying you back. (Correct)

### Question 32

#### Question
"I'm sorting out the garage. Anything broken I'll _____, and the things that still work but I don't need I'll _____ to the charity shop." Which pair is correct?

#### Answer
The answer is **throw away / give away**. **Throw** something **away** means to put it in the rubbish — right for broken things. **Give** something **away** means to give it to somebody else (here a charity shop) because you no longer want it. *Give back* would mean returning items to their owner, which is not the situation; *put away* means storing things in their usual place, which is the opposite of clearing them out; *take back* means returning something to a shop or reclaiming it.

#### Correct variant
¯\_(ツ)_/¯

#### Variants
1. throw away / give back
2. put away / give away
3. throw away / give away (Correct)
4. throw away / take back
