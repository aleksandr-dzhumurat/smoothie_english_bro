# Unit 48 — Reported speech 2

# Theory

## A — When the verb does not need to change

If what was said is **still true now**, you do not have to move the verb into the past. Both versions are correct:

- Direct: Farid said, 'My commute takes two hours.'
  Reported: Farid said his commute **takes** two hours. (still true) *or* … **took** two hours.
- Direct: Elena said, 'I want to learn to sail next summer.'
  Reported: Elena told me she **wants** to learn to sail next summer. *or* … **wanted** …

But if the situation **has changed or is finished**, you must use a past form:

- Kwame stood up and said, 'I have to catch my train.'
  → Kwame stood up and said he **had to** catch his train. (not *has to* — the moment is over)

## B — When the past is obligatory: what was said is not true

You must also use the past when what was said turns out to be **different from reality**:

- On Monday, Rosa told you: 'Ben has moved to Canada.' On Tuesday you meet Ben in the supermarket. You say:
  *Ben! Rosa said you **had moved** to Canada.* (not *have moved* — he clearly hasn't)

## C — say and tell

| Structure | Example |
|---|---|
| **tell + person** | Rosa **told me** (that) Ben had moved. ❌ *Rosa said me* |
| **say** (no person) | Rosa **said** (that) Ben had moved. ❌ *Rosa told that* |
| **say something to somebody** | What did you **say to** the manager? / He **said goodbye to** everyone. ❌ *He said everyone goodbye* |

Rule of thumb: if you mention **who was spoken to**, use *tell*; otherwise use *say*.

## D — Reporting orders and requests: tell / ask somebody to do

Commands and requests are reported with **tell/ask + person + to-infinitive**. Negative: **not to**.

- 'Take a seat,' the receptionist said to me. → The receptionist **told me to take** a seat.
- 'Don't touch the wires,' I said to the children. → I **told the children not to touch** the wires.
- 'Could you send me the file, please?' Aisha said to Tom. → Aisha **asked Tom to send** her the file.

You can also report advice without a person: **say (not) to do**:

- Dan **said not to** wait for him. (❌ *Dan said me not to wait*)

### Question 27

#### Question
This morning your friend Nikhil said: "I'm allergic to peanuts." You are now telling another friend about it while planning dinner. Which report is **acceptable**?

#### Answer
Both "Nikhil said he **is** allergic to peanuts" and "Nikhil said he **was** allergic to peanuts" are correct. The allergy is a permanent fact that is still true, so the present can be kept; moving it to the past is also fine. "Had been allergic" suggests the allergy is over, which is not the case. "Is being allergic" is not a natural form for a permanent state.

#### Correct variant
¯\_(ツ)_/¯

#### Variants
1. Only *he was allergic* — reported speech must always use the past.
2. Either *he is allergic* or *he was allergic* — the situation is still true, so both work. (Correct)
3. Only *he had been allergic* — present simple always becomes past perfect.
4. Only *he is allergic* — the past is impossible for facts.

### Question 28

#### Question
Last week Ola told you: "Sam is working in Berlin now." Today you unexpectedly meet Sam in your local café. What do you say to Sam?

#### Answer
"Sam! Ola said you **were working** in Berlin." When what was said is clearly **not true** in the present situation — Sam is obviously not in Berlin — the verb must be in the past. Saying "Ola said you are working in Berlin" would present the statement as still true while Sam stands in front of you. "Would work" changes the meaning to a future plan, and "have worked" is a present perfect that doesn't correspond to the original.

#### Correct variant
¯\_(ツ)_/¯

#### Variants
1. Ola said you are working in Berlin.
2. Ola said you would work in Berlin.
3. Ola said you were working in Berlin. (Correct)
4. Ola said you have worked in Berlin.

### Question 29

#### Question
Which sentence uses *say* or *tell* correctly?

#### Answer
"The coach **told us** that training was cancelled." *Tell* is followed directly by the person spoken to. "Said us" is wrong because *say* cannot take a person as its direct object — it would need *said to us*. "Told that" is wrong because *tell* needs a person before *that*. "Said me goodbye" should be *said goodbye to me*: with *say*, the person is introduced by *to*.

#### Correct variant
¯\_(ツ)_/¯

#### Variants
1. The coach said us that training was cancelled.
2. The coach told us that training was cancelled. (Correct)
3. The coach told that training was cancelled.
4. The coach said me goodbye after training.

### Question 30

#### Question
The lifeguard shouted to the children: "Don't run near the pool!" Which is the correct reported version?

#### Answer
"The lifeguard **told the children not to run** near the pool." Commands are reported with *tell + person + to-infinitive*, and a negative command uses *not to*. "Told the children to not run" splits the infinitive awkwardly and isn't the standard pattern taught. "Told the children don't run" keeps the direct imperative, which is not possible after *told … *. "Said the children not to run" is wrong because *say* cannot be followed directly by the person.

#### Correct variant
¯\_(ツ)_/¯

#### Variants
1. The lifeguard told the children don't run near the pool.
2. The lifeguard said the children not to run near the pool.
3. The lifeguard told the children to not run near the pool.
4. The lifeguard told the children not to run near the pool. (Correct)

### Question 31

#### Question
Spot the mistake: "Grace was very busy, so she asked me help her with the invoices, and later she said me thanks."

#### Answer
Two corrections are needed. Requests are reported with *ask + person + **to**-infinitive*: "she asked me **to help** her". And *say* cannot take the person as a direct object: "she **said thanks to me**" or "she **thanked me**". The rest of the sentence is fine. Options that fix only one error leave the sentence incorrect.

#### Correct variant
¯\_(ツ)_/¯

#### Variants
1. Only *asked me help* is wrong; *said me thanks* is fine in spoken English.
2. Both are wrong: it should be *asked me to help her* and *said thanks to me*. (Correct)
3. Only *said me thanks* is wrong; *ask somebody help* is the normal pattern.
4. Nothing is wrong.
