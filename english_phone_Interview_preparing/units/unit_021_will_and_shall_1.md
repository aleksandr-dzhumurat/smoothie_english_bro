# Unit 21 — will and shall 1

# Theory

## A. I'll … for decisions made at the moment of speaking

We use **I'll** (= I will) when we decide to do something *right now*, as we speak. Saying "I'll do it" announces a fresh decision:

- Oh no, the kitchen light is still on. **I'll** go and switch it off.
- "What do you want for dessert?" "**I'll** have the lemon tart, please."
- "Have you emailed the landlord?" "Not yet – **I'll** do it straight away."

The present simple is **not** used for this kind of spontaneous decision:

- ❌ I email him now. ✅ **I'll email** him now.

**I think I'll … / I don't think I'll …** softens the decision:

- It's getting late. I think **I'll** head home.
- I don't think **I'll** have another coffee – I've had three already.

In speech, *will not* is almost always **won't**:

- You look busy, so I **won't** keep you.

## B. Typical uses of I'll

| Use | Example |
|---|---|
| Offering | Your suitcase looks heavy. **I'll** carry it for you. (not *I carry*) |
| Agreeing | "Can you pass this note to Dan?" "Sure, **I'll** give it to him at lunch." |
| Promising | Thanks for the loan. **I'll** pay you back next week. / I **won't** tell a soul, I promise. |
| Requesting: *Will you …?* | **Will you** please keep the noise down? |

**won't** also expresses refusal – by a person or even a thing:

- I keep asking Leo to see a doctor, but he **won't** go.
- The printer **won't** connect to the network. (= it "refuses" to)

## C. Not for things already arranged

Do **not** use *will* for something that was decided or planned *before* the moment of speaking. Use the present continuous or *going to* instead:

- We**'re flying** to Lisbon on Thursday. (not *we'll fly* – it's already booked)

Compare:

- I**'m having** dinner with Rosa tonight. (arranged earlier)
- "**I'll** pick you up at seven, OK?" "Great, see you then." (decided now)

## D. shall I …? / shall we …?

*Shall* is used mostly in questions with **I** and **we**, to offer or to ask for a suggestion:

- **Shall I** carry that for you? (= do you want me to?)
- I've missed the last train. What **shall I** do? (= what do you suggest?)
- "**Shall we** start?" "Give me one more minute."
- "Where **shall we** eat tonight?" "How about the Thai place?"

Contrast **Shall I …?** and **Will you …?**:

- **Shall I** close the window? (= do *you* want *me* to?)
- **Will you** close the window? (= *I* want *you* to.)

### Question 36

#### Question
Compare the two questions: (a) "Shall I open the door?" and (b) "Will you open the door?" Which explanation of the difference is correct?

#### Answer
(a) *Shall I open the door?* is an **offer** – the speaker asks whether the listener wants the *speaker* to do it. (b) *Will you open the door?* is a **request** – the speaker wants the *listener* to do it. Both refer to the future, both are polite, and both are idiomatic; the difference lies in who is expected to act, not in politeness or tense.

#### Correct variant
¯\_(ツ)_/¯

#### Variants
1. (a) is an offer: the speaker asks if the listener wants them to do it. (b) is a request: the speaker wants the listener to do it. (Correct)
2. (a) is a request to the listener; (b) is an offer by the speaker.
3. (a) is old-fashioned and rarely used; (b) is the modern equivalent with the same meaning.
4. (a) refers to the future; (b) refers to the present.
