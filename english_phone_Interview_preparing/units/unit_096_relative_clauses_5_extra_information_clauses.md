# Unit 96 — Relative clauses 5: extra information clauses (2)

# Theory

## A preposition + whom / which

In extra-information clauses, a preposition can come directly before **whom** (people) or **which** (things):

| people | things |
|---|---|
| to whom, with whom, about whom, for whom … | of which, without which, from which, in which … |

- Dr Novak, **to whom** I sent my application, has invited me for an interview.
- We had a spare key, **without which** we would have spent the night outside.
- The village, **in which** fewer than fifty people live, has no shop.

This is a formal style. In spoken English we usually keep the preposition **after the verb** and use *who* (not *whom*):

- Dr Novak, **who** I sent my application **to**, has invited me for an interview. ❌ *Dr Novak, whom I sent my application to*
- Tara mentioned a band called 'Grey Harbour', **which** I'd never heard **of**.

Rule: *whom* only when the preposition is directly in front of it. For prepositions in relative clauses, see also Unit 93C.

## B all of / most of / none of … + whom / which

You can use quantity expressions + *of whom* (people) / *of which* (things) to comment on part or all of a group:

| for people | for things |
|---|---|
| all of whom, most of whom, many of whom, some of whom, none of whom, neither of whom, both of whom, one of whom | all of which, most of which, none of which, both of which, one of which, two of which, half of which |

- Sofia has four cousins, **all of whom** live in Argentina.
- The lecturer set ten problems, **most of which** nobody could solve.
- I tried on several coats, **none of which** was warm enough.
- Two women, **neither of whom** I recognised, were waiting at the door.
- They own three properties, **one of which** they rent out.

Similarly with a noun + *of which*: *the cause of which, the name of which, the result of which*:

- The bridge collapsed, **the cause of which** is still being investigated.
- She quoted a poem, **the title of which** I can't recall.

## C which (not what) referring to a whole sentence

*Which* can refer back to the **whole** of the previous clause, i.e. to a fact or situation:

- Two sentences: Anna passed her driving test at the first attempt. This amazed her family.
- One sentence: Anna passed her driving test at the first attempt, **which** amazed her family.

Here *which* = "the fact that Anna passed at the first attempt". Use *which*, never *what*, for this:

- The hotel had no hot water, which was very annoying. ❌ *what was very annoying*
- Our flight left on time, which we hadn't expected. ❌ *what we hadn't expected*

For *what* and *that*, see Units 92D and 93D.

#### Question
Complete the sentence in a formal style: "The conference, ___ over 300 delegates were invited, was held in Geneva."

#### Answer
The correct answer is "**to which** over 300 delegates were invited". The delegates were invited *to the conference*, so the preposition *to* is needed, and since the conference is a thing, the pronoun is *which*. *To whom* is wrong because *whom* is for people. *Which … to* with the preposition at the end is possible in informal speech ("which over 300 delegates were invited to"), but option 3 puts *which* at the front and *to* nowhere, so it is incomplete. *Whom* alone lacks the preposition and refers to people.

#### Correct variant

2. to which (Correct)

#### Question
Rewrite as one sentence: "Marta sent out twenty invitations. Only three of them were answered." Which version is correct?

#### Answer
The correct sentence is "Marta sent out twenty invitations, **only three of which** were answered." For things we use *of which*; for people we would use *of whom*. Option 1 wrongly uses *of whom* for invitations. Option 2 uses *of them*, which is fine in two separate sentences but cannot join the clauses into one sentence with a comma — it creates a run-on. ### Question 32

#### Question
A friend says: "The concert was cancelled at the last minute, ___ was a real disappointment." What goes in the gap, and why?

#### Answer
The correct word is **which**: "The concert was cancelled at the last minute, which was a real disappointment." Here *which* refers back to the whole preceding clause — the fact that the concert was cancelled. *What* cannot be used in this way; *what* means "the thing that" and cannot refer back to a previous statement. *That* is not possible in an extra-information clause after a comma. *It* would need a full new sentence or a conjunction (… and it was a real disappointment).

2. that
3. which (Correct)
4. it
