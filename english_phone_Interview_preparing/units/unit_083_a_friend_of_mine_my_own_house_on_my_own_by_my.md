# Unit 83 — a friend of mine / my own house / on my own / by myself

# Theory

## A a friend of mine / a friend of yours etc.

To say "one of my friends" in a compact way we use **a friend of + possessive pronoun** (mine / yours / his / hers / ours / theirs):

- A cousin of mine has just moved to Lisbon. (❌ a cousin of me)
- We stayed with some friends of ours in Cork. (❌ friends of us)
- Leo had a row with a colleague of his.
- That was a clever suggestion of yours.

The same pattern works with **-’s**: *a friend of my brother’s*, *an idea of Karen’s*:
- The man at the bar is a friend of my brother’s. (= one of my brother’s friends)
- It was a good idea of Karen’s to book early.

## B my own / your own etc.

**own** always follows a possessive (my, your, her, its, our, their, Tom’s). We do not say *an own car* or *the own room*.

| ✅ | ❌ |
|---|---|
| my own flat | an own flat |
| her own desk | the own desk |
| their own business | an own business |

my own … = belonging only to me, not shared or borrowed:
- I’m tired of sharing. I want my own bathroom.
- Nadia and Femi are saving up for their own place.
- The office doesn’t have its own car park.
- It’s his own fault he missed the flight – he left too late.
- Stop borrowing my charger. Use your own.

An alternative order is **a … of my own**: *a room of my own*, *a car of your own*, *worries of her own*:
- I’d love a garden of my own.
- Don’t ask Sam for help – he has enough troubles of his own.

## C He cuts his own hair (= does it himself)

**own** also stresses that we do something ourselves instead of paying or asking somebody else:
- Marta bakes her own bread. (= she bakes it herself)
- I’d like to grow my own tomatoes rather than buy them.
- Do you iron your own shirts?

## D on my own / by myself (= alone)

Both structures mean "alone" and are interchangeable:

| on + possessive + own | by + reflexive |
|---|---|
| on my own | by myself |
| on your own | by yourself / by yourselves |
| on his / her / its own | by himself / herself / itself |
| on our / their own | by ourselves / themselves |

- Zoe prefers travelling on her own. / … by herself.
- The old man sat on his own at the back. / … by himself.
- Did you decorate the flat on your own? / … by yourself?

Do not mix the two patterns: ❌ *by my own*, ❌ *on myself*.

**See also:** Unit 82 (myself/yourself/themselves etc.).
