# Unit 6 — Past continuous (I was doing)

# Theory

## A Form

Imagine Lucas and Mei rehearsing with their band. They began at 7 o'clock and stopped at 9. So at 8 o'clock **they were rehearsing** — they were in the middle of it and had not finished.

The past continuous is **was/were + -ing**:

| Subject | was/were | -ing form |
|---|---|---|
| I / he / she / it | was | reading |
| we / you / they | were | driving |

Negative: *wasn't / weren't + -ing*. Question: *Was she reading? What were you doing?*

## B Meaning: in the middle of an action in the past

**I was doing something** = at a certain past time I was in the middle of doing it. The action started before that moment and had not finished at that moment.

- This time last year I *was working* in Buenos Aires.
- What *were you doing* at midnight on New Year's Eve?
- I called her name, but she *wasn't listening*.

## C Past continuous vs. past simple

| I was doing (in the middle of an action) | I did (complete action) |
|---|---|
| We *were cycling* home when the storm broke. (in the middle of the journey) | We *cycled* home after the concert. (all the way, finished) |
| Sam *was cooking* when the guests arrived. | Sam *cooked* for the whole family every Sunday last year. |

## D One thing in the middle of another

A **past simple** event often interrupts or happens during a **past continuous** background action:

- My phone *rang* while I *was giving* the presentation.
- It *was snowing* when we *left* the house.
- I *saw* you yesterday. You *were standing* outside the bank and *talking* to someone.
- She *twisted* her ankle while she *was jogging*.

But when one thing happens **after** another, both are past simple:

- I *was crossing* the square when I *spotted* Ana. So I *waved*, and we *chatted* for a few minutes.

Compare:

- When Leo *arrived*, we *were eating* dinner. (= dinner had already started before he came)
- When Leo *arrived*, we *ate* dinner. (= he came, and then we started dinner)

## E Verbs not used in the continuous

State verbs such as *know, want, like, believe, belong* (see Unit 4A) are not normally used in continuous forms, including the past continuous:

- We *knew* each other well. (not we were knowing)
- I *was enjoying* the film, but Diego *wanted* to leave. (not was wanting)

### Question 30

#### Question
Which of these two sentences means that the guests arrived first and the meal began afterwards? (a) "When the guests arrived, we had lunch." (b) "When the guests arrived, we were having lunch."

#### Answer
Sentence (a), "When the guests arrived, we had lunch", means the guests arrived and *then* lunch began — two past simple verbs describe one action after another. Sentence (b), with the past continuous "were having", means lunch was already in progress when the guests came in. The two sentences are not interchangeable, and neither is ungrammatical; they simply describe different sequences of events.

#### Correct variant
¯\_(ツ)_/¯

#### Variants
1. Sentence (b) — the past continuous shows the arrival came first.
2. Sentence (a) — two past simple verbs show one action followed the other. (Correct)
3. Both sentences mean the same thing.
4. Neither — sentence (a) is grammatically wrong.
