# Unit 125 — in/at/on (position) 3

# Theory

## A in bed / in hospital; at work / at school

| in | at |
|---|---|
| in bed, in hospital, in prison | at home, at work, at school, at university, at college |

- *Grandpa is still in bed; he's not feeling well.*
- *Her uncle spent three months in hospital after the crash.*
- *I'll be at work until six.* / *Both my cousins are at university.*

**Home**: *be home* or *be at home* are both fine, but when you *do* something there you need *at*:
- *I'll be (at) home tonight.* / *Do you prefer to cook at home or eat out?*

## B at an event

We are **at** a party, a meeting, a wedding, a conference, a concert, a football match:
- *Were there many guests at the wedding?*
- *I bumped into my old boss at a conference last week.*

## C in or at with buildings

Often both work: you can have coffee *in* a café or *at* a café; buy stamps *in* or *at* the post office.

Use **at** when you mean the place where an event or activity happens:
- *The reception is at the Grand Hotel.*
- *The talks were held at the ministry's headquarters in Geneva.*
- *There was a break-in at the chemist's.*

Use **at** with **somebody's house** and with shops/services named after a person: *at Maria's (house), at the dentist's, at the hairdresser's, at the butcher's*.

Use **in** when you are thinking about the interior of the building itself:
- *I was at Maria's last night.* (I visited her) / *It's freezing in Maria's house; the boiler is broken.* (the inside of the building)
- *We had lunch at the hotel.* / *Every room in the hotel has a sea view.* (not ~~at the hotel~~)

Fixed: **at the station / at the airport**: *I'll pick you up at the airport.*

## D in or at with towns

Normally **in** with cities, towns, villages and countries: *The Prado is in Madrid.* (not ~~at Madrid~~) / *They rent a cottage in a village in Wales.*

Use **at** only when the town is a point or stop on a journey: *Does the express stop at Reading?* (= at Reading station)

## E on a bus / in a car

| on | in |
|---|---|
| on a bus, on a train, on a plane, on a ship | in a car, in a taxi |
| on a bike, on a motorbike, on a horse | |

- *There were no free seats on the train.*
- *We came in a taxi.* / *He arrived on his bike.*

**See also:** at school / in hospital etc. → Unit 74; in/at/on (position) → Units 123–124; to/at/in/into → Unit 126; by car / by bike etc. → Unit 128B.

### Question 34

#### Question
Which sentence correctly contrasts *at* and *in* with the same building?

#### Answer
**"We had dinner at the Royal Hotel; the food was good, but it was noisy in the restaurant."** *At* is used to say where the event took place (dinner at the hotel); *in* is used when we think of the interior of the building (noise inside the restaurant). The reversed version treats the interior with *at*, which the book marks as incorrect.

#### Correct variant
¯\_(ツ)_/¯

#### Variants
1. We had dinner in the Royal Hotel; the food was good, but it was noisy at the restaurant.
2. We had dinner at the Royal Hotel; the food was good, but it was noisy in the restaurant. (Correct)
3. We had dinner on the Royal Hotel; the food was good, but it was noisy in the restaurant.
4. We had dinner at the Royal Hotel; the food was good, but it was noisy at the restaurant.

### Question 35

#### Question
One of these is wrong. Which, and why?

#### Answer
**"The Colosseum is at Rome" is wrong; it should be "in Rome".** Cities, towns and villages take *in*. *At* is used with a town only when we see it as a stop on a journey (*the train stops at Rome*). The other sentences are correct: *at the station*, *at a conference*, *at Tom's*.

#### Correct variant
¯\_(ツ)_/¯

#### Variants
1. "Wait for me at the station" — should be *in the station*.
2. "I met her at a conference" — should be *in a conference*.
3. "The Colosseum is at Rome" — should be *in Rome*. (Correct)
4. "We stayed at Tom's last night" — should be *in Tom's*.

### Question 37

#### Question
Which option is NOT acceptable English?

#### Answer
**"I'll stay home and cook at home" is fine, but "I'll cook home tonight" is not acceptable.** *Be home* and *be at home* are both possible, but when you *do* an activity there you need *at*: *cook at home, work at home, eat at home*. The other three sentences are correct.

#### Correct variant
¯\_(ツ)_/¯

#### Variants
1. I'll be home all evening.
2. I'll be at home all evening.
3. I'd rather eat at home tonight.
4. I'll cook home tonight. (Correct)
