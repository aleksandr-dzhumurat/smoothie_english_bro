# Unit 79 — Singular and plural

# Theory

## A Plural nouns for one thing with two parts

Some things made of two parts are named by a **plural** noun:

| clothes | other items |
|---|---|
| trousers, jeans, shorts, tights, leggings, pants, pyjamas | glasses, sunglasses, binoculars, scissors, headphones, tweezers |

They take a **plural verb**: *These shorts are too tight.* ❌ *These shorts is*

To count them, use **a pair of**: *That's a nice pair of trousers.* ❌ *a nice trousers*. Both forms are fine: *Those are lovely sunglasses.* = *That's a lovely pair of sunglasses.* / *I need some new headphones.* = *I need a new pair of headphones.*

## B Nouns ending in -s that are singular

Subjects and activities ending in **-ics** are usually **singular**: *athletics, gymnastics, economics, politics, physics, electronics, mathematics/maths, statistics*.

- Economics was my hardest subject. ❌ *Economics were*

**news** is singular: *The news is on at ten.* / *That's great news!*

Some words look the same in singular and plural:

| | singular | plural |
|---|---|---|
| means | a means of payment | several means of payment |
| series | a new drama series | three series of the show |
| species | an endangered species | thousands of species of beetle |

## C Group nouns: singular or plural verb

Nouns for **groups of people** — *audience, committee, company, family, firm, government, staff, team, class, crew* — can take a **plural** verb when we think of the members as *they*, or a **singular** verb when we think of the group as one unit (*it*):

- The committee have rejected the proposal. / The committee has rejected the proposal.
- Our team are training hard this week. / Our team is top of the league.

Company names and sports teams behave the same way: *Toyota have recalled 200,000 cars.* / *Argentina are playing France on Saturday.*

**police** always takes a **plural** verb: *The police have closed the road.* ❌ *The police has*. For one person: *a police officer / a policeman / a policewoman*. ❌ *a police*

## D person → people

The normal plural of **person** is **people** (a plural noun): *She's an interesting person.* → *They're interesting people.* ❌ *interesting persons*

*People* takes a plural verb: *Most people agree.* ❌ *Most people agrees*

## E Sums, periods, distances → singular verb

A **sum of money, a length of time, a distance, a weight** is thought of as **one amount** and takes a **singular verb**:

- Two hundred euros is too much for that jacket. ❌ *are too much*
- Six months is a long time to wait for an appointment.
- Ten kilometres isn't far on a bike.
- Forty kilos is the maximum luggage allowance.

### Question 45

#### Question
Which explanation of these two sentences is correct?

A. The government has announced new taxes.
B. The government have announced new taxes.

#### Answer
Both sentences are acceptable in British English. *Government* is a **group noun** (like *team, staff, committee, family*): with a **singular** verb we think of the group as one unit (*it*); with a **plural** verb we think of the people in it (*they*). Neither is a mistake. Option 1 and option 2 wrongly declare one form incorrect; option 4 confuses group nouns with *police*, which always takes a plural verb.

#### Correct variant
¯\_(ツ)_/¯

#### Variants
1. Only A is correct; *government* is singular and must take *has*.
2. Only B is correct; *government* is a plural noun like *police*.
3. Both are correct: group nouns take a singular verb when seen as a unit and a plural verb when seen as a number of people. (Correct)
4. Both are correct because *government*, like *police*, can never be wrong with either verb.
