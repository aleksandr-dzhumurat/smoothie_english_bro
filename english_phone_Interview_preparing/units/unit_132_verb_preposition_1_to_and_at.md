# Unit 132 — Verb + preposition 1: to and at

# Theory

## A Verb + to

Several verbs of communication need **to** before the person:

| Verb + TO | Example |
|---|---|
| talk / speak TO somebody (also *with*) | Who was Marta speaking to on the phone? |
| listen TO … | I usually listen to podcasts on the train. (not ~~listen the podcasts~~) |
| apologise TO somebody (for …) | The waiter apologised to us for the delay. (not ~~apologised us~~) |
| explain something TO somebody | Could you explain the rules to me? (not ~~explain me the rules~~) |
| explain / describe (to somebody) what / how / why … | She explained to the class how the machine works. |

The most common mistake is putting the person straight after *explain* or *describe*: ❌ *Describe me the house.* ✅ *Describe the house to me.* / *Describe to me what the house looks like.*

## B Verbs with NO preposition before the person

Some verbs which look similar take the object **directly**:

| Verb (no *to*) | Example |
|---|---|
| phone / call / email / text somebody | I texted Ravi to say I'd be late. (not ~~texted to Ravi~~) |
| answer somebody / something | Nobody answered the phone. (not ~~answered to the phone~~) |
| ask somebody (a question) | Ask the receptionist where the lifts are. (not ~~ask to the receptionist~~) |
| thank somebody (for …) | We thanked our hosts for a lovely evening. (not ~~thanked to our hosts~~) |

Two useful exceptions in the same area:
- **write TO somebody** (a letter) — *I wrote to the council about the noise.*
- **reply TO an email / a letter** — *She never replied to my message.* (but *answer my message*, no preposition)

## C Verb + at

*At* is used with verbs of looking and with aiming/directing something towards a target:

| Verb + AT | Example |
|---|---|
| look / stare / glance AT; have a look / take a look AT | The children stared at the parrot in amazement. |
| laugh AT … | Don't laugh at his accent — he's trying hard. |
| aim / point (something) AT; shoot / fire (a gun) AT | The hunter fired at the deer but missed. |

## D at vs to — a change of meaning

With some verbs both prepositions are possible, but the meaning is different:

| AT (aggression / target) | TO (direction / cooperation) |
|---|---|
| shout **at** somebody = angrily | shout **to** somebody = so they can hear you |
| *The coach shouted at the players for losing.* | *I shouted to Dan across the car park to wait for me.* |
| throw something **at** somebody = to hit them | throw something **to** somebody = for them to catch |
| *A protester threw a tomato at the mayor.* | *Throw the ball to me and I'll catch it.* |

## Extra practice

### Question 33

#### Question
A new colleague doesn't understand the coffee machine. Which sentence is grammatically correct?

#### Answer
The correct sentence is "Can you **explain to me** how the coffee machine works?" With *explain* the person always comes after *to*: *explain something to somebody* or *explain to somebody what/how …*. Putting the person directly after *explain* ("explain me") is a very common error, as is dropping the preposition entirely. Option 4 also uses "explain me", so only option 2 is right.

#### Correct variant
¯\_(ツ)_/¯

#### Variants
1. Can you explain me how the coffee machine works?
2. Can you explain to me how the coffee machine works? (Correct)
3. Can you explain me the coffee machine?
4. Can you explain me, please, how does the coffee machine work?

### Question 34

#### Question
Which of these sentences is wrong, and why?

#### Answer
The wrong sentence is "I phoned to the hotel to change our booking." *Phone / call / email / text somebody* take a direct object with no preposition: "I **phoned the hotel**." The other three sentences are correct: *reply TO an email* needs *to*, *ask somebody* has no preposition, and *write TO somebody* needs *to*.

### Question 35

#### Question
Fill the gap: "Everybody will ______ me if I wear this ridiculous hat to the party."

#### Answer
The correct answer is "laugh **at** me." *Laugh at* somebody/something is the fixed collocation for making fun of or finding something funny. "Laugh to", "laugh on" and "laugh about me" are not used in this sense — *laugh about* can only be used for a situation or event ("we laughed about the mix-up later"), not for a person being ridiculed.

#### Correct variant
¯\_(ツ)_/¯

#### Variants
1. laugh to
2. laugh on
3. laugh at (Correct)
4. laugh with

### Question 36

#### Question
Two situations: (a) Maya was on the far side of a noisy road and wanted her friend to hear her. (b) The manager was furious with his assistant. Which sentence pair uses *shout* correctly?

#### Answer
The correct pair is "(a) Maya shouted **to** her friend across the road. (b) The manager shouted **at** his assistant." *Shout to* somebody means you raise your voice so they can hear you (distance, no anger). *Shout at* somebody means you are angry or aggressive with them. Options that swap the prepositions reverse the meanings, and "shout on" is not English.

#### Correct variant
¯\_(ツ)_/¯

#### Variants
1. (a) Maya shouted at her friend. (b) The manager shouted to his assistant.
2. (a) Maya shouted on her friend. (b) The manager shouted at his assistant.
3. (a) Maya shouted at her friend. (b) The manager shouted at his assistant.
4. (a) Maya shouted to her friend. (b) The manager shouted at his assistant. (Correct)

### Question 37

#### Question
"Catch!" said Ben, and he ______ the car keys ______ his sister from the balcony. Choose the right verb + preposition.

#### Answer
The correct answer is "he **threw** the car keys **to** his sister." *Throw something TO somebody* means you throw it for them to catch — which fits "Catch!". *Throw something AT somebody* means you are trying to hit them, which would be aggressive and does not fit the situation. "Throw for" and "throw towards … for catching" are not the standard collocations here.

#### Correct variant
¯\_(ツ)_/¯

2. threw … to (Correct)
