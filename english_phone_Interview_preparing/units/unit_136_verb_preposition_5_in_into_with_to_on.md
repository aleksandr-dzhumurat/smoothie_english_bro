# Unit 136 — Verb + preposition 5: in/into/with/to/on

# Theory

## A Verb + in

| Verb + IN | Meaning / example |
|---|---|
| believe IN … | believe that something exists, or that it is a good principle: *Do you believe in luck?* / *I believe in getting up early.* |
| specialise IN … | *Dr Okafor specialises in tropical diseases.* |
| succeed IN … (+ -ing) | *After months of trying, she succeeded in passing her driving test.* |

Contrast: **believe something / somebody** (no preposition) = accept it as true:
*I don't believe a word he says.* (not ~~believe in a word~~). *Believe in* is about existence or values, not about whether a statement is true.

## B Verb + into

| Verb + INTO | Example |
|---|---|
| break INTO … | Thieves broke into the warehouse last night. |
| crash / drive / bump / run INTO … | A lorry ran into the back of the bus. |
| divide / cut / split something INTO parts | Cut the cake into eight pieces. / The course is split into four modules. |
| translate FROM one language INTO another | The novel was translated from Norwegian into English. |

## C Verb + with

| Verb + WITH | Example |
|---|---|
| collide WITH … | Two trams collided with each other at the junction. |
| fill something WITH … | She filled the vase with fresh water. (but *full OF* — Unit 131A) |
| provide / supply somebody WITH … | The hotel provides every guest with a bathrobe. |

## D Verb + to

| Verb + TO | Example |
|---|---|
| happen TO … | Whatever happened to your old motorbike? |
| invite somebody TO a party / a wedding | We've been invited to a barbecue on Sunday. |
| prefer one thing TO another | Most of my friends prefer the cinema to the theatre. (not ~~prefer … than~~) |

## E Verb + on

| Verb + ON | Example |
|---|---|
| concentrate ON … | I can't concentrate on my reading with that music playing. |
| insist ON … (+ -ing) | Grandma insisted on paying for everyone. |
| spend (money / time) ON … | He spends a fortune on trainers. |

## F Common traps

| ❌ | ✅ |
|---|---|
| I don't believe in that story. | I don't believe that story. |
| prefer coffee than tea | prefer coffee **to** tea |
| fill the glass of juice | fill the glass **with** juice |
| divide in two groups | divide **into** two groups |
| spend money for clothes | spend money **on** clothes |
| translate to French | translate **into** French |

**See also:** Verb + preposition + -ing → Unit 62; other verbs + to → Unit 132; other verbs + on → Unit 135D.

### Question 38

#### Question
Compare two sentences: (a) "The story sounds made up. I don't ______ it." (b) "Marta doesn't ______ ghosts or fortune-telling." Which options fit (a) and (b)?

#### Answer
The correct pair is (a) **believe** it, (b) **believe in** ghosts. *Believe something* (no preposition) = accept that it is true. *Believe in something* = believe that it exists or that it is a good idea. Using *believe in* for the story or plain *believe* for ghosts changes the meaning or is ungrammatical.

#### Correct variant
¯\_(ツ)_/¯

#### Variants
1. (a) believe in, (b) believe
2. (a) believe, (b) believe in (Correct)
3. (a) believe in, (b) believe in
4. (a) believe, (b) believe

### Question 39

#### Question
Which sentence is wrong?

#### Answer
The wrong sentence is "I definitely prefer cycling than driving in the city." *Prefer one thing TO another*: "prefer cycling **to** driving." *Than* is used with comparatives ("better than"), not with *prefer*. The other sentences correctly use *collide with*, *specialise in* and *break into*.

#### Correct variant
¯\_(ツ)_/¯

#### Variants
1. The ferry collided with a fishing boat in the fog.
2. Our firm specialises in renewable energy projects.
3. I definitely prefer cycling than driving in the city. (Correct)
4. Somebody broke into the garage and took the bikes.

### Question 40

#### Question
Rewrite: "The school gave every pupil a laptop." Which sentence keeps the meaning using *provide*?

#### Answer
The correct sentence is "The school **provided every pupil with** a laptop." The pattern is *provide/supply somebody WITH something*. Option 1 wrongly uses *by*, option 3 puts the thing first without *with*, and option 4 uses *to*. (Note: *provide something FOR somebody* also exists, but it is not among the options here.)

#### Correct variant
¯\_(ツ)_/¯

#### Variants
1. The school provided every pupil by a laptop.
2. The school provided every pupil with a laptop. (Correct)
3. The school provided a laptop every pupil.
4. The school provided every pupil to a laptop.

### Question 41

#### Question
"The teacher ______ the class ______ three groups and gave each one a poster to make." Fill both gaps.

#### Answer
The correct sentence is "The teacher **divided** the class **into** three groups." *Divide / split / cut something INTO parts* — the preposition is always *into*, not *in* or *to*. "Divided … in three groups" is a common error influenced by other languages; "divided … on" is not English.

#### Correct variant
¯\_(ツ)_/¯

#### Variants
1. divided … in
2. divided … to
3. divided … on
4. divided … into (Correct)

### Question 42

#### Question
"I wanted to take the bus, but Aunt Fatima ______ driving me to the airport herself, and I couldn't ______ the road because I was so nervous." Which pair of verb + preposition completions is correct?

#### Answer
The correct completion is "**insisted on** driving me … I couldn't **concentrate on** the road." Both *insist* and *concentrate* take *on*. *Insist on + -ing* is the fixed pattern (not *insist to drive* or *insist for*). *Concentrate in/at* are learner errors; *concentrate on* is the only correct form.

#### Correct variant
¯\_(ツ)_/¯

#### Variants
1. insisted on … concentrate on (Correct)
2. insisted to … concentrate in
3. insisted for … concentrate at
4. insisted on … concentrate in
