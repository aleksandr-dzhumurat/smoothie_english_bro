# Unit 30 — may and might 2

# Theory

## A. may / might for possible future actions

We use **may** and **might** to say that a future action or event is **possible** (but not certain):

- I'm not sure what to do this weekend. I **may drive** to the coast. (= perhaps I will)
- Bring a jacket – it **might get** cold later. (= perhaps it will get cold)
- The doctor is running late. We **might have to** wait a while. (= perhaps we will have to)

Negatives: **may not** and **might not** (**mightn't**):

- Karim **may not come** tonight – he's got a cold. (= perhaps he won't come)
- There **might not be** any seats left by the time we arrive.

Compare certainty and possibility:

| Certain | Possible |
|---|---|
| I**'m going to** change jobs. | I **may / might** change jobs. |
| She**'s coming** on Sunday. | She **might come** on Sunday. |

## B. may or might? — might for unreal situations

Usually **either** is possible:

- I **may** take the train. / I **might** take the train.
- Elif **may be able to** help. / Elif **might be able to** help.

But use **might** (not *may*) when the situation is **unreal / hypothetical**:

- If I had more free time, I **might** take up painting. (not *I may take up*)

The situation (*if I had more free time*) isn't real – I don't have more free time – so *might* is required.

## C. may / might be -ing

Compare *will be -ing* (certain) and *may/might be -ing* (possible):

- Don't ring at nine. I**'ll be putting** the kids to bed. (= I definitely will)
- Don't ring at nine. I **might be putting** the kids to bed. (= perhaps I will be)

*May/might be -ing* is also used for **possible plans**, parallel to the present continuous for definite plans:

- I**'m going** to Oslo next month. (definite arrangement)
- I **might be going** to Oslo next month. (possible arrangement)

## D. might as well / may as well

*We might as well do something* = there is **no better alternative**, so we should do it; there is **no reason not to**:

- Two friends miss the last tram; the walk home is 25 minutes. "Shall we walk?" "We **might as well**. Waiting for a taxi will take longer."
- I've finished everything, so I **might as well** leave early. (or **may as well**)
- Parking here costs £20 – you **might as well** take a cab. (= a cab is as good and no more expensive)

Common errors:

- ❌ If I were you, I may ask for a refund. ✅ … I **might** ask for a refund. (unreal situation → *might*)
- ❌ It might rains. ✅ It **might rain**. (bare infinitive after a modal)
- ❌ We might as well to wait. ✅ We **might as well wait**.

**See also:** will be -ing → Unit 24; may/might 1 → Unit 29; may I …? → Unit 37; might with if → Units 38C, 40D.

### Question 31

#### Question
Compare: (a) "Don't text me at ten – I'll be driving." (b) "Don't text me at ten – I might be driving." Which explanation is correct?

#### Answer
(a) *will be driving* states with **certainty** that the speaker will be in the middle of driving at ten. (b) *might be driving* says it is only **possible** that the speaker will be driving then. Both are future continuous ideas; the difference is degree of certainty. (b) is not about the past, and (a) is not more polite than (b).

#### Correct variant
¯\_(ツ)_/¯

#### Variants
1. (a) is certain about what the speaker will be doing; (b) says it is only possible. (Correct)
2. (a) is about the future; (b) is about the past.
3. (b) is wrong – *might* cannot be followed by *be + -ing*.
4. (a) is polite; (b) is rude; otherwise they mean the same.
