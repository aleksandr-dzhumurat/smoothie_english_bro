# Unit 14 — Present perfect and past 2 (I have done and I did)

# Theory

## A Finished time → past simple

If the sentence names a **finished time** — *yesterday, last month, in 2012, two hours ago, when I was a student* — the present perfect is impossible. Use a past tense.

- The traffic **was** terrible yesterday. ❌ (not *has been*)
- Nadia and Sam **landed** twenty minutes ago. ❌ (not *have landed*)
- **Did** you **have** a pet when you were little? ❌ (not *have you had*)
- I **got** back late last night, **had** a quick shower and **fell** asleep.

Questions with **When … ?** and **What time … ?** ask about a point in the past, so they take the past simple too:

- **When did** the parcel **arrive**? ❌ (not *has … arrived*)
- **What time did** the meeting **end**?

| Present perfect | Past simple |
|---|---|
| Jo **has lost** her glasses. She can't read the menu. | Jo **lost** her glasses yesterday. She couldn't read the menu. |
| Is Eric still here, or **has** he **left**? | **When did** Eric **leave**? |

## B Unfinished period vs finished period

The present perfect goes with a period that **continues up to now** (*today, this week, this year, since 2020, recently, ever/never*). The past simple goes with a period that is **over** (*yesterday, last week, from 2015 to 2019*).

| Present perfect — period still open | Past simple — period closed |
|---|---|
| I**'ve made** three calls this morning. (it is still morning) | I **made** three calls this morning. (it is now afternoon) |
| It **hasn't snowed** this winter. | It **didn't snow** last winter. |
| **Have** you **spoken** to Kai recently? | **Did** you **speak** to Kai on Friday? |
| I**'ve taught** here since 2020. (I still do) | I **taught** there from 2015 to 2019. (I don't now) |
| We**'ve been queuing** for forty minutes. (still queuing) | We **queued** for forty minutes. (queuing is over) |
| Ola lives in Cork. She**'s lived** there for six years. | Ola **lived** in Galway for six years. Now she lives in Cork. |
| I**'ve never tried** sushi. (in my life so far) | I **never ate** fish as a child. |

Two more contrasts:

- "I don't know where Dan is. I **haven't seen** him." (= not recently)
  vs. "Was Dan at the match on Saturday?" "I don't think so. I **didn't see** him."
- On the last evening of a trip: "It**'s been** a wonderful trip. I**'ve loved** every minute."
  After you get home: "It **was** a wonderful trip. I **loved** every minute."

The key question is always: *is the time period I'm talking about finished or not?*

#### Answer
Sentence 3 is wrong: "Elena **has moved** to Prague **in 2021**" should be "Elena **moved** to Prague in 2021." *In 2021* is a finished time, and the present perfect cannot be used with a finished time expression. ### Question 45

#### Question
You want to know the exact time your colleague got to the office. Which question is correct?

#### Answer
"**What time did** you **get** here?" Questions with *when* and *what time* ask about a specific point in the past, so they always take the past simple, even if the person arrived only minutes ago. "What time have you got here?" wrongly combines the present perfect with a question about a point in time. "What time you got here?" is missing the auxiliary *did*. "What time have you been getting here?" describes a repeated habit, not a single arrival.

#### Correct variant

#### Variants
1. What time have you got here?
2. What time did you get here? (Correct)
