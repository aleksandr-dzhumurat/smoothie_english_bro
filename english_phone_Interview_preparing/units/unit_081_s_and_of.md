# Unit 81 — -’s (your sister’s name) and of … (the name of the book)

# Theory

## A -’s for people and animals

The possessive ending **-’s** (apostrophe + s) is used mainly with people and animals:

- Maria’s laptop has crashed again. (not the laptop of Maria)
- How old are Patrick’s daughters?
- Mind the dog’s paws when you close the door.

You can chain possessives: **my brother’s wife’s job**.

-’s can stand alone when the noun is understood from context:
- That’s not my scarf. It’s Rachel’s. (= Rachel’s scarf)

Do **not** attach -’s to a long group of words. Say **the sister of the man who lives downstairs**, ❌ not *the man who lives downstairs’s sister*.

-’s also describes what something is for or comes from: **a woman’s coat** (a coat for a woman), **a girl’s name**, **a hen’s egg**.

## B Singular -’s vs plural -s’

| Noun type | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| singular | noun + ’s | my cousin’s flat (one cousin) |
| regular plural | noun-s + ’ | my cousins’ flat (two or more cousins) |
| family name plural | the Smiths’ garden | Mr and Mrs Smith’s garden |
| irregular plural (no -s) | noun + ’s | the women’s team, a children’s playground |

Two people sharing one thing take a single -’s at the end of the pair: **Olivia and Sam’s wedding**, **Mr and Mrs Reyes’s car**.

## C of … for things and ideas

With things, abstract ideas and parts, we normally use **of**:

- the colour of the door (❌ the door’s colour)
- the title of the film, the manager of the shop, the cause of the delay

Position words always take **of**: **the beginning / end / middle of …**, **the top / bottom of …**, **the front / back / side of …**:
- the end of the year (not the year’s end)
- the bottom of the stairs, the side of the house

## D Organisations and places: both are possible

For a group of people (a company, a government, a club) either structure works:
- the council’s plan **or** the plan of the council
- the team’s victory **or** the victory of the team

Places also take -’s: **the town’s market square**, **Europe’s largest port**, **Brazil’s coastline**.

## E Time words and periods of time

Words like yesterday, today, tomorrow, next week, Friday take -’s:
- Have you read this morning’s headlines?
- Friday’s lesson has been moved to Thursday.

Lengths of time use -’s (singular) or -s’ (plural):
- I’ve got a fortnight’s leave in August.
- It’s a two hours’ flight. / We had five minutes’ warning.
- Stefan took six months’ unpaid leave.

**See also:** Unit 80 (noun + noun, a three-hour journey); Appendix 5.2 (-’s = is / has).
