# Unit 130 — Adjective + preposition 1

# Theory

## A nice of you / nice to me

**Judging someone's action**: *nice / kind / good / generous / polite / honest / stupid / silly / careless / childish* **OF** somebody (**to do** something):
- *It was kind of you to lend me your umbrella.*
- *It was careless of him to leave his passport on the train.*

**Describing behaviour towards someone**: (be) *nice / kind / good / generous / polite / rude / friendly / unfriendly / cruel* **TO** somebody:
- *The staff were very friendly to us.* (not ~~friendly with us~~)
- *Why were you so rude to the waiter?*

## B adjective + about / with

| adjective | preposition | example |
|---|---|---|
| angry / annoyed / furious / upset | **about** something | *She's still upset about the argument.* |
| angry / annoyed / furious | **with** somebody **for** doing something | *Are you annoyed with me for forgetting?* |
| excited / worried / nervous / happy | **about** something | *I'm nervous about the interview.* |
| pleased / satisfied / happy / delighted / disappointed | **with** something you get or experience | *We were disappointed with the hotel.* / *Are you satisfied with the repair?* |

Note the split: *upset **about** the result* (the situation) but *angry **with** Tom **for** being late* (the person and their action).

## C adjective + at / by / with / of

- **surprised / shocked / amazed / astonished / upset AT or BY** something: *I was amazed at (or by) how quickly they finished.*
- **impressed WITH or BY** somebody/something: *The judges were impressed with (or by) her performance.*
- **fed up / bored WITH** something: *I'm fed up with this rain.* / *He got bored with the game.*
- **tired OF** something: *I'm tired of explaining the same thing.* (not ~~tired with~~)

## D sorry about / for

| use | preposition | example |
|---|---|---|
| a situation or something that happened | **sorry about** | *Sorry about the noise last night.* |
| something you did or caused | **sorry for / about** | *I'm sorry for interrupting.* (or *sorry about interrupting*) |
| sympathy for somebody in a bad situation | **(feel) sorry for** | *I feel sorry for the people who lost their homes.* (not ~~sorry about them~~) |

You can also use a clause: *I'm sorry I interrupted you.*

## E Common mistakes

- ❌ *nice with me* → ✅ *nice to me*
- ❌ *angry on you* → ✅ *angry with you*
- ❌ *tired with waiting* → ✅ *tired of waiting*
- ❌ *feel sorry about him* → ✅ *feel sorry for him*

**See also:** Preposition + -ing → Unit 60; Adjective + to … → Unit 65; sorry to / sorry for → Unit 66C; Adjective + preposition 2 → Unit 131.

### Question 39

#### Question
Complete: "It was very generous ___ your aunt to pay for the meal. She's always been so kind ___ us."

#### Answer
**generous of your aunt … kind to us.** When we judge someone's action (*it was generous of her to pay*), the adjective takes *of*. When we describe how someone behaves towards a person (*kind to us*), it takes *to*. *Kind with us* is a frequent error influenced by other languages.

#### Correct variant
¯\_(ツ)_/¯

#### Variants
1. generous from your aunt … kind with us
2. generous of your aunt … kind to us (Correct)
3. generous to your aunt … kind of us
4. generous of your aunt … kind with us

### Question 40

#### Question
Which sentence is correct?

#### Answer
**"Mum is angry with me for breaking the vase, and she's upset about the mess."** With *angry / annoyed / furious*, use *with* + person + *for* + action, and *about* + thing/situation. *Angry on me* and *angry to me* are not English; *upset for the mess* is wrong because a situation takes *about*.

#### Correct variant
¯\_(ツ)_/¯

#### Variants
1. Mum is angry on me for breaking the vase, and she's upset for the mess.
2. Mum is angry to me about breaking the vase, and she's upset with the mess.
3. Mum is angry with me for breaking the vase, and she's upset about the mess. (Correct)
4. Mum is angry with me about breaking the vase, and she's upset for the mess.

### Question 42

#### Question
One sentence contains a mistake. Which one, and what is the correction?

#### Answer
**"I'm tired with doing the washing-up every night" is wrong; it should be "tired of doing".** *Tired* takes *of*, while *fed up* and *bored* take *with*. The others are correct: *bored with*, *surprised at* (or *by*), *impressed by* (or *with*).

#### Correct variant
¯\_(ツ)_/¯

#### Variants
1. "He's bored with his job" — should be *bored of*.
2. "I'm tired with doing the washing-up every night" — should be *tired of doing*. (Correct)
3. "We were surprised at the result" — should be *surprised of*.
4. "I was impressed by the presentation" — should be *impressed on*.

### Question 43

Complete the two apologies and the comment: "Sorry ___ the delay — I'm sorry ___ keeping you waiting. I feel sorry ___ the people at the back who couldn't hear anything."
