# Unit 43 — Passive 2 (be done / been done / being done)

# Theory

The passive is always *be* in some form + past participle. This unit covers the forms of *be* that appear after modals and in perfect and continuous tenses.

## A — Infinitive: (to) be done

After *will, can, must, should, may, might, going to, want to* etc., the passive uses **be + past participle**:

| Active | Passive |
|---|---|
| (to) repair / send / see | (to) **be** repaired / sent / seen |

- The results will be announced on Friday.
- These forms must be signed by a parent.
- The stadium is going to be demolished.
- Nobody wants to be criticised in public.
- The noise could be heard three streets away.

## B — Perfect infinitive: (to) have been done

After a modal referring to the past (*should have, might have, would have, seem to have*):

| Active | Passive |
|---|---|
| (to) have repaired | (to) **have been** repaired |

- The parcel should have been delivered yesterday.
- If the alarm had been set, the shop wouldn't have been robbed.
- The mistake seems to have been corrected already.

## C — Present perfect and past perfect

| Tense | Active | Passive |
|---|---|---|
| Present perfect | has/have + done | has/have **been** + done |
| Past perfect | had + done | had **been** + done |

- Your order has been shipped. (not *has shipped*)
- Have you ever been stopped by the police?
- I haven't been paid for last month yet.
- When we got to the beach, the tide had already been coming in and the towels had been soaked.
- The house had been empty for years and hadn't been looked after.

## D — Present continuous and past continuous

| Tense | Active | Passive |
|---|---|---|
| Present continuous | am/is/are + doing | am/is/are **being** + done |
| Past continuous | was/were + doing | was/were **being** + done |

- The lift is being repaired, so use the stairs.
- Look — I think that man is being arrested.
- The road was being resurfaced when we drove through, so we were delayed.

## Common mistakes

- ❌ The problem must solve quickly. ✅ The problem must **be solved** quickly.
- ❌ The window has broken by the wind. ✅ The window **has been broken** by the wind.
- ❌ My car is repairing at the moment. ✅ My car **is being repaired** at the moment.

Choose active or passive by asking: does the subject do the action or receive it?

#### Question
A notice in a hotel reads: "All valuables ___ in the safe provided." Which form completes it?

#### Answer
"All valuables **should be kept** in the safe provided." After a modal (*should*), the passive is *be + past participle*: the valuables receive the action of keeping. "Should keep" is active and would mean the valuables keep something. "Should be keep" lacks the past participle. "Should been kept" is missing *have* — *should have been kept* would be possible but refers to the past, which does not fit a general instruction.

#### Correct variant

2. should be keep

### Question 33

#### Question
Which sentence is **wrong**, and why?

A. The new motorway is being built north of the town.
B. When I arrived, the meeting room was being prepared.
C. Our website is redesigning at the moment.
D. I think we're being watched.

#### Answer
Sentence C is wrong. The website does not redesign anything — it is having something done to it — so the present continuous passive is needed: *Our website **is being redesigned** at the moment.* A, B and D are all correct continuous passives (*is being built, was being prepared, are being watched*). Learners often drop *being* and produce an active continuous that makes the subject the doer.

#### Correct variant
¯\_(ツ)_/¯

#### Variants
1. Sentence A — you cannot use *being* with *is*; it should be *is built*.
2. Sentence C — the website receives the action, so it must be *is being redesigned*. (Correct)
3. Sentence B — past continuous passive does not exist; it should be *had been prepared*.
4. Sentence D — *watched* needs an agent with *by*.

### Question 35

#### Question
Rewrite without *they*: "They hadn't checked the fire alarms for years, which is why the inspection failed." Which is the correct passive?

#### Answer
"The fire alarms **hadn't been checked** for years, which is why the inspection failed." The original verb is past perfect (*hadn't checked*), so the passive keeps *had* and adds *been*: *hadn't been + checked*. "Weren't checked" is past simple and loses the "before the inspection" meaning of the past perfect. "Hadn't checked" without *been* stays active and makes the alarms the checkers. "Hadn't being checked" confuses *being* with *been*.

#### Correct variant
¯\_(ツ)_/¯

#### Variants
1. The fire alarms hadn't been checked for years. (Correct)
2. The fire alarms weren't checked for years.
3. The fire alarms hadn't checked for years.
4. The fire alarms hadn't being checked for years.
