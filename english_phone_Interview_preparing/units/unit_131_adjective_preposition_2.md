# Unit 131 — Adjective + preposition 2

# Theory

## A Adjective + of

A large group of adjectives take **of**. Learn them in clusters:

| Group | Adjectives + OF | Example |
|---|---|---|
| fear | afraid / scared / frightened / terrified | Leo is terrified of flying, so he always takes the train. |
| feelings towards people | fond / proud / ashamed / jealous / envious | My aunt is very fond of her grandchildren. |
| attitude | suspicious / critical / tolerant | The guard was suspicious of the two men waiting outside. |
| knowledge | aware / conscious | I wasn't aware of the new parking rules until I got a fine. |
| ability | capable / incapable | Nadia is capable of running a marathon in under four hours. |
| quantity | full / short | The fridge is full of food, but we're short of milk. |
| character | typical | It's typical of Marco to forget his keys again. |
| certainty | certain / sure (OF or ABOUT) | I'm not sure of the date. / I'm not sure about the date. |

Note the pattern **capable of + -ing**: *She is capable of solving it herself* (not ~~capable to solve~~).

## B Adjective + at / to / from / in / on / with / for

Each of these adjectives is fixed with one particular preposition:

| Preposition | Adjectives | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **at** | good / bad / brilliant / better / hopeless (skills) | Priya is brilliant at chess but hopeless at cooking. (not ~~good in cooking~~) |
| **to** | married / engaged | Omar is engaged to a girl from Lisbon. (not ~~married with~~) |
| **to** | similar | Your accent is similar to my cousin's. |
| **from / to** | different | This edition is different from the old one. / … different to the old one. |
| **in** | interested | Are you interested in astronomy? |
| **on** | keen | Nobody was keen on the idea of camping in the rain. |
| **on** | dependent (but **independent OF**) | Small farms are still dependent on the weather. |
| **with** | crowded (people etc.) | The beach was crowded with families. (but *full of* families) |
| **for** | famous | Oaxaca is famous for its cuisine. |
| **for** | responsible | The manager is responsible for the whole department. |

## C Watch the traps

- **married to** somebody vs **married with** children: *Hana is married to a pilot* (her husband) / *Hana is married with two children* (= married and has children).
- **crowded with** vs **full of**: *The square was crowded with tourists* / *The square was full of tourists* — both correct, but the prepositions are not interchangeable: ❌ crowded of, ❌ full with.
- **dependent on** but **independent of**: *Teenagers want to be independent of their parents.*
- **good at** for skills: ❌ *good in maths* ✅ *good at maths*.
- **sure of / about** both acceptable; **aware of** only (❌ aware about).

**See also:** Preposition + -ing → Unit 60; afraid of/to … → Unit 66A; Adjective + preposition 1 → Unit 130; American English → Appendix 7.

### Question 40

#### Question
Complete the sentence: "The restaurant was ______ people celebrating the festival, so we couldn't find a free table." Which option is correct?

#### Answer
The correct sentence is "The restaurant was **crowded with** people celebrating the festival." The adjective *crowded* is always followed by *with* when we say what the people/things are. *Full* takes *of* (so "full of people" would also be correct, but "full with" is not). "Crowded of" and "crowded by" are not English collocations.

#### Correct variant
¯\_(ツ)_/¯

#### Variants
1. crowded of
2. full with
3. crowded with (Correct)
4. crowded by
