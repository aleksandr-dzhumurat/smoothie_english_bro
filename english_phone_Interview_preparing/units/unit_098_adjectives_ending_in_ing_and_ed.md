# Unit 98 — Adjectives ending in -ing and -ed (boring/bored etc.)

# Theory

## A boring vs bored: the basic idea

Many adjectives come in pairs: **-ing** (boring, interesting, tiring…) and **-ed** (bored, interested, tired…).

Picture this: Marco has been entering the same numbers into the same spreadsheet for six years. Nothing ever changes.

- Marco's work is **boring**. (the work has this quality)
- Marco is **bored** with his work. (this is how he feels)

The rule: something (or someone) that is **boring** makes other people **bored**. The -ing adjective describes the **cause**; the -ed adjective describes the **feeling** it produces.

- Marco is bored because his work is boring. ❌ *Marco is boring* — that would mean Marco makes other people bored!

A **person** can be boring too, but only if they have that effect on others:

- My uncle tells the same three stories at every dinner. He's very boring.

## B Comparing -ing and -ed adjectives

| -ing: describes the thing / person that causes the feeling | -ed: describes how somebody feels |
|---|---|
| My course is boring. | I'm bored with my course. |
| My course is interesting. | I'm interested in my course. |
| My course is tiring. | I get tired doing my course. |
| My course is satisfying. | I'm satisfied with my course. |
| My course is depressing. | My course makes me depressed. |

More pairs:

| -ing | -ed |
|---|---|
| Nadia finds astronomy fascinating. | Nadia is fascinated by astronomy. ❌ *fascinating by astronomy* |
| Was anybody interesting at the reception? | Are you interested in joining the choir? |
| It was surprising that the shop was open on Sunday. | We were surprised that the shop was open on Sunday. |
| The final episode was disappointing. | The fans were disappointed with the final episode. |
| The photographs were shocking. | Viewers were shocked by the photographs. |
| The hike was exhausting. | After the hike we were exhausted. |
| Her explanation was confusing. | The students were confused by her explanation. |
| The news is exciting. | I'm excited about the news. |
| That story is amusing. | I was amused by that story. |
| The noise is annoying. | I'm annoyed by the noise. |

## C Typical prepositions after -ed adjectives

- bored **with**, interested **in**, satisfied **with**, disappointed **with/by**, excited **about**, surprised **at/by**, shocked **by/at**, annoyed **with** (a person) / **about** (a thing).

## D Quick test

Ask: "Does this word describe the *cause*, or the *reaction*?"

- "The lecture was ___." → the lecture is the cause → **boring**.
- "The audience looked ___." → the audience feel something → **bored**.
- "He's a very ___ speaker." → he causes interest → **interesting**.

#### Answer
The wrong sentence is "I'm very **interesting** in ancient history." Sentence 1 is correct (the film is the cause → *confusing*). Sentence 3 is correct (the guests feel something → *amused*). ### Question 41

#### Question
Which sentence is correct?

#### Answer
The correct sentence is "We were **disappointed with** the hotel — the photos online were very misleading." *Disappointed* is the -ed adjective describing the guests' feeling, and *with* is the usual preposition. Option 1 wrongly uses *disappointing* for the people who feel the disappointment. Option 2 uses the -ed form for the hotel; a hotel cannot feel disappointed — it should be "The hotel was disappointing". Option 4 uses the wrong preposition: we say *disappointed with/by*, not *disappointed of*.

#### Correct variant
