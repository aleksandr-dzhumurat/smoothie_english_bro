# Unit 113 — although / though / even though / in spite of / despite

# Theory

## A The basic idea

All these words introduce a contrast: something happened, and something else that might have prevented it also happened. Imagine Nadia had a terrible cold but still ran the marathon:

- **Although** she had a terrible cold, she ran the marathon. (= She had a cold, **but** she ran …)
- **In spite of** / **Despite** her cold, she ran the marathon.

## B although + subject + verb

After **although** we need a full clause (subject + verb):

- Although the flat was tiny, we loved living there.
- I stayed at the party although I was exhausted.

Compare **although** (contrast) with **because** (reason):

- We cycled to work **although** it was pouring. (the rain didn't stop us)
- We took the bus **because** it was pouring. (the rain was the reason)

## C in spite of / despite + noun / pronoun / -ing

After **in spite of** and **despite** we use a **noun**, a **pronoun** (this / that / what …) or an **-ing** form – not a clause:

| structure | example |
|---|---|
| in spite of / despite + noun | In spite of the delay, we caught our connection. |
| in spite of / despite + pronoun | He felt ill, but despite this he finished the shift. |
| in spite of / despite + what … | Despite what the forecast said, it stayed dry. |
| in spite of / despite + -ing | She got the job in spite of having no experience. |

- **Despite** means exactly the same as **in spite of**, but it has no *of*: ✅ *despite the noise* ❌ *despite of the noise*.
- To add a clause after them, use **the fact (that)**: *We went ahead despite the fact (that) nobody had confirmed.*

Compare **in spite of** (contrast) with **because of** (reason):

- The match went ahead **in spite of** the fog. / The match was cancelled **because of** the fog.

## D although vs in spite of / despite – the key contrast

| clause (subject + verb) | noun / -ing |
|---|---|
| **Although** the road was icy, we arrived safely. | **In spite of** the icy road, we arrived safely. |
| I passed **although** I hadn't revised. | I passed **despite** not having revised. |

❌ *In spite of the road was icy* ❌ *Despite I hadn't revised* – a clause needs *although* (or *the fact that*).

## E though and even though

- **Though** = **although**: *I went out though I had a headache.*
- In informal speech **though** often comes at the **end** of a sentence, meaning "but": *The hotel was noisy. The staff were lovely, though.*
- **Even though** is a stronger *although*: *Even though I'd slept ten hours, I still felt tired.* Remember that **even** alone cannot do this job: ❌ *Even I'd slept ten hours, …*
