# Unit 13 — Present perfect and past 1 (I have done and I did)

# Theory

## A The present perfect tells us about now

Imagine Yara can't get into her office. "Yara **has lost** her keycard" (present perfect) tells us about the situation **now**: she doesn't have it at this moment.

A few minutes later a colleague finds it under a desk. Now we can say: "Yara **lost** her keycard, but she**'s found** it." The past simple *lost* reports only that something happened at some past time; the present perfect *has found* tells us the present result.

| Form | Focus | Example |
|---|---|---|
| present perfect (*has happened*) | present result / situation now | The lift **has broken down**. (= it isn't working now) |
| past simple (*happened*) | a past event only | The lift **broke down**, but it's working again now. |

Compare:

- Our neighbours **have gone** to Spain. They'll be back in a fortnight. (they are away now)
- Our neighbours **went** to Spain, but I think they're home again. ❌ (not *have gone*)
- The noise **has stopped**, so we can sleep. (it's quiet now)
- The noise **stopped** for a while, but it has started again. ❌ (not *has stopped*)

## B New or recent happenings vs things that are not recent

The present perfect is common for **news** and recent events:

- I**'ve fixed** the leaking tap. It's fine now.
- "Amira**'s passed** her driving test!" "Brilliant!"

Often the past simple is also possible with the same recent events: *I fixed the tap. It's fine now.*

But for things that are **not recent** or belong to a closed period of history, only the past simple is possible:

- Marie Curie **was** a physicist. She **discovered** two elements. ❌ (not *has been … has discovered*)
- My grandfather **grew up** in a fishing village. ❌ (not *has grown up*)

Compare: *Someone **has designed** a phone that folds three ways.* (new) / *Who **designed** the first mobile phone?* (history — not *has designed*).

## C Announce with the present perfect, continue with the past simple

We use the present perfect to **give** a piece of news, but once the topic is open and we ask for details or add information, we normally switch to the **past simple**:

- a: Ouch! I**'ve cut** my finger.
- b: How **did** you **do** that? ❌ (not *have you done*)
- a: I **was** slicing bread and the knife **slipped**. ❌ (not *have been … has slipped*)

- a: Look — somebody**'s scratched** the car.
- b: Well, it **wasn't** me. I **didn't touch** it.

### Question 31

#### Question
Your flatmate says, "Where's Tomas?" You know he left for Berlin this morning and is still there. Which reply is correct?

#### Answer
"He**'s gone** to Berlin. He'll be back on Sunday." Tomas is away now, so we need the present perfect *has gone*, which describes the present situation (he isn't here). "He went to Berlin" would be the right choice if we were talking only about a past event, for instance if he had already come back. "He has went" is not a form of English (*went* is the past simple, not the participle), and "He is gone to Berlin" wrongly uses *be* instead of *have* as the auxiliary.

#### Correct variant
¯\_(ツ)_/¯

#### Variants
1. He went to Berlin. He'll be back on Sunday.
2. He's gone to Berlin. He'll be back on Sunday. (Correct)
3. He has went to Berlin. He'll be back on Sunday.

### Question 34

#### Question
Choose the pair of sentences in which both verb forms are correct.

#### Answer
"It **has started** snowing, so the roads will be icy. / It **snowed** for an hour this morning, but the roads are clear now." The first sentence describes a present situation (it is snowing now), so the present perfect fits. The second describes a finished past event whose result no longer holds (roads are clear), so the past simple is needed. Variant 1 wrongly uses the present perfect for a situation that has been reversed ("but it's warm again"). Variant 3 uses the past simple where a present result is meant ("so we can't use it" — the present perfect *has broken* is needed). Variant 4 wrongly uses the present perfect for a past event that has clearly ended ("but she's home now").

#### Correct variant
