# Unit 135 — Verb + preposition 4: of/for/from/on

# Theory

## A Verb + of

| Verb + OF | Example |
|---|---|
| accuse / suspect somebody OF (doing) … | The shopkeeper accused the boy of stealing sweets. |
| approve / disapprove OF … | Her grandmother disapproves of tattoos. |
| die OF / die FROM an illness etc. | Many soldiers died of disease rather than in battle. |
| consist OF … | The exam consists of two papers and an oral test. |

Note *accuse/suspect somebody of + -ing*: ❌ *accused him to steal* ✅ *accused him of stealing*.

## B Verb + for

| Verb + FOR | Example |
|---|---|
| pay (somebody) FOR … | Who's paying for the drinks? (not ~~pay the drinks~~) |
| thank / forgive somebody FOR … | I'll never forgive my brother for selling my bike. |
| apologise (TO somebody) FOR … | He apologised to the neighbours for the noise. |
| blame somebody / something FOR …; be to blame FOR … | Don't blame the referee for the defeat. / The referee wasn't to blame for it. |

Two exceptions to remember:
- **pay a bill / a fine / a fee / tax / rent / a sum of money** — no preposition: *We paid the fine and left.* So: *pay FOR the meal* (the thing you buy) but *pay the bill* (the money itself).
- **blame (a problem) ON somebody**: *He blamed the delay on the weather.* Compare: *He blamed the weather for the delay.*

## C Verb + from

| Verb + FROM | Example |
|---|---|
| suffer FROM an illness etc. | My father suffers from hay fever every spring. |
| protect somebody / something FROM … | A helmet protects your head from injury. |

## D Verb + on

| Verb + ON | Example |
|---|---|
| depend ON …, rely ON … | Whether we go depends on the price. / You can rely on Tariq to be punctual. |
| live ON money / food | Students often live on very little money. |
| congratulate / compliment somebody ON … | Everyone congratulated Lena on her promotion. / I complimented the chef on the dessert. |

*Depend* + question word can drop *on*: *It depends how long it takes.* or *It depends on how long it takes.* — both correct.

## E Quick contrast table

| ❌ Common error | ✅ Correct |
|---|---|
| pay the meal | pay **for** the meal |
| pay for the rent | pay the rent |
| blame the accident to him | blame the accident **on** him / blame him **for** the accident |
| depend of | depend **on** |
| congratulate her for passing | congratulate her **on** passing |

**See also:** Verb + preposition + -ing → Unit 62; other verbs + for → Unit 133; other verbs + on → Unit 136E.

### Question 37

#### Question
After dinner: "The service was terrible, but we still had to ______ the bill and ______ the wine." Which pair is correct?

#### Answer
The correct answer is "**pay** the bill and **pay for** the wine." With *a bill / a fine / rent / tax / a fee / a sum of money* we use *pay* + object directly. With the thing you are buying (the wine, the meal, the tickets) we use *pay FOR*. Options that put *for* before *bill* or drop it before *wine* reverse this rule.

#### Correct variant
¯\_(ツ)_/¯

#### Variants
1. pay for … pay
2. pay … pay for (Correct)
3. pay for … pay for
4. pay … pay

### Question 38

#### Question
Which sentence is wrong, and why?

#### Answer
The wrong sentence is "The manager congratulated me for finishing the project early." *Congratulate* (and *compliment*) somebody **ON** something: "congratulated me **on** finishing." The other sentences are correct: *suffer from*, *rely on* and *accuse somebody of* are the right prepositions.

#### Correct variant
¯\_(ツ)_/¯

#### Variants
1. The manager congratulated me for finishing the project early. (Correct)
2. Many people in the city suffer from asthma.
3. You can always rely on Jonas in an emergency.
4. They accused the driver of falling asleep at the wheel.

### Question 39

#### Question
Two ways to say who is responsible: "The coach blamed the goalkeeper ______ the defeat." and "The coach blamed the defeat ______ the goalkeeper." Fill both gaps.

#### Answer
The correct pair is **for / on**: "blamed the goalkeeper **for** the defeat" and "blamed the defeat **on** the goalkeeper." *Blame somebody FOR a problem* and *blame a problem ON somebody* mean the same thing but use different prepositions depending on which noun comes first. Mixing them ("blame him on", "blame it for him") is a classic error.

#### Correct variant
¯\_(ツ)_/¯

#### Variants
1. on / for
2. for / to
3. of / on
4. for / on (Correct)

### Question 40

#### Question
"My uncle's pension is tiny. He has to ______ about 600 euros a month." Choose the correct verb + preposition.

#### Answer
The correct answer is "he has to **live on** about 600 euros a month." *Live ON money / food* means to have that as the means of survival. "Live from", "live with" and "live by" do not express this meaning in standard British English (*live by* means to follow a principle; *live with* means to tolerate something or share a home).

#### Correct variant
¯\_(ツ)_/¯

1. live from
2. live on (Correct)
3. live with

### Question 41

#### Question
Rewrite: "The police thought that Mr Adeyemi had forged the signature." Which sentence keeps the meaning and is grammatically correct?

#### Answer
The correct sentence is "The police **suspected Mr Adeyemi of forging** the signature." *Suspect somebody OF + -ing*. Option 1 wrongly uses *to* + infinitive, option 2 uses the wrong preposition *for*, and option 4 ("suspected that he was forged") changes the meaning — it says he himself was forged.

#### Correct variant
¯\_(ツ)_/¯

#### Variants
1. The police suspected Mr Adeyemi to forge the signature.
2. The police suspected Mr Adeyemi for forging the signature.
3. The police suspected Mr Adeyemi of forging the signature. (Correct)
