# Unit 59 — prefer and would rather

# Theory

## A prefer to … and prefer -ing (general preferences)

To talk about what you prefer **in general**, use either form:

- I prefer **to cook** at home. / I prefer **cooking** at home.

When you compare two things, the structure depends on which form you use:

| structure | example |
|---|---|
| prefer **something** *to* something else | I prefer **tea to coffee**. |
| prefer **doing** *to* **doing** | I prefer **cycling to driving**. |
| prefer **doing** *rather than* **doing** | I prefer **cycling rather than driving**. |
| prefer **to do** *rather than* **(do)** | I prefer **to cycle rather than drive**. |

❌ ~~I prefer cycling than driving~~ ✅ I prefer cycling **to** driving. (*to* after -ing; *rather than* after either)

## B would prefer (specific situations)

*Would prefer* is for what somebody wants **on a particular occasion**:

- 'Shall we sit inside?' 'I'**d prefer to sit** outside, if that's OK.'
- Would you prefer **wine or beer**?

After *would prefer* we normally use **to …** (not -ing): *I'd prefer **to stay** in tonight rather than go out.*

## C would rather (I'd rather …)

*I'd rather do* = I'd prefer to do. It is followed by the **bare infinitive** (no *to*):

| would prefer | would rather |
|---|---|
| I'd prefer **to walk**. | I'd rather **walk**. (not ~~rather to walk~~) |
| Which would you prefer **to do**? | Which would you rather **do**? |

- The negative is **I'd rather not**: *I'd rather **not discuss** it now.* / 'Fancy a drink?' 'I'd rather not.'
- Comparing two actions: *I'd rather **read** a book **than watch** TV.* (than, not *rather than*)

## D I'd rather somebody did something

To say what you want **someone else** to do, use *I'd rather + subject + past tense*. The past form refers to the **present or future**, not the past:

- 'Shall I book the tickets?' 'I'd rather **you booked** them tomorrow.'
- Are you going to tell Dad, or would you rather **I told** him?
- I'd rather **she came** with us than stayed at home alone.

Compare: *I'd rather **cook** tonight.* (I do it) / *I'd rather **you cooked** tonight.* (you do it — not ~~I'd rather you cook~~)

The negative is **I'd rather you didn't**: *I'd rather **you didn't mention** this to anyone.* / 'Can I open the window?' 'I'd rather **you didn't**.'

**See also:** would prefer → Unit 58B; prefer (one thing) to (another) → Unit 136D.

### Question 36

#### Question
Complete the sentence about your general preference: "I prefer travelling by train ______ flying."

#### Answer
**"I prefer travelling by train to flying."** With *prefer + -ing*, the second item is introduced by **to** (or *rather than*). "Than" alone is a common error — *than* belongs with *would rather* and comparatives, not with *prefer*. "Instead of" is not part of the *prefer* structure. "More than" is also not the pattern used with *prefer + -ing*.

#### Correct variant
¯\_(ツ)_/¯

#### Variants
1. I prefer travelling by train than flying.
2. I prefer travelling by train to flying. (Correct)
3. I prefer travelling by train instead of flying.
4. I prefer travelling by train more than flying.

#### Question
A colleague asks, "Shall we have the meeting in my office?" You would like it to be in the café downstairs instead. Which reply is correct?

#### Answer
**"I'd rather have it in the café."** *Would rather* is followed by the bare infinitive — no *to*. "I'd rather to have" is the most common learner error, mixing *would rather* with the *would prefer to* pattern. "I'd rather having" wrongly uses -ing. "I'd prefer have" drops the *to* that *would prefer* needs; *I'd prefer to have it in the café* would be correct.

### Question 38

#### Question
Your flatmate asks, "Who's going to phone the landlord, you or me?" You want him to do it. Which is the correct way to say so?

#### Answer
**"I'd rather you phoned him."** When the subject of *would rather* and the person doing the action are different, we use *I'd rather + person + past tense*, and the past form refers to the present or future. "I'd rather you phone him" uses the present, which is the mistake the book warns against. "I'd rather you to phone him" imports the *want you to* pattern, which *would rather* does not use. "I'd rather you phoning him" is ungrammatical.

#### Correct variant
¯\_(ツ)_/¯

2. I'd rather you to phone him.
3. I'd rather you phoning him.
4. I'd rather you phoned him. (Correct)

### Question 40

#### Question
Which of these sentences contains a mistake?

#### Answer
Sentence 2 is wrong: **"I'd rather stay in tonight rather than go out"** should be **"I'd rather stay in tonight than go out."** After *would rather*, the second action is introduced by *than*, not *rather than*; *rather than* is used after *prefer* (*I'd prefer to stay in rather than go out*). The others are correct: "prefer to drive rather than take" is the correct *prefer to do rather than (do)* pattern; "would you prefer to eat" uses *to …* after *would prefer*; "I'd rather not go" is the correct negative of *would rather*.

#### Correct variant
¯\_(ツ)_/¯

#### Variants
1. I prefer to drive rather than take the bus.
2. I'd rather stay in tonight rather than go out. (Correct)
3. Would you prefer to eat now or after the film?
4. I'm exhausted — I'd rather not go to the gym today.
