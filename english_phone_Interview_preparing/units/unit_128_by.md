# Unit 128 — by

# Theory

## A by mistake / by accident / by chance

Something can happen **by mistake, by accident, by chance** (no article: not ~~by a mistake~~, ~~by the chance~~):
- *I deleted the file by mistake.*
- *We bumped into our old teacher by chance at the airport.*

The opposite idea is **on purpose** (= intentionally): *She left the door open on purpose so the cat could get in.*

## B by + means of transport / method

**by** + bare noun says *how* you travel: *by car, by train, by plane, by boat, by ship, by bus, by bike, by taxi*.
- *Most of my colleagues come to work by train.*

But once the noun has a determiner (*my, the, a, this*), **by** disappears and we use **in** or **on**:

| by (no article) | with my / the / a … |
|---|---|
| by car, by taxi | **in** my car, **in** a taxi |
| by train, by bus, by bike | **on** the train, **on** the 8.15 bus, **on** her bike |

- *They came in their own car, not in a taxi.*
- *We travelled on the overnight train.* (not ~~by the train~~)

Walking is **on foot** (not usually ~~by foot~~): *Did you come by bus or on foot?*

Other methods: *send it by post / by email, pay by card / by cheque / by bank transfer, make something by hand*.
But: **pay cash / pay in cash** (not usually ~~by cash~~).

## C by in the passive; by vs with; works by an author

- Passive agent: *The window was broken by a stray ball.* / *The bridge was designed by a Swiss engineer.*
- **by** = the person/thing that does the action; **with** = the tool or instrument used:
  - *The safe was opened with a crowbar.* (not ~~by a crowbar~~) — someone used a crowbar.
  - *The safe was opened by a thief with a crowbar.*
- Creators: *a symphony by Mahler, a film by Kurosawa, a novel by Austen*.
  - *"Who's this sculpture by?" "Rodin, I think."*

## D by = next to / beside

- *The umbrella stand is by the front door.*
- *Come and sit by me.* (= next to me)

## E by = the amount of a difference

- *House prices have risen by eight per cent.* (= they are now 8% higher)
- *Our team lost by two goals.* (= the difference was two goals)
- *She missed the bus by thirty seconds.*

**See also:** Passive + by → Unit 42B; by + -ing → Unit 60B; by myself → Unit 83C; by (time) → Unit 120.

### Question 39

#### Question
Which sentence is correct?

#### Answer
**"I usually go by bus, but today I came in Laura's car."** *By* is used only with the bare noun (*by bus, by car*). When the noun has a determiner such as *Laura's*, *my* or *the*, we switch to *in* (cars, taxis) or *on* (bikes, public transport). *By Laura's car* and *on Laura's car* are both wrong.

#### Correct variant
¯\_(ツ)_/¯

#### Variants
1. I usually go by the bus, but today I came by Laura's car.
2. I usually go by bus, but today I came on Laura's car.
3. I usually go by bus, but today I came in Laura's car. (Correct)
4. I usually go on bus, but today I came in Laura's car.

### Question 42

#### Question
Two uses of *by* appear here: "The bookshop is ___ the station, and its sales have gone up ___ 15 per cent since it moved." Which single preposition fits both gaps, and what does it mean each time?

#### Answer
**by … by.** In the first gap *by* means "next to / beside" (*by the station*). In the second, *by* shows the size of a difference or change (*gone up by 15 per cent* = they are now 15% higher). *Near* would work for the first but not the second, and *of / with* do not express an increase in this pattern.

#### Correct variant
¯\_(ツ)_/¯

#### Variants
1. near … of
2. by … with
3. at … by
4. by … by (Correct)
