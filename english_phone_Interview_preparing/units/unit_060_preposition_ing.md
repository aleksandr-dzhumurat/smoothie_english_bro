# Unit 60 — Preposition (in/for/about etc.) + -ing

# Theory

## A Preposition + verb → verb ends in -ing

When a preposition is followed by a verb, the verb takes the **-ing** form. The infinitive is impossible here.

| | preposition | -ing |
|---|---|---|
| Are you interested | **in** | **joining** the choir? |
| Rafa isn't very good | **at** | **remembering** birthdays. |
| I'm fed up | **with** | **commuting**. |
| What's the point | **of** | **arguing**? |
| Thank you | **for** | **looking after** the cat. |
| How | **about** | **going** for a swim? |
| Stay home | **instead of** | **going** out in this weather. |
| He finished the race | **in spite of** | **injuring** his ankle. |

You can put a noun or pronoun between the preposition and the -ing form: *I'm fed up with **people parking** across my drive.* / *Instead of **him doing** it, why don't you?*

## B before / after / by / without + -ing

**before -ing, after -ing**

- **Before leaving** the office, switch off the lights. (not ~~Before to leave~~)
- What did you do **after finishing** university?

(You can also use a clause: *Before I left …*, *after you finished …*)

**by -ing** = how something is done / how a result is achieved:

- You can save money **by cooking** at home.
- She passed the exam **by studying** every evening.
- The thieves escaped **by climbing** over the back wall.
- Most fires here are started **by people dropping** cigarettes.

**without -ing**

- He left **without saying** goodbye.
- I can't concentrate **without having** coffee first.
- She got the job **without anybody helping** her. / … **without being helped**.

## C to + -ing (look forward to doing etc.)

*To* is usually part of the infinitive: *We decided **to leave**.* But *to* is also a **preposition**:

- We drove from Madrid **to** Valencia.
- I prefer tea **to** coffee.
- Are you looking forward **to** the holidays?

When *to* is a preposition and a verb follows, that verb must be **-ing**, exactly like any other preposition:

- I prefer walking **to driving**. (not ~~to drive~~)
- I'm looking forward **to seeing** you. (not ~~looking forward to see~~)

Test: if you can put a noun after *to* (*looking forward to the party*), it's a preposition → use -ing.

**See also:** be/get used to -ing → Unit 61; Verb + preposition + -ing → Unit 62; while/when -ing → Unit 68B; in spite of → Unit 113; Prepositions → Units 121–136.

### Question 32

#### Question
Complete the sentence: "Thanks very much ______ me to the airport yesterday."

#### Answer
**"Thanks very much for driving me to the airport."** *Thank (somebody) for* is followed by a preposition, so the next verb must be -ing. "For to drive" is impossible — no preposition can be followed by *to + infinitive*. "For drive" has no verb form after the preposition. "To drive" drops the necessary preposition *for*; *thanks to drive* is ungrammatical.

#### Correct variant
¯\_(ツ)_/¯

#### Variants
1. Thanks very much for to drive me to the airport yesterday.
2. Thanks very much for driving me to the airport yesterday. (Correct)
3. Thanks very much for drive me to the airport yesterday.
4. Thanks very much to drive me to the airport yesterday.

### Question 34

#### Question
Your cousin is arriving for a visit next month and you are very pleased about it. Which sentence is correct?

#### Answer
**"I'm looking forward to seeing her."** In *look forward to*, *to* is a preposition (you can say *look forward to the visit*), so the following verb must be -ing. "Looking forward to see" is one of the most common mistakes in English — learners treat *to* as part of an infinitive. "Looking forward seeing" drops the preposition, and "looking forward to have seen" is a grammatically confused form.

#### Correct variant
¯\_(ツ)_/¯

#### Variants
1. I'm looking forward to see her.
2. I'm looking forward seeing her.
3. I'm looking forward to have seen her.
4. I'm looking forward to seeing her. (Correct)

#### Answer

"Without they took" tries to put a full clause after *without*, which does not work; if you want a clause you need a different structure (e.g. *and didn't take their coats*).

### Question 36

#### Question
Which of these sentences contains a mistake?

#### Answer
Sentence 1 is wrong: **"Before to sign the contract, read it carefully"** should be **"Before signing the contract, read it carefully"** (or *Before you sign the contract …*). *Before* used as a preposition is followed by -ing, never by *to + infinitive*. The others are correct: "instead of taking" is a correct *preposition + -ing*; "in spite of feeling" is correct; "without anyone noticing" correctly places a noun between the preposition and the -ing form.

#### Correct variant
¯\_(ツ)_/¯

#### Variants
1. Before to sign the contract, read it carefully. (Correct)
2. We walked home instead of taking a taxi.
3. She went to work in spite of feeling unwell.
4. He slipped out of the meeting without anyone noticing.
