# Unit 133 — Verb + preposition 2: about/for/of/after

# Theory

## A Verb + about

| Verb + ABOUT | Example |
|---|---|
| talk / read / know ABOUT … | I've read about the new tram line in the paper. |
| have a discussion ABOUT something | The council had a long discussion about parking charges. |
| do something / nothing ABOUT something (= act to improve a situation) | The lift keeps breaking down and nobody does anything about it. |

Important exception: **discuss something** takes no preposition.
❌ *We discussed about the budget.* ✅ *We discussed the budget.* (but ✅ *We had a discussion about the budget.*)

## B Verb + for

| Verb + FOR | Example |
|---|---|
| ask (somebody) FOR … | The tourists asked me for directions to the cathedral. |
| apply (TO a company / university) FOR a job / a place | Yusuf has applied to three firms for a trainee position. |
| wait FOR somebody / something (to happen) | We waited for the shop to open. |
| search (a person / a place / a bag) FOR … | Customs searched the lorry for hidden goods. |
| leave (a place) FOR another place | The train leaves Bristol for London at 8.15. (not ~~leaves to London~~) |

Exception: **ask somebody the way / the time / a question** — no preposition: *I asked a passer-by the time.*
Note the difference between *search a place* (the place is what you look through) and *search FOR something* (the thing you want to find): *They searched the garden for the lost ring.*

## C take care of, care for, care about

| Expression | Meaning | Example |
|---|---|---|
| take care OF … | look after, be responsible for | My neighbour takes care of my cat when I travel. |
| care FOR somebody | look after them | Nurses care for patients around the clock. |
| not care FOR something | not like it | I don't care for spicy food. |
| care ABOUT … | think it is important | Ivan cares about the environment. |
| care what / where / how … | no preposition | Do whatever you like — I don't care where we eat. |

## D look for vs look after

- **look FOR** = search for, try to find: *I'm looking for a flat near the station.*
- **look AFTER** = take care of: *Who's looking after the children tonight?*

❌ *Someone must look for the baby while I shower.* ✅ *Someone must look after the baby.*

**See also:** Verbs + about/of (think/hear etc.) → Unit 134; other verbs + for → Unit 135B.

### Question 39

#### Question
Which sentence is wrong, and why?

#### Answer
The wrong sentence is "The team discussed about the new schedule for an hour." *Discuss* takes a direct object with no preposition: "The team **discussed** the new schedule." The confusion comes from *talk about* and *have a discussion about*, which do need *about*. The other three sentences are correct.

#### Correct variant
¯\_(ツ)_/¯

#### Variants
1. We talked about the new schedule for an hour.
2. The team discussed about the new schedule for an hour. (Correct)
3. We had a discussion about the new schedule for an hour.
4. Somebody should do something about the new schedule.

### Question 41

#### Question
"Grandad is 90 and can't manage alone. His daughter has moved in to ______ him." Which option fits?

#### Answer
The correct answer is "to **look after** him" (= take care of him). *Look after* means to take care of somebody or something. *Look for* means to search for — that would mean the daughter cannot find him, which makes no sense here. *Care about* means to think somebody is important, not to physically take care of them, and *take care for* is not a correct expression (*take care OF* is).

### Question 42

#### Question
Two sentences about the same journey: "Our ferry ______ Dover ______ Calais at noon." Which pair of prepositions is correct? (Complete the two gaps: after *ferry* and after *Dover*.)

#### Answer
The correct sentence is "Our ferry **leaves** Dover **for** Calais at noon." We *leave (a place) FOR another place* — *for* shows the destination. *Leave to* is a frequent learner error. "Leaves from Dover to Calais" is also non-standard in this pattern; the book's model is *leave X for Y*.

### Question 43

#### Question
Match the meaning: which sentence means "I don't like modern jazz very much"?

#### Answer
The correct answer is "I don't **care for** modern jazz." In the negative, *not care FOR something* means not to like it. *Care about* means to think something is important ("I don't care about modern jazz" = it isn't important to me — a different meaning). *Take care of* means to look after, and "care of" on its own is not a verb pattern.

#### Correct variant
¯\_(ツ)_/¯

#### Variants
1. I don't care about modern jazz.
2. I don't take care of modern jazz.
