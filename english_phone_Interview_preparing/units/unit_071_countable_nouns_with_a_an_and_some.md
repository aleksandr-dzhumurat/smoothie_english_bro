# Unit 71 — Countable nouns with a/an and some

# Theory

## A Singular and plural countable nouns

Countable nouns have a singular and a plural form:

| singular | plural |
|---|---|
| a cat | cats |
| a passenger | some passengers |
| the morning | the mornings |
| this ticket | these tickets |
| an orange | three oranges |

Before a singular countable noun you can put **a/an**: *Have a good trip.* / *Do you want an orange?*

A singular countable noun cannot stand completely alone. It needs **a/an**, **the**, **my**, **this** etc. in front of it:

- She always carries an umbrella. ❌ *carries umbrella*
- Mind the step. / What a strange noise! / Have you broken your arm?

## B a/an to say what something or somebody is

We use **a/an** to classify: to say what kind of thing or person we are talking about.

- That's a lovely photo. / Marco is an engineer. / A whale is a mammal.

In the plural we use the noun **alone** — not *some*:

| singular (a/an) | plural (no article) |
|---|---|
| A whale is a mammal. | Whales are mammals. |
| I'm a pessimist. | They're pessimists. |
| Her brother is a pilot. | Her brothers are both pilots. |
| Leo is a very kind person. | Leo and Mia are very kind people. |
| What a boring film! | What horrible colours! |

❌ *Those are some lovely photos.* → ✅ *Those are lovely photos.*

Describing a person's features also uses **a/an** or no article, never *the*: *Nadia has a round face and green eyes.* ❌ *the round face*, ❌ *the green eyes*

Jobs always take **a/an**: *Priya is an accountant.* ❌ *Priya is accountant.* / *I'd like to be a chef.*

## C some with plural countable nouns

**some** is used with plural countable nouns in two ways.

**(1) some = a number of / a few / a pair of**

- I've bought some new shirts. (❌ *I've bought new shirts* sounds incomplete here)
- Some colleagues of mine are visiting next week.
- I need some new gloves. (= a new pair)

Often **some** is optional: *The office was empty except for a desk and (some) boxes.* / *We need (some) more chairs.*

But do **not** use *some* when you speak about things **in general** (see Unit 75):

- I adore strawberries. ❌ *some strawberries*
- My cousin is a photographer. She takes photos. ❌ *some photos*

**(2) some = some but not all**

- Some students finish the course early. (not all students)
- There will be snow in some areas tonight, but most places will stay dry.

## Extra practice

### Question 46

#### Question
A learner introduces her colleague: "This is Tomasz. He is ___ engineer at a software company." Which option correctly fills the gap?

#### Answer
The correct sentence is *He is an engineer at a software company.* When we say what somebody's job is, we always use **a/an** before the singular countable noun: *an engineer*, *a nurse*, *a teacher*. Leaving the noun bare (*He is engineer*) is a very common learner mistake but is not possible in English, and *the engineer* would point to one specific, already known engineer, which is not the meaning here. *Some engineer* is not used for a single person's job.

#### Correct variant
¯\_(ツ)_/¯

#### Variants
1. He is engineer at a software company.
2. He is an engineer at a software company. (Correct)
3. He is the engineer at a software company.
4. He is some engineer at a software company.

The correct sentence is *Those are beautiful boots.* When we say what kind of things something is (classifying or describing), we use **a/an** in the singular (*That's a beautiful boot*) but the bare plural noun in the plural — no *some*. *Those are some beautiful boots* is a typical error caused by treating *some* as the plural of *a/an*. *A beautiful boots* mixes singular article with plural noun, and *Those are beautiful boot* uses a singular countable noun without any article.

1. Those are some beautiful boots.
2. Those are a beautiful boots.

### Question 48

#### Question
Which of these sentences contains a mistake, and why?

A. Rosa has a long neck and dark eyes.
B. Rosa never wears a scarf.
C. Rosa has the long neck and the dark eyes.
D. What a lovely scarf Rosa is wearing!

#### Answer
Sentence C is wrong. When we describe somebody's physical features we say *a long neck*, *a nice smile*, *dark eyes*, *long fingers* — with **a/an** in the singular and no article in the plural — but **not** *the*. *The long neck* would imply a specific, already identified neck, which makes no sense. Sentence A is the correct form; B correctly uses *a scarf* after *wears* (a singular countable noun cannot stand alone); D uses the exclamation pattern *What a + adjective + singular noun*.

2. Sentence B is wrong: after *never* you cannot use *a*.
3. Sentence D is wrong: exclamations do not take *a*.
4. Sentence C is wrong: physical features are described with *a/an* or no article, not *the*. (Correct)

### Question 50

#### Question
A weather report says: "Tomorrow ___ regions will have fog in the morning, but most of the country will be clear." Which option fits, and what does *some* mean here?

#### Answer
The correct sentence is *Tomorrow some regions will have fog in the morning, but most of the country will be clear.* Here **some** has its second meaning: *some but not all*. The contrast with *most of the country* makes this clear. The bare plural *Regions will have fog* would be a general statement about regions everywhere, which is not intended; *a regions* is impossible (singular article with a plural noun); *the regions* would refer to specific regions already known to the listener.

#### Variants
1. Regions will have fog — *some* is unnecessary before a plural noun.
2. Some regions will have fog — *some* means "some but not all". (Correct)
