# Unit 124 — in/at/on (position) 2

# Theory

## A in — lines, pictures, publications, sky, world, country

| group | examples |
|---|---|
| lines of people/things | in a line, in a row, in a queue |
| work units | in an office, in a department |
| images | in a picture, in a photo(graph) |
| large "containers" | in the sky, in the world |
| printed media | in a newspaper, in a magazine, in a book |
| rural area | in the country (= not in a town) |

- *We stood in a queue for tickets for over an hour.*
- *My cousin works in the marketing department.*
- *Who's the man in this photo?*
- *There wasn't a single cloud in the sky.*
- *I read about it in a magazine.* / *They live in the country.*

## B on — sides, floors, lists, rivers, roads, the way

- **on the left / on the right** (or *on the left-hand side / on the right-hand side*): *In Japan they drive on the left.*
- **on the ground floor / on the first floor / on the top floor**: *The dentist is on the third floor.*
- **on a map, on a menu, on a list, on a page, on a website**: *Is fish on the menu tonight?* / *The timetable is on the school website.*
- a place is **on a river / on a road / on the coast**: *Budapest is on the Danube.* / *Their cottage is on the coast.*
- **on the way** (from A to B): *I bought bread on the way home.*

Note the contrast: information is *in* a newspaper or book but *on* a website, a page or a list.

## C at — top, bottom, end

- **at the top (of …), at the bottom (of …), at the end (of …)**
- *Sign at the bottom of the form.*
- *The bakery is at the end of the road.*
- *There's a date at the top of the letter.*

## D front and back: in, at or on?

| context | preposition | example |
|---|---|---|
| a car | **in** the front / **in** the back | *The kids sat in the back of the car.* |
| a building, cinema, theatre, group of people | **at** the front / **at** the back | *We stood at the back of the crowd.* / *The kitchen is at the back of the house.* |
| an envelope, a card, a piece of paper | **on** the front / **on** the back | *Write the address on the front of the envelope.* |

## E corner: in, at or on?

- **in the corner of a room** (inside, an enclosed angle): *A lamp stood in the corner of the study.*
- **at the corner / on the corner of a street** (a point outside): *The café is on the corner of Oak Street.* or *at the corner*.

**See also:** in the world → Unit 108D; in/at/on (position) → Units 123, 125; American English → Appendix 7.

#### Question
Complete: "Our flat is ___ the fourth floor, and the lift is ___ the end of the corridor ___ your right."

#### Answer
**on the fourth floor … at the end of the corridor … on your right.** Floors take *on*; *the end (of …)* takes *at*; sides take *on* (*on the right / on your right*). *In the fourth floor* and *in the end of* are typical errors — *in the end* exists but means "finally", not a position.

¯\_(ツ)_/¯

4. on the fourth floor … in the end of the corridor … on your right

### Question 34

#### Question
Which sentence is correct?

#### Answer
**"I sat in the back of the taxi while my sister sat in the front."** For cars and taxis we say *in the front / in the back*. *At the back / at the front* is used for buildings, cinemas, groups of people; *on the back / on the front* is for envelopes, cards and paper. So options mixing *at* or *on* with a car are wrong.

#### Correct variant
¯\_(ツ)_/¯

#### Variants
1. I sat at the back of the taxi while my sister sat at the front.
2. I sat on the back of the taxi while my sister sat on the front.
3. I sat in the back of the taxi while my sister sat in the front. (Correct)
4. I sat in the back of the taxi while my sister sat at the front.

### Question 36

#### Question
One of these sentences contains a mistake. Which one, and what is the correction?

#### Answer
**"I read an interesting article on today's paper" is wrong; it should be "in today's paper".** We say *in a newspaper / in a magazine / in a book*, but *on a website / on a page / on a list*. The other three sentences are correct: *on the website*, *in a row*, *on the coast*.

#### Correct variant
¯\_(ツ)_/¯

#### Variants
1. "You'll find the prices on the website" — should be *in the website*.
2. "I read an interesting article on today's paper" — should be *in today's paper*. (Correct)
3. "The chairs were arranged in a row" — should be *on a row*.
4. "Brighton is on the south coast" — should be *at the south coast*.
