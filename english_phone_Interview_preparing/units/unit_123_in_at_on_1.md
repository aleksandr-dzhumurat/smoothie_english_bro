# Unit 123 — in/at/on (position) 1

# Theory

## A in — inside a space or area

Use **in** for something enclosed by a three-dimensional space or contained within an area:

| enclosed spaces | areas / places | water |
|---|---|---|
| in a room, in a cupboard, in a building | in a garden, in a park | in a lake |
| in a bag, in a drawer, in a car | in a town, in the city centre | in the sea |
| in your pocket, in her hand, in your mouth | in a village, in the mountains | in the river |

- *Nobody was in the office when I called.*
- *What's that in your pocket?*
- *My aunt lives in a tiny village in the Alps.*
- *Some children were splashing about in the lake.*

## B at — a point or specific location

Use **at** when we think of a place as a point, a meeting spot or a location along a route, rather than an area:

- at the bus stop, at the door, at the window, at the traffic lights, at the corner, at the roundabout, at the next junction, at her desk, at reception
- *Someone is waiting at the gate.*
- *Turn right at the petrol station, then left at the lights.*
- *Please hand your badge in at reception.*

Compare **in** and **at**:
- *There were dozens of people in the café.* (inside, the interior) / *Turn right at the café.* (a point on the route)
- *Let's meet in the library.* (inside the building) / *Let's meet at the entrance to the library.* (a point outside it)

## C on — a surface or a line

Use **on** for surfaces, things attached to surfaces, and lines/edges:

- on the floor, on the ground, on the grass, on the beach, on a chair
- on the wall, on the ceiling, on the door, on your nose, on your shirt
- on a page, on an island
- *I dropped some sauce on my sleeve.*
- *There's a poster on the wall behind you.*
- *The answers are on page twelve.*
- *The lighthouse stands on a tiny island.*

Compare **in** and **on** with the same object:
- *There's milk in the jug.* (contents) / *There's a crack on the jug.* (surface)

Compare **at** and **on**:
- *Somebody is at the door.* (a person standing at that point) / *Somebody has stuck a note on the door.* (attached to the surface)

## D Choosing quickly

1. Inside something with an interior, or inside a geographical area? → **in**.
2. A point where you stop, meet, turn or stand? → **at**.
3. On top of / attached to a surface, or on a page/island? → **on**.

**See also:** in/at/on (position) 2–3 → Units 124–125.

#### Question
Complete the sentence: "There were about forty people ___ the hall, but the speaker was still standing ___ the door greeting latecomers."

#### Answer
**in the hall … at the door.** The hall is an enclosed space with people inside it, so *in*. The door is a point where somebody stands, so *at the door*. *On the door* would mean attached to its surface (a notice, a sign), not a person standing there.

### Question 34

#### Question
Which sentence is correct?

#### Answer
**"There's a label on the jar but nothing in it."** A label is attached to the surface of the jar, so *on the jar*; the contents (or absence of contents) are *in the jar*. The distractors reverse or misapply the pair: *in the jar* for a label describes something placed inside, and *at the jar* is not used for position on an object.

#### Correct variant
¯\_(ツ)_/¯

#### Variants
1. There's a label in the jar but nothing on it.
2. There's a label at the jar but nothing in it.
3. There's a label on the jar but nothing in it. (Correct)
4. There's a label on the jar but nothing at it.

### Question 36

#### Question
One of these sentences has a mistake. Which one, and how should it be corrected?

#### Answer
**"We'll meet in the entrance to the museum" is wrong; it should be "at the entrance to the museum".** An entrance is a meeting point, not a space you are inside, so *at*. The other sentences are correct: *in the museum* (inside the building), *on the ceiling* (a surface), *in the river* (in water).

#### Correct variant
¯\_(ツ)_/¯

#### Variants
1. "We'll meet in the entrance to the museum" — should be *at the entrance*. (Correct)
2. "It was cold in the museum" — should be *at the museum*.
3. "There's a damp patch on the ceiling" — should be *in the ceiling*.
4. "Some boys were swimming in the river" — should be *on the river*.
