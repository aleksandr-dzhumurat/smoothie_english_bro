# Unit 66 — to … (afraid to do) and preposition + -ing (afraid of -ing)

# Theory

## A afraid to do / afraid of doing

| structure | meaning | example |
|---|---|---|
| **afraid to do** something | I don’t want to do it because it is dangerous or the result could be bad | The dog looked fierce, so the children were **afraid to go** into the garden. |
| **afraid of** something **happening** | I am afraid that something bad will happen (often not under my control) | The ladder wobbled and I was **afraid of falling**. |

- *Afraid to do* refers to a deliberate action you choose not to do.
- *Afraid of -ing* refers to an unwanted event or result.

The two are often linked: *I was **afraid to complain** because I was **afraid of losing** my job.* (I didn’t complain = afraid to; the bad result = afraid of)

## B interested in doing / interested to hear

| structure | meaning | example |
|---|---|---|
| **interested in doing** | I would like to do it / I’m thinking of doing it | Are you **interested in joining** the choir? (❌ *interested to join*) |
| **interested to hear / see / know / learn / find out** | it was (or would be) interesting for me to hear/see it | I was **interested to learn** that the building is 400 years old. |

*Interested to* is used only with verbs of receiving information (*hear, see, know, learn, read, find out*) and behaves like *surprised to …*, *glad to …* (Unit 65C).

## C sorry for doing / sorry to do

| structure | use | example |
|---|---|---|
| **sorry for doing** | apologising for something already done | I’m **sorry for waking** you up last night. (❌ *sorry to wake* for a past action) |
| **sorry (that) I did** | same meaning | I’m sorry I woke you up. |
| **sorry to hear / see / learn** | regret about something that happens | I was **sorry to hear** that your dog died. (❌ *sorry for hearing*) |
| **sorry to do** (at that moment) | apologising while you do it | **Sorry to interrupt**, but there’s a call for you. |

Also: *I’ll be **sorry to leave** this town.* (= I will regret leaving)

## D Verbs that take to … vs verbs that take preposition + -ing

Contrast the infinitive verbs with their prepositional near-synonyms:

| to + infinitive | preposition + -ing |
|---|---|
| I **want to** move / I’**d like to** move | I’m **thinking of** moving |
| I **hope to** travel more | I **dream of** travelling more |
| She **failed to** convince them | She **succeeded in** convincing them |
| They **allowed us to** enter | They **prevented us from** entering |
| We **plan to** meet on Friday | We’re **looking forward to** meeting on Friday |
| He **promised to** pay | He **insisted on** paying |

**See also:** Verb + to … → Units 54–55; Verb + preposition + -ing → Unit 62; sorry to … → Unit 65C; Adjective + preposition → Units 130–131; sorry about/for → Unit 130

### Question 35

#### Question
Kenji is carrying his grandmother’s antique teapot across the room very slowly. Which sentence best explains why?

#### Answer
"He’s **afraid of dropping** it." *Afraid of -ing* expresses fear that something bad will happen – here, that the teapot might fall. *Afraid to drop it* would mean he deliberately chooses not to perform the action of dropping, which makes no sense because dropping isn’t something he wants to do. *Afraid for dropping* and *afraid to dropping* are ungrammatical.

#### Correct variant
¯\_(ツ)_/¯

#### Variants
1. He’s afraid to drop it.
2. He’s afraid of dropping it. (Correct)
3. He’s afraid for dropping it.
4. He’s afraid to dropping it.

### Question 36

#### Question
Complete the two gaps: "I wasn’t ______ (interested / apply) for the job myself, but I was ______ (interested / hear) that Sofia got it."

#### Answer
"I wasn’t **interested in applying** for the job myself, but I was **interested to hear** that Sofia got it." *Interested in -ing* = wanting to do something (a wish or plan). *Interested to hear/know/learn* = it was interesting for me to receive that information. Reversing them – *interested to apply* or *interested in hearing* – is the typical learner mix-up; the first is simply wrong, the second is possible in other contexts but here the sentence describes a reaction to news, so *interested to hear* is the form taught.

#### Correct variant
¯\_(ツ)_/¯

#### Variants
1. interested to apply … interested to hear
2. interested in applying … interested in hearing
3. interested in applying … interested to hear (Correct)
4. interested to apply … interested in hearing

### Question 37

#### Question
Which of these sentences is wrong, and why?

A. Sorry to bother you, but is this seat taken?
B. I’m sorry for losing my temper at the meeting yesterday.
C. We were sorry for hearing that the café is closing down.
D. I’ll be sorry to leave this job.

#### Answer
Sentence **C** is wrong. To express regret about something that happens, we say *sorry to hear*: "We were **sorry to hear** that the café is closing down." *Sorry for -ing* is only for apologising for something you did (sentence B). Sentence A correctly uses *sorry to …* to apologise at the moment of doing something, and sentence D correctly expresses future regret.

#### Correct variant
¯\_(ツ)_/¯

#### Variants
1. Sentence A – *sorry to bother* should be *sorry for bothering*.
2. Sentence B – *sorry for losing* should be *sorry to lose*.
3. Sentence C – *sorry for hearing* should be *sorry to hear*. (Correct)
4. Sentence D – *sorry to leave* should be *sorry for leaving*.

### Question 38

#### Question
Choose the correct pair to complete: "The rescue team ______ the trapped climbers, but bad visibility ______ the helicopter ______ earlier."

#### Answer
"The rescue team **succeeded in reaching** the trapped climbers, but bad visibility **prevented the helicopter from taking off** earlier." *Succeed* takes *in* + -ing (unlike *manage to* / *fail to*), and *prevent* takes object + *from* + -ing. *Succeeded to reach* wrongly gives *succeed* an infinitive; *prevented … to take off* wrongly gives *prevent* an infinitive – learners confuse it with *allow somebody to do*.

#### Correct variant

### Question 39

#### Question
Which sentence is grammatically correct?

#### Answer
"Bea **wouldn’t dream of asking** her parents for money." *Dream of* is a verb + preposition combination and is followed by -ing. The other options contain errors: *dream to ask* treats *dream* like *hope* or *want*; *insisted to pay* should be *insisted on paying*; and *looking forward to see* should be *looking forward to seeing*, because *to* is a preposition there.

#### Correct variant
¯\_(ツ)_/¯

#### Variants
1. Bea wouldn’t dream to ask her parents for money.
2. Bea insisted to pay for everyone’s dinner.
3. Bea is looking forward to see her cousins next month.
4. Bea wouldn’t dream of asking her parents for money. (Correct)
