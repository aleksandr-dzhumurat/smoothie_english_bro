# Unit 99 — Adjectives: a nice new house, you look tired

# Theory

## A Opinion adjectives before fact adjectives

We often put two or more adjectives in front of a noun:

- They've bought a lovely old cottage.
- On the wall hung an enormous dark oil painting.

**Fact adjectives** (old, dark, square, cotton) give objective information: age, size, colour, material, origin.
**Opinion adjectives** (lovely, dreadful, tasty) say what the speaker thinks.

Opinion comes **before** fact:

| | opinion | fact | noun |
|---|---|---|---|
| a | gorgeous | sunny | afternoon |
| an | annoying | little | dog |
| | tasty | cold | noodle salad |
| a | comfortable | big leather | armchair |

## B Order of fact adjectives

When several fact adjectives appear together, the usual (not absolute) order is:

| 1 size / how big? | 2 age / how old? | 3 colour | 4 origin / where from? | 5 material / made of? | noun |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| a small | | | | plastic | box (1 → 5) |
| a tall | young | | | | woman (1 → 2) |
| | an old | | Italian | | song (2 → 4) |
| a huge | | grey | | stone | wall (1 → 3 → 5) |
| | a new | red | | silk | scarf (2 → 3 → 5) |

Size and length adjectives (*big, small, tall, long, short*) normally come **before** shape and width adjectives (*round, square, thin, wide, narrow*):

- a big square cushion, a long narrow corridor, a tall slim vase

Two or more **colour** adjectives are joined with **and**:

- a blue and yellow kite; a red, black and gold uniform

With other adjectives we do not use *and* before a noun:

- a short grey coat ❌ *a short and grey coat*

## C Adjectives after be / get / become / seem and sense verbs

Adjectives (not adverbs) follow *be, get, become, seem*:

- Be patient. / It's getting dark. / The queue became longer and longer. / Your idea seems sensible.

They also follow verbs of perception — *look, feel, sound, taste, smell* — when we describe how something **is**, not how it does something:

- You look exhausted. / I feel dizzy. / That plan sounds risky.
- The bread smells wonderful. / This milk tastes sour.

To describe **how somebody does** an action, use an **adverb** (see Units 100–101):

- Speak clearly! ❌ *Speak clear*
- He swims very well. ❌ *He swims very good*

Compare: The pianist looks nervous (adjective — her appearance) vs She looked at the score nervously (adverb — how she looked).

## D the first two days / the next few weeks / the last ten minutes

Numbers and words like *few* go **after** *first / next / last*:

- I hated the first three weeks in my new job. ❌ *the three first weeks*
- The shop will be closed for the next few days. ❌ *the few next days*
- We only arrived for the last ten minutes of the match.

**See also:** Adverbs ➜ Units 100–101; Comparative (cheaper etc.) ➜ Units 105–107; Superlative (cheapest etc.) ➜ Unit 108.
