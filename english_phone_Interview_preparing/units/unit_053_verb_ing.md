# Unit 53 — Verb + -ing (enjoy doing / stop doing etc.)

# Theory

## A Verbs followed by -ing

Certain verbs take the **-ing** form of the following verb, never *to …*:

| verb | example |
|---|---|
| enjoy | I enjoy **cooking** for friends. (not ~~enjoy to cook~~) |
| mind | Would you mind **turning** the music down? (not ~~mind to turn~~) |
| suggest | Priya suggested **taking** the later train. (not ~~suggested to take~~) |

More verbs in this group: **stop, finish, recommend, consider, admit, deny, avoid, risk, imagine, fancy**.

- The engine stopped **running** halfway up the hill.
- Have you finished **painting** the fence?
- She avoided **looking** at me.
- I don't fancy **cooking** tonight — shall we order in? (*fancy* = feel like)
- He denied **breaking** the window.

The negative is **not -ing**: *On Sundays I enjoy **not setting** an alarm.*

## B Phrasal verbs + -ing

These multi-word verbs are also followed by -ing:

| phrasal verb | meaning | example |
|---|---|---|
| give up | stop | Hassan has given up **eating** meat. |
| put off | postpone | Don't put off **booking** the tickets — they'll sell out. |
| go on / carry on | continue | The band went on **playing** despite the rain. |
| keep / keep on | do repeatedly | The printer keeps **jamming**. |

## C Verb + somebody + -ing

Some of these verbs can have an object before the -ing form:

- You can't stop **the children making** noise.
- I can't imagine **Grandad using** a smartphone.
- I don't remember **you mentioning** that.
- Sorry to keep **you waiting**.

## D having done

For a completed action you may use **having + past participle**, but plain -ing is usually enough:

- She admitted **having lied** to the police. *or* She admitted **lying** to the police.
- I regret **having said** that. *or* I regret **saying** that.

## E that-clauses with admit, deny, suggest, recommend

These four verbs also allow a *that*-clause:

- He denied (that) he **had taken** the money. (= denied taking)
- Priya suggested (that) we **take** the later train. (= suggested taking)
- I recommend (that) you **book** early. (= recommend booking)

❌ ~~She suggested to leave early.~~ ✅ She suggested **leaving** early. / She suggested **that we leave** early.

### Question 30

#### Question
Complete the sentence: "The neighbours have been playing loud music all evening. I wish they'd stop ______."

#### Answer
**"stop doing it"** (or *stop playing it*). *Stop* is followed by the -ing form when it means "cease an activity". "Stop to do it" would change the meaning to "pause in order to do it", which makes no sense here. "Stop do it" is missing a verb form entirely, and "stop that they do it" is not a possible structure after *stop*.

#### Correct variant
¯\_(ツ)_/¯

#### Variants
1. stop to do it
2. stop doing it (Correct)
3. stop do it
4. stop that they do it

### Question 31

#### Question
Your friend says the weather looks good and proposes an activity. Which sentence reports this correctly?

#### Answer
**"She suggested going for a hike."** *Suggest* is followed by -ing (or by a *that*-clause: *She suggested that we go for a hike*). It is never followed by *to …* or by *object + to …*: ❌ *suggested to go*, ❌ *suggested us to go*. Option 4 mixes a *that*-clause with a *to*-infinitive, which is also impossible.

#### Correct variant
¯\_(ツ)_/¯

#### Variants
1. She suggested to go for a hike.
2. She suggested us to go for a hike.
3. She suggested going for a hike. (Correct)
4. She suggested that to go for a hike.

### Question 32

#### Question
Which sentence correctly uses a negative -ing form?

#### Answer
**"Would you mind not parking in front of my gate?"** The negative of a verb + -ing structure is *not + -ing*, placed directly before the -ing form. "Would you mind don't park" wrongly inserts an imperative form after *mind*. "Would you not mind parking" negates *mind* instead of *park*, which changes the meaning entirely. "Would you mind to not park" uses *to*, which *mind* never takes.

#### Correct variant
¯\_(ツ)_/¯

#### Variants
1. Would you mind don't park in front of my gate?
2. Would you not mind parking in front of my gate?
3. Would you mind to not park in front of my gate?
4. Would you mind not parking in front of my gate? (Correct)

### Question 33

#### Question
Put the words in the right order: "I / imagine / can't / a motorbike / Aunt Rose / riding". Which sentence is correct?

#### Answer
**"I can't imagine Aunt Rose riding a motorbike."** With verbs like *imagine, stop, remember, keep*, the object (the person) goes between the verb and the -ing form: *imagine + somebody + -ing*. Placing *riding* before *Aunt Rose* (option 1) reads as if a motorbike is riding Aunt Rose. Option 3 splits the phrase illogically, and option 4 uses *to ride*, which *imagine* does not take.

#### Correct variant
¯\_(ツ)_/¯

#### Variants
1. I can't imagine riding Aunt Rose a motorbike.
2. I can't imagine Aunt Rose riding a motorbike. (Correct)
3. I can't imagine a motorbike Aunt Rose riding.
4. I can't imagine Aunt Rose to ride a motorbike.

### Question 34

#### Question
Which of these sentences contains a mistake?

#### Answer
Sentence 4 is wrong: **"I've put off to call the dentist for weeks"** should be **"I've put off calling the dentist for weeks."** *Put off* (= postpone) is followed by -ing, like *give up, carry on* and *keep on*. The other sentences are correct: "He admitted having forgotten" is a valid perfect -ing form; "They denied that they had seen anything" is a correct *that*-clause after *deny*; "Carry on working" uses -ing after *carry on*.

#### Correct variant
¯\_(ツ)_/¯

#### Variants
1. He admitted having forgotten the appointment.
2. They denied that they had seen anything.
3. Carry on working — I'll come back later.
4. I've put off to call the dentist for weeks. (Correct)
