# Unit 97 — -ing and -ed clauses (the woman talking to Tom, the boy injured in the accident)

# Theory

## A -ing clauses

A clause is a part of a sentence. An **-ing clause** comes straight after a noun and works like a shortened relative clause with an **active** meaning.

Use it to say what somebody or something **is or was doing** at a particular moment:

- Who's the man **standing by the window**? (= the man is standing by the window)
- Firefighters **tackling the blaze** worked through the night. (= firefighters were tackling the blaze)
- Who were those students **queuing outside the office**? (= they were queuing)
- I was distracted by a phone **buzzing in someone's bag**. (= a phone was buzzing)

An -ing clause can also describe what happens **all the time**, a permanent characteristic:

- The path **running along the cliff** is closed in winter. (= the path runs along the cliff)
- We rented a cottage **facing the lake**. (= the cottage faces the lake)
- Think of an animal **beginning with the letter Z**. (= its name begins with Z)

| full relative clause | -ing clause |
|---|---|
| the man who is standing by the window | the man standing by the window |
| a path that runs along the cliff | a path running along the cliff |

## B -ed clauses (past participle clauses)

An **-ed clause** also follows a noun, but it has a **passive** meaning:

- The cyclist **knocked down at the junction** has a broken arm. (= the cyclist was knocked down)
- Olga showed me a rug **woven by her grandmother**. (= the rug was woven by her grandmother)
- The passwords **leaked in the attack** have all been changed. (= the passwords were leaked)

"-ed" here really means "past participle". Many past participles are irregular (*stolen, made, built, written, broken*…):

- Nobody has recovered the jewels **stolen from the museum**.
- Most of the furniture **made in this workshop** is sold abroad.

| full relative clause | -ed clause |
|---|---|
| the cyclist who was knocked down | the cyclist knocked down |
| a rug which was woven by hand | a rug woven by hand |

Contrast: **-ing = doing** (active); **-ed = done to** (passive):

- the driver **causing** the delay (he is causing it) vs the delay **caused** by the driver (it was caused)

## C there is / there was + -ing / -ed clause

- There were dozens of people **queuing for tickets**.
- Is there anyone **sitting here**?
- There was a van **parked across our driveway**.

**Left** is used this way with the meaning "remaining, not used":

- We've drunk most of the juice. There's only a little **left**.
- Are there any seats left?

### Question 33

#### Question
Join these two sentences using an -ing clause: "A lorry was blocking the road. It had a flat tyre." Which version is correct?

#### Answer
The correct sentence is "The lorry **blocking the road** had a flat tyre." The lorry was actively blocking the road, so an -ing clause (active meaning) follows the noun. "The lorry blocked the road had a flat tyre" mixes two finite verbs without a relative pronoun, so it is ungrammatical. "The lorry which blocking the road" is wrong because after *which* you need a full verb (*which was blocking*). "The lorry was blocking the road had a flat tyre" is again two main verbs with no connector.

#### Correct variant
¯\_(ツ)_/¯

#### Variants
1. The lorry blocked the road had a flat tyre.
2. The lorry blocking the road had a flat tyre. (Correct)
3. The lorry which blocking the road had a flat tyre.
4. The lorry was blocking the road had a flat tyre.

Compare the two phrases: (a) "the students taking the exam" and (b) "the exam taken by the students". Which statement about them is correct?

Option 1 is wrong because both are fine. Option 3 is wrong because the -ing form does not refer only to the past; it can describe present, past, or permanent actions. Option 4 reverses the active/passive meanings.
