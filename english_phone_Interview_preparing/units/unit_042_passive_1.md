# Unit 42 — Passive 1 (is done / was done)

# Theory

## A — Active vs. passive

In an **active** sentence the subject performs the action; in a **passive** sentence the subject receives it — we say what *happens to* the subject.

- Active: *A local firm designed the new library.* (subject = the firm, which did the designing)
- Passive: *The new library was designed by a local firm.* (subject = the library, which received the action)

The object of the active sentence becomes the subject of the passive one.

## B — Why use the passive?

We choose the passive when the person or thing that does the action is **unknown**, **obvious** or **unimportant**:

- My bike was stolen last night. (I don't know who took it)
- Are these offices cleaned at weekends? (who cleans them doesn't matter)
- Spanish is spoken in most of South America.

If we do want to mention the doer (the *agent*), we add **by**:

- The goal was scored by a seventeen-year-old.
- Our bread is baked by a family bakery in the next street.

## C — Form: be + past participle

The passive is always **a form of *be*** + **past participle** (*done / painted / written / taken …*). Regular participles end in *-ed*; many common verbs are irregular (*built, sold, caught, spoken*).

| Tense | Active | Passive |
|---|---|---|
| Present simple | clean(s) / make(s) | am / is / are + cleaned / made |
| Past simple | cleaned / made | was / were + cleaned / made |

Present simple passive:

- Most of our rubbish is recycled. (not *is recycle*)
- Thousands of emails are sent from this office every day.
- How is your surname spelt?
- I'm not usually asked to give presentations.

Past simple passive:

- The match was cancelled because of the storm.
- Were you told about the change of plan?
- The thieves weren't caught.
- How many people were injured?

Choosing between active and passive often depends on **what the sentence is about**. Compare:

- *The factory employs 300 people.* (about the factory)
- *300 people are employed by the factory.* (about the people)

Note: intransitive verbs (no object — *die, disappear, happen, fall, resign*) have **no passive**: ❌ *The accident was happened* ✅ *The accident happened*.

#### Question
A tour guide is explaining the history of a castle. Complete: "This castle ___ in the twelfth century by a Norman lord."

#### Answer
"This castle **was built** in the twelfth century by a Norman lord." The castle did not do the building — it received the action — so a passive is needed, and *in the twelfth century* fixes the time as past simple: *was + built*. "Built" alone is active and would mean the castle built something. "Was build" uses the base form instead of the past participle *built*. "Is built" is present simple, which does not fit a finished event in the twelfth century.

#### Correct variant

2. was built (Correct)

### Question 45

#### Question
Which sentence correctly uses the passive to describe a general fact?

#### Answer
"Rice is grown in many parts of Asia." A general, repeated fact takes the present simple passive: *is + grown* (irregular past participle of *grow*). "Rice grows in many parts of Asia" is grammatical but active, so it does not test the passive and changes the focus. "Rice is grow" and "rice is growed" are both wrong: the passive needs a past participle, and *grow* is irregular (*grow – grew – grown*).

In B, *collapse* is intransitive — it has no object — so it cannot be made passive; *The old bridge collapsed* is the only correct form. The same applies to verbs like *happen, die, disappear, fall, resign*.

### Question 48

#### Question
Spot the mistake. A student wrote: "How much this painting was sold for at the auction?"

#### Answer
The correct question is "How much **was this painting sold** for at the auction?" In a passive question the auxiliary *be* (*was*) must come before the subject (*this painting*), exactly as in any other question. The student kept statement word order after the question word. The passive itself (*was sold*) is correctly formed; the problem is only the order of *was* and the subject. Options that change *sold* to *sell* or add *did* break the passive form.
