# Unit 55 — Verb (+ object) + to … (I want you to …)

# Theory

## A Verbs that work with or without an object

*want, expect, ask, help, would like, would prefer* can be followed directly by *to …* or by **object + to …**:

| verb + to … | verb + object + to … |
|---|---|
| I want **to leave** early. | I want **you to leave** early. |
| We expected **to win**. | We expected **our team to win**. |
| Would you like **to sit** down? | Would you like **me to sit** down? |
| She asked **to see** the manager. | She asked **the waiter to bring** the bill. |

We do **not** say *want that*: ❌ ~~Do you want that I help you?~~ ✅ Do you want **me to help** you?

*Help* works with or without *to*: *Can you help me **(to) carry** these bags?*

## B Verbs that always take object + to …

| | | |
|---|---|---|
| tell | advise | remind |
| warn | invite | encourage |
| persuade | get | force |
| teach | allow | enable |

- The doctor advised **him to rest** for a week.
- Can you remind **me to send** that invoice?
- The guide warned **us not to swim** near the rocks.
- I couldn't fix the bike myself, so I got **a friend to look** at it.
- Her scholarship enabled **her to study** abroad.

These verbs are common in the **passive**: *I was advised **to rest**.* / *Are we allowed **to take** photos here?*

*Suggest* is **not** in this group: ❌ ~~She suggested me to wait.~~ ✅ She suggested **(that) I wait**. / She suggested **waiting**.

## C make and let — no *to*

**make somebody do** and **let somebody do** take the bare infinitive:

- The film made **me cry**. (not ~~made me to cry~~)
- Her parents wouldn't let **her stay** out late. (= wouldn't allow her to stay)
- Let **me help** you with that.

But in the **passive**, *make* takes *to*: *We were made **to wait** outside.* (= They made us wait outside.) *Let* has no common passive form; use *be allowed to* instead.

| active | passive |
|---|---|
| They made us **wait**. | We were made **to wait**. |
| They allowed us **to leave**. | We were allowed **to leave**. |
| They let us **leave**. | We were allowed **to leave**. |

**See also:** suggest → Units 34, 53; tell/ask somebody to … → Unit 48D; Verb + -ing → Unit 53; Verb + to … → Unit 54; Verb + to … and -ing → Units 56–58; help → Unit 57C.

### Question 31

#### Question
You are going to the shop and you want to know if your flatmate wants you to buy anything. Which question is correct?

#### Answer
**"Do you want me to get anything from the shop?"** *Want* is followed by *object + to …*; the object pronoun *me* comes directly after *want*. "Do you want that I get" copies a structure from other languages that English does not use with *want*. "Do you want I get" is missing both the object form and *to*. "Do you want me getting" uses -ing, which *want* does not take.

#### Correct variant
¯\_(ツ)_/¯

#### Variants
1. Do you want that I get anything from the shop?
2. Do you want me to get anything from the shop? (Correct)
3. Do you want I get anything from the shop?
4. Do you want me getting anything from the shop?

### Question 32

#### Question
Your sister said: "Don't go near the edge of the cliff." Which sentence reports her words correctly?

#### Answer
**"She warned me not to go near the edge."** *Warn* takes *object + (not) to + infinitive*. "Warned me to not go" puts *not* after *to*, which is not the standard form. "Warned that I don't go" — *warn* can take a *that*-clause, but it would need a modal or different tense (*warned me that I shouldn't go*), and the option as written is ungrammatical. "Warned me not going" wrongly uses -ing.

#### Correct variant
¯\_(ツ)_/¯

#### Variants
1. She warned me to not go near the edge.
2. She warned that I don't go near the edge.
3. She warned me not to go near the edge. (Correct)
4. She warned me not going near the edge.

#### Answer

"Made everyone cried" wrongly uses a past form after the object.

### Question 34

#### Question
At the airport security desk, the officers insisted that all passengers remove their shoes. Which passive sentence describes this correctly?

#### Answer
**"All passengers were made to remove their shoes."** In the active voice, *make* takes the bare infinitive (*They made us remove our shoes*), but in the passive voice *to* reappears: *be made to do*. "Were made remove" keeps the active pattern in the passive, which is wrong. "Were let to remove" is not a natural form — *let* has no usual passive; *were allowed to remove* would be needed, but that changes the meaning (permission rather than compulsion). "Were made removing" wrongly uses -ing.

#### Correct variant
¯\_(ツ)_/¯

#### Variants
1. All passengers were made remove their shoes.
2. All passengers were made to remove their shoes. (Correct)
3. All passengers were let to remove their shoes.
4. All passengers were made removing their shoes.

### Question 35

#### Question
Which of these sentences contains a mistake?

#### Answer
Sentence 3 is wrong: **"My tutor suggested me to read the introduction first"** should be **"My tutor suggested (that) I read the introduction first"** or **"… suggested reading the introduction first."** Unlike *advise* or *tell*, *suggest* never takes *object + to …*. The other sentences are correct: "let me pay" uses the bare infinitive after *let*; "persuaded her to come" is a correct *verb + object + to …* structure; "help you move" is fine because *help* can be used with or without *to*.

#### Correct variant
¯\_(ツ)_/¯

#### Variants
1. Please let me pay for dinner this time.
2. We finally persuaded her to come with us.
3. My tutor suggested me to read the introduction first. (Correct)
4. I'll help you move the sofa if you like.
