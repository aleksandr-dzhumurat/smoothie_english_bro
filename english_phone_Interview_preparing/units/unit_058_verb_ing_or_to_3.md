# Unit 58 — Verb + -ing or to … 3 (like / would like etc.)

# Theory

## A like / love / hate

For **repeated actions or general preferences**, *like, love, hate* can take either -ing or *to …*:

- Do you like **swimming** in the sea? / Do you like **to swim** in the sea?
- Tomás hates **queuing**. / Tomás hates **to queue**.
- I don't like **being interrupted**. / I don't like **to be interrupted**.
- I don't like **people phoning** me late at night. / … **people to phone** me late at night.

Two important exceptions:

**(1)** When you talk about a situation that **already exists (or existed)**, use **-ing** only:

- Yumi lives by the sea now. She loves **living** there. (she lives there and she loves it)
- Do you like **being** self-employed? (you are self-employed — do you like it?)
- My first job was in a call centre. I hated **working** there.

**(2)** Sometimes *like doing* and *like to do* differ in meaning:

| I like **doing** | I like **to do** |
|---|---|
| = I do it and I enjoy it. | = I choose to do it because I think it's a good idea (maybe not for enjoyment). |
| I like **ironing** — it's relaxing. | I like **to iron** my shirts the night before so I'm not rushed in the morning. |

*Enjoy* and *mind* take **-ing only**: ❌ ~~I enjoy to travel~~ ✅ I enjoy **travelling**; ❌ ~~I don't mind to wait~~ ✅ I don't mind **waiting**.

## B would like / would love / would hate / would prefer

These are followed by **to …** and refer to a **specific wish**, not a general preference:

- I'**d like to visit** Kyoto one day.
- What **would** you **like to eat**?
- I **wouldn't like to be** in his shoes.
- **Would** you **prefer to walk** or take the bus?

Compare **like** and **would like**:

- I like **playing** chess. / I like **to play** chess. (in general)
- I'd like **to play** chess this evening. (I want to, now)

**would mind** is followed by **-ing**: *Would you mind **opening** the window?* (not ~~mind to open~~)

## C would like to have (done)

**would like / love / hate / prefer to have done** = regret about something that did not happen:

- It's a pity you missed the concert. I would like **to have seen** your face when they played your song.
- We'd love **to have stayed** longer, but the last train was at ten.
- I'd hate **to have been** the person who broke the news.
- I'd prefer **to have travelled** by train rather than by coach.

**See also:** enjoy/mind → Unit 53; would like → Units 37E, 55A; prefer → Unit 59.

### Question 34

#### Question
Compare: (a) "I like to check the tyres before a long drive." (b) "I like checking the tyres before a long drive." Which statement is correct?

#### Answer
**(a) suggests I choose to do it because it's sensible, not necessarily for pleasure; (b) suggests I actually enjoy the activity.** *Like to do* = I think it is a good idea to do it; *like doing* = I do it and it gives me pleasure. Option 1 is wrong because there is a difference. Option 3 reverses the meanings. Option 4 is wrong: both are grammatical — *like* can take both forms.

#### Correct variant
¯\_(ツ)_/¯

#### Variants
1. The two sentences mean exactly the same; there is never any difference between *like to do* and *like doing*.
2. (a) suggests I choose to do it because it's sensible; (b) suggests I actually enjoy doing it. (Correct)
3. (a) suggests I enjoy it; (b) suggests I do it only out of duty.
4. Only (b) is correct; *like* is never followed by *to …*.

### Question 36

#### Question
Your favourite band played in your town last month, but you were abroad and missed the concert. Which sentence expresses your regret?

#### Answer
**"I would like to have seen them."** *Would like to have done* refers to something you regret not doing or not being able to do in the past. "I would like to see them" refers to a future wish, not a past regret. "I would have liked seeing them" is possible in some varieties but is not the target structure and uses -ing after *like* in a way the book does not teach here. "I would like to saw them" is ungrammatical — an infinitive cannot be a past form.

#### Variants

2. I would like to have seen them. (Correct)
3. I would have liked seeing them.
4. I would like to saw them.

### Question 37

#### Question
Which of these sentences contains a mistake?

#### Answer
Sentence 4 is wrong: **"Would you mind to turn the heating up?"** should be **"Would you mind turning the heating up?"** Unlike *would like / would love / would prefer*, *would mind* is always followed by -ing. The others are correct: "hates to be kept waiting" and "hates being kept waiting" are both possible for a general dislike; "would prefer to leave" is a correct *would prefer + to …*; "enjoy not having" is a correct negative -ing after *enjoy*.

#### Correct variant
¯\_(ツ)_/¯

#### Variants
1. My boss hates to be kept waiting.
2. I'd prefer to leave before the traffic gets bad.
3. On holiday I enjoy not having to check my email.
4. Would you mind to turn the heating up? (Correct)
