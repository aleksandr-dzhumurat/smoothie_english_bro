# Unit 64 — to … , for … and so that …

# Theory

## A to … for purpose

The **to-infinitive** answers the question *why?* – it tells us the purpose of an action, a possession or a need:
- I went online **to renew** my passport.
- What do you need **to build** a website?
- She rang the bell **to let** the staff know she had arrived.
- This email is **to remind** you about Friday’s deadline.
- The museum employs guards **to protect** the paintings.

## B a place to park, something to eat, work to do

A noun or pronoun can be followed by **to …** to say what it is for or what can/must be done with it:
- It’s hard to find **a table to work at** in this café. (= a table where you can work)
- Do you want **something to drink**? (= something that you can drink)
- I’ve got **loads of emails to answer**. (= emails that I must answer)

If the verb normally has a preposition, keep it at the end:
- Is there **a hook to hang** my coat **on**?
- He’s lonely – he has **nobody to talk to**.
- I need **a pen to sign this with**.

Nouns like **money / time / chance / opportunity / energy / courage** + **to …**:
- My grandparents gave me **money to pay** the deposit.
- Did you get **a chance to look** at the report?
- We need **a week to decide**.

## C for … and to …

| for + noun | to + verb |
|---|---|
| We stopped **for coffee**. | We stopped **to buy coffee**. |
| I went out **for some fresh air**. | I went out **to get some fresh air**. |

- **for somebody to do something**: There was no room **for the children to play**, so they went to the park.
- **for -ing** describes what a thing is **used for**: *This knife is **for cutting** bread.*
- But **for -ing** is NOT used to say why somebody does something: *I went upstairs **to fetch** my keys.* (❌ *for fetching my keys*)
- **What … for?** asks about purpose: *What’s this button **for**?* / *What did you say that **for**?* (= why?)

## D so that

Use **so that** (not *to …*) when the purpose has its own subject or contains **can/could/will/would**:
- He’s saving money **so that he can travel** next year.
- We left early **so that we wouldn’t miss** the train.
- I texted her the address **so that she could find** the house.

*That* can be dropped: *I left early **so** I wouldn’t miss the train.*

Use **so that** rather than *to …* when the subject changes: *I spoke slowly **so that everyone could follow**.* (not *to everyone follow*)

### Question 35

#### Question
Complete the sentence: "I went to the post office ______ a parcel to my sister."

#### Answer
"I went to the post office **to send** a parcel to my sister." The to-infinitive expresses why somebody does something. *For sending* is a classic error: *for -ing* describes what a thing is used for (*a box for sending parcels*), not the purpose of a person’s action. *For send* is ungrammatical, and *so that send* lacks a subject and modal verb.

#### Correct variant
¯\_(ツ)_/¯

#### Variants
1. for sending
2. to send (Correct)
3. for send
4. so that send

### Question 36

#### Question
Which sentence is correct?

#### Answer
"Is there **a chair to sit on**?" When a noun is followed by *to …* and the verb needs a preposition (*sit on a chair*), the preposition stays at the end of the phrase. Dropping it (*a chair to sit*) leaves *sit* without its preposition; *a chair for sit on* uses *for* with a bare infinitive, which is impossible; and *a chair to sit on it* wrongly repeats the object as a pronoun.

#### Correct variant
¯\_(ツ)_/¯

#### Variants
1. Is there a chair to sit?
2. Is there a chair for sit on?
3. Is there a chair to sit on? (Correct)
4. Is there a chair to sit on it?

#### Answer

#### Answer

### Question 39

#### Question
Which of these sentences contains a mistake, and why?

A. This spray is for cleaning glass.
B. She turned the music down so that the baby could sleep.
C. We need somebody to help us with the move.
D. I opened the window for letting some air in.

#### Answer
Sentence **D** is wrong. It describes why a person did something, so a to-infinitive is needed: "I opened the window **to let** some air in." *For -ing* is only used to say what a thing is used for, as in sentence A (*a spray for cleaning glass*), which is correct. Sentence B correctly uses *so that* with *could* and a different subject, and sentence C correctly uses noun + *to …*.

#### Correct variant
¯\_(ツ)_/¯

#### Variants
1. Sentence A – *for cleaning* should be *to clean*.
2. Sentence B – *so that* cannot be used with *could*.
3. Sentence C – *somebody to help* should be *somebody for helping*.
4. Sentence D – *for letting* should be *to let*. (Correct)
