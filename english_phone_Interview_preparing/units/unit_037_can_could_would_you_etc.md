# Unit 37 — can/could/would you … ? etc. (Requests, offers, permission and invitations)

# Theory

## A. Asking people to do things (requests)

Use **Can you …?** or **Could you …?** to ask somebody to do something. *Could* is a little more polite/tentative:

- **Can you** pass me the stapler, please?
- **Could you** hold the lift for a second?
- Excuse me, **could you** tell me where platform 4 is?

A softer, longer form is **Do you think you could …?**:

- **Do you think you could** look after my plants while I'm away? ❌ *Do you think you can*

## B. Asking for things

To ask for something (in a shop, café, etc.), use **Can I have …? / Could I have …? / Can I get …?**:

- **Can I have** a receipt, please?
- **Could we have** two more chairs, please?
- **Can I get** a large latte to take away?

**May I have …?** is also possible and more formal:

- **May I have** your attention, please?

## C. Asking to do things (permission)

Use **Can I …?** or **Could I …?** to ask if you are allowed to do something:

- (on the phone) Hello, **can I speak to** Mrs Okafor, please?
- **Could I** leave my suitcase here for an hour?
- **Do you think I could** borrow your umbrella?

**May I …?** is more formal:

- **May I** come in?

Other common ways to ask permission:

| structure | example |
|---|---|
| Do you mind if I …? | **Do you mind if I** open the window? |
| Is it all right if I …? | **Is it all right if I** park here? |
| Is it OK if I …? | **Is it OK if I** bring a friend? |

Note the replies: to *Do you mind if I …?* the "yes, go ahead" answer is **No, not at all** (= I don't mind).

## D. Offering and inviting

**Can I …?** is used to **offer** to do something:

- **Can I** carry that for you? – No, thanks, I'm fine.
- **Can I** get you a glass of water? – That would be lovely.

To **offer** something or **invite**, use **Would you like …?** – never *Do you like* (which asks about general tastes):

| Would you like …? (offer / invitation) | Do you like …? (general preference) |
|---|---|
| **Would you like** a biscuit? (= do you want one now?) | **Do you like** biscuits? (= in general) |
| **Would you like to** join us for lunch? | **Do you like** eating out? |

**I'd like … (= I would like)** is a polite way of saying what you want:

- (at the station) **I'd like** a return to Bristol, please.
- (in a restaurant) **I'd like to** see the dessert menu, please.

### Question 26

#### Question
You are at a friend's house. It's hot and you want to open a window. Which is a correct and natural way to ask permission?

#### Answer
"**Do you mind if I** open the window?" This is a standard permission structure: *Do you mind if I + present simple*. "Do you mind if I would open" wrongly uses *would* after *if*. "Do you mind me open the window" is not a possible pattern (you could say *Do you mind me opening*, with -ing). "Would you like I open the window" is ungrammatical – *would you like* is followed by a noun or *to*-infinitive and is for offers, not for asking permission.

#### Correct variant
¯\_(ツ)_/¯

#### Variants
1. Do you mind if I would open the window?
2. Do you mind if I open the window? (Correct)
3. Do you mind me open the window?
4. Would you like I open the window?

### Question 28

#### Question
You want to ask a stranger a big favour – to help you carry a heavy pram up a long flight of stairs. Which request is the most polite and grammatically correct?

#### Answer
"**Do you think you could** help me carry this up the stairs?" *Do you think you could …?* is a longer, softer request form, suitable for a big favour from a stranger. "Do you think you can" is not the form the book recommends – *could* is required after *Do you think*. "Can you to help" wrongly adds *to* after *can*. "Would you like to help me" is an invitation/offer form, not a polite request for help – it sounds as if you are offering the stranger the pleasure of carrying your pram.

### Question 30

#### Question
A shop assistant asks a customer, "Do you like a bag for that?" Why is this sentence wrong, and what should the assistant say?

#### Answer
The assistant is making an **offer**, and offers use **Would you like …?**: *Would you like a bag for that?* *Do you like …?* asks about a person's general tastes (e.g. *Do you like shopping?*), so it does not work as an offer. The sentence is not fine as it stands, *Can you like* is not English, and *Are you liking a bag* misuses the continuous with a state verb and is not an offer either.

#### Correct variant
¯\_(ツ)_/¯

#### Variants
1. It's fine – "Do you like" and "Would you like" mean the same in shops.
2. It should be "Can you like a bag for that?", because *can* is used for offers.
3. Offers use "Would you like …?": the assistant should say "Would you like a bag for that?" (Correct)
4. It should be "Are you liking a bag for that?", because the offer is happening now.
