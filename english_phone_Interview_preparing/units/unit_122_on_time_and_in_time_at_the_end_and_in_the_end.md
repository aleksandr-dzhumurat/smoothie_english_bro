# Unit 122 — on time and in time / at the end and in the end

# Theory

## A on time vs in time

| expression | meaning | typical use |
|---|---|---|
| **on time** | punctual; exactly at the planned/scheduled time | *The 9.15 bus left on time.* |
| **in time (for sth / to do sth)** | soon enough; before a deadline or before it is too late | *Did the parcel arrive in time for her birthday?* |

- **on time** is about schedules. If the seminar was due to begin at 10.00 and began at 10.00, it began *on time*. *The wedding ran smoothly; everything happened on time.*
- **in time** is about having enough time to do or catch something: *Hurry, or we won't be back in time to see the kick-off.*
- The opposite of *in time* is **too late**: *We got back too late to see the kick-off.*
- **just in time** = almost too late: *The lifeguard reached the swimmer just in time.* / *I grabbed my phone just in time before it fell into the water.*

Common mistake: ❌ *Please arrive in time for the interview at 10.00 sharp* when you mean punctuality → ✅ *Please arrive on time.*

## B at the end vs in the end

**at the end (of something)** = at the point when something finishes. It is normally followed by *of* + noun:
- at the end of the week / at the end of March / at the end of the lecture / at the end of the match / at the end of the book
- *Everyone stood up at the end of the performance.*
- *My contract runs out at the end of the year.*

Do not say ~~in the end of March~~. The opposite of *at the end* is **at the beginning** (not ~~in the beginning~~): *Rent is due at the beginning of the month.*

**in the end** = finally, eventually; it introduces the final result after a process, doubt or difficulty:
- *We argued about it for hours. In the end we agreed to split the cost.*
- *She kept changing her mind about the colour. In the end she chose grey.*
- *The keys were nowhere to be found, so in the end we called a locksmith.* (not ~~at the end~~)

The opposite of *in the end* is **at first**: *At first the puppy was shy, but in the end it followed me everywhere.*

## C Quick test

- Is there an *of* + noun after it, marking the moment something finishes? → **at the end**.
- Does it mean "finally / after everything"? → **in the end**.
- Is it about being punctual? → **on time**.
- Is it about being early enough / not too late? → **in time**.

**See also:** at/on/in (time) → Unit 121.

### Question 35

#### Question
Which of these sentences is wrong, and why?

#### Answer
**"I'm getting a bonus in the end of the year" is wrong.** When we refer to the point at which a period finishes, we use *at the end of …*: *at the end of the year*. *In the end* means "finally" and is never followed by *of*. The other sentences are all correct uses.

#### Correct variant
¯\_(ツ)_/¯

#### Variants
1. "The audience clapped at the end of the speech" — should be *in the end of*.
2. "I'm getting a bonus in the end of the year" — should be *at the end of the year*. (Correct)
3. "We couldn't agree, so in the end we tossed a coin" — should be *at the end*.
4. "Term starts at the beginning of September" — should be *in the beginning of*.

### Question 37

#### Question
