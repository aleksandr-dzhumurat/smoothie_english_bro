# Unit 138 — Phrasal verbs 2 in/out

# Theory

## A in and out with movement

*In* = into a room, building, car, water etc. *Out* = out of it. Both are used **without** a following noun; when you name the place, use *into* / *out of*.

| in | out |
|---|---|
| The door was open, so the cat walked in. | Wait in the taxi — don't get out. |
| Use this code to let yourself in. | I forgot my card and got locked out. |
| He ran to the pool and jumped in. | She swam a few lengths and climbed out. |
| We're moving in at the end of the month. | Tom leaned out of the window and looked out. |
| I checked in at the hotel at 3 pm. | We checked out early to catch the flight. |

Similar verbs: *go in / come in / break in* and *go out / get out / move out / let somebody out*.

| Compare in / into | Compare out / out of |
|---|---|
| We're moving **in** next week. | He climbed **out**. |
| We're moving **into** the new house next week. | He climbed **out of** the taxi. |

❌ *She got out the car.* ✅ *She got out of the car.* / *She got out.*

## B Other verbs + in

| Phrasal verb | Meaning | Example |
|---|---|---|
| drop in | visit somebody without arranging it | Feel free to drop in whenever you're passing. |
| join in | take part in something already happening | The kids were dancing, so Grandpa joined in. |
| plug in (a machine) | connect it to the electricity | The printer won't work — it isn't plugged in. |
| take somebody in | deceive them | The fake website took thousands of people in. |
| fill in / fill out (a form) | write the information on it | Please fill in this form and hand it to reception. |

## C Other verbs + out

| Phrasal verb | Meaning | Example |
|---|---|---|
| eat out | eat at a restaurant | We were too tired to cook, so we ate out. |
| drop out (of a course / a race) | leave before finishing | Half the runners dropped out before the finish. |
| get out of (something arranged) | avoid doing it | I'd love to get out of the dinner on Friday. |
| leave something out | omit it | You've left out the date on the letter. |
| cross something out | draw a line through it | Cross out the wrong answer and write the right one. |

Remember the pronoun rule (Unit 137): *fill it in*, *cross them out*, *leave it out*, *plug it in* — not ~~fill in it~~.

### Question 37

#### Question
"The taxi stopped outside the theatre and we all ______." Which option is correct?

#### Answer
The correct answer is "we all **got out**." When we do not name the vehicle, we use *out* on its own. If we name it, we need *out of*: "got out of the taxi." "Got out the taxi" (without *of*) and "got out from" are wrong, and "got off" is used for buses, trains and bikes rather than for a taxi or car.

#### Correct variant
¯\_(ツ)_/¯

#### Variants
1. got out the taxi
2. got out (Correct)
3. got out from
4. got off

### Question 39

#### Question
"Farid started an engineering degree, but he ______ after the first year and became a chef." Choose the right phrasal verb.

#### Answer
The correct answer is "he **dropped out** after the first year." *Drop out (of a course / university)* means to leave before finishing. *Drop in* means to visit somebody casually, *get out of* means to avoid doing something you had arranged, and *leave out* means to omit something — none of these fits leaving a degree.

#### Correct variant
¯\_(ツ)_/¯

#### Variants
1. dropped in
2. got out
3. dropped out (Correct)
4. left out

### Question 41

#### Question
At the passport office: "There's a mistake in the address on this form." "Never mind — just ______ and write the correct one above." Which is the correct way to complete the reply?

#### Answer
The correct reply is "just **cross it out** and write the correct one above." *Cross something out* = draw a line through it. With a pronoun object, *it* goes between *cross* and *out* ("cross out it" is wrong). *Fill it in* would mean complete the form, and *leave it out* would mean omit it, which don't match "write the correct one above."

#### Correct variant
¯\_(ツ)_/¯
