# Unit 51 — Auxiliary verbs (have/do/can etc.) I think so / I hope so etc.

# Theory

## A Auxiliary verb + main verb

Most verb phrases contain an **auxiliary** (helping) verb and a **main** verb:

| auxiliary | main verb | example |
|---|---|---|
| have / has / had | past participle | We **have** *finished* the report. |
| am / is / are / was / were | -ing or past participle | The bridge **was** *repaired* last spring. |
| can / will / must / might … | infinitive | Olivia **can't** *attend* the meeting. |
| do / does / did | infinitive | Why **do** you *need* a new laptop? |

The auxiliary can stand alone when you don't want to repeat the whole phrase:

- 'Have you paid the bill?' 'Yes, I **have**.' (= I have paid the bill)
- Marta wasn't listening, but Tom **was**.
- Ben could help us, but he **won't**.

For the present simple and past simple, the "stand-in" auxiliary is **do/does/did**:

- 'Do you eat fish?' 'Yes, I **do**.'
- 'Does Luca still work here?' 'He **did**, but he **doesn't** any more.'

You also use a bare auxiliary to contradict what someone says:

- 'You're standing on my foot.' 'No, I'**m not**.'
- 'You didn't send the email.' 'Yes, I **did**.'

## B Short questions to show interest or surprise

*Have you? / Isn't he? / Did they?* etc. are echo-questions. They show the listener is interested or surprised:

- 'I've just bought a boat.' '**Have you?** Where do you keep it?'
- 'Nadia isn't coming.' '**Isn't she?** Why not?'
- 'It snowed in May.' '**Did it?** That's unusual.'

Use the auxiliary that matches the tense of the statement; for present/past simple, use *do/does/did*.

## C so and neither

To say that the same is true for someone else, use **so** (after a positive statement) or **neither** (after a negative one) + auxiliary + subject. Note the word order: **verb before subject**.

- 'I'm freezing.' '**So am I**.' (not ~~so I am~~)
- 'I've never been to Japan.' '**Neither have I**.'
- Jo can't swim and **neither can** her brother.
- I failed the test and **so did** Kim.

Alternatives to *neither*: **nor** (*Nor do I*) and **not … either** (*I don't either*).

## D I think so / I hope so etc.

*So* replaces a whole clause after verbs of opinion: *think, suppose, expect, hope, guess, be afraid*.

- 'Is the shop open on Sundays?' 'I **think so**.' (= I think it's open)
- 'Will Dan be at the party?' 'I **expect so**.'

The negative forms differ:

| positive | negative |
|---|---|
| I think so / I expect so | I **don't think so** / I **don't expect so** |
| I hope so / I'm afraid so | I **hope not** / I'm **afraid not** |
| I guess so / I suppose so | I **guess not** / I **suppose not** |

- 'Will it be cold tonight?' 'I **hope not**.' ❌ ~~I don't hope so~~ ✅ I hope not.
- 'Is the flight delayed?' 'I'm **afraid so**.'

### Question 35

#### Question
Your colleague says: "I usually cycle to work, but yesterday I ______." Which auxiliary correctly fills the gap?

#### Answer
The correct form is **didn't**: "I usually cycle to work, but yesterday I didn't." The first clause is present simple (*cycle*), but *yesterday* shifts the second clause to the past simple, whose stand-in auxiliary is *did/didn't*. *Don't* would refer to the present, *wasn't* would need a continuous form (*wasn't cycling*) that isn't in the first clause, and *haven't* (present perfect) clashes with the finished time *yesterday*.

#### Correct variant
¯\_(ツ)_/¯

#### Variants

2. I usually cycle to work, but yesterday I wasn't.
3. I usually cycle to work, but yesterday I didn't. (Correct)
4. I usually cycle to work, but yesterday I haven't.

### Question 36

#### Question
A friend tells you: "I've never eaten sushi." The same is true for you. Which reply is correct?

#### Answer
**"Neither have I."** After a negative statement you agree with *neither* (or *nor*) + auxiliary + subject, and the auxiliary must match the tense of the statement — here present perfect *have*. "So have I" is wrong because *so* is used after positive statements. "Neither I have" has the wrong word order (the verb must come before the subject). "Neither do I" uses the wrong auxiliary; *do* would fit a present simple statement such as *I never eat sushi*.

2. Neither have I. (Correct)

#### Answer
Sentence 2 is wrong: **"I passed the exam and so Rob did"** should be **"… and so did Rob."** After *so* and *neither* the auxiliary must come before the subject. The other sentences are correct: "No, I'm not" correctly contradicts a present continuous statement with the bare auxiliary; "I'm afraid not" is the standard negative of *I'm afraid so*; "Nor can Elsa" uses *nor* as an alternative to *neither*, with correct inversion.
