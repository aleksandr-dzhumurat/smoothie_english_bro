# Unit 72 — a/an and the

# Theory

## A First mention → a/an; later mention → the

When we mention a countable thing for the **first time**, we use **a/an**. Once the listener knows which thing we mean, we switch to **the**.

> I ordered a pizza and a salad. The pizza was cold, but the salad was fresh.

*a pizza* — the listener hears about it for the first time; *the pizza* — now we both know which pizza (the one I ordered).

More examples of the switch:

- A boy and a girl were waiting at the bus stop. The boy was reading, and the girl was on her phone.
- On our trip we rented a cottage. Some evenings we cooked at the cottage, other evenings we found a pub.

## B the = a specific thing; a/an = what kind of thing

| a/an (not specific / a type) | the (a specific one) |
|---|---|
| Nina sat down on a bench. (one of several in the park) | Nina sat down on the bench under the oak tree. |
| Do you have a bike? | I've just repaired the bike. (= my bike) |
| We stayed in a tiny hostel. (a kind of hostel) | The hostel we stayed in was tiny. |

**a/an** answers "what kind?"; **the** answers "which one?".

## C the when it is clear which one we mean

In a given situation there is often only one obvious candidate, so **the** is used even on first mention:

- In a room: *Could you open the window?* (= the window in this room), *the floor*, *the ceiling*, *the door*.
- In a town: *We walked to the station.* (= the station of that town)
- In a shop: *Can I see the manager?* (= the manager of this shop)

We also say **(go to) the bank / the post office** and **(go to) the doctor / the dentist**, because the speaker normally has a particular one in mind:

- I need to go to the post office before it closes.
- Ahmed has a toothache, so he's going to the dentist tomorrow.

Compare **the** (specific/usual) with **a/an** (any / type / job):

| the | a/an |
|---|---|
| I'm going to the bank at lunchtime. | Is there a bank in this village? |
| She hates going to the dentist. | Her cousin is a dentist. |

## D a/an in rates and frequency

**a/an** means "per" in expressions of frequency, price and speed:

- twice a week / three times a day / once a year
- €2 a litre / £9 an hour
- She trains for two hours a day, five days a week.

### Question 56

#### Question
Fill the gaps: "Last night I watched ___ documentary and ___ comedy. ___ documentary was fascinating, but ___ comedy wasn't funny at all."

#### Answer
The correct version is *a documentary and a comedy. The documentary was fascinating, but the comedy wasn't funny.* On first mention we introduce the two programmes with **a**; the second time, the listener already knows which documentary and which comedy we mean, so we use **the**. Using *the* on the first mention (option 2) assumes the listener already knows which programmes, and repeating *a* on the second mention (option 3) sounds as if we are talking about different programmes.

2. the … the … The … the

### Question 57

#### Question
Which sentence is correct in a conversation between two colleagues in an office with one printer?

#### Answer
The correct sentence is *Could you switch off the printer before you leave?* In an office there is one obvious printer, so it is clear which one is meant, and **the** is used even though the printer has not been mentioned before. *A printer* would suggest "any printer, it doesn't matter which one", which is odd here; *printer* alone is a singular countable noun without an article, which is not possible; *some printer* is not used for a single specific object.

#### Correct variant
¯\_(ツ)_/¯

#### Variants
1. Could you switch off a printer before you leave?
2. Could you switch off the printer before you leave? (Correct)
3. Could you switch off printer before you leave?
4. Could you switch off some printer before you leave?

### Question 58

#### Question
Compare the two sentences. Which explanation of the difference is right?

A. Marta has gone to the doctor.
B. Marta's sister is a doctor.

#### Answer
Both sentences are correct, but they use the articles for different reasons. In A, *go to the doctor* is a fixed expression like *go to the bank / the dentist*: the speaker has a particular, usual doctor in mind, so **the** is used. In B we are saying what kind of person (which job) Marta's sister is, and jobs always take **a/an**. Option 1 wrongly claims A is a mistake; option 3 wrongly says *the* is needed for jobs; option 4 misreads *the doctor* as "the only doctor in the world".

#### Correct variant
¯\_(ツ)_/¯

#### Variants
1. Sentence A is wrong; it should be *gone to a doctor* because no particular doctor was mentioned.
2. Both are correct: *the doctor* is the usual expression for a visit to a specific/usual doctor, while jobs are stated with *a/an*. (Correct)
3. Sentence B is wrong; it should be *is the doctor* because we know which sister.
4. Sentence A means there is only one doctor in the country.

### Question 59

#### Question
Spot the mistake: "We spent the weekend in a small town by the sea. The hotel we stayed in was right on the beach, and it was a hotel where we stayed that had the best view."

#### Answer
The faulty clause is *it was a hotel where we stayed that had the best view*. Once the hotel has been identified (by the clause *where we stayed*, and by the earlier mention *The hotel we stayed in*), it is a specific hotel and needs **the**: *the hotel where we stayed had the best view*. **a/an** is for saying what kind of thing something is (*a small town*, *a very cheap hotel*); **the** is for a specific, identified one. *A small town* is correct (first mention, a type of place), *the sea* and *the beach* are correct because it is clear which ones are meant.

#### Correct variant
¯\_(ツ)_/¯

#### Variants
1. *a small town* should be *the small town*.
2. *the sea* should be *a sea*.
3. *a hotel where we stayed* should be *the hotel where we stayed*, because the relative clause identifies a specific hotel. (Correct)
4. *The hotel we stayed in* should be *A hotel we stayed in*.

### Question 60

#### Question
A customer asks about the price of cherries. Which reply is grammatically correct?

#### Answer
The correct reply is *They're £4 a kilo.* In expressions of price, frequency and speed **a/an** means "per": *£4 a kilo*, *twice a week*, *60 miles an hour*. *The kilo* is not used in this pattern in British English, *£4 for kilo* omits the article that a singular countable noun requires, and *£4 a kilos* combines a singular article with a plural noun.

#### Correct variant
¯\_(ツ)_/¯

#### Variants
1. They're £4 the kilo.
2. They're £4 for kilo.
3. They're £4 a kilos.
4. They're £4 a kilo. (Correct)
