# Unit 89 — both / both of / neither / neither of / either / either of

# Theory

## A both / neither / either = two things

These three words are used only when talking about **two** items.

| word | meaning | example |
|---|---|---|
| both | the one and the other | Both bridges are closed for repairs. (❌ the both bridges) |
| neither | not the one and not the other | Neither bridge is open. |
| either | the one or the other, it doesn’t matter which | We can cross by either bridge. |

With a negative verb, *either* = "not one or the other":
- I haven’t tried either bakery yet.

They can stand alone without a noun:
- "Tea or coffee?" "Either – I don’t mind."
- "Is Lars Swedish or Danish?" "Neither. He’s Norwegian."
- "Do you prefer the blue or the green?" "Both, actually."

## B both of / neither of / either of

Before *the / these / those / my / your / Sofia’s* etc. we use **of**:
- Both of the applicants were impressive.
- Neither of the routes we took was quick.
- I don’t like either of those colours.

Do not say ❌ *both of applicants* (no determiner). After **both**, *of* is optional before a determiner:
- Both of my sisters are doctors. **or** Both my sisters are doctors.

Before **us / you / them**, *of* is compulsory:
- Can either of you drive? (to two people)
- I emailed two suppliers, but neither of them replied.
- Both of us were exhausted. (❌ both us)

After **neither of + plural** the verb may be singular or plural:
- Neither of them has a key. / Neither of them have a key.

## C both … and / neither … nor / either … or

| pattern | example |
|---|---|
| both … and … | Both Ines and Tarek passed. / The soup was both cold and salty. |
| neither … nor … | Neither Ines nor Tarek passed. / We neither heard nor saw the explosion. |
| either … or … | She’s either at the gym or at the library. / Either you pay now, or you lose the booking. |

## D Two vs more than two

| two | more than two |
|---|---|
| either of them | any of them |
| neither of them | none of them |
| both of them | all of them |

- There are two pharmacies nearby. You can go to either of them. / There are several pharmacies. You can go to any of them.
- We asked two neighbours; neither of them knew. / We asked many neighbours; none of them knew.
- Both of the doors were locked. / All of the doors were locked.

**See also:** Unit 51C (neither do I / I don’t either), Units 85–86 (any), Units 86A, 88 (none, all), Unit 96B (both of whom / neither of which), Unit 110D (both).

### Question 35

#### Question
Which sentence is wrong, and how should it be corrected?

A) Both of my grandparents grew up on farms.
B) Both my grandparents grew up on farms.
C) Both us grew up on farms.
D) Both of us grew up on farms.

#### Answer
**C is wrong.** Before the pronouns *us / you / them* the word *of* is compulsory: **Both of us grew up on farms.** *Of* is optional only before a determiner + noun (*my grandparents*), which is why both A and B are correct. D is correct.

#### Correct variant
¯\_(ツ)_/¯

#### Variants
1. A — *of* is not allowed after *both*; it should be *Both my grandparents*.
2. B — *of* is missing; it should be *Both of my grandparents*.
3. C — *of* is required before *us*; it should be *Both of us*. (Correct)
4. D — it should be *Both we grew up on farms*.

### Question 36

#### Question
Combine the two sentences: "Malik didn’t call. Malik didn’t send a message."

#### Answer
**Malik neither called nor sent a message.** *Neither … nor* joins two negative ideas with a positive verb form. *Neither called or* mixes the pairs; *either … nor* is also a mixed pair; *didn’t neither call nor send* is a double negative, which English avoids.

#### Correct variant
¯\_(ツ)_/¯

#### Variants
1. Malik neither called or sent a message.
2. Malik either called nor sent a message.
3. Malik didn’t neither call nor send a message.
4. Malik neither called nor sent a message. (Correct)

### Question 38

#### Question
Two shops sold the jacket you wanted. Which sentence correctly says that you did not buy it in the first shop and did not buy it in the second?

#### Answer
**"I didn’t buy it in either of the shops."** With a negative verb, *either of* = not one or the other. *Neither of the shops* would create a double negative with *didn’t*. *Both of the shops* with a negative verb is unnatural and ambiguous. *Either of shops* is missing the determiner *the*.

#### Correct variant
¯\_(ツ)_/¯

#### Variants
1. I didn’t buy it in neither of the shops.
2. I didn’t buy it in either of the shops. (Correct)
3. I didn’t buy it in both of the shops.
4. I didn’t buy it in either of shops.
