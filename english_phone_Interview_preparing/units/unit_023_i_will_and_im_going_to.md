# Unit 23 — I will and I'm going to

# Theory

## A. Future actions: decision now vs decision before

Both *will* and *(be) going to* talk about future actions, but they differ in **when the decision was made**.

| Form | When the decision is made | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **will** (I'll …) | **now**, at the moment of speaking | "We've run out of milk." "Have we? **I'll** pop to the shop." |
| **(be) going to** | **before** the moment of speaking | "Why are you putting your coat on?" "**I'm going to** pop to the shop." |

A short story to show the shift:

- Monday, Priya to Jamal: "Let's organise a leaving party for Sam." Jamal: "Good idea. **We'll** book the upstairs room at the pub." (new idea → *will*)
- Tuesday, Jamal to a colleague: "Priya and I are organising a leaving party. **We're going to** book the room at the pub." (already decided → *going to*)

More contrasts:

- "Your sister rang earlier." "Did she? OK, **I'll** ring her back." (decides now)
- "Your sister rang earlier." "I know. **I'm going to** ring her after dinner." (already planned)
- "Tom's broken his arm." "Oh no! **I'll** send him a card." (decides now)
- "Tom's broken his arm." "Yes, I heard. **I'm going to** visit him tomorrow." (already planned)

## B. Predictions: future happenings and situations

For predicting what **will happen** (not what someone will do), both forms are usually possible:

- I think the traffic **will be** bad later. / I think the traffic **is going to be** bad later.
- This laptop is well built. It**'ll** last for years. / It**'s going to** last for years.

However, use **going to** when the present situation gives clear **evidence** that something is about to happen:

- Look at the sky – it**'s going to** pour down. (not *it will pour* – the clouds are visible now)
- Slow down! You**'re going to** miss the turning. (I can see it coming)

Compare evidence-based *going to* with belief-based *will*:

- We**'re going to** miss the train. It leaves in three minutes and we're still two streets away. (the situation now makes it obvious)
- Marco **will** miss the train. He never leaves the house on time. (I believe this from what I know of Marco)

Common learner errors:

- ❌ Look at those clouds. It will rain. ✅ It**'s going to** rain.
- ❌ "I'm thirsty." "I'm going to get you some water." (as a spontaneous offer) ✅ "**I'll** get you some water."

### Question 32

#### Question
Read the two sentences and choose the correct explanation. (a) "We're going to be late – the meeting starts in five minutes and the lift is broken." (b) "Dana will be late – she's never on time for anything."

#### Answer
In (a), the present situation (five minutes left, broken lift) gives **evidence** that lateness is unavoidable, so *going to* is used. In (b), the speaker predicts Dana's lateness from **what they know about her character**, not from anything visible now, so *will* is used. Both sentences are correct; the difference is the basis for the prediction, not the degree of certainty or the time reference.

#### Correct variant
¯\_(ツ)_/¯

#### Variants
1. (a) is based on evidence in the present situation; (b) is based on the speaker's general knowledge or belief. (Correct)
2. (a) is about the distant future; (b) is about the very near future.
3. (b) is wrong – *will* cannot be used to predict lateness.
4. (a) is less certain than (b) because *going to* is always less definite.
