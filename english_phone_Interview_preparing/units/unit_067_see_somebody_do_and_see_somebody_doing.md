# Unit 67 — see somebody do and see somebody doing

# Theory

## A see somebody do (the whole action)

Picture this: a courier puts a parcel on your doorstep, rings the bell and leaves. You watched the whole thing. You can say:

- **I saw the courier leave** a parcel and ring the bell.

**see + somebody + bare infinitive** (*do*, not *to do*) = the person did the action and I witnessed it. The action is seen as complete.

Other perception verbs work the same way:

| verb | + object + bare infinitive |
|---|---|
| hear | I **heard the door slam**. |
| listen to | We **listened to the choir sing** the whole anthem. |
| watch | She **watched the plane take off**. |
| feel | He **felt something brush** against his leg. |

## B see somebody doing (in the middle of the action)

Now picture this: you drive past the park and notice your neighbour on a bench. She is reading. You can say:

- **I saw my neighbour reading** on a bench.

**see + somebody + -ing** = the person was in the middle of doing something when I noticed it. I did not necessarily see the beginning or the end.

The same pattern works with:

| verb | + object + -ing |
|---|---|
| hear | I can **hear someone playing** the piano upstairs. |
| listen to | **Listen to the wind howling**! |
| watch | We **watched the kids building** a sandcastle. |
| feel | I **felt the floor shaking**. |
| smell | Can you **smell something burning**? |
| find | We **found the cat sleeping** in the laundry basket. |

## C The difference in meaning

| I saw him **do** it | I saw him **doing** it |
|---|---|
| complete action, from start to finish | action in progress at the moment I saw it |
| *She locked the door and walked off. I saw this.* → I **saw her lock** the door and walk off. | *She was washing the car when I passed.* → I **saw her washing** the car. |
| *They came in. I heard this.* → I **heard them come in**. | *They were arguing.* → I **heard them arguing**. |

Compare: *I saw him **cross** the road* (I watched the whole crossing) vs *I saw him **crossing** the road* (he was halfway across when I looked).

Sometimes the distinction hardly matters and either is fine: *I’ve never heard her **sing** / **singing**.*

Common error: ❌ *I saw him **to leave**.* Never use *to* + infinitive after *see/hear/watch/feel* in this pattern.

**See also:** Past simple (I did) → Unit 5; Past continuous (I was doing) → Unit 6

### Question 29

#### Question
Your neighbour’s car alarm went off at 3 a.m., and you heard the whole thing – it started, blared for a minute and then stopped. Which sentence reports this correctly?

#### Answer
"I **heard the alarm go off** and then stop." After *hear* + object we use the bare infinitive (*go off*, *stop*) for a complete action. *To go off* is wrong – perception verbs never take *to* + infinitive in this pattern. *Went off* is a finite past form that cannot follow an object here. *Going off* would suggest you noticed the alarm already in progress rather than hearing the whole event start and stop.

#### Correct variant
¯\_(ツ)_/¯

#### Variants
1. I heard the alarm to go off and then stop.
2. I heard the alarm went off and then stopped.
3. I heard the alarm go off and then stop. (Correct)
4. I heard the alarm goes off and then stop.

You walked past the school playground at lunchtime. The children were in the middle of a football game when you passed. Which sentence describes what you saw?

#### Answer

The -ing form shows that the action was in progress when you noticed it; you saw part of the game, not the whole match. *Saw the children play football* would suggest you watched a complete action from start to finish, which does not fit "walked past". *To play* is never used after *see*, and *played* is a finite verb form that cannot follow the object.

#### Correct variant
¯\_(ツ)_/¯

#### Variants

### Question 31

#### Question
Which sentence contains a mistake, and why?

A. Can you smell something burning in the kitchen?
B. We watched the sun set behind the hills.
C. I felt someone to tap me on the shoulder.
D. When I got home I found my brother sitting on the doorstep.

#### Answer
Sentence **C** is wrong: "I felt someone **tap** me on the shoulder." After *feel* + object we use the bare infinitive (*tap*) or the -ing form (*tapping*), never *to* + infinitive. Sentence A correctly uses *smell something -ing* (an ongoing situation). Sentence B correctly uses *watch + object + bare infinitive* for a complete event. Sentence D correctly uses *find somebody doing*.

#### Correct variant
¯\_(ツ)_/¯

#### Variants
1. Sentence A – *smell something burning* should be *smell something burn*.
2. Sentence B – *watched the sun set* should be *watched the sun to set*.
3. Sentence C – *felt someone to tap* should be *felt someone tap*. (Correct)
4. Sentence D – *found my brother sitting* should be *found my brother to sit*.

### Question 32

#### Question
Compare: (1) "I heard Yara close the front door." (2) "I heard Yara closing the front door." Which statement about the difference is correct?

#### Answer
Sentence (1) means the door was closed – the action was complete – and I heard it happen; sentence (2) means I heard the sound of the door while it was being closed (the action in progress). Both are grammatical, so the option saying (2) is wrong is incorrect. The bare infinitive is not more formal, and there is no difference of tense: both are about the past.

#### Correct variant
¯\_(ツ)_/¯

#### Variants
1. Sentence (1) describes the complete action; sentence (2) describes the action in progress when I heard it. (Correct)
2. Sentence (2) is ungrammatical; only the bare infinitive is possible after *hear*.
3. Sentence (1) is more formal; otherwise the meaning is identical.
4. Sentence (1) refers to the past and sentence (2) to the present.

#### Question

#### Answer
"Listen! I can hear a baby **crying** next door." The situation is happening right now and is ongoing, so the -ing form after *hear + object* is the natural choice. *Cry* (bare infinitive) is grammatically possible but suggests a single complete event rather than an ongoing sound, so it is not the best fit with "Listen!". *To cry* and *cries* are ungrammatical after *hear a baby*.
