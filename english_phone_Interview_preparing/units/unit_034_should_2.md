# Unit 34 — should 2

# Theory

## A. should after certain verbs and adjectives

*Should* is often used in a **that**-clause after verbs that express a demand, recommendation or suggestion:

| verb | example |
|---|---|
| insist | The manager **insisted that** every visitor **should sign** in. |
| demand | Residents are **demanding that** the council **should repair** the road. |
| recommend | The vet **recommended that** the dog **should lose** some weight. |
| suggest | What do you **suggest** we **should bring**? |
| propose | She **proposed that** the meeting **should move** online. |

The same pattern follows **It's important / vital / necessary / essential that …**:

- It's **vital that** the documents **should reach** the embassy before Friday.

## B. leaving out should – the subjunctive

In all the sentences in A you can drop *should* and use the **bare infinitive** (the same for every person: *be / do / sign*). This form is called the **subjunctive**:

- It's essential that every visitor **sign** in. (= should sign)
- She proposed that the meeting **move** online.
- Residents are demanding that the road **be repaired**.

Ordinary present or past forms are also possible:

- It's essential that every visitor **signs** in.
- The manager insisted that we **signed** in.

## C. suggest is never followed by to

With **suggest**, three patterns are correct and one is a common mistake:

| ✅ | ❌ |
|---|---|
| I suggested that he **should take** a break. | *I suggested him to take a break.* |
| I suggested that he **take** a break. | *What do you suggest me to do?* |
| I suggested that he **took** a break. | |
| What do you suggest **doing**? (-ing, see Unit 53) | |

## D. should after adjectives of surprise / typicality

After adjectives such as **strange, odd, funny, typical, natural, interesting, surprised, surprising**, *should* is common in the *that*-clause:

- It's **odd that** the lights **should** still **be on** at this hour.
- I was **surprised that** she **should refuse** such a generous offer.
- It's only **natural that** children **should** ask difficult questions.

## E. if … should … / Should … 

**If something should happen** presents the possibility as smaller or less expected than *if something happens*:

- We're fully booked, but **if a room should become** available, we'll call you.

You can also start the sentence with **Should**:

- **Should a room become** available, we'll call you. (= If a room becomes available …)

## F. I should … = my advice

**I should / I shouldn't** can mean *if I were you, I would / wouldn't* – a way of giving advice:

- "Shall I book the earlier flight?" "**I should** wait until the prices drop." (= I advise you to wait)
- "Is the water safe to drink?" "**I shouldn't** risk it. Buy bottled."

**See also:** should 1 ➜ Unit 33; American English ➜ Appendix 7.

### Question 31

#### Question
A landlord said to his tenants: "The deposit has to be paid before you move in." Which sentence correctly reports this with *demand*?

#### Answer
"The landlord **demanded that** the tenants **should pay** the deposit before moving in." After *demand*, *insist*, *recommend*, *suggest* and *propose*, we use a *that*-clause with *should* + bare infinitive (or the subjunctive *pay*, or an ordinary tense *paid*). "Demanded the tenants to pay" is wrong because *demand* is not followed by object + *to*-infinitive. "Demanded that the tenants should to pay" wrongly adds *to* after *should*, and "should paying" is not a possible form.

2. The landlord demanded that the tenants should to pay the deposit before moving in.
3. The landlord demanded that the tenants should pay the deposit before moving in. (Correct)

### Question 33

#### Question
Complete the sentence in the formal style of a notice: "It is essential that every applicant ______ present in person at the interview." Which option uses the subjunctive correctly?

#### Answer
"It is essential that every applicant **be** present in person." When *should* is left out after *It's essential/important/vital that …*, the verb takes the bare infinitive form (*be*), identical for all persons – this is the subjunctive. "Is" would also be a correct ordinary present-tense form, but of the four options only *be* is a correct subjunctive; "to be" wrongly inserts *to*, "should to be" wrongly adds *to* after *should*, and "being" is not possible after *that every applicant*.

#### Correct variant
¯\_(ツ)_/¯

#### Variants
1. to be
2. being
3. be (Correct)
4. should to be

### Question 34

#### Question
A hotel receptionist says: "We have no vacancies this week, but ______, we will let you know at once." Which option correctly expresses a small possibility?

#### Answer
"… but **should a room become available**, we will let you know at once." Beginning a clause with *Should* + subject + bare infinitive is the same as *If a room should become available*; *should* here signals that the speaker regards the possibility as fairly small. "If a room would become available" is wrong because *would* is not normally used in the *if*-clause. "Should a room becomes available" wrongly adds -s to the infinitive after *should*. "If should a room become available" combines *if* and inverted *should* incorrectly – use one or the other.

#### Correct variant

### Question 35

#### Question
Your friend asks: "Shall I send the complaint email tonight while I'm angry?" You reply: "No, I should sleep on it." What does *I should* mean in your reply?

#### Answer
Here *I should sleep on it* means "**if I were you, I would** sleep on it" – i.e. *I advise you to wait until tomorrow*. This is a special use of *I should / I shouldn't* to give advice; it is not a statement about the speaker's own plans or obligations. So it does not mean that the speaker personally needs to sleep, nor that the speaker expects to sleep on it, nor is it a deduction about the past.

#### Correct variant
¯\_(ツ)_/¯

#### Variants
1. The speaker is saying that they themselves have an obligation to go to bed.
2. The speaker is predicting that they will probably fall asleep.
3. The speaker is advising the friend: "if I were you, I would wait until tomorrow". (Correct)
4. The speaker is expressing regret about something they did in the past.
