# Unit 116 — as (as I walked … / as I was … etc.)

# Theory

## A as = at the same time as

We use **as** when two things happen **together**, at the same moment:

- The crowd cheered **as** the runners crossed the line. (cheering and crossing happened together)
- **As** I cycled through the park, I could smell the blossom.
- Could you lock the door **as** you leave?

**As** is also used when something happens **in the middle of** another action. The longer action is often in the past continuous (see Unit 6):

- Nour dropped her keys **as** she **was getting** out of the taxi.
- We bumped into Ali **as** we **were coming** out of the cinema.

**Just as** = exactly at that moment:

- **Just as** the film started, my phone rang.
- The rain began **just as** we were setting up the tent.

**As** also links two gradual changes that develop together over a longer period:

- **As** the evening went on, the music got louder.
- The job became easier **as** I gained experience.

### as vs when

| as | when |
|---|---|
| two things happen **at the same time** | one thing happens **after** another |
| **As** I drove to work, I listened to the news. (at the same time) | **When** I arrived at work, I made a coffee. ❌ *as I arrived* |

Use **when**, not **as**, for a single event that is followed by another, and for ages and periods: *When I was a child …*, *She left home when she was eighteen.* (not *as she was eighteen*).

## B as = because

**As** can also mean **because**, usually at the start of the sentence:

- **As** it was getting dark, we headed back to the car. (= because it was getting dark)
- **As** you're here anyway, could you sign this form?
- We stayed in all weekend **as** the weather was miserable.

**Since** is used in the same way: *Since it was getting dark, we headed back.*

### as (= because) vs when

| as = because | when = at the time that |
|---|---|
| Mia missed the meeting **as** she was ill. (= because she was ill) | Mia caught a cold **when** she was on holiday. (= during that time) |
| **As** we lived by the sea, we swam most days. (reason) | **When** we lived by the sea, we swam most days. (time period) |

## Summary of the two meanings

| meaning | typical clue | example |
|---|---|---|
| at the same time as | continuous action, *just as*, gradual change | She waved **as** the train pulled out. |
| because | a reason for a decision or state | **As** nobody answered, I left a note. |

### Question 34

#### Question
Compare the two sentences: (a) "Sam wasn't at the party **as** he was working abroad." (b) "Sam broke his arm **when** he was working abroad." Which explanation of the difference is correct?

#### Answer
In (a) *as* means *because*: working abroad is the reason he wasn't at the party. In (b) *when* means "during the time that": the accident happened during his period abroad, and there is no idea of cause. Options 1 and 3 reverse or blur this contrast, and option 4 is wrong because *as* would not be natural in (b) – a single event that happened during a longer period is expressed with *when* or *while*, not *as* in the "because" sense.

#### Correct variant
¯\_(ツ)_/¯

#### Variants
1. Both *as* and *when* mean "at the time that" here; the sentences differ only in style.
2. In (a) *as* = because (a reason); in (b) *when* = during the time that (a time, not a reason). (Correct)
3. In (a) *as* = at the same time as; in (b) *when* = because.
4. The words are interchangeable: (b) could equally be "Sam broke his arm as he was working abroad" with the same meaning.

### Question 35

#### Question
You want to say that your phone rang at the exact moment you sat down to eat. Which sentence expresses this most precisely?

#### Answer
"**Just as** I sat down to eat, my phone rang." *Just as* means "exactly at that moment" and links two events that coincide. "When I sat down" is grammatical but simply orders the events without stressing that they happened at the same instant. "As soon as" means immediately after – it does not convey simultaneity in the same way. "Because I sat down" turns sitting down into the cause of the call, which is nonsense.

#### Correct variant
¯\_(ツ)_/¯

#### Variants
1. Because I sat down to eat, my phone rang.
2. As soon as I sat down to eat, my phone rang.
3. Just as I sat down to eat, my phone rang. (Correct)
4. When I sat down to eat, my phone rang.
