# Unit 2 — Present simple (I do)

# Theory

## A Form

Imagine Priya, a pilot, who is at home watching a film. She *isn't flying* a plane right now, **but** *she flies* planes — that is her job. *Fly / flies*, *work / works*, *do / does* are the present simple.

| Subject | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| I / we / you / they | base verb | I teach, they live |
| he / she / it | base verb + -s / -es | she teaches, it lives |

Spelling notes: *watch → watches, go → goes, study → studies, have → has*. ❌ *He work* ✅ *He works*.

## B General truths and repeated actions

The present simple is for things that are true in general, happen all the time, or happen repeatedly:

- Dentists *look after* people's teeth.
- I usually *visit* my grandmother on Sundays.
- Water *freezes* at 0 degrees Celsius.
- The library *closes* at 8 p.m. on weekdays.

## C Questions and negatives with do/does

| | Question | Negative |
|---|---|---|
| I / we / you / they | **Do** you live here? | I **don't** live here. |
| he / she / it | **Does** she work? | He **doesn't** work. |

- 'Where *do* you *come* from?' 'From Ghana.'
- Bananas *don't grow* in Norway.
- What *does* this sign *mean*? (not What means this sign?)

*Do* can also be the main verb, so you get *do … do* / *doesn't do*:

- 'What *do* you *do*?' 'I'm an accountant.'
- He never *does* anything around the house.

## D How often

Use the present simple with frequency expressions (every day, once a week, often, never):

- I *check* my email every morning.
- How often *do* you *go* swimming?
- Marco *doesn't eat* meat very often.
- We usually *travel* abroad once or twice a year.

## E Performative verbs: I promise / I apologise

Some verbs perform an action just by being said. When you promise, suggest, apologise, advise, insist, agree or refuse, use the present simple, not the continuous:

- *I promise* I'll call you tonight. (not I'm promising)
- 'What *do* you *suggest*?' '*I suggest* we take the train.'
- *I apologise* for the delay. *I agree* with you completely. *I refuse* to sign this.

❌ *I'm apologising for being late.* ✅ *I apologise for being late.*

### Question 37

#### Question
Choose the correct sentence about a general fact.

#### Answer
"The Nile flows through Egypt" is correct. General truths and permanent facts take the present simple, and with a third-person singular subject (the Nile = it) the verb needs -s: *flows*. "The Nile flow" is missing the -s ending; "The Nile is flowing" uses the continuous, which suggests a temporary action in progress rather than a permanent geographical fact; "The Nile does flow" is grammatically possible only for emphasis and is not the neutral way to state a fact.

#### Correct variant
¯\_(ツ)_/¯

1. The Nile flow through Egypt.
2. The Nile is flowing through Egypt.
3. The Nile flows through Egypt. (Correct)

### Question 38

#### Question
You see an unfamiliar word in a text and ask a friend. Which question is correct?

#### Answer
"What does this word mean?" is correct. Present simple questions with a third-person singular subject need the auxiliary *does* + subject + base verb (without -s). "What means this word?" puts the main verb before the subject without an auxiliary — a very common learner error. "What does this word means?" doubles the -s marking (does + means). "What do this word mean?" uses *do* instead of *does* for a singular subject.

#### Correct variant
¯\_(ツ)_/¯

#### Variants
1. What means this word?
2. What does this word means?
3. What do this word mean?
4. What does this word mean? (Correct)

#### Question
Your friend Ahmed says he is a firefighter. You want to ask about his job in general. Which question uses *do* correctly as both auxiliary and main verb?

#### Answer
"What do you do exactly?" is correct. Here the first *do* is the auxiliary needed for a present simple question, and the second *do* is the main verb meaning "work as / perform". "What do you exactly?" drops the main verb entirely. "What are you doing exactly?" (present continuous) asks about the activity in progress right now, not his occupation. "What you do exactly?" is missing the auxiliary.

### Question 40

#### Question
A waiter brings the wrong dish. Which apology is grammatically appropriate?

#### Answer
"I apologise — I'll bring the right one immediately" is correct. *Apologise*, like *promise*, *suggest*, *insist*, *agree* and *refuse*, is a verb that performs an action simply by being said, so it takes the present simple even though the action is happening right now. "I'm apologising" (continuous) is the tempting error but is not used in this way. "I apologises" has a wrong -s for the subject *I*, and "I do apologising" is not a valid form.

#### Correct variant
¯\_(ツ)_/¯

#### Variants
1. I'm apologising — I'll bring the right one immediately.
2. I apologises — I'll bring the right one immediately.
3. I apologise — I'll bring the right one immediately. (Correct)
4. I do apologising — I'll bring the right one immediately.
