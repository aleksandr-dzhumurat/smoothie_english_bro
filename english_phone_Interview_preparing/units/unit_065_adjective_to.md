# Unit 65 — Adjective + to …

# Theory

## A hard to understand, easy to use etc.

Two ways of saying the same thing:

| (a) it + adjective + to … + object | (b) subject + adjective + to … |
|---|---|
| It is difficult **to read his handwriting**. | His handwriting is difficult **to read**. |
| It’s dangerous **to swim in this river**. | This river is dangerous **to swim in**. |

In pattern (b) the thing being talked about is already the subject, so we do **not** repeat it as an object after the verb:
- ✅ His handwriting is difficult to read. ❌ His handwriting is difficult to read **it**.
- ✅ Our neighbours are nice to talk to. ❌ … nice to talk to **them**.

Adjectives used this way include: *easy, difficult, hard, impossible, safe, dangerous, cheap, expensive, nice, good, interesting, exciting.*

The pattern also works with **adjective + noun + to …**:
- That’s **an easy mistake to make**. (❌ *to make it*)
- Kyoto is **a wonderful city to visit**.

## B nice of you to …

To comment on somebody’s behaviour, use **it’s + adjective + of + person + to …**:
- **It was kind of Leo to lend** me his bike.
- **It’s careless of them to leave** the door unlocked.
- **It was typical of Mia to arrive** with a cake for everyone.

Adjectives here describe people’s character or actions: *kind, nice, generous, careless, silly, stupid, inconsiderate, unfair, typical.*

## C sorry to … / glad to … (reactions)

Adjective + **to …** describes how somebody reacts to something:
- **I was sorry to hear** about the accident.
- **We were relieved to find** the passports in the drawer.
- **Were you surprised to get** the promotion?
- **They were disappointed to learn** the concert was cancelled.

Adjectives: *glad, pleased, happy, relieved, sorry, sad, surprised, amazed, shocked, disappointed.*

## D the first / the last / the only … to …

After **the first, the second, the next, the last, the only** (etc.) use **to …**:
- Amara was **the first person to finish** the marathon.
- **The next bus to leave** is the number 12.
- He was **the only student to get** full marks.
- You’ll be **the last to hear** about it, as usual.

## E sure / likely / bound to …

- The road is icy – **there’s bound to be** an accident. (= it is sure to happen)
- She trains every day, so **she’s sure to win**.
- **It isn’t likely to snow** in April here. (= it is not probable)

### Question 30

#### Question
Rewrite "It is impossible to open this jar" starting with *This jar*. Which version is correct?

#### Answer
"**This jar is impossible to open.**" When the object of the infinitive becomes the subject of the sentence, it must not be repeated after the verb, so *to open it* is wrong. *For opening* is not used with this adjective pattern, and *to be opened* is unnecessary – the active infinitive is the normal form after adjectives like *impossible*, *easy*, *hard*.

#### Correct variant
¯\_(ツ)_/¯

#### Variants
1. This jar is impossible to open it.
2. This jar is impossible to open. (Correct)
3. This jar is impossible for opening.
4. This jar is impossible to be opened.

### Question 31

#### Question
Your colleague stayed late to help you finish a report she wasn’t responsible for. Which sentence is the correct way to thank her?

#### Answer
"It was **kind of you to stay** and help." To comment on a person’s action we use *it’s + adjective + of + person + to-infinitive*. *Kind for you* is a frequent error – *for* is used with adjectives describing difficulty (*hard for me to say*), not with adjectives of character. *Kind of you staying* and *kind of you that you stay* do not follow the pattern.

#### Correct variant
¯\_(ツ)_/¯

#### Variants
1. It was kind for you to stay and help.
2. It was kind of you staying and help.
3. It was kind of you to stay and help. (Correct)
4. It was kind of you that you stay and help.

### Question 32

#### Question
Which sentence contains a mistake, and why?

A. We were relieved to hear that the flight had landed safely.
B. I was surprised of seeing my old teacher at the concert.
C. Everyone was glad to get out of the rain.
D. He was disappointed not to be chosen for the team.

#### Answer
Sentence **B** is wrong. Adjectives expressing a reaction (*surprised, glad, relieved, sorry, disappointed*) are followed by a to-infinitive: "I was **surprised to see** my old teacher." *Surprised of seeing* mixes up the reaction pattern with a preposition structure (*surprised at/by something*). Sentences A and C use adjective + *to …* correctly, and D shows the correct negative form *not to be chosen*.

#### Correct variant
¯\_(ツ)_/¯

#### Variants
1. Sentence A – *relieved to hear* should be *relieved hearing*.
2. Sentence B – *surprised of seeing* should be *surprised to see*. (Correct)
3. Sentence C – *glad to get* should be *glad for getting*.
4. Sentence D – *not to be chosen* should be *to not been chosen*.

### Question 33

#### Question
Complete the sentence: "Nobody else in the office had used the new software before Lena. She was the first ______ it."

#### Answer
"She was **the first to use** it." After *the first / the last / the only / the next* we use a to-infinitive. *The first using it* and *the first who using it* are ungrammatical. *The first who used it* is possible English, but it is not the adjective + *to …* structure this unit teaches and is not among the correct uses here – the standard, most natural form is *the first to use it*.

#### Correct variant
¯\_(ツ)_/¯

#### Variants
1. the first using
2. the first to use (Correct)
3. the first who using
4. the first for using

### Question 34

#### Question
The weather forecast says there is a 90 per cent chance of thunderstorms this evening. Which sentence expresses this idea correctly?

#### Answer
"There **are bound to be** thunderstorms this evening." *Bound to* means "sure to happen" and is followed by a to-infinitive; with *there* the verb agrees with the plural noun *thunderstorms*, so *are bound to be*. *Bound for being* misuses *for -ing*; *likely that to be* is not a real structure; and *bound to are* puts a finite verb after *to*.

#### Correct variant
¯\_(ツ)_/¯

#### Variants
1. There are bound for being thunderstorms this evening.
2. There are bound to are thunderstorms this evening.
3. There is likely that to be thunderstorms this evening.
4. There are bound to be thunderstorms this evening. (Correct)
