## Dialog — Daily Standup & Sprint Planning

**Samira:** All right, let's kick off, shall we? I suggest we start by having a look at the current tasks.

In sprint 14 we managed to complete tasks 301, 302, 303, and 304. And I can see that task 305 is in progress. Anton, when do you expect to complete it?

**Anton:** I think it should take me another two days unless something goes wrong, so I think I'll have it done by Wednesday.

**Samira:** OK, great. And Sasha seems to have completed all his tasks from the previous sprint except for Task 306 which is marked as blocked for the time being. Sasha, what's preventing you from completing it?

**Sasha:** Well, I discovered that we don't have the weather data in the database. Amir promised to add it this week so I expect to finish task 306 by the end of next week at the latest. Would that be okay?

**Samira:** Well, it's not exactly a top priority item — I suggest we don't include it in this sprint then. All right, now let's go over the backlog.

**Anne:** Sorry, can I jump in here? I've got an update on task 307. The marketing team's plans changed a little, so this task is really urgent now — it's very important that we add it to this week's sprint.

**Samira:** OK, let's change its priority to "high". Right then, let's assign owners. Anton, shall we assign task 307 to you?

**Anton:** Well, I might not be able to accomplish it in this sprint. I'll be working on the research until Wednesday, and I may not have enough time for task 307 — it's a bit too complex to rush through. How about breaking it down and working on the technical approach alongside the business approval?

**Anne:** I'm afraid it's not a very good idea to work on the technical part before we approve the methodology — but we might not have another choice, considering the deadline.

**Sasha:** I think I'll have time to do the technical part, so you can assign it to me. As a ballpark figure I'd say 15 hours should be okay.

**Samira:** Great, thanks! OK, we also have task 309, which is high priority. Sasha, I'm assuming you'll be working on that too?

**Sasha:** I'm not sure it'd be possible considering my time estimates. I promised Mark I'd interpret the results of the A/B test — that's task 312. And I've just realized that my original estimate is a bit off, it could potentially take longer because of the noisy samples. As for task 309, would it be a good idea to adjust its priority or scope?

**Samira:** Well, it doesn't seem feasible to fit it into the current sprint, so perhaps we could leave it out.

## Dealing with Misunderstandings

### Asking to Repeat
- What do you mean by …?
- Could you elaborate on …?
- Sorry, I didn't catch *[that]*. Could you say *[the last bit]* again?
- Would you mind repeating that to me again / going over that one more time?
- Could you be more specific?

### Rephrasing to Check Understanding
- I think you are saying …
- In other words …
- If I understand you correctly, …
- So basically what you're saying is … Is that correct/right?
- Am I reading your suggestion right, when you said …?

### Asking for Clarification
- What do you mean by "minor changes"?
- Could you elaborate on this process please?

### Building Rapport
- Thank you for clearing that up. I understand better now.
- Thank you for the clarification. It makes more sense to me.

### Summarizing
- To make sure we're on the same page, let me sum up what we've *[discussed / talked about / agreed on]*.
- So, (just) to recap, …
- We seem to be on the same page now.

## Dialog — Requirements Gathering with Clarification

**Maria:** So, we're here today to get a better understanding of the problem you'd like me to look into. Could you briefly explain to me what the problem is?

**Amit:** Sure. Two weeks ago we made a few changes to the app onboarding — added new product tour screens and updated the Terms and Conditions, and since then there's been a significant decline in the number of users that completed registration. This drop doesn't seem to have anything to do with the number of downloads.

**Maria:** So basically what you're saying is that the number of downloads hasn't changed, so the problem should lie somewhere in the updated onboarding. Is that correct?

**Amit:** Yes, pretty much.

**Maria:** Could you tell me why it was decided to change the onboarding?

**Amit:** We discovered that quite a large proportion of users found it difficult to locate some app functions. So we added a couple of screens showing how to navigate through the app more easily.

**Maria:** Erm… What do you mean by "changed slightly"? Could you elaborate on that?

**Amit:** The UX designers insisted on replacing some images and fonts — those alterations were mostly design-related.

**Maria:** OK, thank you for clearing that up. … So, what I'm planning to do is build a funnel to see the onboarding stages where users drop off. Do we already have analytics events set up?

**Maria:** Sorry, I didn't catch that — you were breaking up a little. Could you say the last bit again?

**Maria:** … If I understand you correctly, at this stage we won't be looking at users from other cities, and we won't be considering those whose device is neither iOS nor Android. Is that right?

**Maria:** Great. So, to make sure we're on the same page, let me sum up what we've discussed. The journey data has been collected since last week and the events were also identified. We'll be looking at users from Moscow and St Petersburg who use iOS and Android operating systems. I'll prepare a report in Jupyter Notebook with my findings.

### Politely Interjecting in Meetings
1. **Asking for Permission:** "Do you mind if I jump in?"
2. **Acknowledging Contributions:** "I appreciate your insights, but I'd like to add..."
3. **Seeking Clarification:** "Forgive me, but I'm a little confused about..."
4. **Disagreeing Politely:** "I respectfully disagree..." / "To play devil's advocate, what if we considered...?"
5. **Transitioning:** "Can we save this for another meeting?" / "Let's focus on our agenda for now."

## Retrospectives

### Resources
- [The secret to giving great feedback](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=wtl5UrrgU8c)
- [How to Give and Receive Feedback in English](https://youtu.be/1hJOKm01F10)

### Tips for Retros
1. Invest time in preparation. Think over positives and what contributed to them; analyze negatives and what they resulted from.
2. On a Miro board, formulate a short phrase that signalizes a problem or highlights a success.
3. When speaking, provide facts and details to show reasons and results.
4. Analyze the team's performance without personalizing it.
5. Describe what you think objectively, not emotionally.
6. Take part in discussions of action items: join, disagree, deal with disagreements politely.

### Saying What Went Well
- I thought it was very helpful to …
- It was really nice to see how …

### Saying What Didn't Go Well
- I wish I'd/we'd [3rd form of verb]
  - *I wish I'd found out about this request before the sprint started.*
- I had a hard time [verb+*ing*] because …
  - *I had a hard time figuring out how to re-prioritize my tasks because of the new requirement.*
- because/as/since [subject+verb]
- because of [noun]
- So, / As a result, [subject+verb]

### Suggesting Improvements
- I think it would be very helpful if we could …
- It might be better to …
- We've got to make sure that …

### Retro Examples
- I wish we'd approved the methodology before we started working on the technical part.
- It would be very helpful if the team could have a tech meet-up once a week for a sanity check.
- I had a hard time meeting the deadlines because of the changing requirements.
- It might be better to put such tasks on hold instead of trying to squeeze them into the sprint.
- I thought it was very helpful to discuss the estimates as a team in the presence of the customer.
- We've got to make sure we write follow-up emails after every meeting with the stakeholders.
- It was really nice to see how the team kind of self-managed and was able to come to a decision on their own.

## Having a Productive Discussion

### Taking and Keeping Your Turn
- Sorry to interrupt.
- Sorry to butt in. *(informal)*
- Can I just say something here?
- Sorry, just one more thing.

### Stopping an Unproductive Discussion
- I suppose that's a little off topic.
- Can we park that (until the end of the meeting) and first look at …?

### Dealing with Misunderstanding
- Sorry, that's not what I mean.
- Sorry if I didn't make that clear.

### Discussion Case

> **Naomi:** The switch to the new VM hasn't been smooth. Unfortunately it's not as powerful as the previous one and it's slowing us down.
> **Linda:** I see your point but we had to make the decision fast.
> **Keira:** Oh, by the way, have you heard that the business team has a new team member?
> **Naomi:** Yeah, but I suppose that's a little off topic. Let's focus on our VM first.
> **Jan:** Sorry to butt in. I have to add that we've already lost five working days just adapting to it.
> **Linda:** So what can we do to improve the situation?
> **Naomi:** Well, it would be great if the machines had at least 16 GB of RAM. And we've got to make sure that all the drivers for working with databases are already installed. What do you think?
> **Linda:** Right, that makes sense. I'll see what we can do about that.
> **Keira:** Do you mean you'll ask the support team to install the necessary drivers?
> **Linda:** Sorry, that's not what I mean. I wanted to say that I'll find out if we can change the current VMs.

## Performance Reviews

### Resources
- [YouTube Video 1](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=_9Gr4cE4S0I)
- [YouTube Video 2](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=dugO0ISh2-Y)

### Tips for a Successful Performance Review
1. Review the agenda, prepare a list of questions.
2. Come up with examples referring to specific tasks that demonstrate your best work. Mention the impact and value.
3. Think of professional areas that could be improved. Understanding weak points is as important as describing strengths.
4. Use "positive language" — avoid "I'm bad at…", instead say "Here's an area I want to work on".
5. Ask about future expectations from your manager.
6. Think of examples when you showed initiative or helped team members.

### Setting Objectives
- One thing I'm going to do is …
- I intend to …
- I'd like to focus on …

### Commenting on Progress
- I'm really happy with …
- I've put a lot of effort in …
- I'm making progress in / with …
- *[Over the last month]* I've been working with [someone] / on [project]
- I feel I did a great job *[leading the team]*
- I struggled to …

### Talking About Areas for Improvement
- Something I need to work on is …
- I think … is an area for improvement.

### Performance Review Vocabulary
- **become better at something** = get more professional at something
- **a sense of achievement** = a proud feeling of success
- **room for improvement** = the possibility to improve/grow
- **be involved in something** = having a part in something
- **benefit from something** = receive an advantage
- **expand my skills/knowledge** = develop some skills/knowledge
- **try my hand at something** = start doing some new activity

### Example: Natasha (Senior Analyst)
> I'm really happy with how I managed to improve my leadership skills. Before the current project I'd been actively involved in mentoring. But this project was the first time I had a chance to lead a team. So I've put a lot of effort into communicating goals, setting specific deadlines, and facilitating meetings. I feel I did a great job leading five people and I got really positive feedback during the team retro. I intend to develop this skill set further.
>
> I believe that my ability to work in a team and take the initiative has been quite valuable. I've been involved in a lot of processes and communicated proactively with the team and stakeholders. The stakeholders really appreciated that and their feedback gave me a great sense of achievement.
>
> Something I'd like to work on in the next 6 months is my AWS expertise. One thing I'm going to do is take a training course.

### Example: Pasha (Junior Analyst)
> On my current project I'm involved in requirements gathering. At first, when I was conducting meetings with stakeholders, I struggled to cover all the important things and stay within the time limit. So, I decided to learn more about meeting facilitation. I applied some tips — I started sharing the agenda before the meeting and sending follow-up emails after it. I feel I'm making progress with meeting facilitation but I believe there's still room for improvement.
>
> For the next 6 months I'd like to focus on mentoring. I've been working with one mentee for the last month and I feel it has helped me expand my communication and organizational skills. I'd like to try my hand at mentoring a group.

### Rephrasing Drills
- *I had a chance to try my hand at machine learning* ➞ I had an opportunity to work with machine learning.
- *I intend to expand my skills in machine learning.* ➞ I'm going to learn more about machine learning.
- *I've become better at data storytelling.* ➞ My data storytelling skills have improved.
- *Demonstrating my dashboard for the first time gave me a real sense of achievement.* ➞ I felt really proud when I first demonstrated my dashboard.
- *I think I can benefit from a training course on negotiations.* ➞ A training course on negotiations may be helpful for me.

## Brainstorming Tips

1. Each person should have a chance to contribute.
2. Wait for the right moment to interrupt if you want to link a point to someone's idea.
3. Avoid criticizing others' ideas — say *"That's a valid point, but…"*
4. Build on other people's ideas.
5. Ideas should lead to making a decision or identifying next steps.

### Tips on Demonstrating Critical Thinking in Interviews

1. **Know what to demonstrate:** identifying needs, communicating ideas, choosing the right type of communication, convincing people.
2. **Research the company culture** to choose the most relevant case.
3. **Choose a specific example:** brainstorming session, making a difficult decision, resolving a disagreement, convincing a stakeholder.
4. **Focus on your actions:** describe how you gathered, analyzed, prioritized, and communicated information.
5. **Use the STAR method.**

### Useful Language — Critical Thinking

**Starting the story:**
- I must admit *[I haven't had a lot of situations like that]*.

**Focusing on your actions:**
- I highlighted (that …) / I pointed out (that …)
- What we did was we *[tested both approaches]*.

**Sequencing actions:**
- While/before/after [verb+*ing*] …, I [past form] …

**Mentioning details:**
- Through my analysis, it became clear (that …)
- As it turned out …

**Signaling the end:**
- (I think) that's basically it.

## Product Owner / Stakeholder Case Study

> While I understand that Helen was trying very hard to make everyone happy, from my perspective, it wasn't right to plan the release dates before discussing them with the development team.
>
> I just wanted to add that Helen doesn't have a technical background. It's common for the development team to see problems with the requirements that Helen can't notice. So, in my opinion, Helen should trust the development team more.
>
> While people criticizing Helen do have a valid point, there is a problem with the development team's behavior — they aren't really making an effort to help her understand what's blocking the development.
>
> To be honest, I think the main problem is understanding the responsibilities within the team. I think you should sit together, talk about ways of working, and agree on what your roles and responsibilities are, what is acceptable, and what isn't.
